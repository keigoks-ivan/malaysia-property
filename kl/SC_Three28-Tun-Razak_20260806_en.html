◆ Deep Dive

Project deep dive

Three28 Tun Razak sits on Jalan Tun Razak, directly adjacent to the site of the former Ampang Park shopping centre — now rebuilt as the Ampang Park MRT2 station (Putrajaya Line, opened March 2023), a 610m, roughly 7-minute walk away. This is the first scorecard on this site to cover the Keramat / Jalan Tun Razak district.

Title classification is this project's central question and the core finding of this review: although some sources describe it as a "freehold residential condominium," Tan & Tan Developments' own website, PropertyGuru and iProperty all independently and consistently classify Three28 as a "freehold condominium with commercial title" — in substance a Service Residence, not a residential-titled property. This matches the pattern seen across this site's earlier reviews of near-MRT KL condos like Aria Residences, Nadi Bangsar and 8 Kia Peng: even a project completed in 2016, before the "commercial SA" trend fully took over the market, still carries commercial title. The hypothesis that residential title could help a near-KLCC location break through this site's scoring ceiling again fails to hold here.

On scarcity, the project's low-density configuration — 166 units on 0.76 acres, a single block — is genuinely rare within the Jalan Tun Razak corridor, especially set against Residensi Tun Razak 2 (MKH Group, 918 units across twin towers) now under construction in the same corridor. The Brickz same-building transacted median of ~RM 958 psf runs about 69% above the district comparable M City (Ampang, ~RM 566 psf), showing the market genuinely pays a premium for this low-density, freehold product — this is not entirely "false scarcity." But the land layer remains uncapped — large new projects continue to be built in the corridor — and only about 5 verifiable Brickz transactions exist for the project itself, so the floor scarcity provides is limited.

On valuation, this review resolved the near-2x gap flagged in initial research between a "Brickz median of RM 656,500 (~RM 425 psf)" and current listing prices of RM 851-1,421 psf. That RM 425 psf figure turned out to be Brickz's aggregate median across the entire "Keramat" administrative district — 52 transactions spanning 14 different projects — not Three28's own data; its P75 of RM 7.8 million clearly mixes in an entirely different property type (likely a landed mansion elsewhere in the district, or a data error). The 5 verifiable transactions for Three28 itself (November 2021 through October 2025) carry a median price of RM 1,150,000 and median psf of RM 958 — almost exactly matching EdgeProp's current listing average of RM 958 psf (range RM 914-1,093). The truth is neither "a deep discount opportunity" nor "listing prices are a mirage" — the two original figures simply described different populations, and once compared against the correct population, the gap disappears.

On rent, 2BR units let for roughly RM 4,000-4,500/month, translating to a gross yield of ~5.0% against the RM 1,020,000 representative price — better than most KLCC-core projects reviewed on this site (Stonor 3's 4.23%, for example). But two hard exit metrics are less encouraging: a listing rate of ~34.3% (57 listings against 166 units) exceeds the 30% exit-crush threshold, while the verifiable Brickz transaction depth of only ~5 deals is far below what a healthy secondary market would show — sellers clearly outnumber closing buyers. On the foreign side, the representative unit's RM 1,020,000 price clears the RM 1 million Federal Territory threshold by less than 2%, leaving the foreign / MM2H buyer pool structurally fragile to even a small price pullback.

◆ Conclusion

Highlights & caveats

✦ Highlights
  • Title cross-verified across the developer's own site, PropertyGuru and iProperty, all consistently showing "commercial title / Service Residence" — more transparent than many comparable projects where title information is hard to verify at all
  • Ampang Park MRT2 (Putrajaya Line) is a 610m walk away and only opened in March 2023 — a genuine post-completion (2016) transit upgrade that landed after the project was built
  • 166 low-density units on 0.76 acres is genuinely rare within the corridor; Brickz same-building median of RM 958 psf runs ~69% above the district comp M City, evidence the market pays a verifiable premium
  • Gross rental yield of ~5.0% beats most KLCC-core projects reviewed on this site (Stonor 3's 4.23%, for example), giving it a comparatively healthy cash-flow profile
⚠ Points to note
  • The confirmed commercial (Service Residence) title means this site's scoring uses a "Freehold commercial SA 6.5" baseline rather than "Freehold residential 7.5," and the exit buyer pool is structurally limited by commercial-title financing friction for next buyers (LTV ~80-85%, rate premium 0.5-1%)
  • The representative unit's RM 1,020,000 price clears the KL Federal Territory foreign-purchase floor of RM 1M by a razor-thin margin — foreign buyers should choose 2BR-or-larger units to keep a safety margin against price swings
  • Only ~5 verifiable same-building Brickz transactions exist (2021-2026) despite the project being nearly 10 years post-completion — an unusually thin record; the ~34.3% listing rate already exceeds the 30% exit-crush threshold, so buyers should call at least 3-5 local agents before committing to verify actual transaction depth
  • The Jalan Tun Razak / Keramat corridor still has large new supply under construction (Residensi Tun Razak 2's 918 units across two towers), which may continue to pressure pricing within the corridor over the next 5 years

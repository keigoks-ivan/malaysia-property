◆ Deep dive

Project deep dive

Kiaramas deDaun sits on Jalan Desa Kiara in Mont Kiara, KL's most established expatriate enclave. Developed from the 1990s by Sunrise (now UEM Sunrise) as KL's first integrated high-end neighbourhood, Mont Kiara has accumulated thirty years of school, retail and medical infrastructure: four major international schools (MKIS, Garden, French, IGB), 1 Mont Kiara / Plaza Mont Kiara / Solaris Dutamas retail clusters, the American School and several international hospitals. deDaun is the sixth project in Asia Quest's Kiaramas Collection, on a parcel adjacent to the 2013-completed Kiaramas Danai — same sponsor, same brand, same freehold residential structure. The most material structural gap is the absence of any existing MRT: the planned MRT3 Mont Kiara station only breaks ground in 2027 and opens in 2032, meaning the next 6-7 years remain car-dependent — a Mont Kiara-wide condition rather than a deDaun-specific one.

The development covers 8.54 acres with three 40-storey residential towers totalling 661 units. Phase 1 comprises Block A (223 units) and Block B (213 units) = 436 units; Phase 2 Block C adds ~225 units launched in the same window. The unit mix: Type B 2BR (1,313-1,389 sqft, eight sub-types B/B1/Ba/Bb/Bab/B1ab etc., 252 units — the dominant cohort); Type A 3+1BR (2,508 sqft); Type C 3BR (1,884 sqft); Type D 7BR penthouse (4,692-4,693 sqft); Type E 4+1BR (2,713-2,777 sqft). Amenities include a 760-foot meandering pool spanning the three towers, rooftop sky lounges and private dining on each block, a Rooftop Elevated Zone (REZ) walkway, EV charging, pneumatic waste collection and private lift lobbies — differentiated but tangible rather than marketing fluff, consistent with Mont Kiara's integrated high-end positioning.

The sponsor, Asia Quest Group, was founded in 1991 and is privately held (not listed on Bursa, so no public quarterly disclosures — though land bank 68 acres / 39 acres developed is publicly disclosed). The first five Kiaramas projects — Sutera (1997), Cendana (2001), Ayuria (2006), Gateway (2007), Danai (2013) — were all delivered on time with zero abandonment; the first three were ~85% Malaysian-buyer and 15% foreign. The 10-year gap between Danai (2013) and deDaun (2024 launch) signals discipline rather than over-extension, and the site is a direct extension of the Danai land parcel — execution risk is structurally low. But "private + unlisted" also means parent-company financial transparency is structurally weaker than Pavilion Damansara Heights' CPPIB JV or Times Square 2's Berjaya group.

Pricing: Phase 1+2 launch range is RM 844-918 psf, weighted average approximately RM 880 psf. The cleanest market check is same-sponsor, adjacent Kiaramas Danai: 13 years old, freehold residential, Brickz 2024-25 cumulative 6 transactions, median RM 865 psf. deDaun's launch average lines up almost exactly with Danai's secondary-market median — effectively buying a brand-new freehold off-plan at the resale price of a 13-year-old comparable. This is a clearly quantifiable safety margin (compare: PavDH +29% premium over Damansara Heights apartment median; TS2 +20% over Bukit Bintang SA; PavSq +30% over Bukit Bintang SA). Against the Mont Kiara overall median of RM 808 psf, the ~9% premium reflects new-build + collection-brand value and remains reasonable. Caveat: rebate opacity (5-15% negotiation space) plus Block A's 97% take-up tilt pricing in the developer's favour, so reliable secondary-market calibration should wait until post-VP Brickz accumulates 20-30 transactions.

Rental market: for the 1,378 sqft Type B 2BR base case, monthly rent is estimated at RM 5,500 (Mont Kiara 2BR range RM 3,000-6,500 via Speedhome, Kiaramas Danai actuals RM 5,500-9,500 as the anchor). Gross yield is ~5.4%, clearing the v2.0 5% calibration baseline (vs PavDH ~4.4%, TS2 ~4.5%, PavSq ~4.6%). Three drivers: (1) freehold residential title = residential electricity tariffs, avoiding the 1.3-1.6× commercial electricity premium; (2) management fee RM 0.36 psf (vs PavDH RM 0.67, TS2/PavSq commercial SA typically RM 0.45-0.65); (3) Mont Kiara expat-company leases are typically 2 years+, with shorter vacancy gaps than tourism-driven Bukit Bintang SAs. However, Malaysian net yield ~3.6% vs 4.55% mortgage rate remains a small negative spread; foreign net yield after 30% non-resident tax is ~2.8% with spread −2.3%, so foreigners are leaning on capital appreciation rather than cash flow to make returns.

Holding considerations: the freehold residential title is the only non-commercial-SA structure among the four projects scorecarded so far — residential 90% LTV vs commercial 70-85%, HDA Schedule H protection, residential electricity, and no SA overhang cap pressure (KL SA overhang Q3 2025 = 3,643 units). This structural advantage explains the gap between deDaun's foreign score (6.3) and PavDH (5.9) / PavSq (4.8). MM2H asset eligibility: the smallest Type B at 1,313 sqft × RM 880 psf = RM 1.156M ≥ the RM 1M Federal Territory threshold ✓ qualified. The 2-year off-plan wait (VP May 2028) is time friction, but Asia Quest's zero-delay record across five prior projects keeps execution risk low. Phase 2 Block C completing in the same window adds internal supply (225 units) and may stretch the initial 1-2 year lease-up period; MRT3 opening in 2032 is a clear long-dated catalyst, with the 2026-2032 window functioning as a pre-catalyst price-discovery period.

10-year scenario baseline: Assuming entry May 2026, exit May 2036:
  • Base case: MRT3 opens on time in 2032, MK holds YoY +3-4%, rent +3% pa, vacancy 1 month/yr. Malaysian 10-year net ~RM 365K, IRR ~5-6%; Foreign ~RM 206K, IRR ~4-5%.
  • Bull case: MRT3 opens early + MK international school enrolment rebounds + price +5% pa. Malaysian IRR ~8-9% (RM 700K+), Foreign IRR ~6-7% (RM 510K+).
  • Bear case: MRT3 slips to 2034+, Phase 2 internal supply stretches vacancy to 2-3 months/yr, price 0% pa. Malaysian IRR approaches −1% (10-year loss RM 42K), Foreign IRR closer to −3% (loss RM 162K) given 8% stamp duty + 30% rental tax + permanent 10% RPGT.
10-year exit liquidity: Kiaramas Danai's 13-year track of 6 Brickz transactions in 2 years = ~0.75 per quarter — thin. deDaun's 661 units should generate a thicker Brickz sample post-VP; expected listing-to-sale 3-6 months, list-to-sale discount 5-8%.
◆ Verdict

Highlights & things to note

✦ Highlights
  • Freehold residential title — the only non-commercial-SA structure among the four scorecarded so far; residential 90% LTV + HDA protection + residential electricity + no SA overhang cap → low foreign structural friction
  • Asia Quest's 30-year brand and zero-delay record across five prior Kiaramas projects (Sutera, Cendana, Ayuria, Gateway, Danai); 10 years between Danai and deDaun signals discipline not over-extension
  • Launch RM 880 psf is essentially flat against the same-sponsor adjacent Kiaramas Danai's Brickz median RM 865 psf — "buying a new-build at the secondary-market price of a 13-year-old comp"; a clearly quantifiable safety margin
  • Gross yield 5.4% (based on RM 5,500/month) + management fee RM 0.36 psf + residential electricity materially reduce yield leakage
  • MM2H asset eligibility clears the threshold (Type B 1,313 sqft @ RM 880 = RM 1.156M ≥ RM 1M); Mont Kiara is KL's most international-school-dense + MM2H-favoured district
  • Block A 97% take-up + Phase 1 95% non-Bumi sold are strong market validation; buyer base is primarily owner-occupiers or long-term holders, limiting speculative vacancy
⚠ Things to note
  • No existing MRT — MRT3 Mont Kiara station only opens 2032 (construction 2027); the next 6-7 years remain car / Grab dependent; MRT3 slippage is a known risk
  • Off-plan with 2-year wait (VP May 2028); progress payments required and no rent during the construction window. Asia Quest's zero-delay record across five prior projects keeps execution risk low, but a 2026-2028 market downturn would limit exit options
  • Phase 2 Block C ~225 units VP at the same time and enters the rental market together — first 1-2 year lease-up period may see stretched vacancy; same-brand competition is more anchor than threat over the long run
  • Asia Quest is privately held (non-Bursa) — parent-company financial transparency is structurally weaker than PavDH (CPPIB JV) or TS2 (Berjaya); credit assessment leans on delivery record rather than published financials
  • Kiaramas Danai's 6 Brickz transactions over 2 years signal a thin secondary market — pricing calibration carries uncertainty until 1-2 years post-VP samples accumulate
  • Large layouts (Type B starts at 1,313 sqft; no studio / 1BR) make each transaction sizable and narrow the buyer pool vs small-format SA — but matches expat-family rental demand, which is the Mont Kiara core thesis

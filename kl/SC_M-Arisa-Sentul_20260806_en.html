◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Dive

M Arisa sits on Jalan Sentul Pasar, next to the Sentul traditional market, launched by Mah Sing Group in November 2019 and handed over in early 2025 — a flagship of Mah Sing's presence in the Sentul corridor and an extension of the group's affordable-boutique "M-series" line. Built on 3.31 acres across two 55-storey towers totalling 1,598 units, unit sizes range from 550 sqft one-bedrooms to 1,025 sqft four-bedrooms. The project achieved 80% take-up at launch and is now fully sold, with a QLASSIC score of 84 and SHASSIC 5-star rating — solid delivery quality for its price tier.

The project's location story needs to be unpacked in two layers. The first is the marketing narrative: "Sentul" as a place name has warmed up in recent years thanks to the MRT3 Circle Line plan — Sentul East and Sentul West are confirmed as interchange stations integrating with the existing KTM/LRT system, theoretically elevating Sentul from a peripheral residential area to a future multi-line node. The second layer is on-the-ground verification: M Arisa itself sits roughly 2.4km from the nearest LRT Sentul Timur station and 2.6km from KTM Sentul, requiring a car or the developer's shuttle — not the walkable rail-adjacent product the "Sentul" branding might imply. MRT3's own construction and opening timeline is also inconsistent across sources — land acquisition expected to finish by end-2026, construction pegged to around 2027, phased opening as early as 2028, with the full line targeted for 2030-2032 — a long-horizon catalyst with genuine timing uncertainty, not a reason for near-term urgency.

The scarcity audit reaches an equally conservative conclusion: the land layer is uncapped — the Sentul corridor has at least 7 more new launches competing in 2026, including Mah Sing's own M Centura selling a similar product 390 metres away; the product layer shows no distinctive configuration either — a standardized 1-4 bedroom high-rise service residence format highly homogeneous with area comps like The Saffron, M Centura, and The Capers; and the legal layer has no height cap or master plan locking in future supply. All three layers fail the audit, meaning this project's long-term value proposition cannot rest on "location scarcity" — it should instead rest on cashflow and buyer-pool depth.

On pricing, this project's own Brickz transacted median is RM624 psf / RM500,000 (just 4 transactions, August 2024 to July 2025); the representative 2BR unit (708 sqft) currently indicates around RM550 psf / RM390,000 on the secondary market, roughly a 12% discount to its own transacted median — though with such a thin sample, valuation confidence can only be labelled low-medium. Buyers should understand actual transacted prices may vary from asking due to negotiation room, renovation condition, and floor/facing, and shouldn't rely purely on listed prices.

On the rental market, actual listings for the same-building 708 sqft 2-bedroom type range from RM1,700 to RM2,000, implying a gross yield of roughly 5.4% — above-average for the KL market overall, and this project's strongest fundamental. Worth noting: market reports indicate sub-60% occupancy at some newly completed high-rises in the Sentul and neighbouring Titiwangsa corridor — not M Arisa's own data directly, but a signal of competitive pressure in the same corridor worth verifying independently for rental-focused buyers during due diligence.

The exit picture is the most critical, and most important-to-understand, part of this project: at a price of RM390,000, the next-buyer household income threshold is only around RM4,290, far below KL's median household income of RM10,805 — giving this project an unusually wide local first-time-buyer and upgrader pool, its most standout advantage relative to most cards on this site. But the foreign buyer pool is an entirely different story — this project's representative unit, and even its largest unit type (roughly RM550,000-600,000), both price below KL Federal Territory's RM1,000,000 foreign-buyer minimum threshold, meaning foreign individual buyers cannot legally purchase any unit here under current rules — unrelated to any specific MM2H tier, and instead the higher-order Federal Territory transaction-value rule excluding them outright. This isn't the familiar "high foreign dependency, high policy risk" script — it's the structural fact that foreign buyers were never in the exit pool to begin with. Developer Mah Sing, a Bursa Main Market-listed group with nearly 30 years and 50+ delivered projects, and this project's own on-schedule, fully-sold handover, provide a genuine confidence anchor.

◆ Conclusion

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • Low entry price: representative unit ~RM390,000 (≈RM550psf); next-buyer household income threshold only ~RM4,290, far below KL's median household income of RM10,805 — a wide local buyer pool
  • Gross rental yield ~5.4%, above-average among projects reviewed on this site; 10-year break-even threshold as low as ~0.5%/year (Malaysian buyer), not reliant on major price appreciation
  • Freehold residential title (not commercial SA) — the next buyer gets standard 90% LTV residential financing, avoiding the financing-friction penalty seen at commercial-titled projects
  • Developer Mah Sing Group is a Bursa Main Market-listed group with nearly 30 years and 50+ delivered projects; this project scored QLASSIC 84 and SHASSIC 5-star, completed on schedule in early 2025, fully sold
  • MRT3 Circle Line has received final approval; Sentul East/West interchange stations will integrate the existing KTM/LRT system — a long-term (2028-2032) positive catalyst
⚠ Caveats
  • Actual distance to the nearest LRT/KTM station is ~2.4-2.6km, requiring a car or developer shuttle — the "Sentul" branding can mislead buyers into assuming rail-adjacent access; verify actual commute times on the ground
  • Price sits below KL Federal Territory's RM1,000,000 foreign-buyer minimum threshold (even the largest unit type falls short) — foreign individual buyers cannot legally purchase any unit here under current rules; this is a structural exclusion, not the usual "foreign penalty" seen elsewhere on this site
  • Real oversupply pressure exists in the Sentul corridor — at least 7 new launches compete in 2026, and market reports flag sub-60% occupancy at some newly completed high-rises in the area; rental-focused buyers should verify actual leasing demand on the ground

Sunway Cochrane

You pay a 15% premium for the only new-build on the street that is freehold and residential-title — and that title gap is real in transacted data (One Cochrane, freehold residential, RM 1,048 psf vs Sunway Velocity, leasehold, RM 836 psf: a 25% spread). It lifts the exit dimension out of the narrow commercial-SA pool and back into the mainstream local upgrader band (exit 7.0, the highest of any Sunway project scored here). The cost is equally real: 3.9% gross yield, negative carry for both buyer types, entry valuation 5.0. Ten-year returns rest almost entirely on capital gain. The two numbers to watch: 1,296 units completing at once, and KL serviced-apartment overhang rising to 4,181 with 58.5% of it sitting in this project's exact price band.

🌏Foreign buyer
5.5 / 10 T4 · Niche thesis
Materially stronger than comparable leasehold commercial-SA stock nearby (foreign exit 5.0 vs Sunway Velocity 3's 2.6) — the representative unit at RM 1,050,000 clears the RM 1m Federal Territory threshold and sits in the local/foreign dual-pool sweet spot. But it clears by only 5%; fall below and foreign resale eligibility disappears. With the 30% non-resident rental tax, net yield is roughly 2.0% and carry is −3.1%, pushing the foreign breakeven to 4.5% p.a. price growth.
Foreign purchase threshold — this project is a borderline case: the minimum foreign purchase price in KL (Federal Territory) is RM 1,000,000. This card's representative unit, Type C 2+1BR 872 sqft mid-floor at approximately RM 1,050,000, clears the threshold by only 5%. Type A (650 sqft, from about RM 749,300) and Type B (732 sqft, from about RM 830,000) both sit below the threshold and cannot be bought by foreigners; low-floor Type C (around RM 970,800) is also below it. The threshold cuts both ways: it restricts which units a foreign buyer can enter, and it means that ten years out, if the price has not held above RM 1m, the foreign resale pool vanishes entirely and the unit can only be sold to a local buyer. Foreign buyers should obtain written confirmation from the developer that the final net price of the specific unit is genuinely at or above RM 1,000,000 — early-bird discounts and furniture packages can push the net figure below the line.

◆ EXIT REALITY CHECK

Exit Reality Check

The v5.0 trio: scarcity audit (is there a supply-side moat), next-buyer pyramid (who buys this in ten years) and secondary-market evidence (turnover, listings, discount). This is an off-plan project with statutory completion in Q4 2029, so it has no Brickz or listing history of its own. All secondary-market evidence is proxied from One Cochrane Residences (448 units) on the same Jalan Cochrane, also freehold residential title, completed 2023. Confidence: medium — same street, same title, same product form, which is the closest available proxy.

5A · Scarcity audit (three supply-side layers)
Could the market produce another one of these in ten years? Two layers fail, one passes — and the one that passes has transacted evidence behind it.
✗Land layer: Sunway bought 6.59 acres in 2021 and this project uses only 5.32–5.4 acres, leaving a residual parcel. The Jalan Peel–Jalan Cochrane corridor remains under active redevelopment — the same developer's SV3 three towers due 2028, Lavile, land around MyTOWN and IKEA. Replicability on this micro-location is high.
✗Product layer: 1,296 units across two standard serviced-apartment towers, with every unit type in the 650–1,001 sqft investor band. The same developer is building near-identical product one kilometre away; the configuration is reproducible.
✓Regulatory layer: freehold plus residential title, the only such new supply in this precinct — Sunway Velocity TWO and SV3 are both leasehold with commercial title. What the title layer locks in is not the quantity of supply but the terms on which the next buyer can borrow, which is a hard constraint at the exit.
Effective scarcity verdict: the regulatory layer is genuine scarcity; the land and product layers are not. Demand-side evidence supports it — One Cochrane Residences on the same street (freehold residential title) transacts at a Brickz median of RM 1,048 psf against Sunway Velocity (leasehold) at RM 836 psf, a 25% title premium in actual transactions, not a marketing claim. But this layer does not cap supply: One Cochrane is itself freehold residential title, so is Lavile, and further same-title projects can appear along the corridor. The scarcity adjustment is therefore +0.3 rather than the +1.0 ceiling.
5B · Next-buyer pyramid
Quantum test: representative unit at RM 1,050,000 → next-buyer household income threshold of approximately RM 11,550/month (price × 1.1%: 90% LTV, 4.3%, 30 years, 40% debt-service ratio) → against a KL median household income of RM 10,805/month (DOSM Household Income Survey 2024, the highest in Malaysia), the representative unit sits just above the median KL household's capacity, implying a buyer pool of roughly the top 45% of KL households. This is not a rich-only asset; it is a standard metropolitan upgrader product, and the depth of that local pool is the substance of the exit thesis.

In one sentence: in ten years the most likely buyer is a local upgrader household earning around RM 12,000 a month or more, roughly the top 45% of KL, and what makes that work is the freehold residential title giving that buyer 90% LTV at standard mortgage pricing; if the price fails to hold above RM 1,000,000 — and the representative unit clears that line by only 5% — the foreign buyer pool disappears entirely and the exit falls back on the local pool alone, at a moment when the other 1,295 units in this project and One Cochrane's existing 23% listing overhang are also on the market.
◆ ANALYSIS

Project Deep Dive

Sunway Cochrane sits on Jalan Cochrane, at the Maluri–Cheras boundary, and is Sunway Property's third large residential cluster in this precinct — and the closest of the three to TRX. The site runs 5.32 to 5.4 acres, carrying twin 55-storey towers with 1,296 units (Tower A 641, Tower B 655), unit sizes from 650 to 1,001 sqft, a 3.4-acre elevated lifestyle deck, more than seventy facilities and two levels of retail. The land's provenance is itself a clue: in April 2021 Sunway paid RM 233.4M to Boustead's Mutiara Rini for 6.59 acres, and Mutiara Rini is the developer of One Cochrane Residences on the same street. The most relevant valuation anchor for this project, in other words, is the building put up by the party that sold Sunway the land.

The entire investment case rests on one attribute: freehold tenure with residential title. The competing new supply on this street — Sunway Velocity TWO and Sunway Velocity 3 — is leasehold with commercial title. That difference is not merely documentary. One Cochrane Residences (freehold residential title, completed 2023) transacts at a Brickz median of RM 1,048 psf; Sunway Velocity (leasehold) sits at RM 836 psf. The title premium in actual transactions is 25%. Where residential title genuinely earns its keep is at the exit: the next buyer qualifies for 90% LTV at standard residential mortgage pricing, rather than the 80–85% LTV and 0.5 to 1.0 point rate loading that commercial-title stock attracts. The 7.0 exit score, the highest of any Sunway project on this site, comes almost entirely from there.

Connectivity is the second pillar. A 60-metre covered link bridge reaches Cochrane MRT on the Kajang line — one stop to TRX, two to Bukit Bintang, three to Merdeka. A second bridge of 210 metres runs into Sunway Velocity Mall; Sunway Medical Centre Velocity, with an International Patient Centre, is roughly 800 metres away, and MyTOWN and IKEA Cheras about 500 metres. The dimension scores 8.0 rather than higher because two Tier 9 conditions fail: Velocity Mall is a regional-format centre, not Pavilion or IGB class, and this is not an international-school cluster. The developer dimension is also 8.0 — Sunway Berhad is Bursa Tier 1, 1QFY26 core pre-tax profit rose 52%, and both the mall and the hospital across those bridges are its own assets, aligning landlord and operator interests.

The cost shows up in price and rent. The representative Type C 2+1 room 872 sqft mid-floor unit runs about RM 1,050,000, or RM 1,204 psf, a 14.9% premium to One Cochrane's transacted median — the framework's "elevated, needs a catalyst" rung, hence 5.0 on entry valuation. And the MRT is already running, so that catalyst is a settled fact and already in the price. The rental side is tighter still: 872 sqft at an estimated RM 3,400 a month gives a 3.89% gross yield, roughly 2.5% net after tax for a Malaysian buyer and 2.0% for a foreign one, against mortgage rates of 4.55% and 5.05% — clearly negative carry on both sides. Of One Cochrane's 448 units, 157 are listed to let, a 35% rental listing rate that tells you more about competition for tenants than any estimate could. This is not a carry asset; the ten-year return is almost wholly a bet on capital gain.

Supply is the other side to watch. In the scarcity audit, both the land and product layers fail — Sunway bought 6.59 acres and used 5.32 to 5.4, the Jalan Peel–Cochrane corridor is still being redeveloped, and the 1,296-unit standard serviced-apartment format is being replicated by the same developer a kilometre away at SV3, due 2028. The harder pressure is in the aggregate figures: NAPIC's Q1 2026 data puts KL serviced-apartment overhang at 4,181 units, up from 3,643 in Q3 2025; KL residential overhang stands at 3,733 after the largest single-quarter increase in the country; and 58.5% of the national serviced-apartment overhang sits in the RM 500k–1m band, which is this project's core price band. The project itself is only half sold into the market: Tower B's 655 units launched in October 2025 with a GDV of RM 607M and 55% take-up over the sales preview weekend, while Tower A's 641 units remain unpriced.

A final item buyers can verify for themselves. Sunway's own APDL disclosure and its Q4 FY2025 briefing pack both state statutory completion in Q4 2029, while marketing collateral, agent pages and most media coverage say "August 2028" — a gap of roughly 14 months. The project is not yet due, so this is not a delivery failure and no developer deduction applies. But for rental start dates, financing schedules and interim accommodation, 14 months is a material difference, and only the date in the sale and purchase agreement and the APDL carries legal weight.

Taken together, Sunway Cochrane and nearby Sunway Velocity 3 are close to two sides of the same coin: 6.1 versus 6.1 for Malaysian buyers, 5.5 versus 5.4 for foreign buyers — near-identical totals arrived at from opposite directions. SV3 wins on entry price (RM 943 psf, a discount to precinct transactions) and yield (5.3% gross), and loses on leasehold commercial title and a foreign exit pool locked out by the RM 1m threshold (foreign exit 2.6). Sunway Cochrane inverts that: it wins on freehold residential title and the exit quality that title supports (7.0 local, 5.0 foreign), and loses on a 15% entry premium and a gross yield down to 3.9%. Choosing between them is really a choice between paying a premium up front for exit quality and capturing the discount while accepting exit friction — not a question of which is better.

10-year scenario baseline: assuming entry in 2026, statutory completion Q4 2029, and a ten-year hold from handover (representative unit RM 1,050,000):
  • Base case (3% p.a. price growth): Sunway delivers on schedule, the title premium holds, and TRX employment spillover supports tenant demand. Malaysian buyer cumulative net return approximately RM 178,000, IRR around 5.3%; foreign buyer approximately RM 78,000, IRR around 1.4%.
  • Bull case (5% p.a.): the Jalan Peel–Cochrane regeneration delivers, TRX spillover accelerates, and the residential-title premium widens. Malaysian buyer approximately RM 470,000, IRR around 11.1%; foreign buyer approximately RM 341,000, IRR around 5.2%.
  • Bear case (1% p.a.): KL serviced-apartment overhang keeps accumulating, this project's 1,296 units hit the market at once, and Tower A adds further pressure on both rents and prices. Malaysian buyer loses approximately RM 68,000, IRR around −2.6%; foreign buyer loses approximately RM 145,000, IRR around −3.0%.
Ten-year exit liquidity, extrapolated from One Cochrane's actuals (2.9% annual turnover, 23.2% for-sale listing rate): Malaysian sellers should expect five to nine months from listing to sale at a 6–10% discount; foreign sellers, constrained by the RM 1m threshold and the fact that this is not a foreign-buyer district, should expect nine to fifteen months at an 8–13% discount. Price growth required to break even on all costs plus a 5% opportunity cost: approximately 2.4% p.a. for a Malaysian buyer and 4.5% p.a. for a foreign buyer.
◆ CONCLUSION

Highlights & Points to Note

✦ Highlights
  • Freehold plus residential title — the only such new supply in this precinct (SV Two and SV3 are both leasehold commercial). The title premium measures 25% in same-street transactions (One Cochrane RM 1,048 psf vs Sunway Velocity RM 836 psf)
  • Exit & next-buyer pool 7.0, the highest of any Sunway project scored here: the next buyer qualifies for 90% LTV at standard residential mortgage pricing, with none of the commercial-title financing friction
  • Connectivity 8.0: 60m covered link bridge to Cochrane MRT, one stop to TRX and two to Bukit Bintang; 210m bridge into Sunway Velocity Mall; Sunway Medical Centre Velocity roughly 800m
  • Developer 8.0: Sunway Berhad (Bursa T1), 1QFY26 core PBT +52%, and both the linked mall and hospital are its own assets, aligning landlord and operator interests
  • Low foreign dependency (<20%): the exit rests on a self-sufficient local upgrader pool, so MM2H or foreign-ownership policy changes will not halve it
  • The representative unit at RM 1.05m clears the RM 1m foreign threshold and sits in the local/foreign dual-pool sweet spot, giving a foreign exit score of 5.0 against 2.6 for comparable leasehold commercial-SA stock nearby
⚠ Points to note
  • Completion-date disclosure gap: the APDL and Sunway's briefing pack state Q4 2029 while marketing collateral commonly states 2028-08, roughly 14 months apart — plan financing and rental start dates against the SPA and APDL
  • Gross yield only 3.9%, with negative carry for both buyer types (−2.1% local, −3.1% foreign): this is not a carry asset and the ten-year return rests almost wholly on capital gain
  • Entry valuation 5.0: the representative RM 1,204 psf carries a 14.9% premium to the same-street transacted median, and the MRT catalyst is already operational and priced in
  • The letting market is already crowded: 157 of One Cochrane's 448 units are listed to let (35%), and this project's 1,296 units will add to that when they complete together
  • Supply side: KL serviced-apartment overhang rose to 4,181 units in Q1 2026, with 58.5% of the national figure in the RM 500k–1m band that this project occupies; Tower A's 641 units are not yet launched and the same developer's SV3 three towers complete in 2028
  • Foreign threshold headroom is only 5%: if the RM 1,050,000 representative unit falls below RM 1m through discounting or a market correction, foreign eligibility disappears — obtain written confirmation of the net price before signing
  • The maintenance fee has not been published; this card estimates RM 0.50/sqft/month including sinking fund. Actual upkeep for 70-plus facilities and a 3.4-acre elevated deck may exceed that, and should be confirmed with the sales team

Fit: this is the project's most natural use. Can compromise: Scarcity (income-focused buyers can accept a non-prime district)
Fit: for pure income, this is one of the weaker projects on this site. 🌳Generational hold (20+ years)
Critical dimensions: Scarcity & location moat (6.2), Capital appreciation (5.2, the long-run anchor), Exit & next-buyer pool (7.0 local, for the next generation's disposal)
Can compromise: Short-term yield
Fit: among Sunway's projects and the leasehold commercial-SA stock nearby, this is the strongest on generational characteristics — freehold means no 99-year decay, and residential title keeps inheritance, resale, taxation and financing on residential terms for the next generation. Over a twenty-year horizon those are substantive differences. Two limits deserve honesty: (1) the 6.2 scarcity score comes from the regulatory layer rather than the land layer, and further same-title projects can appear along the corridor (One Cochrane and Lavile are precedents), diluting the title advantage; (2) building depreciation and management-committee quality in a 1,296-unit community over twenty years are harder to forecast than title, and the long-run upkeep cost of 70-plus facilities and a 3.4-acre elevated deck is particularly worth tracking. Against assets where the land layer itself is scarce, such as Damansara Heights or Desa ParkCity, this project remains mid-tier on generational hold.

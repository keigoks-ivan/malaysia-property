◆ Deep dive

Project deep dive

Park Regent sits at the lakeside core of Desa ParkCity, on a 5.6-acre parcel adjacent to the 70-acre Central Park and its signature Central Lake. Desa ParkCity is the only single-master-developer planned community within KL city — ParkCity Group has progressively developed the entire 473 acres since 1995, controlling planning, architectural standards and community management to deliver a low-density, park-oriented living experience comparable to Singapore's Bukit Timah. The community integrates The Waterfront mall, Plaza Arkadia's F&B and retail, International School @ ParkCity and the ParkCity Medical Centre (also owned by ParkCity Group), giving DPC a "medical-tier" lifestyle integration that is rare in KL's private estates. The largest structural gap: no existing MRT and no direct MRT3 coverage — car commute is the long-term default, ~25 minutes to KLCC, which is the norm for owner-occupiers but is a liquidity dampener for pure investors.

Park Regent is a joint venture between ParkCity Group and CapitaLand (mainboard-listed on Singapore Exchange, Southeast Asia's largest private developer, with Singapore GLC backing). The project launched in July 2019, sold 70% on day one, fully sold during the pandemic; launch price RM 1,100 psf was the first DPC project to break RM 1,000 psf. It completed on time in November 2023 and has now passed the 2-year defect liability window. The ParkCity CEO publicly compared Park Regent to the "BMW 7 Series" within the DPC portfolio, and The Edge awarded it the 2024 Value Creation Excellence Award — confirming its flagship positioning within DPC. The development comprises two residential towers totalling 505 units across six unit types: 1BR 872-893 sqft (investor entry), 1BR+study 1,098-1,163, 2BR+study 1,464-2,433, 3BR 2,519-2,723 (dominant family cohort), 3BR+family 2,917-3,100, 4BR 4,446-4,887 sqft (penthouse tier). Amenities include lakeside pools, private concierge service, Sky Lounge and gym; the absence of any studio layout reflects the family-residential positioning.

Pricing: the Brickz 70-transaction median Jun 2024-May 2025 at RM 1,541 psf is one of the deepest publicly disclosed market validations among KL high-end projects — buyers do not need to rely on developer pricing or agent quotes and can anchor directly to actual transacted PSF. The 5-year trajectory from 2019 launch RM 1,100 psf to 2024-25 RM 1,541 represents +40% appreciation realised, well above the Mont Kiara +10-20% over the same period, validating the durability of DPC's location scarcity. Against the JLL Q2 2025 KL high-end average RM 1,100-1,200 psf, the ~30% premium reflects DPC's integrated-community premium and is reasonable; a 2025 transaction at RM 12.5M / RM 2,771 psf set a single-unit record, signalling top-end buyer willingness to pay. Brickz 5.8 transactions per month is comfortably above the 3/month "healthy liquidity" benchmark; iProperty 308 listings on a 505-unit pool (61% listing rate) is normal for large-format luxury — owners use listings to test best execution price rather than to exit urgently — so liquidity should be judged by transaction velocity, not by static listing rates.

Rental market: for the 2,557 sqft 3BR representative unit, monthly rent is estimated at RM 14,000, with a gross yield of approximately 4.26%. The anchor is The Edge 2024's published Park Regent case study: a 4,511 sqft unit leased at RM 25,000/month = RM 5.54 psf/month = 5.20% gross. Applying similar per-sqft rent to 2,557 sqft yields RM 14,000/month, consistent with the Park Regent 1.5-2× premium over the broader DPC market (RM 2,500-4,500 for 900-1,800 sqft). Malaysian net yield is approximately 3.0% with spread −1.6%; foreign net yield after 30% non-resident tax is approximately 2.3% with spread −2.8% — meaningfully negative. This is the trade-off inherent to large-format luxury: the positioning is capital growth + lifestyle hold, not pure cash flow. International comparables (Singapore Bukit Timah, Hong Kong Mid-Levels) show the same yield-compressed pattern. The tenant base — DPC expat families, corporate-posted executives, local HNW owner-occupiers — typically signs 2-year+ corporate leases, keeping vacancy structurally low; the 1-month/year vacancy model is conservative.

Holding structure: freehold residential title (non-commercial-SA) + HDA Schedule H protection + residential 90% LTV + residential electricity tariffs together create a structurally low holding-friction regime. MM2H asset eligibility: the 1BR entry at 872 sqft × RM 1,400-1,500 psf ≈ RM 1.22-1.31M clears the RM 1M Federal Territory threshold ✓, and the 3BR mid-floor RM 3.94M clears it comfortably. Completion in Nov 2023 means buyers can physically inspect, validate management quality and verify JMB operations directly — execution risk is zero. On exit liquidity, Brickz 5.8 transactions/month is the deepest publicly validated track among the scorecarded buildings to date; DPC's secondary buyer pool spans local HNW, corporate expat families and MM2H holders, providing structurally diversified demand. 10-year exit expectations: at base 3% pa appreciation, Year 10 price ≈ RM 5.3M, Malaysian net P&L approximately +RM 845K (IRR 5-6%), Foreign +RM 455K (IRR 4-5%).

Things to note: (1) no MRT and no MRT3 coverage is a DPC-wide condition, meaning the catalyst side lacks a hard transit trigger — capital appreciation relies on DPC supply lock-in + KL high-end demand growth; (2) the large-format positioning (smallest unit 872 sqft 1BR; 3BR median 2,557 sqft) means each transaction is sizable and the buyer pool is narrower than small-format SA investor products; (3) Brickz P25 RM 1.18M and P75 RM 4.45M show wide variation by unit size — buyers should anchor to the PSF appropriate to their target size, not blindly apply the median; (4) if third parties acquire any DPC parcels for new development, the scarcity assumption is at risk and ParkCity announcements should be monitored; (5) MYR currency depreciation is a latent risk for all KL property, with foreign buyers needing to incorporate FX volatility into position sizing.

10-year scenario baseline: Assuming entry May 2026, exit May 2036:
  • Base case: DPC supply lock-in holds, KL high-end +3-4% pa, rent +3% pa, vacancy 1 month/yr. Malaysian 10-year net ~RM 845K, IRR ~5-6%; Foreign ~RM 455K, IRR ~4-5%.
  • Bull case: MM2H 2,637 pending qualifications fully convert + KL new construction starts −14.9% tightens premium supply + price +5% pa. Malaysian IRR ~7-8% (RM 1.94M+), Foreign IRR ~6% (RM 1.44M+).
  • Bear case: Third-party DPC supply dilutes scarcity + MYR depreciation + price 0% pa. Malaysian IRR approaches −1% (10-year loss RM 477K), Foreign IRR closer to −2% (loss RM 738K) given 8% stamp duty + 30% rental tax + permanent 10% RPGT.
10-year exit liquidity: 5.8 transactions/month is a validated baseline; expected listing-to-sale 2-4 months for large units, list-to-sale discount 3-6%. Liquidity is mature — not explosive, but relatively stable.

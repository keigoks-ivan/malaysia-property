River Park @ Bangsar South

Bangsar South (officially Kampung Kerinchi) is a distinct, lower-price district from core Bangsar — real Brickz transactions show a ~33% psf gap (RM 511 vs core Bangsar's RM 765 median), so d1's baseline resets to 6.0. Actual transit is Angkasapuri KTM 300m (a legacy low-frequency commuter line, not MRT/LRT); the marketed "walk to Pantai Permai MRT" station is 6+ years from operating (construction starts 2027, line opens 2032), so it earns zero 5-year catalyst credit. The defining structural fact: every 2BR unit currently on sale (RM 600k–846k) sits below Malaysia's RM 1,000,000 KL foreign-ownership floor — foreigners cannot legally buy any unit type still available in this project.

5.8

District call (the central judgment here): Bangsar South (officially Kampung Kerinchi) does not inherit core Bangsar's 7.0 baseline; it's scored as a distinct district at 6.0. Four reasons: (1) The area's administrative name has always been Kampung Kerinchi — "Bangsar South" was a 2012 rebrand by developer UOA Group purely to borrow investment cachet from the Bangsar name; a 2018 renaming controversy exposed a real split between long-time residents and property investors over whether the identity is genuinely shared. (2) UOA acquired the land in 2005 and built it out as a 60-acre office-plus-high-density-condo mixed-use park — a completely different development lineage from core Bangsar's low-density residential streets and Bangsar Village/Telawi lifestyle strip. (3) Transit systems differ: core Bangsar is served directly by the LRT Kelana Jaya Line's Bank Rakyat-Bangsar station (600m walk); Bangsar South has its own Kerinchi LRT station on the same line, but reaching it requires an 800m pedestrian bridge into Bangsar South Trade Centre, and River Park itself sits closer to the Federal Highway/NPE side, further still on foot. (4) The clinching evidence: Brickz transaction data shows Kampung Kerinchi (Bangsar South) residential median at just RM 511 psf (65 transactions, Nov 2024–Oct 2025) — about 33% below core Bangsar's overall median of RM 765 psf. That real transacted gap confirms these are two different price tiers, not one market; keeping the 7.0 baseline would materially overstate this project's location moat. Three-layer supply audit: land layer ✗ — Bangsar South's high-density build-out continues (Merbau, Duo Tower and other concurrent launches); product layer ✗ — a 1,332-unit standard high-density tower is a replicable format matched by KL Gateway, TRIA Seputeh, Secoya and others nearby; regulatory layer ✗ — leasehold, not freehold, no masterplan supply cap. Micro-location: the Angkasapuri KTM 300m covered walkway is a genuine, usable daily-commute convenience (+0.1), offset by high-tension cables (HTC) adjacent to the site, flagged in an independent review (−0.3). Net 6.0 + 0.1 − 0.3 = 5.8.

◆ IN-DEPTH ANALYSIS

Project Deep-Dive

River Park sits in a part of Kuala Lumpur that markets itself under a borrowed name. The land is officially Kampung Kerinchi; "Bangsar South" is a 2012 rebrand by developer UOA Group, adopted purely to lend the project investment cachet by association with Bangsar. That's not incidental trivia — it's the reason this card treats the district as its own entity rather than an extension of the Bangsar Hill Park card already on this site. Real Brickz transactions bear it out: Kampung Kerinchi's residential median over the past year sits at RM 511 psf against core Bangsar's RM 765 psf, a genuine 33% gap that no amount of marketing can paper over. The district baseline here is reset to 6.0, not 7.0, and every downstream number in this card follows from that call.

The developer is Kumpulan Gapadu Sdn Bhd, verified as a wholly-owned subsidiary of Malton Berhad (Bursa: 6181) — the same listed group behind this site's earlier Park Green Pavilion card in Bukit Jalil. River Park itself, however, carries a distinct and separately relevant history: it was first announced with a 2019 completion target, then shelved for several years while Malton's resources went toward the Pavilion flagship series, before resuming and structurally topping out in August 2025. It is now targeting Q3 2026 completion under the marketing banner "Final Phase" — roughly six to seven years later than first promised. That delay is strategic, not distress-driven (no abandonment or "sick project" signal turned up in the record, and Malton's group financials, while carrying elevated leverage, show H1 FY2026 profit up 100% YoY), but it is real, and it means the flagship-delivery credit this site gave Malton for Pavilion Bukit Jalil Mall's on-time opening doesn't transfer cleanly to this project. The developer score nets out at a middling 6.0 — the Tier 2 group baseline plus the Pavilion credit, minus this project's own delay history.

The three-tower, 46-storey, 1,332-unit project sits on 5.10 acres — a density of roughly 261 units per acre — and offers a genuinely replicable product: Type A1/A2 at 812 sqft and Type A3/A4 at 861 sqft, both 2-bedroom, plus a 3-bedroom Type B at 1,180 sqft that has already sold out. This scorecard uses the larger of the two active 2BR types (A3/A4, 861 sqft, with balcony) as its representative unit, priced at approximately RM 765,000, or RM 889 psf — a 7.2% premium over the best available comparable anchor, KL Gateway Premium Residences, a nearby Bangsar South serviced residence with 16 recorded Brickz transactions at a median of RM 829 psf. That premium sits comfortably in the "reasonable new-launch" bracket, and independent reviews note River Park's pricing is not aggressive relative to neighbouring launches like TRIA Seputeh and Secoya.

The transit story deserves the most scrutiny of anything in this card, because the marketing and the facts diverge sharply. River Park's promotional materials tout "walking distance to Pantai Permai MRT" — but Pantai Permai is a planned station on the MRT3 Circle Line, whose final alignment the Ministry of Transport only approved in July 2025. Land acquisition is targeted to wrap up by the end of 2026; construction is not expected to begin before 2027; and the line's target opening is 2032. That is over six years away, comfortably outside this framework's five-year catalyst window, and this card gives it zero credit in the capital-appreciation dimension as a result. What actually exists today is a 300-metre covered walkway to Angkasapuri KTM Komuter station — a real, verifiable, currently-usable convenience, but one running on a legacy low-frequency commuter line, not the MRT or LRT service the marketing language implies. The nearest genuine LRT connection, Kerinchi station on the Kelana Jaya Line, requires an 800-metre pedestrian bridge from Bangsar South Trade Centre — further still from River Park's actual footprint. Meanwhile, Bangsar South's office-and-residential build-out shows no signs of slowing: Tan & Tan's Merbau project (GDV RM 655 million, launching Q3 2026) and UOA's Duo Tower (GDV RM 1.3 billion, completing FY2027) both add competing supply, on top of a serviced-apartment segment still working through its 2022–2024 completion wave.

The single fact that should dominate any foreign buyer's read of this project sits in the exit dimension. Every 2BR unit currently on sale — Type A1/A2 at RM 600,000–768,000 and Type A3/A4 (this card's representative type) at RM 683,000–846,000 — is priced below Malaysia's RM 1,000,000 floor for foreign property purchases in the Federal Territory. The only unit type that could conceivably clear that bar, the 1,180 sqft, 3-bedroom Type B priced RM 906,000–1,040,000, is already sold out, and even its lower band falls short. This is not the "narrow buffer above the threshold" caveat this site has flagged on other projects — it is a structural absence of any currently-purchasable inventory for foreign buyers. The foreign exit score reflects that reality at 2.9, and the foreign cash-flow figure of 3.7 is presented explicitly as a hypothetical construct, not a live transaction path. For local buyers the picture is considerably more workable: the representative unit's RM 765,000 price implies a next-buyer household income requirement of about RM 8,500 a month, only 79% of KL's median household income of roughly RM 10,800 — putting more than half of Kuala Lumpur's households within theoretical reach, and squarely positioning this as a first-time-buyer and young-professional product.

Key watch items: (1) Bangsar South is a genuinely different, lower-tier district from core Bangsar — buyers should not let the shared name imply a shared location moat; (2) every on-sale 2BR unit is currently ineligible for foreign purchase under Malaysian law; (3) Merbau and Duo Tower's concurrent supply will compete for both tenants and resale buyers over the medium term; (4) the marketed "walk to Pantai Permai MRT" is a station that will not open until roughly 2032 — the real, usable transit today is the lower-frequency Angkasapuri KTM 300m away; (5) high-tension cables sit adjacent to the site, per an independent field review; (6) River Park's own multi-year slip from a 2019 to a 2026 completion target, while apparently strategic rather than distress-driven, is worth tracking through to final handover quality; (7) the exact leasehold expiry date is not publicly confirmed and should be verified with the land office before signing.

Foreign demand is structurally absent for now, leaving exit entirely dependent on the local first-time-buyer and young-professional pool.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Dive

Kenny Hills Residence sits in Bukit Tunku (formerly Kenny Hills) — grouped with Damansara Heights and Federal Hill as KL's "Three Sacred Hills," home to old-money families, royalty, and diplomatic households. Completed in March 2014 by Sime Darby Property (via its predecessor joint venture Sime Darby Brunsfield), the project holds just 63 units across 5 storeys on 4.75 acres, a density of only 13 units per acre — an unusually low-density condominium for KL. The location's scarcity is real, but not absolute: the 88-acre Kenny Heights mixed-use project (a Dutaland/KH Estates × Olympia Properties joint venture) has been rolling out 9 parcels since 2008, and the district already hosts five or six precedent boutique condos — Tijani 1, Tijani 2 North, Villa Bukit Tunku, Sutera Bukit Tunku, Arata — meaning "a condo on the sacred hill" is not an unprecedented product, only a slowly replicated one, with no new launch in the past 12 years.

Every unit here is oversized: Grande 1&2 (4+1 bed, 3,627-3,714 sf), Albizia Duplex (4+1 bed, 4,694-5,102 sf), and Morinda Duplex (5+1 bed, 6,738 sf) — there is no 2-bedroom or small-format product, positioning the project squarely for large families and high-net-worth owner-occupiers. Sime Darby Property, a Bursa-listed Tier 1 developer, posted a Q1 FY2026 (quarter ended March 2026) net profit of RM 158.8 million, up 34% year-on-year, with RM 4.1 billion in unbilled sales giving more than three years of earnings visibility — a financially sound parent. The project itself was delivered on schedule in March 2014, and its JMB has been formally registered and operating for years with no known handover disputes.

On pricing: the project's own Brickz building-level transactions (Aug 2024–Jul 2025, 9 sales) show a median of RM 715 psf — one of the few high-confidence, self-anchored valuation cases on this site. Current asking PSF runs RM 668-779 (midpoint ~RM 723), a premium of only about +1% over that anchor — a clean market-clearing price. The original 2012-13 off-plan launch was priced around RM 900 psf; twelve years post-completion, actual transactions have settled to RM 715 psf, showing the launch-era premium has been fully absorbed and today's buyers are paying a price supported by genuine demand rather than developer marketing markup. Over the past three years PSF climbed from RM 465.5 to RM 570.3 (+22.5%, roughly +7%/year) — a moderate but real appreciation track.

On rental: using the 3,627 sf Grande 1 as the representative unit, market rents run roughly RM 11,000-18,000/month depending on size, translating to RM 2.67-3.31 psf/month — a gross yield of about 5.1% for the representative unit, actually higher than many comparable luxury condos elsewhere in KL (Desa ParkCity's Park Regent, for instance, runs closer to 4.3%), largely because Kenny Hills Residence's entry price (from RM 2.59m) is lower relative to the size of unit it delivers. That yield figure, however, rests on a narrow tenant pool (corporate executives, diplomats, international-school families) and thin active rental listings, meaning real-world vacancy risk could run higher than the modeled assumption. The RM 0.55 psf/month maintenance fee stays within a reasonable range.

Exit is where the honest caution lives: Brickz shows only 9 transactions in the past 12 months (a nominal 14.3% turnover — "very active" on paper), yet EdgeProp/iProperty listing counts (deduplicated estimate) run 30-38 units, close to half the 63-unit building — far above the 30% "exit-run" threshold. That combination suggests a large number of owners are simultaneously attempting to sell, but actual completed sales remain limited, implying a longer listing-to-transaction window and likely wider discounts than the headline turnover number suggests. The quantum test shows a next-buyer household income threshold of roughly RM 28,530/month, well above the KL median of RM 10,805 — the local buyer pool is inherently narrow, and since Bukit Tunku has never been a heavily expat-concentrated district the foreign buyer pool isn't especially deep either. Both pools running thin at once — not tax friction — is the real exit risk here.

10-Year Scenario Baseline: assuming entry in August 2026 and exit in August 2036:
  • Base case: PSF growth decelerates from the recent ~7%/year track to 3%/year, rent grows 3%/year, 1-month vacancy, listing overhang persists but transactions still clear. Malaysian buyers see a 10-year cumulative net return of roughly RM 584,000, IRR ~4-5%; foreign buyers roughly RM 279,000, IRR ~2-3%.
  • Bull case: the surrounding Kenny Heights development lifts the district's overall profile, prices grow 5%/year, rental demand firms up. Malaysian IRR ~6-7% (RM 1.307m+), foreign IRR ~4-5% (RM 928,000+).
  • Bear case: the listing overhang converts into real discounting, prices are flat, and the exit waiting period stretches beyond two years. Malaysian buyers see a 10-year net loss of roughly RM 286,000 (IRR ~−1%); foreign buyers a net loss of roughly RM 502,000 (IRR ~−2%).
10-year exit liquidity: a listing rate near 50% signals a serious backlog of would-be sellers, and Brickz depth of only 9 transactions in 12 months is shallow. Expect a listing-to-sale window of 6-12 months or longer (typical for large, high-ticket units) and discounts in the 10-20% range — liquidity well below active, deep-market projects such as DPC or TRX.
◆ Verdict

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • Bukit Tunku is one of KL's "Three Sacred Hills," an old-money, royal, and elite address with a pedigree no developer can replicate
  • Sime Darby Property is a Bursa-listed Tier 1 developer with Q1 FY2026 net profit up 34% and RM 4.1bn in unbilled sales — a financially sound, non-distressed parent
  • Completed in 2014 and delivered on schedule — zero off-plan execution risk; the project's own 12-year Brickz history gives a rare building-level, high-confidence valuation anchor (RM 715 psf, only +1% premium)
  • 4.75 acres, 63 units, ultra-low density (13 units/acre); every unit is oversized (3,627-6,738 sf) — a rare large-family owner-occupier product line
  • Freehold residential title + gross yield of ~5.1% (above many comparable luxury condos) + MM2H compliance comfortably clears both Gold and Platinum thresholds, keeping foreign holding friction structurally low
  • The break-even appreciation rate required is not especially demanding (~1.3%/year Malaysian, ~1.9%/year foreign), aided by the relatively strong rental yield
⚠ Caveats
  • Estimated listing rate near 50% (close to half the building simultaneously for sale), far above the 30% exit-run threshold — a large number of owners appear to be trying to sell at once, with limited completed transactions
  • Only 9 Brickz transactions in 12 months, below the 10-transaction confidence floor; the tension between a nominally "active" 14.3% turnover rate and the listing overhang warrants on-the-ground verification of real listing-to-sale timelines across multiple agents
  • Quantum threshold of roughly RM 28,530/month household income is well above the KL median, narrowing the local buyer pool; Bukit Tunku's traditional buyer base skews local old-money rather than expat-heavy, so the foreign pool is not especially deep either — both pools run thin
  • The in-district Kenny Heights 88-acre mixed-use project, though rolling out slowly, still represents genuine medium-term competing supply over its 10-15-year horizon — scarcity is not an absolute cap
  • The building is now 12 years old, with facility specifications (no large clubhouse, no modernized gym systems) trailing newer luxury launches — renovation and upgrade costs should be factored into long-term holding budgets
  • The ~5.1% gross yield rests on a narrow tenant pool (corporate executives, diplomats) and thin active rental listings, so real vacancy risk could run higher than the modeled assumption

Core Residence @ TRX

Shares the same TRX MRT 2-line interchange and Exchange TRX mall catchment as sister tower TRX Residences by Lendlease; developer is a CCCG (a Chinese state-owned enterprise) × WCT Holdings Berhad JV, and this is CCCG's debut property development in Malaysia. ◆ Deep Narrative

Project Deep Summary

Core Residence @ TRX sits within Persiaran TRX 2, Tun Razak Exchange, 55188 Kuala Lumpur — one of two flagship residential projects inside TRX's 70-acre international financial district master plan, the other being sister card TRX Residences by Lendlease already scored on this site. Behind it stands Core Precious Development Sdn Bhd, a joint venture between China Communications Construction Group (CCCG, #61 on the 2025 Fortune Global 500, ~US$106.8B revenue) holding 80%, and WCT Holdings Berhad (a Bursa-listed Malaysian construction and property group, KLSE:9679) holding 20%. The original plan called for 3 towers, 700 units, and roughly RM 1.4B in gross development value; the project ultimately completed as 2 towers and 580 units, originally targeted for 2023 but handed over in 2024, a slip of about a year.

On location, the TRX district carries this site's 7.0 baseline. The scarcity audit runs into the same structural problem TRX Residences faces on both the land and product layers: supply is shared across the wider TRX master plan (TRX Residences' own 6-tower plan, and this project's launch scope was itself trimmed down), and the commercial-SA product type is highly replicable in-district. Freehold is no longer a differentiator between the two TRX projects, since both carry it. On integration strength, this project sits roughly 100 metres from TRX MRT (Kajang + Putrajaya line interchange), comparable to TRX Residences; but reaching Exchange TRX mall requires a 5-minute street-level walk rather than TRX Residences' indoor connection directly atop the mall — a genuinely weaker integration. Net scarcity score: 6.5, a shade below TRX Residences' 6.8.

On the developer, CCCG brings 20 years of Malaysia infrastructure experience (Port Klang land reclamation, the Sultan Abdul Halim Muadzam Shah Bridge in Penang) and enormous parent-level financial scale — the strongest pillar behind this project's brand backing. But this is CCCG's first-ever property development in Malaysia, with no comparable delivery record to check against, and the JV structure is a pure commercial partnership between CCCG and WCT, unlike TRX Residences' government-anchored structure through the Ministry of Finance's wholly-owned TRX City subsidiary. WCT Holdings' Q1 FY2026 (ended 2026-03-31) profit attributable to equity holders was RM 4.6M, down 61.7% from RM 12M a year earlier — a deteriorating trend worth watching. Sponsor score: 6.5, below TRX Residences' 7.5.

On pricing, this project's original launch price was roughly RM 1,900-2,059 psf (2019-2020), against a current asking range of RM 2,200-2,400 psf — implying substantial cumulative appreciation, but there is no verifiable Brickz subsale record to confirm it: nearly two years post-completion, the secondary market here remains a blank page, a sharp contrast with TRX Residences, which accumulated 59 transactions within 14 months of completion, a median of RM 2,293 psf, and +4% appreciation over 12 months. Falling back on TRX Residences' Brickz median as a same-location anchor, this project's asking sits only ~+0.3% above it — essentially at par — but confidence is only medium, and the valuation score is 5.8.

On rental economics, the representative 2BR 850 sqft mid-level unit, priced at roughly RM 1.95M (@ RM 2,300 psf), carries an asking rent of RM 10-10.70 psf/month (roughly RM 8,800/month), implying a gross yield of ~5.4% — nominally better than TRX Residences' 4.9%. But iProperty/PropertyGuru simultaneously show 516-578 units listed for rent — nearly the entire 580-unit stock — signaling intense rental competition, and actual achieved rents are very plausibly well below asking. Cash flow scores: 5.5 (local) / 3.5 (foreign), only a modest improvement over TRX Residences' 5.2/3.2.

On holding structure, this is a freehold commercial serviced-apartment title (Commercial Strata under HDA), the same class as TRX Residences, with identical financing friction (LTV 80-85%, rate +0.5-1%). It is on exit that the two projects genuinely diverge: 217-238 units (over 35%) sit listed for sale simultaneously, yet Brickz shows zero verified subsale transactions — a pattern that reads far more like "investor stock concentrated on entry, still searching for an exit buyer" than TRX Residences' healthy, high-turnover secondary market that clears its listings. Exit scores here are just 4.6 (local) / 3.1 (foreign), materially below TRX Residences' 6.4/5.5 — the single biggest point of divergence between the two cards.

Key watchpoints: (1) zero Brickz transactions mean there is no independent third-party data to verify whether asking prices are realistic, leaving both buyer negotiating leverage and seller price anchors without an objective reference; (2) as CCCG's debut property development, delivery quality and long-term property-management standards remain unproven over time; (3) WCT's weakening profitability is worth tracking in the next quarterly results; (4) the RM 0.80 psf maintenance fee, the highest among TRX peers, erodes net yield; (5) a CCCG-linked buyer base may concentrate in a specific origin, meaning any tightening of related policy (foreign stamp duty, capital controls) could hit the exit pool harder than it would a broadly international one.

  • Bull case: TRX financial district ecosystem fully matures, a stable exit-buyer pool forms, and prices grow +5%/yr. Local buyer ~RM 1.08M+, foreign buyer ~RM 751,000+.
  • Bear case: transaction evidence continues to be absent, listing pressure persists, no exit-buyer pool forms, and prices grow 0%/yr. Local buyer loses RM 121,000 over 10 years, foreign buyer loses RM 326,000.
10-year exit liquidity: the core risk is that if an active secondary market still fails to exist after 10 years, even a paper price gain may not translate into a realistic exit timeline or price — a starkly different picture from TRX Residences, whose 50+ Brickz transaction depth supports an expectation of clearing a listing within 3-6 months.

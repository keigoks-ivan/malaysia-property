A large-format upgrader play where exit rests on locals, not foreigners — but with 11 concurrent competing launches and a valuation premium, scarcity comes at a discount. This is Sunway's first phase in Bukit Jalil: freehold residential title, 748 units (1,055–2,562 sqft, entirely 3-bedroom-plus, no small investor units), completing very soon (December 2026). The exit thesis is clean — residential title gives the next buyer 90% LTV, and the large-format unit mix genuinely appeals to local upgrader families, lifting local exit to 7.0. The cost is equally clear: verification confirms Bukit Jalil's Pavilion mall belongs to Malton/Pavilion REIT and KL Wellness City belongs to KLWC — Sunway owns no mall or hospital here to integrate with, capping the developer score at 7.5 (below Sunway Velocity 3/Cochrane's 8.0); freehold is not rare on this corridor either (Flora 2, Flex Suite, and Park Green are all freehold), holding scarcity to 5.5; and the representative unit sits over 20% above the best available transacted anchor, limiting valuation to 4.8.

Foreign buyer threshold notice (entry units here are off-limits to foreigners): The minimum foreign purchase threshold in KL (Federal Territory) is RM1,000,000. This project's 1,055 sqft (~RM700,000) and 1,206 sqft (~RM850,000) unit types are both below the threshold and cannot be bought by foreigners. This card's representative unit — 1,507 sqft, mid-floor, ~RM1,085,000 — clears the threshold by only about 8.5%, buying but with thin buffer. Only the 2,562 sqft penthouse duplex (~RM2,671,000) sits comfortably and substantially above the threshold. In practical terms, only a limited share of this project's 748 units are actually purchasable by foreigners, and most of those sit near the threshold's edge. Foreign buyers should confirm in writing with the developer, before signing, that the actual net transacted price for the specific unit is indeed ≥ RM1,000,000 (early-bird discounts can push net price below the threshold).

◆ Exit Reality Check

Exit Reality Check

The v5.0 three-part check: scarcity audit (is there a supply-side moat), next-buyer pyramid (who buys in 10 years), and secondary-market evidence (turnover/listing/discount). This project completes December 2026, about 4 months out, and has no full transfer-history Brickz record of its own yet; turnover rate uses The Z Residence, also a Bukit Jalil freehold condo (1,136 units, completed 2014) as a proxy, while the listing ratio uses this project's own current listings (still thin, during the pre-completion sales period).

5A · Scarcity Audit (Three Supply-Side Layers)
Can the market reproduce an identical asset in 10 years? All three layers fail here — the weakest scarcity case among projects analyzed on this site.
✗Land layer: Bukit Jalil still has large tracts under construction or undeveloped; KL Wellness City's 26.5-acre master plan is only partially complete; at least 11 new launches are underway concurrently, far from a supply cap.
✗Product layer: Freehold high-rise condos are not unique on this corridor — Flex Suite and Park Green (commercial SA) are both freehold; the most direct evidence of replicability is the developer's own next phase, Sunway Flora 2 (also freehold, completing 2029), a near-identical positioning.
✗Regulatory layer: Multiple freehold projects already coexist on this corridor, so freehold status cannot generate the kind of "uniqueness" premium seen on Sunway Cochrane's street (where One Cochrane was the sole freehold comparator); there is no height cap or master-plan ceiling protecting supply scarcity here.
Effective-scarcity verdict: all three layers are false scarcity. This project's moat, if any, lies not in supply but in demand-side product differentiation — all 748 units are 3-bedroom-plus large format with no ≤650 sqft investor-type small units, a relatively uncommon positioning against Bukit Jalil's mostly small-unit investor-driven new supply. But the framework requires demand-side transacted-premium evidence ("rare and wanted"), which this project lacks (no measurable evidence that the large-format mix commands a transacted premium), so the scarcity adjustment is limited to −0.5 (high replicability) — no further penalty, no bonus.
5B · Next-Buyer Pyramid
Quantum test: Representative unit RM1,085,000 → next-buyer household monthly income threshold of about RM11,935 (price × 1.1%: 90% LTV, 4.3%, 30 years, 40% debt-service ratio) → against KL's median household income of RM10,805 (DOSM Household Income Survey 2024, highest nationally), the representative unit's threshold sits slightly above what the median KL household can service, putting the buyer pool at roughly the top 40% of KL households. This is a solid urban-upgrader band, not a first-time-buyer or ultra-wealthy niche.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep-Dive Summary

Sunway Flora is Sunway Property's first residential project in Bukit Jalil, sited in Mutiara Bukit Jalil on 5.37 acres of freehold land, comprising 748 units across twin towers — Tower A with 379 units over 38 storeys, Tower B with 369 units over 40 storeys. Unit types run from 1,055 sqft (3-bedroom) to a 2,562 sqft penthouse duplex, entirely large-format 3-bedroom-plus configurations, wrapped around a 1.3-acre private linear park and a 50-metre infinity pool, and carrying GreenRE Platinum certification — the first project in Bukit Jalil to achieve it. Launched in 2023 as part of the "Sunway Signature Series 2024," the project is expected to complete in December 2026, roughly four months from now, making it the nearest-term completion of any project analyzed on this site.

The first thing worth understanding about this project is what the "Sunway" name actually means in Bukit Jalil — and the answer is: considerably less than it means in the Cheras/Maluri corridor. Part of what lifts Sunway Velocity 3 and Sunway Cochrane's scores is that Sunway itself built the mall, the hospital, and the university there, with covered bridges connecting directly in — landlord and operator are the same entity. Verification confirms Bukit Jalil is nothing like that: the area's landmark, Pavilion Bukit Jalil (1.8 million sqft), was developed by Malton Berhad and sold to Pavilion REIT in 2023; and the much-discussed KL Wellness City medical township (including the 624–645-bed KL International Hospital) is led by KLWC Sdn Bhd, founded by physician Dato' Dr Colin Lee. Neither has any connection to Sunway. This project is Sunway's pure residential play here, and the developer dimension is scored at a 7.5 baseline rather than the 8.0 given to its two sibling projects — this is not a knock on Sunway's balance sheet or construction capability (1QFY26 core pre-tax profit is still up 52%), it simply reflects that the "owned-ecosystem-integration" bonus does not apply to this particular location.

The transport and location story follows a similar pattern: a 700-metre covered walkway connects directly to Muhibbah LRT (Ampang Line, Sri Petaling branch) — considerably longer than Cochrane's 60 metres, but still a genuine pedestrian route; Regent International School within the area and Tzu Chi International School nearby round out international-school coverage, and KL International Hospital is expected to open around the same year this project completes. Transport scores 7.0, held back on two counts: the walk to the LRT exceeds the framework's top-tier cutoff distance, and Pavilion Bukit Jalil is about 3km away by car, not a direct mall connection. Scarcity is this project's weakest dimension at 5.5 — all three supply-side audit layers (land, product, regulatory) fail: at least 11 new launches are underway concurrently in Bukit Jalil, freehold is not rare on this corridor, and the developer's own next phase, Sunway Flora 2, due three years later, is itself the most direct evidence of replicability.

Price and cash flow are the second layer of cost. The representative 1,507 sqft 4-bedroom mid-floor unit runs about RM1,085,000 (≈ RM720 psf), against the best transacted anchor this site could find — The Treez Jalil Residence (also freehold, completed 2013), Brickz median RM596 psf — a premium of roughly +20.8%, sitting at the boundary between the framework's "elevated" and "needs strict scrutiny" bands, so valuation is scored at 4.8. That premium deserves a discount for context: Treez is 13 years old, while this project is a brand-new, GreenRE Platinum-certified product — the two were never going to trade at the same price; and against Bukit Jalil's "near-MRT new launches at roughly RM1,000–1,500 psf," this project's RM720 psf actually sits at the upper edge of the "ready subsale market at RM650–900 psf," not the expensive end of comparable new supply. The rental side is blunter: the 1,507 sqft unit is estimated to rent for RM2,800/month, a gross yield of only about 3.1%, with negative carry on both sides (local −2.25% / foreign −3.25%) — the absolute rent on a large-format unit simply cannot keep pace with the absolute price. The 10-year return, again, rests almost entirely on capital appreciation, and the entry cost is not cheap to begin with.

Exit is where this project genuinely shines. Freehold residential title gives the next buyer 90% LTV and standard mortgage pricing, identical to Cochrane's advantage; and all 748 units being 3-bedroom-plus large format with no ≤650 sqft investor units means this project serves families genuinely needing more space, not pure flip-oriented investors. Local exit scores 7.0 — the quantum test shows the representative unit's threshold (monthly income RM11,935) sits slightly above KL's median household income (RM10,805), putting the buyer pool at roughly the top 40% of KL households, a solid urban-upgrader band rather than a speculative one. Foreign exit scores 5.0: the representative unit at RM1,085,000 clears the RM1M threshold by only 8.5%, a thin buffer, and this project's entry-level 1,055 sqft (~RM700k) and 1,206 sqft (~RM850k) units are entirely off-limits to foreign buyers — only the 1,507 sqft-plus configurations are actually sellable to foreigners.

Taken together across all seven dimensions, Sunway Flora presents a clear but not cheap upgrader play: transport and the exit structure are genuine strengths, scarcity and valuation are genuine costs, and developer ecosystem integration — the factor that lifts this developer's Cheras projects — simply doesn't exist in Bukit Jalil. Buyers need to be clear-eyed that the premium they're paying (+20.8%) and the modest gross yield (~3.1%) buy an asset whose 10-year exit speed still depends on how the broader market absorbs at least 11 concurrent competing launches — not, as with Cochrane, an institutional moat they can lean on.

10-Year Scenario Baseline: assuming completion in late 2026 and a 10-year hold from handover (representative unit RM1,085,000):
  • Base case (+3%/year price growth): Sunway delivers on schedule, Bukit Jalil upgrader demand holds steady, and KLIH's opening provides some spillover. Local 10-year cumulative net return is about RM65,000, IRR about 2.2%; foreign is about −RM37,000, IRR about −1.1%.
  • Bull case (+5%/year): KL Wellness City fully completes, Bukit Jalil upgrader demand accelerates, and the large-format positioning is gradually recognized by the market. Local is about RM367,000, IRR about 8.4%; foreign is about RM234,000, IRR about 4.3%.
  • Bear case (+1%/year): The 11 concurrent projects complete in sequence, KL residential overhang keeps building, and Sunway Flora 2 joins the competition three years later. Local 10-year loss is about RM189,000, IRR about −4.2%; foreign loss is about RM265,000, IRR about −4.6%.
10-year exit liquidity: extrapolating from The Z Residence's actual figures (1.4% annual turnover), local exit is expected to take 8–14 months from listing to sale with a 6-10% discount; foreign buyers, constrained by the RM1M threshold and this area not being a foreign-buyer hotspot, face a narrower pool and an expected 12–18 months with an 8-13% discount. Annual price appreciation needed to break even on all costs plus the 5% opportunity cost: about 2.5%/year for locals, 3.6%/year for foreigners.

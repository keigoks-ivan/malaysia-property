◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Dive

Razak City Residences (RC Residences) sits on Jalan Razak Mansion in Sungai Besi, on the site of Razak Mansion — public housing opened in 1962 by then-Prime Minister Tun Abdul Razak. This is the first scorecard on this site to cover the Sungai Besi submarket, and it is also the most direct real-world match to this brief's original thesis (ex-air-force-base redevelopment, LRT access, Bandar Malaysia periphery): the project sits directly across the road from the future Bandar Malaysia site, the former 197-hectare Royal Malaysian Air Force base.

But verification revealed the "Bandar Malaysia catalyst" is more complicated than it first appears. MRT Corp did build two dedicated underground stations for the site — Bandar Malaysia Utara and Bandar Malaysia Selatan, both on the Putrajaya Line — but when the Kuala Lumpur-Singapore High-Speed Rail project was cancelled in 2017, the wider Bandar Malaysia development was mothballed along with it, and both stations remain closed to the public to this day; trains simply pass through without stopping. It was not until November 2025 that the Finance Ministry announced the redevelopment is expected to break ground "end-2026," with the masterplan still under review and 50 acres gazetted as Malay Reserve Land. In other words, this project sits next to a catalyst that has not yet arrived, but has already stalled once before — exactly the risk this brief asked us to verify rather than assume away, and it is reflected honestly in both the appreciation score (4.8) and the exit narrative below.

On the scarcity audit, this is one of the least scarce projects reviewed on this site: 5,748 service-residence units plus 121 shop-offices on 22 acres — far larger than any prior case we've covered — and essentially a scaled-up repeat of developer Akisama Group's own OUG Parklane formula (4,225 units, 100% sold). Land, product, and regulatory layers all fail the audit, pulling the scarcity score (5.2) down 0.8 points from the "other KL" default of 6.0 — the only dimension in this review requiring an independent written rationale for its deviation.

The project's genuine differentiator is cashflow and affordability. The representative unit (800 sqft 2BR) currently trades around RM370,000, a roughly 10.5% discount to the same-project Brickz transacted median (RM517 psf); gross rental yield runs around 6.0%, with maintenance fees of only about RM0.15 psf — among the best cashflow profiles reviewed on this site. The quantum test shows a next-buyer income threshold of only about RM4,070/month, far below the national household income median, giving the project (especially first-time buyers and investors) an extremely wide local buyer pool. But verification also confirmed a hard structural fact: not a single unit type in the entire project — including the largest, 1,140 sqft, launched at RM549,000 — reaches the RM1,000,000 KL federal-territory foreign-buyer threshold. This is not a case of the representative unit sitting just at the boundary; it is a complete legal exclusion. Foreign buyers have no lawful path to purchase anything here, which makes this the only scorecard on this site to date with structurally zero foreign dependency, and it is the core reason the Foreign score (5.2) sits meaningfully below the Malaysian score (6.0).

Developer Akisama Group is a private company founded in 1979, with at least 10 completed prior projects, including OUG Parklane — a proven precedent for exactly this large-format commercial-SA playbook. Forum sentiment describes the developer as "still reliable," with no MACC investigation record found, but because it is privately held, no independently verifiable public financial statements could be located — a known limitation.

◆ Verdict

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • ~6.0% gross yield with only ~RM0.15 psf maintenance is one of the best cashflow profiles reviewed on this site; the Malaysian break-even appreciation rate is only ~0-0.5%/year, well below the 1.5-4.5%/year typical of comparable cases
  • Quantum income threshold is only ~RM4,070/month, far below the national household median (RM7,017), giving local first-time buyers and investors an extremely wide pool; listing rate is only ~2.1% (healthy, no exit-queue pressure)
  • Current listings (~RM462.5 psf) sit roughly 10.5% below the same-project Brickz transacted median (RM517 psf), offering a real margin of safety
  • Directly across the road from Bandar Malaysia (the former 197-hectare air-force base) — if redevelopment breaks ground on schedule end-2026, this is the most directly-matched case to this brief's original location thesis
⚠ Caveats
  • Foreign buyers are legally ineligible to purchase any unit here — every unit type, including the largest (1,140 sqft, RM549,000), prices below the RM1,000,000 KL foreign-buyer threshold. This is a structural exclusion, not a boundary risk; foreign investors should rule this project out entirely
  • The Bandar Malaysia catalyst has already been "mothballed" once (its dedicated MRT stations have sat unopened since the 2017 HSR cancellation); the November 2025 re-announcement of an end-2026 groundbreaking should be treated as "announced, not yet started" rather than "confirmed"
  • All three scarcity-audit layers fail (land uncapped, product highly standardized and replicable, no regulatory lock-in); at 5,748 units plus at least 7 recent/under-construction corridor competitors, long-term scarcity is weak
  • Only 6 Brickz transactions and ~0.10% annual turnover indicate a thin secondary market — buyers should call at least 3-5 local agents before committing to confirm real transaction depth and listing quality

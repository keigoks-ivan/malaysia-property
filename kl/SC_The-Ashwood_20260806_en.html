◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Dive

The Ashwood @ U-Thant sits inside Taman U-Thant/Ampang Hilir — historically KL's diplomatic quarter, "Embassy Row," where the French, Thai, Swiss and other embassies and ambassadorial residences have clustered since the 1960s under a deliberately low-density plan (80-150 persons/acre, height-restricted). It's a rare intersection of old money and the diplomatic corps. Developed by Paramount Property (Cityview) Sdn Bhd, whose parent Paramount Corporation Berhad is Bursa main-board listed (KLSE:PARAMON), the project sits on 3.59 acres and comprises 354 units across three formats: 302 standard condominium units (Tower A at 49 storeys, Tower B at 9), 40 two-storey duplexes, and 12 three-to-four-storey villas — the largest and tallest development U-Thant has ever seen. There's a tension worth stating plainly: the tower's own 49-storey scale is itself the clearest evidence that the marketed "low-density, height-capped embassy enclave" narrative does not actually apply to the Jalan Ampang Hilir boundary parcel this project occupies — which is exactly why the scarcity audit's land and regulatory layers came back only partially holding rather than fully capped.

The project has a genuinely rare amenity: both Sayfol International School and the ISKL Ampang Hilir campus sit inside Taman U-Thant itself, walkable from the site — a dual international-school cluster that's uncommon anywhere in KL. Add Gleneagles Kuala Lumpur roughly 2km away and KLCC just 1.8km out, and the appeal to diplomatic and multinational-corporate families is clear. But the nearest transit — Ampang Park (MRT/LRT) and Dato' Keramat (LRT) — sits 1.1-1.3km away, beyond comfortable walking distance and more of a short drive, and there's no mall-tier retail directly connected, so daily errands still lean on a car.

Since launching in May 2024, the project posted 70% take-up within two months and has recently been reported at 72% — real evidence of demand for this positioning. But it's worth flagging that Paramount is simultaneously pursuing a second project, "The Atrium," on the very same U-Thant street: in December 2024 it acquired the neighbouring 93-unit Brunsfield Residence site for RM145m, with a planned RM300m redevelopment into another high-end strata product — the most directly comparable competing supply this project will face within five years. The same land deal has also drawn a RM313m third-party lawsuit against a 70%-owned Paramount subsidiary; while unconnected to The Ashwood's own land, it signals rising legal and execution risk as the developer pushes further into this district. At the group level, Paramount remains financially solid — FY2025 net profit of RM118.82 million (+16% YoY) — with a 35-year track record showing no public delivery-delay disputes.

On pricing: as a fresh off-plan launch with no transaction history of its own, and no recent comparable new completion in the district, the valuation anchor has to fall back to the next-best option — the secondary Brickz median for Sastra U-Thant, a comparable freehold large-format condo in the same district, at RM 1,065 psf (confidence: medium). The representative unit (a 1,544 sqft median simplex) launches at roughly RM 1,200 psf, a premium of about +13% over that anchor — landing in the "elevated, needs a catalyst" valuation tier. On rent, using district large-format listing comparables (RM 2.25-4.67 psf/month), the representative unit's gross yield comes out to only around 3.0%, well below the 5% cash-flow baseline. The required 10-year appreciation to break even — roughly 3.2%/year for Malaysians, 3.8%/year for foreigners — is therefore higher than most KL scorecards' typical threshold: this asset's return is heavily weighted toward capital gains rather than holding-period income.

The exit picture deserves equal caution. Since the project is still under construction, the closest available evidence comes from two comparable district freehold condos, Sastra U-Thant and U-Thant Residence, used as proxies — both carry secondary listing rates in the 40-55% range, far past the 30% exit-crunch threshold, suggesting the Embassy Row brand narrative hasn't fully translated into fast secondary-market liquidity. The quantum test puts the next-buyer household income threshold at roughly RM 20,350/month, in the top 15-20% of KL households by income — a moderately narrow local pool. Foreign MM2H owner-occupiers clear the entire product range against the Gold threshold with room to spare, and the exit price sits in the sweet spot — but Embassy Row's historical expat concentration also means foreign-dependency risk needs ongoing monitoring rather than an assumption that the local pool can simply absorb the slack.

Highlights & Caveats

  • A rare combination of Embassy Row's historical standing plus walkable access to two international schools (Sayfol, ISKL) — one of KL's few genuine diplomatic/international-living zones
  • Paramount Corporation Berhad is Bursa main-board listed, FY2025 net profit RM 118.82 million (+16% YoY), financially solid with a 35-year track record and no public major delivery-delay disputes
  • First-year take-up of 70-72% since the May 2024 launch demonstrates real market demand for this positioning
  • Freehold residential tenure, 354 units across 302 condo + 40 duplex + 12 villa formats, offering a range of unit types

Bukit Jalil City · Kuala Lumpur ◆ FREEHOLD · COMMERCIAL-TITLE SERVICED APARTMENT (HDA) ◆ Data as of 2026-08-05

Park Green Pavilion

Low density plus a private bridge into the mall is the scarcity pitch; commercial title and a supply wave drag on cash flow and exit. At 453 units across just two 47-storey towers with 6-7 units per floor, this is the lowest-density launch currently selling in Bukit Jalil, and it is the only one connected to Pavilion Bukit Jalil Mall by a dedicated covered bridge — the one feature among 11-plus concurrent launches in the precinct that genuinely cannot be copied. Set against that: commercial title means real next-buyer financing friction, gross rental yield on the representative unit is only about 2.7%, and Bukit Jalil alone has 2,800-plus units completing in 2026-2027. Ten-year returns rest almost entirely on two things that have not happened yet: whether the scarcity pitch converts into a resale premium, and whether the MRT3 Circle Line opens on schedule.

Foreign purchase threshold — this project is a borderline case: the minimum foreign purchase price in KL (Federal Territory) is RM 1,000,000. This card's representative unit (the smallest comparable size, 1,201 sqft, entry price about RM 1,080,000) clears the threshold by only about 8% — a thin buffer. If early-bird discounts, furniture packages or market negotiation push the net transacted price below the line, that unit no longer qualifies for foreign purchase. The same threshold shapes the ten-year exit: if the price has not held above RM 1m, the foreign resale pool vanishes entirely. Foreign buyers should get written confirmation from the developer of the actual net price of the specific unit before signing. Also note: this project is a commercial-title serviced apartment, not residential title — the next buyer's financing terms (80-85% LTV, mortgage rate +0.5 to 1.0 points) are tighter than residential-title stock, which is a structural deduction in the exit dimension.

◆ EXIT REALITY CHECK

Exit Reality Check

The v5.0 three-part check: a scarcity audit (is there a supply-side moat), a next-buyer pyramid (who buys it in ten years), and secondary-market evidence (turnover, listings, discounts). This is an off-plan project (statutory completion around 2029) with no post-completion Brickz history of its own; secondary-market evidence is proxied from "Residensi Park" (a freehold serviced apartment in the same precinct), confidence: medium (same precinct, same product form — the closest proxy available on this site — though the name correspondence has not been fully verified).

5A · Scarcity audit (three supply-side layers)
Can the market reproduce an equivalent asset in ten years? Two of the three layers fail here, and the one that passes is narrow in scope.
✗Land layer: the broader Bukit Jalil precinct is still held by multiple landowners (Exsim, Sunway, Chin Hin, Ho Hup, Berjaya), with at least 11 concurrent launches known as of August 2026 — replicability in the wider precinct is high.
✗Product layer: freehold serviced apartments are not rare in this supply wave (Sunway Flora, Flora 2, Flex Suite and this project are all freehold) — other landowners can replicate the same product form.
✓ (narrow scope)Regulatory layer: this project is the final residential phase of Malton's own 50-acre Bukit Jalil City master plan, and the specific parcel integrated with Pavilion Bukit Jalil Mall via a dedicated bridge cannot itself be reproduced — but this scarcity is confined to this one site and does not cap supply across Bukit Jalil as a whole.
Effective-scarcity verdict: the regulatory-layer scarcity is real but very narrow in scope; the land and product layers are both false scarcity. This project has no Brickz resale history of its own to confirm whether the "low density plus mall-bridge link" differentiation has converted, or will convert, into a premium the market is willing to pay — a take-up rate above 65% is only a primary-sale demand signal, not evidence of a secondary-market scarcity premium. The net scarcity adjustment is therefore only −0.3 (the +0.2 micro-location bonus is more than offset by the −0.5 high-replicability deduction), not a scarcity bonus.
5B · Next-buyer pyramid
Quantum test: representative unit RM 1,080,000 → next-buyer household income threshold of about RM 11,880/month (price × 1.1%: 90% LTV, 4.3%, 30 years, mortgage burden at 40% of income) → against KL's median household income of RM 10,802-10,805/month (DOSM Household Income Survey 2024/2025, the highest in Malaysia), the representative unit's threshold sits slightly above the median household's affordability, putting the addressable pool at roughly the top 40-45% of KL households. This is a standard metro upgrader band, but the 3-4 bedroom, large-format layout also naturally excludes first-time buyers and small households.

◆ IN DEPTH

Project Deep Dive

Park Green Pavilion is the final residential phase of Malton Berhad's 50-acre Bukit Jalil City master plan — two 47-storey towers, 453 units, only 6-7 per floor, making it the lowest-density launch among at least 11 concurrent projects currently selling in Bukit Jalil. Its most substantive point of difference is not the land title: freehold is common in this supply wave, with Sunway Flora, Flora 2 and Flex Suite all freehold too. It is a dedicated covered bridge that carries residents directly into Pavilion Bukit Jalil Mall, a 2024 FIABCI World Prix d'Excellence winner in retail. That is the only claim in the scarcity dimension that actually holds up: the land-layer and product-layer audits both fail, and only the parcel physically integrated with the mall cannot be replicated.

But that regulatory-layer scarcity is narrow — it locks up this one parcel, not the whole of Bukit Jalil. As of August 2026, at least ten competing launches are selling concurrently — Ren Residence, Sunway Flora, Quaver, The Jalil Residence, D'Velada, Ayanna, Flex Suite, The Vividz, Sunway Flora 2 and Kingswoodz — and EdgeProp estimates more than 2,800 units completing in 2026-2027 alone. Market commentary already states the conclusion plainly: serviced apartments in outer Bukit Jalil are expected to underperform in 2026, with price growth likely flat or slightly negative. That supply wave is the main reason capital appreciation momentum scores only 4.7, even though the MRT3 Circle Line station at Bukit Jalil broke ground in Q3 2025 and targets a 2030 opening that would put KLCC just 28 minutes away direct — a real but as-yet-unrealised catalyst that this project's medium-term appreciation case leans on heavily.

Entry valuation is, by contrast, this project's clearest strength. With no 2BR type, every unit runs 1,201-1,905 sqft across 3-4 bedrooms, and the smallest, 1,201 sqft, enters at about RM 1,080,000 (≈ RM 899 psf). Against the closest genuine transacted anchor available on this site — "Residensi Park," a freehold serviced apartment in the same precinct with a Brickz transacted median of RM 958 psf (its own median price, coincidentally, also lands around RM 1.05m, a comparable quantum) — the representative unit is actually priced at a 6.2% discount, not a premium. That is worth recording honestly: several concurrent new launches, such as The Vividz, are already asking RM 800-980 psf or higher, so this project's RM 899 psf entry is not aggressive by comparison. Valuation scores 7.0, the highest of the seven dimensions here.

The cost shows up in the combination of rent and exit. The 1,201 sqft representative unit is estimated to rent for RM 2,450/month, a gross yield of only about 2.7% — large family-format units simply carry lower rent per square foot than small investor units, a structural feature rather than an estimation error. Post-tax net yield runs about 2.3% for Malaysian buyers and 1.9% for foreign buyers, against mortgage rates of 4.55% and 5.05% — both sides carry clearly negative carry, and this is not a carry asset. On exit, the commercial title (not residential) means the next buyer faces 80-85% LTV and a rate premium of 0.5-1.0 points, capping Malaysian exit at 6.2. Foreign exit is further constrained by the RM 1m threshold — the representative unit at RM 1,080,000 clears it by only about 8%, a thin buffer that vanishes entirely if a discount or a soft market pushes the price below the line, leaving foreign exit at just 4.5.

On the developer side, Malton Berhad's financial profile sits in the framework's Tier 2 "financial concerns" band: debt-to-equity of 60.6% is elevated, and the share price is down 34.78% year to date, reflecting real market caution about leverage. But a careful read of the latest results does not support treating this as the framework's defined "material 12-month loss": the 20.8% year-on-year revenue decline in FY2025 is mostly the high-base effect of the one-off RM 2.2 billion gain from selling 51% of Pavilion Bukit Jalil Mall to Pavilion REIT in 2023, not a new operating loss, and H1 FY2026 net profit of RM 11.5 million, up 100% year on year, shows earnings have already stabilised. Combined with the mall's on-schedule opening in late 2021 and its subsequent international award, the developer dimension scores 6.5 — sitting between Tier 1 and Tier 2, sturdier than the raw leverage numbers alone suggest, but still well short of an integrated Tier 1 name like Sunway or SP Setia.

Taken together, Park Green Pavilion is a scorecard with a clear pitch and a thin margin. Its genuine point of difference — low density, a bridge straight into the mall — is currently only a marketing claim; the project has no post-completion Brickz history of its own to confirm whether that differentiation has converted, or will convert, into a premium the market actually pays. A 65% take-up rate is primary-sale enthusiasm, not secondary-market scarcity evidence. What a buyer is really underwriting here is whether that pitch, together with the MRT3 line, can hold up prices against a supply wave of several thousand competing units released by 11-plus concurrent launches.

10-year scenario baseline: assuming entry in 2026, statutory completion around 2029, and a 10-year hold from handover (representative unit RM 1,080,000):
  • Base case (3%/year price growth): the project delivers on schedule, the low-density and mall-bridge scarcity pitch partly converts into a premium, and MRT3 keeps progressing. Malaysian 10-year cumulative net return about RM 50,000, IRR about 2.0%; foreign about −RM 46,000, IRR about −1.0%.
  • Bull case (5%/year): MRT3 opens on schedule in 2030, Bukit Jalil's supply wave gets absorbed faster than expected, and the mall-bridge scarcity premium is confirmed in resale data. Malaysian about RM 351,000, IRR about 8.0%; foreign about RM 224,000, IRR about 3.5%.
  • Bear case (1%/year): the 11-plus concurrent launches keep adding pressure, MRT3 slips, and demand for large-format commercial-SA rentals stays weak. Malaysian 10-year loss about RM 203,000, IRR about −4.5%; foreign loss about RM 273,000, IRR about −4.8%.
Ten-year exit liquidity: proxied from "Residensi Park" in the same precinct (estimated annual turnover 3-5%, this project's own for-sale listing rate 7.3%), a Malaysian seller can expect roughly 6-10 months from listing to sale with a 5-10% discount; foreign sellers, constrained by the RM 1m threshold (only about 8% of headroom) and Bukit Jalil's non-cluster foreign-buyer profile, face a narrower pool and roughly 10-16 months with an 8-15% discount. Price growth of about 2.6%/year (Malaysian) and 4.2%/year (foreign) is needed to break even against all costs and the 5% opportunity cost.

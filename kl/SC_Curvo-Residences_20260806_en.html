◆ In-Depth Summary

Project Deep Dive

Curvo Residences sits in Setapak as the third and final residential phase of SkyArena — SkyWorld Development's 28-acre, RM 2.9 billion, four-phase integrated township. The whole SkyArena concept is built around health and sport: a 9.4-acre multi-facility sports complex offers up to 40 sports facilities, including a FINA-certified 10-lane Olympic pool, 14 badminton courts, four squash courts, a track and a football field. Curvo itself is designed around a "wabi-sabi" philosophy, packing over 50 nature-themed amenities into the building — from a forest jog trail and cycling lane at ground level to an infinity pool and urban farm on the sky deck. This positioning earned the project the 2023 Star Property Awards (Skyline Awards) and the Asia Pacific Property Awards 2023 "Residential High-Rise Architecture" category — one of relatively few KL projects on this site with a genuine third-party architecture award behind it.

The central investment question is whether the "SkyArena finale" narrative can genuinely support a scarcity premium. The three-layer audit gives an honest, conservative answer: both the land and product layers point to un-scarce broader supply in Setapak — at least 6 competing projects (PV22 Residences, M Astra, Residensi MH Platinum 3, SkySanctuary, EkoTitiwangsa, Platinum Melati) are under construction or upcoming concurrently, and an 830-unit, twin-tower, high-density leasehold format is itself a highly replicable standard. The one claim that does hold up is narrower: Curvo is genuinely the last residential tower to be built on this specific SkyArena parcel — a still-unbuilt commercial/SoHo/hotel component remains on the drawing board, but no further residential towers will rise from this site. That's a real but limited positive, not enough to pull the scarcity dimension (5.4) above this site's median, and the card reflects that honestly rather than borrowing the "final phase" marketing language to inflate the score.

Connectivity requires the same discipline between "operational" and "planned." Sri Rampai, Wangsa Maju, and Setiawangsa — all Kelana Jaya Line LRT stations — sit within roughly 1.3-2km, forming multi-station redundancy rather than reliance on a single line; these have been running for years already. But the distance still requires a car or bus connection, not a walkable TOD product. The genuine long-term catalyst is the planned MRT3 Circle Line "Setapak" station, approved by the Transport Ministry in July 2025 but not due to break ground until 2027, with an opening projected around 2031 — more than five years out, so it counts only as a marginal pipeline catalyst under the capital appreciation dimension rather than connectivity, and receives only partial credit to avoid mistaking "approved" for "imminent."

On pricing, since Curvo just completed and has no resale history of its own, the valuation anchor falls back to an earlier SkyArena phase — Bennington Residences (completed 2019, same developer), with a Brickz transacted median of RM 531 psf. The representative Type A unit's indicative price of ~RM 588 psf sits roughly 8-11% above that anchor, at the boundary between "reasonable new-launch premium" and "elevated," and confidence can only be rated medium since this is ultimately a cross-phase rather than same-building comparison. Worth noting: against concurrent Setapak new-launch competitor PV22 Residences (RM 737.50-761.22 psf), Curvo is priced notably more conservatively — one of the few places this card credits it in the peer-aggression check.

Exit and next-buyer pool is the card's most striking dimension, and also where the score gap is widest. On the local side: a quantum of just RM 560,000 implies a next-buyer income threshold of only ~RM 6,160/month, just 57% of KL's median household income (RM 10,805), giving a deep and broad buyer pool — the local exit score (7.3) is the highest dimension on this card. But the foreign side tells a completely different story: the full unit price range, RM 405,000 to RM 745,000, sits entirely below KL Federal Territory's minimum foreign purchase threshold of RM 1,000,000 — this isn't a "just short of the cutoff" boundary risk but total, structural exclusion; no MM2H tier, Silver, Gold, or Platinum, allows a foreign buyer to legally purchase any unit at Curvo under current pricing. Ten years from now, the buyer is almost certain to be determined 100% by the local market — both the project's greatest certainty (insulated from shifting foreign-buyer policy) and its clearest ceiling (permanently unable to tap foreign demand as an additional source of price support). Developer SkyWorld is financially sound overall but worth watching for its recent margin decline; its delivery track record has been solid, and that remains a genuine point of confidence for this project.

◆ Conclusion

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • A quantum of just RM 560,000 implies a next-buyer income threshold at only 57% of KL's median household income, giving a deep, broad local next-buyer pool — exit for Malaysian buyers (7.3) is the highest dimension on this card
  • Gross rental yield of ~5.36%, notably better than most premium-segment cards on this site; the annual price growth needed to break even over 10 years is only ~0.0%/year, a genuinely resilient cashflow profile
  • SkyWorld's delivery track record has been solid (Ascenda 2017, Bennington 2019, Valley Residences 2023 all on time); Curvo itself won the 2023 Star Property Awards and the Asia Pacific Property Awards "Residential High-Rise Architecture" category
  • Three already-operational Kelana Jaya Line LRT stations (Sri Rampai, Wangsa Maju, Setiawangsa) within 1.3-2km give multi-station redundancy, plus a planned MRT3 Circle Line "Setapak" station (already approved, a long-term catalyst)
  • SkyArena's own 9.4-acre sports complex (40 sports facilities) plus over 50 nature-themed amenities within the building itself represent a standout amenity package for the price point
⚠ Points to Note
  • Foreign buyers are entirely excluded: every unit type (RM 405,000-745,000) is priced below KL's RM 1,000,000 foreign purchase threshold, so no MM2H tier makes any unit legally purchasable — this is a structural fact, not a boundary risk
  • Broader Setapak land supply is not scarce, with at least 6 competing projects under construction or upcoming; both scarcity (5.4) and appreciation (5.7) are capped by this
  • The nearest LRT stations are ~1.3-2km away, requiring a car or transfer rather than a walk; the MRT3 Setapak station isn't projected to open until 2031, a marginal catalyst at best
  • The project just completed (2026-08) with no Brickz resale history of its own, so the valuation anchor had to fall back to an earlier masterplan phase (medium confidence); secondary-market evidence (turnover, listing rate) is only available via estimated proxies
  • Developer SkyWorld's FYE2026 PATAMI fell 47% YoY with gross margin compressed to 7.0% (still profitable, not a loss); management fee and some secondary-market figures are estimates and should be reconfirmed with the developer or agent

◆ The Narrative

Project Deep-Dive

Tucked along Jalan Batai, a quiet, old-money street inside Damansara Heights, The Batai is Tan & Tan Developments' first low-density strata villa product in the embassy-belt heart of Kuala Lumpur's most storied residential enclave. The entire development is just 4.8 acres and 48 four-storey villas — roughly 10 units per acre, well below a typical high-rise condominium and even below the direct precedent, Kenny Hills Residence, at 13 units per acre. Each villa comes with a private lift, a 5+1 bedroom layout, and four to five car bays, aimed squarely at multi-generational households and ultra-high-net-worth families who need genuine separation of space rather than the typical urban small-family buyer.

The central question this card had to answer is whether scarcity is strong enough to justify the premium being asked. The three-layer audit finds real scarcity at the land level — DH is essentially built out, and a contiguous 4.8-acre release of this kind is unlikely to recur — but urges caution at the product level. Ultra-low-density freehold strata villas are not a category The Batai invented: Kenny Hills Residence launched a nearly identical concept in Bukit Tunku over a decade ago, at roughly RM900 psf, and now resells at a Brickz median of just RM715 psf — a nominal decline of about 20% across a holding period longer than most buyers here will actually hold. That precedent goes directly to this card's most consequential decision: should the capital appreciation dimension anchor on a "residential strata 6.0" baseline or a "landed 7.0" baseline? Simply inferring, from "freehold + ultra-low density + top-tier address," that this project will appreciate like a true bungalow would ignore an almost perfectly matched real-world counter-example — same tenure logic, same density logic, same prestige-address label — that never delivered the "villa equals landed" appreciation assumption. This card therefore anchors conservatively on residential strata, allowing only a small upward nudge for the single-phase structure (no Phase 2 supply risk) and the even lower density, and lands back at 6.0. That is not a hedge chosen to prove or disprove whether a villa can break the site's scoring ceiling — it is what the actual comparable evidence supports.

Pricing tells a similarly disciplined story: the representative unit at RM1,198 psf sits roughly 55% above the real Damansara Heights landed transaction median (RM773 psf, 50 Brickz transactions, Feb 2025 to Jan 2026) — a steep entry premium that closely echoes the trajectory Kenny Hills followed at launch. Because this is a genuinely new product category, there is no same-project or same-type Brickz data to compare against, forcing the valuation anchor down to a cross-product-type comparison with only medium confidence.

Connectivity cannot simply mirror this site's Pavilion Damansara Heights card (connectivity 8.5, a 200m covered walk directly into an MRT-linked mall). The Batai sits roughly 2.5km from Pusat Bandar Damansara MRT, requiring a car, and the community has no mall integration of its own — the built-in trade-off of low-density villa living: quiet and privacy in exchange for walkable urban amenity. A cluster of international schools (Cempaka, Mont Kiara International, Garden International) within about a 10-minute drive is a genuine plus, but overall connectivity lands only in the middle of the pack.

The exit-and-next-buyer dimension is where this project's real situation shows up most clearly. A from-price quantum of RM7 million implies a next-buyer household-income threshold of about RM77,000 per month — roughly 7.1 times the KL median household income (RM10,805, DOSM 2024) — a bar so high that the "household income" framework itself starts to lose relevance; the realistic buyer is a business owner, a multi-generational family balance sheet, or a cash purchaser, not a salaried professional qualifying via a mortgage. Interestingly, because the threshold is so extreme, foreign MM2H eligibility becomes almost trivially easy (RM7M clears the Platinum RM2M bar by a wide margin), which narrows the usual foreign-buyer penalty on this site more than elsewhere — but that does not mean the foreign buyer pool is actually deep. DH has long been a market where expatriates rent, not buy.

On the holding side, freehold residential (not commercial) tenure is a genuine structural advantage relative to this site's other DH cards (Pavilion DH, Southpoint, and Stonor 3 are all commercial-titled) — standard residential LTV, lower financing rates, and no commercial electricity tariff. An estimated gross yield of 4.5-5.0% also beats the usual "mansions barely yield anything" assumption, a byproduct of DH's genuinely active expat-executive rental demand. But the tenant pool able to actually rent a unit this size — corporate executives, diplomats, ultra-high-net-worth families — is narrow enough that real achieved rent and vacancy risk carry real uncertainty, something only post-handover data can confirm. Tan & Tan's own direct delivery record (setting aside the separate Pavilion Group JV entity) is a genuine point of confidence: both Southpoint and Stonor 3 completed on schedule.

◆ The Verdict

Highlights & Items to Note

✦ Highlights
  • Damansara Heights is a nearly fully built-out KL prime address; a contiguous 4.8-acre release is genuinely hard to reproduce — real land-layer scarcity
  • ~10 units/acre density with a private lift and a single 5+1 layout maps precisely to multi-generational / UHNW owner-occupier demand
  • Freehold residential (not commercial) title — every other DH card on this site is commercial-titled; this one gets standard residential financing, residential electricity rates, and no commercial-financing friction on exit
  • Single-phase project with no Phase 2 supply risk; solid sponsor credit (Tan & Tan / IGB, Bursa T1, clean recent on-time delivery record)
  • Extreme quantum makes the MM2H Platinum threshold trivially easy to clear for foreign buyers (though see caveats below for the full picture)
⚠ Items to note
  • The Kenny Hills Residence precedent: a comparable ultra-low-density freehold strata villa product launched at ~RM900 psf and now transacts at a Brickz median of just RM715 psf over a decade later — this card's 55% entry premium should not be assumed to appreciate like true landed property
  • The representative unit sits ~55% above the real DH landed transaction median, with no same-project/same-type Brickz data to validate it — valuation confidence is only medium
  • Roughly 2.5km from Pusat Bandar Damansara MRT, requiring a car, with no mall integration — connectivity is materially weaker than this site's Pavilion Damansara Heights card
  • Quantum from RM7M is far beyond typical urban upgrader/first-home reach, narrowing the local buyer pool to a small band of UHNW owner-occupiers / multi-generational families
  • Only 5 months into sales, completing only in January 2029 — there is currently zero secondary-market evidence (turnover, listing rate, discount all uncomputable); rent estimates rest on a thin sample and should be confirmed by negotiation

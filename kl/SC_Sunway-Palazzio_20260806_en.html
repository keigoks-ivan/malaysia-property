◆ In Depth

Project Deep Dive

Sunway Palazzio sits on Jalan Sri Hartamas, right next to Mont Kiara — a corridor that has developed since the late 1990s into one of KL's international lifestyle hubs. Sri Hartamas and Mont Kiara share a single road and the same catchment of international schools (Garden International, Mont'Kiara International) and retail/lifestyle anchors (Publika, Plaza Mont Kiara), yet Sri Hartamas itself has a more mixed development history — a mix of landed terraces and bungalows (a separate piece of this site's research once found "Desa Sri Hartamas" landed housing trading at a median price above RM 1.9 million, a distinct product from this high-rise condo and not to be confused with it) alongside international-clientele high-rises like Sunway Palazzio. This card establishes, for the first time, an independent Sri Hartamas district baseline of 7.0 — below Mont Kiara's 7.5 and above the generic "other KL" default of 6.0 — reflecting genuine geographic continuity and a shared lifestyle corridor, while acknowledging Sri Hartamas is not universally branded as "KL's premier expat enclave" the way MK is. Notably, Sri Hartamas already has a functioning MRT station (Sri Hartamas, MRT Putrajaya Line, opened 2023), giving it a real transport edge over Mont Kiara proper, which has zero MRT coverage until MRT3 arrives in 2032.

The development comprises two 40-storey towers totaling 160 units (including six penthouses), with only 3-5 units per floor — an unusually low density for KL. Unit sizes span from 2,507 sqft (Type C/D) to 10,000 sqft penthouses, all 3-bedroom or larger with no Studio/1BR/2BR product — the key constraint behind this card's choice of Type C/D (2,507 sqft) as the representative unit under the v5.0 methodology. Completed in April 2010, it was Malaysia's first high-rise residential development to earn the BCA Green Mark Gold award and won Best High-Rise Residential at the 2009 Asia Pacific Property Awards. Facilities include dual-level infinity pools (with a 40-metre main pool), a gym, sauna, childcare centre, concierge/housekeeping services, and 24-hour security. The developer, Sunway Integrated Properties Sdn Bhd, is a subsidiary of Sunway City Bhd, itself under Bursa-listed parent Sunway Berhad (code 5211) — a diversified conglomerate spanning property, construction, healthcare, education, and hospitality, whose Q1 FY2026 core pre-tax profit rose 52% YoY to RM 462.4 million. Public commentary does note the project "faced slow sales" shortly after completion — a real reminder that scarcity alone doesn't guarantee fast absorption; market sentiment and pricing strategy still matter.

On pricing: the project launched at roughly RM 846 psf around 2007-2010. Fifteen years on, this building's own Brickz transaction median has reached RM 901 psf (12 transactions across all of 2025), up 5.5% from RM 854 psf in 2023 — a steady but unspectacular ~2.7%/year pace. The representative Type C/D unit is currently asking around RM 917 psf (RM 2.27M-2.38M across three cross-checked listings), just a 1.8% premium to the same-building Brickz median — reasonably priced with high confidence, since the anchor is same-building actual transactions rather than a district-level proxy. Worth noting: Sunway Palazzio's own Brickz median PSF (901) actually exceeds Mont Kiara's district-wide Brickz median (808, per this site's separate MK research) — evidence that a genuinely low-density, large-format freehold product can reach a price tier that standard MK tower stock does not.

On rental: for the 2,507 sqft Type C/D representative unit, market listings put achievable rent around RM 6,200-7,000/month (cross-checked against the next size up, 2,819 sqft, at RM 8,000-8,500/month); this card uses RM 6,500/month, implying a gross yield of ~3.4% — below this site's 4.0% cashflow threshold and therefore floored at the anchor-table minimum of 4.0 (per v5.0 design, scores below 4.0% do not decline further). The building's own published yield index runs closer to 4.0-4.04% (2026, historical range 3.74-4.8%), but that figure blends in larger, higher-rent units (3,336sf+, renting for RM 13,000-27,000/month) whose rent-to-price ratio differs from the smallest units; this card uses the representative-unit basis for cross-project comparability. Malaysian after-tax net yield lands around 2.0% with a clearly negative spread (−2.5%); foreign buyers, taxed at 30% flat, net only ~1.2% with an even more negative spread (−3.8%) — a cashflow-constrained asset whose 10-year case leans heavily on capital gains and exit liquidity rather than income, in sharp contrast to this site's higher-yield projects (The MINH's 6.4%, for example).

On holding structure: freehold residential tenure, HDA protection, 90% residential LTV, and residential (not commercial) utility rates keep institutional friction low. The MM2H mandatory-asset test is comfortably cleared — the RM 2.3M representative unit is far above the RM 1M Federal Territory minimum, not a marginal pass. Maintenance is only RM 0.40 psf/month (incl. sinking fund) — low for a high-end KL project, reflecting efficient cost-sharing across a small 160-unit base. The single strongest point in this card's favour is liquidity: 12 Brickz transactions in 2025 against a total stock of just 160 units works out to a 7.5% annual turnover rate — among the most active resale markets across this site's KL cards, and notably better than what a typical 15-year-old luxury development tends to show (liquidity usually fades with age). This corroborates the scarcity audit's "genuine product-layer scarcity" verdict: low density, freehold tenure, and a proven completed track record combine to give Sunway Palazzio solid exit evidence even though its cashflow is weak. Ten-year exit expectation: at the base-case 3%/year appreciation, Year-10 price reaches roughly RM 3.09 million, with a 10-year net gain of approximately +RM 276,000 for Malaysian buyers and +RM 73,000 for foreign buyers.

Exit Reality Check

The v5.0 triad: scarcity audit (can supply be replicated) + next-buyer pyramid (who buys in 10 years) + secondary-market evidence (turnover / listing rate / discount). Data as researched 2026-07-24 (data confidence: medium).

5A · Scarcity audit (supply-side, 3 layers)

Land ✗ KLCC district supply is uncapped — new trophy towers keep launching (Four Seasons and more), with a luxury >RM1M overhang of 2,287 units (NAPIC Q3 2025). The Persiaran Stonor micro-position near Embassy Row and KLCC Park has brand appeal, but there is no land-cap mechanism at the district level.
Product ✗ A 400-unit single tower is mid-density, not low-density scarce; KLCC has many peer freehold trophies (Binjai / Troika / Four Seasons / Stonor Park) and the configuration is replicable. Mitsubishi Jisho JV quality is a selling point but not supply scarcity.
Regulation ✓ Freehold (the only positive at the tenure layer), yet KLCC has no height cap / master-plan cap; most KLCC comps are also freehold, so freehold is non-differentiating in this district.
Effective scarcity Weak / leaning false-scarcity — Stonor 3 Brickz ~RM 1,252 psf is mid-range for KLCC (vs Binjai 1,867, Four Seasons 3,010) with no scarcity premium; freehold + a KLCC address only sets a price floor. Scarcity is already priced-in as location brand, not add-on structural scarcity.

◆ The Narrative

Project Deep-Dive

Stonor 3 sits at 3 Lorong Stonor — the quietest, most scarce micro-position within KLCC's Golden Triangle. Adjacent to Embassy Row, it is a 10-minute walk to KLCC Park and Suria KLCC, and 500m to Conlay MRT station (Putrajaya Line). Compared to the bustling Jalan Ampang, Lorong Stonor delivers the distinctive combination of "KLCC address + Embassy Row tranquillity", appealing to expatriate executives and globally-mobile high-net-worth tenants seeking long-term rentals.

Project scale: a single 41-storey tower with 400 units, GDV RM 640M, at a moderate density. Unit sizes span 689 sqft (1BR), 871 sqft (2BR — the most popular plan), 1,031 sqft and 1,232 sqft (3BR). The single 2BR sub-type simplifies cross-project comparison. All units delivered fully furnished, featuring Burmese Teak flooring, Toto Neorest sanitaryware, and De Dietrich kitchen appliances. The project earned GreenRE Gold certification — KLCC's first residential project to do so. Amenities are boutique-positioned: sky lounge, gym, pool on upper floors.

The developer is Tan & Tan Developments, a subsidiary of IGB Corp (Bursa T1 listed) with a 50-year track record. The JV partner is Mitsubishi Jisho Residence — a subsidiary of Mitsubishi Jisho, one of Japan's leading residential developers — making this one of KL's rare Japanese institutional JVs, with clearly Japanese design and quality standards. Completed December 2019, the project has been operating stably for 6 years with full JMB handover; execution risk is fully resolved (HDA protection has no practical effect on completed projects).

On pricing: launch median was RM 2,000 psf in 2016 — at the time, this was near the Binjai-on-the-Park tier of KLCC top-end residential. The 2025 2BR transaction median is RM 1,320 psf (iRumah synthesis of multiple Brickz records), representing a 34% decline over 9 years. Listing PSF spans RM 1,338-1,771 (iProperty 140 listings, 2026-04), indicating some sellers remain anchored to higher marks — implying negotiation room. Same-area comparables: The Conlay (E&O × Mitsui Fudosan) launched at RM 2,450 psf; Stonor 3's RM 1,320 reflects a fully-corrected market price.

On rental market: iProperty shows 136 active rental listings; 2BR 871 sqft asks median ~RM 4,500/mo, giving gross yield 4.23% (iRumah 2025) — down from the 2023 peak of 4.73% in two consecutive years. Drivers of the decline: (1) KL broad SR overhang of 8,000-9,000 units; (2) the project's own 69% listing rate (140 sale + 136 rental of 400 units) creating intense tenant competition; (3) TRX-area new supply arriving as that district matures. Tenant mix skews to expat corporate executives on long-let (40-50%), drawing on the Embassy Row catchment plus KL Convention Centre / bank / law firm postings to sustain rental levels.

Hold-period notes: Commercial-under-HDA is a hybrid structure between residential and pure-commercial SOVO — HDA protects completion risk (no practical difference for a completed project), residential electricity tariff is available, but State Consent has slightly more friction than pure residential, and some banks treat it as commercial financing (tighter LTV and rate). The buyer pool is narrower than pure residential (Park Place, R22) but wider than pure SOVO (Armani Hallson); secondary-market liquidity sits in between. The 10-year return thesis rests on three things: (1) the Conlay-MRT walkable premium continues to be priced in; (2) KL's broad SR overhang begins to clear from 2027-2028 (per upstream forecasts), easing supply pressure; (3) the Tan & Tan × Mitsubishi quality reputation preserves rental floors at 4%+ gross for expat tenants. The foreign opportunity-cost breakeven of ~5.5%/yr is genuinely demanding — requiring a structural KLCC mid-tier SR recovery to clear, achievable within 10 years but contingent on a clear catalyst.

◆ The Verdict

Highlights & Items to Note

✦ Highlights
  • KLCC flagship freehold + Embassy Row Lorong Stonor — a scarce micro-position quietly distinct from the busy Jalan Ampang stretch
  • Conlay MRT 500m walk (5-7 min) — rare walkable MRT access in the KLCC corridor; genuine differentiator vs comparables like Suria Stonor and Aria KLCC
  • Tan & Tan (IGB Corp, Bursa T1, 50-yr track record) × Mitsubishi Jisho institutional Japanese JV: clearly higher quality standard; GreenRE Gold (KLCC's first residential)
  • Launch RM 2,000 psf (2016) corrected to RM 1,320 psf (2025) at −34% — fully discounted entry price with ample seller-side negotiation room
  • Meets the RM 1M MM2H mandatory-asset threshold + 6 years stable operation post-completion + JMB handover complete
⚠ Items to note
  • 69% listing rate (140 sale + 136 rental of 400 units) is among the highest in the comparable set; the upstream KL report explicitly flags "KLCC 2016-2019 SR as old stock overhang"
  • Foreign net yield 1.9-2.3% (lowest in the comparable set); opportunity-cost breakeven needs ~5.5%/yr growth — 10-year upside depends on a structural KLCC mid-tier SR recovery
  • Commercial-under-HDA: State Consent has more friction than pure residential; bank loan treatment varies — pre-confirm with at least 2-3 banks before entry
  • Foreign stamp duty doubled to 8% from 4% in 2026-01 — RM 1.15M entry adds RM 46K to foreign buyers' upfront cost
  • Gross yield trended down from 4.73% (2023) to 4.23% (2025) for two consecutive years — monitor iRumah yield reversing to 4.5%+ as an entry signal

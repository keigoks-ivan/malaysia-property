A mature KLCC diplomatic-enclave freehold commercial-title tower, 598 units 7 years post-VP, with 29 Brickz transactions in 12 months (4.8% turnover — among the more active KLCC resale markets on this site). ◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Dive

Aria Residences sits on Jalan Tun Razak's diplomatic enclave, directly across from the US Embassy, and was launched in 2015-16 and completed in 2019 by Hap Seng Land (the property arm of Hap Seng Consolidated Berhad, Bursa Main Market: 3034) — a twin-tower, 45-storey service residence with 598 units across 1.9 acres, covering the full range from 1BR 630 sqft to 3BR 1,502 sqft across nine layout types. The brief for this card started with a stated assumption that Aria carries a residential title — if true, this would have been the structural edge letting this project break this site's 7.5 ceiling for KLCC cards. Cross-verification does not support that assumption. iProperty's building page Tenure field explicitly reads "Freehold / Commercial." myjia.org's title page directly states "Title: Commercial Under HDA." EdgeProp-based aggregated data repeatedly labels it a "Commercial Strata property." All three are independent and specific. The only source suggesting "residential" is an aggregated summary lacking a specific citation, one that contradicts itself within the same paragraph (using both "Commercial Strata" and "residential" language), and a separate aggregator even misstated the developer SPV's location (incorrectly citing "Balakong" rather than the actual Jalan Tun Razak address) — a reliability red flag. This card therefore adopts the commercial-title classification. Aria sits in the same category as this site's three existing KLCC cards — The Conlay, Stonor 3, Armani Hallson — all freehold commercial SA, and no evidence surfaced of any regulatory advantage over those commercial-title peers.

On location, Aria carries the KLCC district's 8.0 anchor baseline; under the v5.0 three-layer supply audit both the land and product layers score ✗ — the immediate diplomatic-enclave land is partly embassy-use and hard to redevelop, but the wider KLCC fringe (Jalan Conlay, Jalan Stonor, Jalan Kia Peng) keeps producing branded luxury towers, and the 598 standard service-residence units carry no brand premium and are readily replicated elsewhere — leaving only the freehold regulatory layer ✓, for a net Scarcity & Location Moat of 7.5. On amenities, the nearest Ampang Park LRT/MRT interchange is 782m away, about an 11-minute walk — meaningfully further than this site's other KLCC cards (Conlay 5 minutes, Stonor 3/Armani/CloutHaus 5-8 minutes). Medical coverage is solid (Prince Court Medical Centre 500m, Gleneagles KL nearby) and the international school cluster (Etonhouse, St John's International and others) is complete; the developer also runs a shuttle to Pavilion/Suria KLCC/Fahrenheit88/Starhill/Lot 10, though that is a private shuttle rather than direct walkable retail integration.

The project spans 1.9 acres, two 45-storey towers, 598 units covering 1BR 630 sqft through 3BR 1,502 sqft, with 35+ facilities across 1.5 acres including a 50-metre Olympic pool, gym and sky lounges, with four facility floors named Bass, Tenor, Baritone and Soprano as a signature identity touch. Management fees of RM 0.50-0.55 psf sit below most KLCC cards on this site (RM 0.60-0.70 psf), a structural positive for the cashflow dimension. PropertyGuru user reviews describe solid facilities and generally professional management, alongside isolated complaints about slow maintenance follow-through, concierge friendliness and noise-complaint response — routine management friction rather than a structural problem.

On the developer side, Hap Seng Consolidated Berhad is a Bursa Main Market-listed diversified conglomerate spanning plantations, credit and leasing, building materials/trading (including the Mercedes-Benz Malaysia distributorship) and property, with over 40 years' history originally rooted in Sabah and expanded into West Malaysia in recent decades — Aria is one of the group's flagship high-rise entries into the KL luxury residential market, alongside the Menara Hap Seng office tower series (Menara Hap Seng 3 holds a Malaysia Book of Records certificate for "highest indoor green wall") and Horizon Residences. This is the first time this site has scored this developer. Delivery record: launched 2015-16, completed on schedule in 2019 with no reported major delay — a normal 3-4 year build cycle — though the group's repeat track record in this exact KL high-rise residential category remains limited, and buyers should keep tracking the parent's quarterly financial transparency and its subsequent KL pipeline.

On pricing, this is one of the few completed projects on this site where the building itself supplies a high-confidence valuation anchor — Aria has been completed for 7 years, and Brickz records 29 same-building transactions over the most recent 12 months (2024-05~2025-04) at a median of RM 1,445 psf, ample sample depth for a clean anchor without needing a district cluster or asking-vs-asking comparison. Current asking PSF ranges RM 1,225-2,330 (PropertyGuru); the representative unit's current asking of about RM 1,510 psf is roughly +3.8% above the Brickz transacted median, a reasonable new-supply premium band scoring 6.0. But the more important thing for buyers to notice is the project's long-run price trajectory: the 2015-16 launch PSF was RM 1,500-1,600, and nearly a decade later the Brickz median is only RM 1,445 psf — nominally flat to modestly down (roughly −4% to −10%), and clearly negative once inflation is factored in. This means the project's "scarcity" story has not, in real market data, translated into a premium — the appreciation dimension scores only 4.8, and this is the card's central honest disclosure: a good location and a clean freehold title do not automatically mean the price will rise.

On the rental market, 2BR 991 sqft long-lets run roughly RM 5,000-7,580/month (representative RM 6,200/month), implying a gross yield of about 5.2% — beating most of this site's KLCC branded-luxury cards (Conlay 4.9%, Four Seasons 4.15%), the key structural positive in the cashflow dimension. Malaysian net yield is roughly 3.7% (spread −1.3%); foreign net yield after the 30% non-resident tax is roughly 3.1% (spread −2.4%) — both still negative-carry, but by a smaller margin than most comparable cards on this site. The diplomatic enclave has long attracted multinational professionals, embassy staff and business travelers as a stable tenant base, though buyers should note that iProperty shows over 500 rental listings against 598 total units — a strikingly high proportion — reflecting a heavily investor-owned building with a relatively thin owner-occupier community; buyers should verify in-person the actual mix and quality of long-term residents.

On the ownership structure, this is a freehold commercial-title service residence (Commercial Under HDA, matching The Conlay/Stonor 3) with Malaysian commercial financing at ~85% LTV (vs 90% for residential) and commercial electricity tariffs — moderate structural friction. On the MM2H mandatory asset test, the 2BR 991 sqft representative unit at RM 1,432,000 comfortably clears both the RM 1M Federal Territory floor and the MM2H Gold asset threshold by +43%, landing the exit price in the RM 1-2M sweet spot — a genuine structural advantage from this project's affordable quantum, letting the exit price satisfy foreign MM2H eligibility without leaning too heavily on top-end foreign demand. On a 10-year outlook at the base 3%/year price-growth assumption, Year 10 price would reach about RM 1,925,000: Malaysian net P&L of roughly +RM 365,000 (breakeven hurdle only ~1%/year), foreign of roughly +RM 189,000 (breakeven hurdle ~1.7%/year) — both notably lower than this site's other KLCC commercial-title cards, reflecting how the project's smaller quantum and higher yield partly offset its weak appreciation momentum and commercial-title friction.

Key caveats: (1) this project's title cross-verifies as commercial, not residential — the brief's original "residential title" assumption did not hold up, and buyers should plan financing and tax around commercial-title terms; (2) nominal PSF has grown essentially zero to slightly negative over 7-9 years, so the capital-gain path is weak and the thesis should rest on cashflow and a broader local exit pool rather than an appreciation story; (3) the de-duplicated listing-rate estimate of 18-23% remains above a healthy water level, consistent with a heavily investor-owned building and a relatively thin owner-occupier community; (4) the nearest transit station is an 11-minute walk, somewhat behind this site's other KLCC cards; (5) Hap Seng's repeat track record in this exact KL high-rise residential category remains limited, and buyers should keep tracking the group's subsequent KL pipeline and quarterly transparency; (6) KL's broad commercial-SA overhang of over 3,000 units bears more directly on a non-branded service residence like this one than on branded-luxury peers.

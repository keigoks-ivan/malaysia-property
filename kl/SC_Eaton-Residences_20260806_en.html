◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Dive

Eaton Residences sits on Jalan Kia Peng at the heart of KL's embassy district, near the Japanese, US, Singapore, Chinese, UK and Vietnamese embassies. It's a 52-storey, single-tower serviced residence developed by City View Ventures Sdn Bhd — a wholly-owned subsidiary of Singapore-listed GSH Corporation Limited — launched in May 2016 and completed in 2022, with 632 units on a 1.42-acre parcel, marketed around "KL's first cantilevered sky pool" with unobstructed Petronas Twin Towers views. This is the first KLCC card commissioned on this site where the central diligence question was explicitly the title: residential or commercial? Cross-verification confirms commercial leasehold (99 years, to 2114, ~88 years remaining) — iProperty, EdgeProp, klcc-residences.com and independent valuer pages at lhcn.com all consistently classify it this way. That finding determines which v5.0 baseline applies — the stricter "leasehold commercial SA" bar (exit baseline 6.0) rather than the more forgiving "leasehold residential" bar (6.5) — and is the load-bearing fact behind much of this card's scoring.

On location, Eaton anchors to the KLCC district baseline of 8.0. Under v5.0's three-layer supply audit, all three layers fail: the embassy district keeps absorbing new supply (Conlay, Stonor 3, Aria and CloutHaus already up or under construction; Centrix, Phoeniz Suites and Pavilion Square launching in 2026), the 632-unit standard tower carries no structural product moat, and — unlike this site's five existing KLCC cards — the title itself is leasehold rather than freehold. That's the first time all three layers have failed on a KLCC card here, landing scarcity & location at 7.2. On the ground, though, Eaton holds the best transit position of any KLCC card on this site: Conlay MRT (MRT Putrajaya/Purple Line, operational since 2023) is only ~100m away, under 3 minutes on foot — closer than The Conlay (200m), Armani/CloutHaus (300-400m) or Stonor 3 (500m). Medical coverage is solid (Prince Court Medical Centre, Gleneagles KL nearby) and EtonHouse Malaysia International School is walkable, though Suria KLCC/Pavilion KL sit roughly 800m-1.2km away — not a direct sky-bridge connection.

The project spans 1.42 acres, a single 52-storey tower with 632 units, from the 840 sqft Type A5 one-bedroom up to the 2,831 sqft Type P4 four-bedroom penthouse. Type C4 (1,410 sqft) is the only true "2-bed 2-bath" configuration, and is this card's standard representative unit. The developer's own current price list still shows Min RM 1,019,810 / Max RM 6,008,795 — a decade after launch, remaining inventory hasn't been meaningfully discounted. PropertyGuru and Lowyat forum commentary is generally positive on facilities and views, with occasional gripes about "somewhat messy management" or service falling short of price expectations — routine management friction, not evidence of structural defects.

On the developer, City View Ventures is a wholly-owned subsidiary of GSH Corporation Limited (SGX main board: BDX), a group with development and hospitality operations spanning Singapore, Malaysia and China. Diligence surfaced two facts this card treats as material: the parent posted two consecutive years of net losses (FY2024: S$15.5M; FY2025: S$25.3M, including a S$14.7M non-cash write-down on Chongqing development property), and this project itself has a documented completion delay (targeted December 2020/Q1 2021, actually delivered in 2022 — roughly 18 months late). There are recent stabilization signals — net gearing improved from 1.7x to 1.02x after a November 2025 share placement, and dividends resumed after a gap period — but S$289.9M of GSH's S$504.4M total borrowings is due within a year, so near-term refinancing pressure is real. This card deliberately treats the turnaround conservatively rather than crediting it.

On pricing, the building has been complete for roughly four years, giving a Brickz same-building 12-month record (2024-04~2025-04) of 23 transactions at a median RM 1,580 psf — usable as a same-building anchor, but with one honest caveat: the implied transacted size is roughly 910 sqft, smaller than the 1,410 sqft representative Type C4, and larger KLCC units typically trade at lower psf than smaller ones — so this card downgrades confidence to "medium" and scores valuation more conservatively rather than than crediting a full high-confidence anchor. More telling still is the ten-year price trajectory: launched May 2016 at RM 1,600 psf, still sitting at RM 1,580 psf a decade later — essentially flat in nominal terms, meaningfully negative in real terms. The "location plus sky pool" story has not converted into real price appreciation, landing capital appreciation momentum at just 4.3 — the sharpest quantified contrast this card draws against leasehold-versus-freehold KLCC economics.

On rental, the 2BR (Type C4) 1,410 sqft unit is estimated to let for roughly RM 8,500-9,800/month (extrapolated from the same building's smaller 2BR range), implying a gross yield of ~5.2%, broadly in line with the site's other KLCC commercial-title cards. Malaysian net yield is ~3.5% with a −1.5% spread; foreign net yield after the 30% non-resident tax is ~2.9% with a −2.6% spread — both negative. The embassy-row expat and diplomatic professional community is the main structural support for rental demand here.

On holding structure, this is a 99-year leasehold commercial serviced residence (a title class shared with The Conlay, Stonor 3, Armani Hallson and CloutHaus — but all of those are freehold), with local commercial financing at ~85% LTV (versus 90% for residential) and commercial utility rates — moderately-to-elevated structural friction. On MM2H eligibility, the RM 2,228,000 representative unit comfortably clears both the RM 1M federal-territory threshold and the MM2H Gold asset requirement, by +123% — but that same larger quantum sits just above the RM 1-2M "both local and foreign pools can absorb it" sweet spot, pricing out local first-time buyers and much of the upgrader segment and tilting exit dependency toward foreign buyers. At the base 3%/yr price-growth scenario, Year-10 price is projected at roughly RM 2,994,000; Malaysian net P&L is about +RM 542,000 (breakeven ~0.9%/yr), foreign about +RM 279,000 (breakeven ~1.9%/yr) — both breakeven bars below typical benchmarks, meaning the project's cashflow carries its economics even though both the appreciation story and the exit pool are structurally weaker than this site's freehold KLCC peers.

Key caveats: (1) title cross-verifies as commercial, not residential, and this is the site's first leasehold KLCC card — buyers should evaluate the long-hold logic against the stricter leasehold-commercial bar, not a freehold one; (2) a decade of essentially flat nominal PSF means the "location plus sky pool" story has not converted into real capital gains; (3) the parent's two-year loss streak plus this project's own ~18-month completion delay are the core developer-dimension drags, worth tracking through GSH's ongoing quarterly disclosures and refinancing progress; (4) the RM 2.23M quantum is materially larger than Aria's RM 1.43M, narrowing the local first-time-buyer and upgrader exit pool and tilting dependency toward foreign buyers, more exposed to MM2H/stamp-duty policy shifts; (5) the valuation anchor carries a genuine size mismatch (Brickz sample ~910sf vs. the 1,410sf representative unit) and confidence has been downgraded to medium accordingly — buyers should ask agents for same-size, same-floor comps before relying on this card's premium estimate.

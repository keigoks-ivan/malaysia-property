5A · Location Moat Check
✓ Baseline JB CBD／Bukit Chagar／JB Sentral is anchored at 7.0 — the hard cap for this dimension on the JB scale. No JB district carries an irreversible supply lock, so this is the ceiling for the entire market, not just this project.
✓ CIQ proximity A genuine, measured pedestrian route to the CIQ complex exists (1.9km／28min via public streets), and the project sits inside the same Bukit Chagar precinct as the future RTS terminus — a real, geography-anchored draw even though the marketing "3-minute walk" framing is not corroborated by street-level routing.
✗ Product ceiling The location moat's value is capped by what it can be turned into: this is a commercial-title serviced apartment in a market where Johor alone holds 50.5% of the nation's service-apartment overhang (9,477 units, 2025 full-year, R-J1). A strong location does not offset a structurally oversupplied product category — this is precisely the gap this framework's R-J1 rule was built to catch.
Verdict: the location itself is genuinely strong and geography-anchored, but it sits underneath a product category (commercial-title serviced apartment) carrying the single heaviest structural overhang risk in this entire framework — the location cannot rescue the product-type economics.

◆ In-Depth Narrative

Project Deep Dive

TriTower Residence @ Johor Bahru Sentral sits on Jalan Tengku Azizah in Bukit Chagar, within the same JB CBD precinct as this framework's other anchor case, The Astaka @ 1 Bukit Senyum, and directly adjacent to both the CIQ complex and the future Bukit Chagar RTS terminus. Its name reflects its architecture: three towers in total — two 55-storey residential blocks connected by a Level-35 sky bridge (housing an infinity pool, sky gym and sky lounge), plus a third, separate tower occupied by a 316-key Capri by Fraser hotel. Completed in 2017 with 360 units across eight to ten configurations ranging from 668 sqft studios to 1,347 sqft three-bedroom units (some sources cite dual-key layouts to 2,290 sqft), the project is held on freehold title. A material classification question runs through this entire card: multiple sources, including the developer's own project page, describe this as a "Commercial Strata" serviced-apartment product, while some listing portals brand it more loosely as a "condominium." This card resolves that conflict conservatively, treating it as commercial-title per this framework's R-J6 rule — with real consequences for three of the seven scoring dimensions.

The developer, SKS Southern Sdn Bhd, operates under SKS Group, a Johor Bahru-headquartered conglomerate with other local projects including the SKS City Mall／SKS Tower redevelopment of the former Pacific Mall, SKS Habitat, and Larkin Junction. SKS Group is not one of the ten developers with an established track record in this framework's locked developer table, so a neutral off-table baseline of 5.5 applies. A genuinely unresolved question surfaced during this round's research: several secondary sources describe "SKS Group" as also known as "MB World" and formerly "Mahabuilders" — a claim that, if accurate, would connect this developer to the entity this framework credits elsewhere with a 2023 white-knight rescue of the stalled Greenland Danga Bay project. Neither company's own official website confirms this, and it is explicitly not the same corporate entity as the SGX-listed Astaka Holdings Limited (a different listing venue and corporate lineage), which carries a serious governance penalty elsewhere in this framework. This card applies neither the positive nor the negative interpretation, disclosing the ambiguity rather than resolving it by assumption. A Joint Management Body is already established for this project, confirming handover has occurred.

On location and connectivity, this card re-verified the CIQ walk under the newly-added (2026-08-07) d2 walking-evidence priority hierarchy. Street route (priority 2): Google Maps walking directions from the project directly to the Immigration Department's Bangunan Sultan Iskandar building return 1.9 km and 28 minutes, routed via Jalan Yahya Aldatar. But this round confirmed that a genuine, existing indoor covered connector — a walkalator plus several escalator segments — links the CIQ complex directly to JB Sentral, independently walked and documented by a blogger in March 2026 in roughly 5 minutes total (priority-1 evidence: a named physical connector plus a third-party first-hand account). TriTower itself is not physically inside the JB Sentral terminal; a street-level segment of roughly 449-740m (≈7-11 minutes, per multiple property-agent estimates) separates the building from that connector's entrance, giving a combined-route estimate of roughly 12-16 minutes — materially shorter than the naive 28-minute street route, and straddling the 15-minute bonus threshold, making this card's genuine boundary case. Per the "take the conservative side under uncertainty" principle, this card takes the combined estimate's conservative end (≈16 minutes), which narrowly misses the threshold, so no individual-level bonus applies and the A-tier district baseline (6.5) stands unchanged — but this is a correctly-evaluated "narrowly misses" boundary case, not the earlier round's mistaken "obviously overstated marketing, clearly non-triggering" framing.

Pricing tells a story of two disagreeing sources and a project that already prices above even the more conservative one. brickz.my's own three recorded transactions (June 2024 to January 2025) show a median of RM1,062 psf, while EdgeProp.my's 48-month window shows a lower RM839 psf median — a roughly 27% gap between two legitimate same-project data sources. This card scores against the more conservative, lower figure, per its general rule for conflicting anchors. Against that anchor, the representative 942 sqft, 2-bedroom unit's current RM1,000,000 asking price (≈RM1,062 psf) sits about 27% above — landing in the "developer stretch" valuation band. Other unit types in the building show even wider dispersion: one aggregator-flagged "most popular" 1,262 sqft, 3-bedroom configuration currently asks around RM1,270 psf, more than 50% above the conservative anchor.

The exit picture is where this card's central finding lives. Using the project's own transacted median (RM915,000) as the quantum test input, the Malaysian buyer pool clears this framework's core "RM70–100万" upgrader bracket cleanly — but that same RM915,000 figure sits only about 8.5% below Johor's statewide RM1,000,000 minimum purchase threshold for foreign buyers. That proximity is not a coincidence worth glossing over: it means that at this project's own established price level, there is currently no legal path for a general foreign buyer — including a Singaporean buyer, despite the project's obvious appeal to that segment — to purchase a unit here. This triggers this framework's R-J2 rule directly, and the foreign exit score (1.5, floor-clamped) is among the weakest structural readings this framework produces. Layered on top of R-J1's commercial-title overhang caps on appreciation and exit, and a thin same-project resale depth of just three transactions against 47 simultaneously active listings (roughly 13% of all 360 units), the combined picture is a project whose CIQ-adjacent location is real, but whose product category and price positioning sit at the exact intersection of this framework's two most consequential Johor-specific hard rules.

CTC SkyOne @ Bukit Chagar

Developer: CTC Development Malaysia (subsidiary of Singapore's CTC Group)

This card carries the widest gap in this batch between RTS marketing claims and measured reality. The developer markets "3-minute walk to CIQ" and "300m to RTS" — but this card's 2026-08-07 Google Maps walking-route measurement from the sales gallery puts CIQ / Johor Bahru checkpoint at 1.3km, 19 minutes, and the RTS Bukit Chagar station at 1.3km, 16 minutes — both beyond this framework's 15-minute bonus threshold, so neither marketing claim holds up under measurement. The more decisive finding is the collision between the representative unit's quantum and the foreign purchase threshold: this card's representative unit (700 sq ft, ≈RM936,000) falls **below** Johor's RM1,000,000 strata threshold for foreign buyers, and this is not Forest City (the only project with the MM2H exception) — meaning foreign buyers have **no legal route to purchase** this unit size, and the exit dimension collapses accordingly. The developer, CTC Development Malaysia, has **zero delivered projects** in Johor Bahru to date (its only sister project, Oasis HiTown, is still under construction).

5A · Location Moat Audit
✓ Position JB CBD／Bukit Chagar IS the location of CIQ (Bangunan Sultan Iskandar) and the RTS terminus — a geographic fact, not a measurement, and this "border-crossing position exclusivity" will still hold in 10 years. But this card's own measurement shows the project's walking distance to CIQ/RTS (1.3km, 19 minutes) falls well short of the marketed "3-minute" claim — district-level position exclusivity is not the same as project-level walkable convenience.
✗ Supply Land around JB CBD is not scarce: SKS Pavillion Residences (completed) sits within 500m of this project, and Twin Tower Residence and TriTower Residence (both completed serviced-apartment projects) sit within 1km — plus the RM2.6bn MRT Corp/Sunway joint-venture Bukit Chagar integrated development is under construction right next to the RTS station itself. Supply of the same product category (commercial-strata serviced apartment) keeps arriving, and this project has no "scarce parcel" moat.
✗ Regulatory JB CBD carries no irreversible height limit or heritage lock (unlike George Town in Penang), and commercial strata title is not itself a scarce regulatory product — Johor's serviced-apartment completed-unsold stock of 9,477-9,972 units, 50.5% of the national total, is exactly the structural oversupply this product category and regulatory framework produce, not scarcity.
Effective-scarcity verdict: this project's 7.0 location anchor reflects a genuine geographic fact about JB CBD — but "geographically exclusive position" is not the same as "this project has a strong moat." Both the supply and regulatory layers fail, and this project's own product category (commercial-title serviced apartment) sits in the single most oversupplied segment in Johor Bahru, and arguably in the whole state. The most direct 10-year threat is not competition from a different location — it is the next wave of new launches within the same 500m-1km walking radius, marketed with the same "near CIQ／near RTS" narrative.

◆ In-Depth Narrative

Project Deep Dive

CTC SkyOne @ Bukit Chagar sits in Johor Bahru's CBD at Bukit Chagar — the Malaysia-side terminus of the RTS Link and the location of CIQ (Bangunan Sultan Iskandar). The developer, CTC Development Malaysia, is the Malaysian arm of Singapore's CTC Group (founded 1990, originally the travel agency CTC Travel, diversified into real estate from 1994), incorporated in Malaysia in 2012. This project is a freehold, commercial-strata-titled serviced-apartment development comprising 1,605 serviced-apartment units plus 22 retail units across 3 towers of 56-57 storeys (some marketing cites "up to 58 storeys," a discrepancy disclosed here), on a ≈1.64-acre site. Unit sizes range from 463 to 1,270 sq ft (one source cites an upper bound of 1,646 sq ft; the sources disagree), configured as dual-key／triple-key investment units. It launched around October 2025, and its handover timeline — "≈2029" — appears only in marketing material, with no official approval document or APDL primary source found to confirm it; this card discloses that as unverified.

The single most important step in scoring this project was subjecting its marketing claims to actual measurement. CTC SkyOne's marketing heavily features "~300m to RTS" and "3-minute walk to CIQ" — exactly the terrain where Johor's scoring rules are most error-prone. Per R-J3, any walking-distance claim to the RTS station scores zero before opening, disclosure-only; CIQ, while an existing facility that can in principle be scored, must be based on a real measured distance, not a marketing claim. This card measured both routes via Google Maps on 2026-08-07: the sales gallery to CIQ／Johor Bahru checkpoint is actually 1.3km, 19 minutes; to the RTS Bukit Chagar station, 1.3km, 16 minutes — both far exceeding the marketed claims, and both beyond this framework's "walk ≤15 minutes" bonus threshold. That means, beyond the JB CBD district's geographic-fact starting score (6.5, Band A), this project earned no individual-level walking bonus at all. The same verification pass also confirmed a named, completed competitor (SKS Pavillion Residences) sits just 350-400m away, with two more completed serviced-apartment competitors (Twin Tower Residence, TriTower Residence) within 1km — all three of the same product category, directly triggering both the d1 "developable adjacent parcel within 500m" and d4 "≥3 named competing supply within 1km" individual-level deductions.

On capital appreciation momentum, this project carries the serviced-apartment／SoHo commercial-title baseline of 4.0, reduced to 3.7 by the within-1km competing-supply deduction. Johor's serviced-apartment average psf has indeed risen sharply (RM549 psf in 2024 to RM711 psf by 2025 Q3, a cumulative +29.5%), but per the R-J3 hard rule and the A6 fact card's conflict ruling, this surge has been explicitly named by JLL／EdgeProp themselves as a compositional effect from newly launched four-figure-psf units pulling up the average — it cannot be used as positive evidence for this project. Over the same period, Johor's serviced-apartment completed-unsold stock has held in the 9,477-9,972-unit range, 50.5% of the national total — the most concentrated structural oversupply anywhere in Johor's (and especially Johor Bahru's) housing market. RTS opening, JS-SEZ's RM76.98bn approved-investment figure and similar catalysts all score zero and are disclosure-only per the rules — real developments, but not convertible into a scoring bonus for this specific project.

Holding cashflow and exit are where this project's score is pulled down most. With the project still under construction, no same-project transaction or signed lease exists to reference; a cross-project proxy (interpolated from JB CBD's small-unit asking rent and the nearby TriTower Residence's asking rent) estimates the representative unit's (700 sq ft, ≈RM936,000) gross yield at ≈3.72%, a neutral band with no adjustment either way. The pivotal moment in the exit dimension is the collision between quantum and the foreign purchase threshold: the representative unit's estimated total price, ≈RM936,000, falls below Johor's RM1,000,000 strata threshold for foreign buyers, and this project is not Forest City (the sole project carrying the MM2H exception) — per R-J2, this means general foreign buyers (including Singaporean nationals and permanent residents) have no legal route to purchase this unit size, and the foreign exit score falls sharply from the Malaysian score of 3.4 to the clamp floor of 1.5. This conclusion depends heavily on this card's representative-unit assumption; a buyer choosing a larger unit within this project (total price above RM1,000,000) would clear the threshold, though still facing state-consent friction, 8% stamp duty and a permanent 10% RPGT among other general foreign costs. The developer, CTC Development Malaysia, has no delivered project in Johor Bahru to evaluate, and its financial information is unavailable — this card applies the neutral baseline of 5.5 per the rules, neither rewarding "no negative record found" nor penalizing "no track record exists."

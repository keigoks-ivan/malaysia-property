In the base case (+3%／year), the Malaysian 10-year net P&L is about +RM358,800 versus the foreign buyer's +RM145,200 — foreign buyer pays about RM214,000 more over 10 years, roughly 10% of the entry price. This gap is driven by the flat 8% foreign stamp duty, the 3% consent levy, 30% non-resident rental tax and a permanent 10% RPGT, only partly offset by the foreign buyer's smaller mortgage balance from a lower 65% LTV. Breakeven price growth needed to cover total cash friction costs (excluding opportunity cost): roughly 1.6%／year for the Malaysian buyer and 2.4%／year for the foreign buyer — both comfortably below the +3% base case and the 2025P high-rise MHPI momentum, though that momentum is a single-year state figure and not guaranteed to persist. Including the opportunity cost of the capital tied up in the down payment (illustrative 4.5%／year alternative-investment benchmark, disclosed assumption): breakeven rises to roughly 2.1%／year for the Malaysian buyer and 4.1%／year for the foreign buyer — the foreign buyer's much larger 65%-LTV down payment (RM743,750 versus RM212,500) means more idle capital is at stake, so foreign economics are more sensitive to the opportunity-cost assumption than to the friction costs alone. ◆ In-Depth Narrative

Project Deep Dive

The Astaka @ 1 Bukit Senyum occupies one of Johor Bahru's most geographically defined addresses: JB CBD, immediately adjacent to Bangunan Sultan Iskandar (the CIQ) and the Bukit Chagar RTS terminus. This is not a case of being "close to" the crossing — the district is definitionally the crossing point itself, which is why this card's connectivity score (6.5) rests on geography rather than a third-party travel-time estimate (an earlier Rome2Rio figure for this route was found to be a routing artifact and discarded). This round measured the specific walking route for the first time: Google Maps puts the pedestrian distance from The Astaka's own address to the CIQ checkpoint at 1.3km, a 19-minute walk via Jalan Yahya Aldatar — real, but four minutes outside the ≤15-minute band this framework rewards, so district-level geography earns the A-tier baseline without an individual-level bonus on top. A second new measurement this round cuts the other way for the location dimension: CTC SkyOne Residence, a currently-marketed new launch by CTC Group, sits a mere 350m (6-minute walk) from The Astaka's own tower — concrete, named, geocodable evidence that this still-developing precinct has real spare capacity nearby, which trims the location score from this scale's 7.0 ceiling to 6.7. Completed in June 2018 as Phase 1 of a twin-tower development (279m／70 storeys and 256m／65 storeys, marketed at launch as Southeast Asia's tallest residential towers), the project comprises 435 units on developer-stated figures, though some third-party aggregators cite 438 — a minor, disclosed discrepancy. Some listing sites also state a 2017 completion; this card uses 2018 per developer-side sourcing. Held on freehold, residential title (not commercial or serviced-apartment), the project's own floor plans start well above the generic 1,000–1,300 sqft convention used elsewhere in this framework entirely — its own smallest unit type, Type A1 at 2,207 sqft (3+1 bedroom, 4 bathroom), is used as the representative unit here, a disclosed departure from the generic table.

Developer background carries the single largest negative in this card. Astaka Holdings Limited, SGX-listed, operates locally through Astaka Padu Sdn Bhd. During construction, main contractor China State Construction Engineering sent three letters of demand between October 2018 and July 2019 that were not promptly disclosed; SGX issued a formal reprimand, and then-CEO Zamani Bin Kasim was ordered to resign all posts. The company separately went through a trading-resumption-proposal process, confirming a halt occurred at some point. This is not merely a reputational note — it drove the developer dimension from a 5.5 baseline down to 4.0, the steepest deduction in this framework's Johor developer table. A Joint Management Body is already established, per jmbmalaysia.org.

On pricing, this is one of the few JB CBD projects with a genuine same-project transaction anchor: a 2025 full-year median of RM1,100 psf and RM2,520,000 total price (≈31 transactions, though that count itself only surfaces via a secondary AI-summarised search result and needs manual re-verification against brickz.my). Against this anchor, the representative Type A1 unit's current asking price — a midpoint of RM2,125,000 (≈RM963 psf) drawn from five observed listings ranging RM1.75m–2.65m — sits roughly 12.5% below the transaction anchor. Because the asking-side input is low confidence while the anchor is medium confidence, this card takes the conservative lower end of the applicable discount band, scoring valuation at 7.0 rather than higher.

The rental market here is at least well-populated by asking-only listings, unlike some JB precincts: iProperty alone carries 96 active rental listings for The Astaka itself, comfortably clearing this framework's bar for full-weight yield evidence. Even so, no institutional or signed-lease rent data exists for JB CBD, and this card's RM7,000／month estimate is a midpoint of scattered same-project listings (RM6,400–8,000), implying a gross yield of roughly 3.95% — a hair under the 4.0% line that would have earned a further scoring bonus, so this card takes the conservative, unadjusted reading rather than round up across a boundary this close. Two thematic narratives are deliberately excluded from this card's positive scoring: the RTS terminus and the JS-SEZ agreement (signed 2025-01-07) are disclosed but score exactly zero on the appreciation dimension, per this framework's R-J3 guardrail — and the much-cited +29.5% Johor-city service-apartment average-PSF surge (2024–2025Q3) is a different, commercial-title product category that JLL and EdgeProp themselves attribute to a new-launch composition effect, not applicable evidence for this residential-title project.

On exit, the picture splits sharply by nationality. Using the project's own locked transaction median for the quantum test (a genuine inconsistency with the representative unit's lower asking price, disclosed above as a rule-gap) places this case in the >RM2.5m bracket, thinning the Malaysian buyer pool to mostly out-of-state investors, with local first-time buyers and typical upgraders effectively excluded. No assumption is made about Singapore-linked buyer or tenant demand: despite the location's obvious appeal to Malaysians working in Singapore and to Singaporean buyers themselves, no case-specific evidence of this project's actual buyer or tenant composition exists, so both segments of the next-buyer pyramid are marked unmeasured rather than assumed favourable. Foreign economics are structurally weaker still: after the fixed 30% non-resident rental-tax deduction and a state-consent friction penalty only partly offset by MM2H eligibility, the foreign exit score lands at 2.0 — one of the weakest readings in this framework, and the primary reason the foreign total score (5.0) falls into T3 while the Malaysian score (5.6) holds T2.

◆ Conclusion

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • JB CBD／Bukit Chagar is definitionally the CIQ and RTS-terminus point itself — a geography-defined moat, not a proximity estimate, anchoring both location (6.7, off this scale's 7.0 hard cap) and connectivity (6.5, A-tier) scores
  • One of the few JB CBD projects with a genuine same-project transaction history: 2025 full-year median RM1,100 psf／RM2,520,000, and current asking sits roughly 12.5% below that anchor
  • Residential title (not commercial／serviced-apartment) means R-J1's commercial-title overhang veto does not apply, and gross yield (≈4.0%, estimate) is stronger than most comparables elsewhere in this framework
  • Twin-tower scale (435 units, marketed as Southeast Asia's tallest residential towers) with an already-established JMB
⚠ Caveats
  • [Developer governance] Main contractor letters of demand were not promptly disclosed during construction; SGX issued a formal reprimand and ordered the then-CEO to resign all posts — the steepest deduction (5.5→4.0) in this framework's Johor developer table
  • [Foreign economics] Foreign exit score is 2.0, foreign cashflow 3.0 — among the weakest readings in this framework; the fixed 30% non-resident rental tax and state-consent friction are only partly offset by MM2H eligibility, and the foreign score (5.0) falls into T3 while the Malaysian score (5.6) holds T2
  • [Data confidence] Pricing and rental inputs are thin: the transaction-anchor sample count needs manual brickz.my re-verification, and asking-price／rent data are low-confidence listing aggregations, not systematic datasets
  • [Thematic caveat] RTS terminus and JS-SEZ narratives are already disclosed but deliberately score zero on appreciation (R-J3); the widely-cited +29.5% Johor service-apartment price surge is a different commercial-title product category and does not apply here
  • [Location moat] A newly-measured 350m walk finds CTC SkyOne Residence, a currently-marketed new launch, immediately next door — concrete evidence this precinct's land is still being actively built out, not a fixed, unrepeatable moat; the CIQ walk itself measures 19 minutes on foot, outside this framework's ≤15-minute connectivity bonus band

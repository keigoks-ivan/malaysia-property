Mutiara Rini Sdn Bhd is not on this framework's ten-developer anchor tables §5.1／5.2／5.3, so per the 2026-08-07 "table-external developer" processing rule: the starting point is baseline 5.5 — this 5.5 means "financial evidence unavailable at the subsidiary level," not "no negative record," two distinct statements. Verification paths tried: KPKT's private-developer registry (confirms registration status only, no subsidiary-level financials obtainable), SSM company registry (no independent Mutiara Rini Sdn Bhd financial filing found), Bursa／KLSE announcements (not applicable — the subsidiary itself was never listed, and the parent has been delisted since 2023), CTOS paywall (not accessed) — all four paths failed to yield subsidiary-level financials, so the starting point remains baseline 5.5. Corrected on second review: Boustead Holdings' 2023 delisting itself is not usable as a deduction basis, and has been removed — re-verification confirmed this event was a voluntary take-private acquisition by controlling shareholder LTAT, not an exchange-enforced or distress-driven delisting: LTAT launched a conditional voluntary takeover for the 40.58% it did not hold on 2023-03-02, crossed 90% ownership by May 2023, and delisted the company on 2023-06-28; LTAT's own press release framed this as "the cornerstone of LTAT's transformation journey" and part of its Strategic Asset Allocation Framework's portfolio-rebalancing plan (LTAT official press release, 2023-05-17) — a controlling shareholder's strategic consolidation of full ownership, which does not itself constitute a negative record and is not used as a deduction basis; this card also no longer uses the word "delisted" in a way that could be misread as a forced removal. The individual-level negative facts instead rest on three independent, dated pieces of evidence, deducted per §5.1 logic: first, Malaysia's Parliamentary Public Accounts Committee (PAC), in remarks dated 2024-11-19 and attributed to committee chair Datuk Mas Ermieyati Samsudin, formally characterised the Boustead group's condition as a "critical cash flow position and multiple financial crises," warning that LTAT's RM5 billion investment in Boustead and its subsidiaries (against LTAT's RM11.5 billion in assets under management as of end-2023) could face impairment of up to 50% (cross-reported by The Edge Malaysia, New Straits Times, Free Malaysia Today, Yahoo News Malaysia, and KLSE Screener, all describing the same parliamentary remarks — confidence high). Second, the group's debt level: roughly RM6.8–7.6 billion in FY2022/FY2023 (per The Edge Malaysia's "Boustead in race against time to trim RM7 bil debt load"), reduced to roughly RM3.4 billion by the time of a 2026-02-25 The Edge Malaysia article on the "Project Moonshot" restructuring plan — achieved mainly through the September 2024 sale of a roughly 27% Affin Bank stake to the Sarawak state government (RM1.78 billion) and 2024 land disposals (roughly RM1.1 billion for 828.47 hectares), a real, executed, roughly-halved deleveraging trend, moving in the opposite direction from this framework's IOI Properties precedent (§5.1, whose gearing is explicitly still rising). Third, as of this card's own verification date (2026-08-07), a same-day The Edge Malaysia article quotes LTAT's CEO stating that Boustead Plantations specifically still needs 3–5 more years of financial and operational strengthening before relisting is considered — confirming the group's financial repair is not yet complete, still ongoing. Deduction calibration: together, these three items constitute concrete, cross-verified, dated evidence spanning nearly two years (2024-11 through 2026-08) consistently pointing the same direction — but the character of this evidence differs from IOI Properties' single, clean, still-worsening financial ratio; it more closely resembles "serious but genuinely improving," matching this framework's Tropicana precedent (§5.1, −0.7: "still lossmaking, financially the weakest among Johor's major developers, deleveraging but still near the edge") more closely than IOI's (−1.0: worsening leverage plus an independent consumer dispute). This card applies the Tropicana-tier deduction: −0.7: baseline 5.5 − 0.7 = 4.8. It must be equally clearly disclosed that all of this evidence sits at the group／holding-company level — no document found specifically names Taman Mutiara Rini itself as an affected or possible-disposal asset; this project itself has no negative handover record found (no delay, defect, or abandonment), and the township's own planning has in fact won two industry awards (1998, 1999), with financial media separately reporting "nearly 6,000 units sold" and a "100% take-up rate" (the latter two disclosed as context only, not scored); re-verification also confirms the subsidiary Mutiara Rini Sdn Bhd remains, as of 2025–2026, a current member of Boustead Properties Berhad (the Boustead／LTAT group), with no sale or divestment found, so the parent-subsidiary linkage remains valid. Group-level financial risk and this project's own 30-year delivery record are two independent facts, presented side by side, neither concealing the other.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Dive

Taman Mutiara Rini is a 1,438-acre integrated township that Mutiara Rini Sdn Bhd, a member of the Boustead and LTAT group, has been developing continuously in Skudai since 1996. The land was formerly government land, privatised to LTAT in exchange for building army housing in Penang, Melaka, and the Klang Valley — a piece of institutional history distinctive to this project. Roughly 6,000 units have been sold across three decades of rolling, multi-phase development, and the township is currently selling its final phase, Rini Homes 8 Finale (Phase 6L). This is not a single discrete-phase launch. In the spirit of R-J5 — a phased project must have its "which phase to score" selected before scoring — this card selects the currently on-sale Phase 6L Finale (4-bed／4-bath, 2,063–2,436 sqft, launch price from RM695,000) as the representative unit, while the quantum test and Exit dimension use this project's own trailing-12-month transaction median (all unit types blended, RM700,000／151 units) per the framework's rules; the two different bases are disclosed transparently. The tenure is leasehold 991 years — this is the most direct structural difference between this project and its co-produced, same-sub-district comparison case, Taman Bukit Indah (SP Setia, freehold): a 991-year lease is practically indistinguishable from freehold in holding-period terms, but the title category itself is genuinely different, and this card records it honestly as leasehold, not freehold.

On location, Bukit Indah／Skudai／Sutera is this framework's designated deepest transaction-evidence sub-district across all eight JB districts, with a baseline of 6.3 (against this scale's ceiling of 7.0). This project's own 151 transactions／12 months is itself one of three mutually corroborating datasets supporting the district baseline (the other two being Taman Bukit Indah terrace's 218 transactions and its non-landed 436 transactions) — this kind of moat rests on 30 years of accumulated, mature catchment and verifiable transaction depth, not an irreplaceable physical constraint: land supply is structurally unconstrained across JB's districts, which is precisely why this scale caps d1 at 7.0 rather than KL／Penang's 8.0. Per the evidentiary discipline, this card does not stack a case-level bonus on top of the same fact already used to set the district baseline, to avoid double-counting. On connectivity, this project's driving-distance proxy to CIQ／Bangunan Sultan Iskandar is roughly 20–24km, far beyond any reasonable walking range — the B-tier baseline of 5.5 holds with no case-level bonus triggered, a sharp contrast to a project like The Astaka where the district itself is geographically the CIQ endpoint.

The developer dimension is the primary source of the score gap between this card and Taman Bukit Indah, and it is also the part most substantially revised after a second review. Mutiara Rini Sdn Bhd is not on this framework's ten-developer anchor tables, so per the "table-external developer" processing rule the starting point is baseline 5.5 (meaning subsidiary-level financial evidence is unavailable, not that no negative record exists); re-verification confirms the subsidiary remains, as of 2025–2026, a current member of the Boustead／LTAT group. The initial version of this card treated the parent's "2023 privatisation and delisting" itself as a negative signal — that judgment was mistaken. Re-verification confirms this event was a voluntary take-private acquisition launched by controlling shareholder LTAT (proposed 2023-03-02, crossing 90% ownership by May 2023, delisted 2023-06-28), which LTAT's own press release framed as part of its strategic asset-allocation and portfolio-rebalancing plan — a shareholder-level ownership consolidation, not evidence of distress or a forced delisting, and it has been removed from the deduction basis. What actually supports a deduction are three independent, dated pieces of evidence: Malaysia's Parliamentary Public Accounts Committee formally characterised the Boustead group's condition as a "critical cash flow position and multiple financial crises" on 2024-11-19, warning that up to 50% of LTAT's RM5 billion investment in Boustead could be impaired; group debt has fallen from roughly RM6.8–7.6 billion in FY2022/FY2023 to roughly RM3.4 billion by early 2026, through executed asset sales including a roughly RM1.78 billion Affin Bank stake sale to the Sarawak government — a real, direction-opposite-to-IOI-Properties deleveraging trend; and as of this card's own verification date (2026-08-07), LTAT's CEO still describes Boustead Plantations as needing 3–5 more years of strengthening, confirming the repair is not yet complete. Weighed together, this evidence points to a serious but genuinely improving group-level condition, which this card now calibrates against the Tropicana precedent (−0.7) rather than IOI Properties' worsening-trend precedent (−1.0), bringing the developer dimension to 4.8. It must equally be disclosed that all of this evidence sits at the group／holding-company level — no document specifically names Taman Mutiara Rini itself as an affected asset; this project itself has no negative handover record (no delay, dispute, or abandonment found), and its township planning has in fact won two industry awards (1998, 1999), alongside financial-media reports of "nearly 6,000 units sold" and a "100% take-up rate" (disclosed as context only, not methodologically unpacked). Group-level financial risk and this project's own 30-year delivery record are two independent facts, presented side by side rather than one obscuring the other.

On pricing, this project is among the few in JB's eight districts with both a genuine self-project transaction anchor and sufficient depth: 151 transactions over the trailing 12 months, median RM700,000 (an alternate cut of 137 units／RM725,000 also found, with the more conservative former figure used for scoring). The current phase, Rini Homes 8 Finale, launches from RM695,000 — about 0.7% below the anchor, technically a discount, but the gap sits within the comparability margin of error between a "launch-from" price and a blended, multi-phase transaction median; this card takes the conservative reading, scoring 6.0 (effectively near-parity) rather than rounding up into the discount band, consistent with how this framework has handled other boundary cases. On rent, this round completed a full re-verification (superseding an earlier pass interrupted mid-way when a browser tab was pre-empted by a concurrent session, leaving only 3 listings checked): iProperty was re-searched listing by listing, yielding n=11 same-project asking rentals, median RM2,600／month; mudah.my was used as a second-site cross-check, yielding n=6, also median RM2,600／month — an exact match (edgeprop.my was soft-blocked this round, a technical non-result, excluded from the sample). This implies a gross yield of roughly 4.46%, clearing into the §3.1 bonus band of 4.0–5.0%, lifting the cashflow dimension from baseline to 6.0 (Malaysian) ／4.0 (foreign).

On exit, the Malaysian and foreign sides diverge as sharply as almost anywhere in this framework. The quantum test, using this project's own transaction median of RM700,000, falls into this framework's most generous "≤RM750,000" band, and combined with the 151-transaction depth (the ≥50-unit band), the Malaysian exit score reaches 6.3 — among the most solidly evidenced Malaysian-side exit economics in this framework, with local upgraders as the core buyer segment, exactly the profile described by the quantum empirical centre (RM700–715k). But the exit price sits structurally below Johor's statewide foreign threshold of RM1,000,000, and this is not Forest City, so under R-J2 there is no legal general foreign-buyer pool — the foreign exit score is capped at 2.5, with foreign dependence logged at ≈0. This "solid Malaysian exit, structurally zero foreign exit" divergence is not a flaw unique to this project, but an honest depiction this framework applies to mid-priced landed-residential products in general: the closer a project's quantum sits to the local mainstream empirical band, the harder it structurally becomes to simultaneously clear the foreign minimum-purchase-price threshold — the two pull in opposite directions by design.

◆ Verdict

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • Bukit Indah／Skudai／Sutera is the deepest transaction-evidence sub-district across all eight JB districts; this project's own 151 transactions／12 months is one of three mutually corroborating sources, with no adverse handover or distress record found anywhere
  • The quantum (RM700,000) falls into this framework's most generous "≤RM750,000" band, and combined with 151-transaction depth, the Malaysian exit score reaches 6.3 — among the most solidly evidenced Malaysian-side exit economics in this framework
  • Landed-residential title (not commercial／serviced apartment) means R-J1's commercial-overhang veto does not apply; gross yield ≈4.46% (two-site cross-corroborated, n=17) clears into the §3.1 bonus band, and the current phase's launch price sits almost exactly at parity with the self-project transaction anchor
  • Three decades of continuous development, nearly 6,000 units sold cumulatively, township planning has won two industry awards (1998, 1999)
⚠ Caveats
  • 【Parent developer】 Boustead Holdings (taken private by LTAT in 2023, not a forced delisting) was formally characterised by Parliament's PAC on 2024-11-19 as in a "cash flow crisis," with roughly RM5 billion in LTAT exposure flagged; group debt has fallen from RM6.8–7.6 billion to roughly RM3.4 billion (2026-02), deleveraging but not yet complete as of 2026-08 — the developer dimension therefore drops from 5.5 to 4.8; but all of this evidence sits at the group level, with no document specifically naming this project
  • 【Foreign economics】 Foreign exit score is only 2.5 — R-J2 triggered because the exit price is below Johor's statewide RM1,000,000 threshold and this is not Forest City, so no legal general foreign-buyer pool exists, foreign dependence logged at ≈0; foreign cashflow has since risen to 4.0 on the expanded rental evidence, and the foreign total now computes to 5.010, sitting solidly in T3 (no longer the previous version's knife-edge 4.950); the Malaysian total of 5.9 is T2
  • 【Tenure】 Leasehold 991 years, not freehold — practically near-freehold, but a genuinely different title category from a same-district freehold comparison case (e.g. Taman Bukit Indah), which should be weighed honestly
  • 【Data confidence】 Both transaction-anchor cuts (151 and 137 units) are secondary mirror-site summaries, not independently verified against the brickz.my primary source; rent and yield figures are asking-side estimates, not verified signed leases
  • 【Connectivity】 Driving-distance proxy to CIQ is roughly 20–24km, far beyond any reasonable walking range, so no case-level walking bonus applies; the Singapore-based-Malaysian-worker buyer／tenant segment lacks case-level evidence and is not assumed favourable

Taman Bukit Indah Terrace (SP Setia)

The JB series' first landed-housing card: Location (6.3) and Exit (Malaysian 6.3) both benefit from the deepest transaction evidence across all eight JB districts — 218 transactions in 12 months (2024-07 to 2025-06), a quantum median of RM715,500 sits squarely in the core local-buyer clearing band, and the Malaysian score (6.1) is among the highest in this batch. But that same quantum sits below Johor's statewide RM1,000,000 foreign threshold, triggering R-J2's strictest tier — foreign buyers have no legal path at current pricing, and the foreign score (5.1) trails the Malaysian score by a wide margin. Scope note: this card scores the broad township-wide "Taman Bukit Indah double-storey terrace" category, not any single sub-series (Aveiro / The Springs / Camelia, etc.) or specific street — see full disclosure in Block 3. 2026-08-07 cashflow evidence finalized after a third verification round: this community's rental evidence went through three independent estimates (a judgment value of 3.45%, then 2.9% from six itemized listings that were later confirmed dead links, then 4.78% from a third-round dual-site cross-check of iProperty n=20 and mudah.my n=5). This card uses the third-round result; the Malaysian total score/Tier has moved back up to 6.1/T1 — see the d3 note in Block 2.

Key Data

⚠️ Product-Scope Note — read this before the scores above: Taman Bukit Indah is a large township under rolling development since 1997 (per secondary aggregation from StarProperty/Grokipedia: roughly 1,521 acres, 10,000+ units across all product types, population ≈60,000, GDV ≈RM5.2 billion — medium confidence, these are township-level figures, not official first-hand numbers specific to the double-storey-terrace subset scored here). The township contains named sub-series — Aveiro, The Springs, Camelia, Orion — but transaction data for each individual sub-series could not be found; sources such as brickz and list.my only report township-aggregate figures. This card therefore scores at the level of "Taman Bukit Indah township-wide double-storey-terrace product" — the score represents this broad category, not any single sub-series or specific street. This scope limitation directly affects the testability of two items in the d1/d4 individual-adjustment tables — "developable adjacent land within 500m" and "competing supply within a 1km radius" — both of which default to non-triggered because a single address cannot be pinned down, as disclosed under each dimension. Readers should treat this as a structural limitation, not evidence that the score already accounts for sub-series-level differences. R-J5 phase-selection rule, applied by analogy: R-J5 itself targets the brand/phase decoupling of Chinese-backed legacy cases (Forest City, Country Garden Danga Bay, R&F Princess Cove, etc.); SP Setia is not such a case, so R-J5 does not literally trigger. But this card applies R-J5's underlying methodological principle — define the scoring scope before scoring — by analogy: Taman Bukit Indah is a continuously rolling township development (not a single discrete-phase launch), so the scope selected is "township-wide + double-storey-terrace product," for three reasons: (1) this is the only scope with deep, verifiable transaction data (218/12 months); (2) sub-series-level data is systematically unavailable, not a result of insufficient search effort; (3) this scope choice is consistent with this card's role as the JB series' first landed card and the benchmark for future landed cards — future landed cards should reference the scoping convention established here and clearly state their own scope-selection rationale.
⚠️ Valuation and pricing transparency note: This case's anchor is its own transaction median, total price RM715,500 / RM500 psf (218 transactions, 2024-07 to 2025-06, high confidence); the representative unit's market value estimate uses the same anchor. JB has no MLS and no official days-on-market or listing-to-transaction discount data; actual transaction prices may fall below asking prices due to negotiation, and buyers should still confirm final net pricing in writing with the agent or developer.
⚠️ R-J2 threshold reminder — read this before the table below: Johor's statewide minimum purchase threshold for foreigners is RM1,000,000 (landed and strata share the same table and price, with no distinction by product type). This case's own transaction median is RM715,500 — RM284,500 (≈28%) below the threshold. Taman Bukit Indah is not Forest City, so the MM2H SEZ RM500k exception does not apply. In practice, foreign buyers have no legal path to purchase a typical unit at this case's current pricing, unless they specifically buy a larger unit (e.g. a corner lot or an extended unit) priced above RM1,000,000, or look elsewhere. The "Foreign" column in the table below therefore models a hypothetical scenario — what the economics would look like if a legal transaction were possible — rather than the actual accessible path for this case's typical unit today.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Dive

Taman Bukit Indah is a large township that SP Setia has developed on a rolling basis since 1997 in JB's Bukit Indah/Skudai/Sutera sub-district — per secondary aggregation, roughly 1,521 acres, over 10,000 units across all product types, a population of ≈60,000, and a GDV of ≈RM5.2 billion, administratively under the Iskandar Puteri Municipal Council (MBIP). The township contains named sub-series including Aveiro, The Springs, Camelia, and Orion, but transaction data for each individual sub-series could not be found in major sources such as brickz and list.my — only township-aggregate figures are available. This creates a methodological scoping question this card had to address honestly: it therefore scores at the level of "Taman Bukit Indah township-wide double-storey-terrace product," representing this broad category rather than any single sub-series or street. R-J5 literally applies only to the brand/phase decoupling of Chinese-backed legacy cases (this case's developer, SP Setia, plainly does not fall into that category), but this card applies its underlying "define the scoring scope before scoring" methodology by analogy to this rolling-development township — the rationale for choosing "township + double-storey terrace" as the scope is that it is the only scope with deep, verifiable market data, and this choice is consistent with this case's role as the JB series' first landed card and the benchmark for future landed cards.

On location, the Bukit Indah/Skudai/Sutera sub-district base is 6.3 (raised from 6.0 on 2026-08-07) — per framework ANCHORS §1, this is the sub-district judged to have the deepest transaction evidence across all eight JB districts: this case's own terrace transactions (218/12 months, median total price RM715,500, high confidence), the non-landed scope (436/9 years), and nearby Taman Mutiara Rini (151) all corroborate one another, with no adverse delivery or distress record found. But this case is ≈22km, about 19 minutes by car, from CIQ/Bangunan Sultan Iskandar (Rome2Rio, checked 2026-08-07) — not walkable under any reasonable definition; the Tier B connectivity base (5.5) receives no individual bonus. This case's geographic appeal is fundamentally different from the Straits-corridor cases: its draw is not a cross-border commuting location, but the depth of an established township's community and local buyer pool.

Developer SP Setia Berhad is a Bursa-listed company whose financial position has continued to improve (net gearing FY2023 0.49 → FY2024 0.35 → currently 0.33), but delivery records and resale discounts within Johor (including for Setia Tropika) could not be found for specific cases — per this framework's asymmetric rule, insufficient evidence means a neutral ceiling rather than a bonus for good financials, so d5 is recorded as a genuinely neutral 5.5. This round's check also turned up a Lowyat forum post complaining about poor after-sales service at "Indah Residence II," another SP Setia sub-project within the township (a condominium product, not this case's terrace category) — but this is an informal forum source, not a court or regulatory record, and it targets a different product line, so per rule it is not scored, only disclosed. On product type, this case is freehold landed housing, not subject to the R-J1 commercial-title overhang veto — Johor's serviced-apartment overhang is 50.5% of the national total (9,477 units), while general residential overhang is only 3,705 units, and this case sits structurally outside that pressure. But per rule this contrast can only be written as "not bound by the rule," not treated as a reason for a bonus, so the Appreciation dimension still scores at the landed baseline of 6.0 without any additional lift from this fact.

On pricing, this case has the highest valuation confidence among this batch of JB cards: 218 same-case transactions (median RM715,500 / RM500 psf) directly exempt it from the general 5.5 cap for cases lacking a comparable anchor. Current listings span a wide range (RM450k–1.2M, with corner-lot premiums up to RM855k–988k), but the one specific non-corner comparison point found (RM699,000) sits roughly at or slightly below the anchor, showing no systematic premium. On rent, this card's cashflow evidence went through three verification rounds before it was finalized: round 1 was a listing-range-midpoint judgment value; round 2 itemized six same-case listings, but an independent re-check afterward found the links all dead, plus one listing was a platform mislabeling of a separate UEM Sunrise township as Taman Bukit Indah itself; round 3 corrected the methodology to actually open and confirm each listing live, giving a dual-site cross-check of iProperty (n=20, median RM2,850) and mudah.my (n=5, median RM3,000), with the township's higher-spec Avenue 26/Villosa sub-series (n=3, median RM3,500) disclosed separately and not merged into the main sample. The round-3 result corresponds to a gross yield of ≈4.78%, falling in the 4.0–5.0% band, which lifted the d3 cashflow score from a 5.5 baseline to 6.0 (Malaysian) / 4.0 (foreign). Transaction depth, rental demand, and the corrected cashflow reading together make this case's Malaysian exit score (6.3, the highest in this batch of seven cards) and total score (6.1, Tier 1) the highest in this batch — and the first JB card to return to Tier 1 in the landed category. The foreign-side story is different: this case's own transaction median of RM715,500 sits 28% below Johor's statewide RM1,000,000 threshold, triggering R-J2's strictest tier — foreign buyers have no legal path to purchase a typical unit at current pricing, and the foreign exit score is capped at 2.5; even with the cashflow now at 4.0, the foreign total score is 5.1, placing it in Tier 3. The most likely next buyer in ten years is a local upgrader or an out-of-state Malaysian investor; the foreign buyer pool would only open once this case's own price crosses the RM1,000,000 threshold, or a buyer specifically targets a unit priced above it.

◆ Conclusion

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • Deepest transaction evidence across all eight JB districts (218/12 months); Malaysian exit score (6.3) and total score (6.1, Tier 1) are both the highest in this batch of seven JB cards — this is the JB series' first landed card and the benchmark for future landed cards
  • Freehold landed housing, not subject to the R-J1 commercial-title overhang veto; an established township (since 1997, 1,521 acres, population ≈60,000) with complete amenities and ample rental-demand evidence (EdgeProp single page lists 207 landed rentals)
  • Developer SP Setia is a Bursa-listed company with continuously improving financials (net gearing down to 0.33) and no negative Johor delivery record found
  • Quantum RM715,500 falls squarely in the core local-buyer clearing band (≤RM750k); all three of the local first-time-buyer/upgrader/out-of-state-investor segments have substantive supporting evidence
⚠ Caveats
  • [Scope limitation] This card scores the broad township + double-storey-terrace scope, not any single sub-series (Aveiro/Camelia/The Springs, etc.) or specific street; individual sub-series or streets may perform differently and this card cannot verify that breakdown, so the 500m/1km-radius individual-level tests default to non-triggered in both d1 and d4
  • [Foreign eligibility] Quantum RM715,500 is 28% below Johor's statewide RM1,000,000 threshold, triggering R-J2's strictest tier — no legal general foreign-buyer path at current pricing (foreign exit score only 2.5)
  • [Geography] ≈22km/19min by car to CIQ, not walkable — the appeal for Singapore commuting is clearly weaker than the Straits-corridor cases; no case-level evidence of Singapore-working-Malaysian buyer/tenant composition found
  • [Data-verification history] The cashflow evidence took three rounds to finalize (a listing-midpoint judgment value → six listings later confirmed as all dead links → a dual-site cross-check of iProperty n=20 and mudah.my n=5), underscoring how difficult listing-based data verification is in JB; the final gross yield of ≈4.78% is still asking-price rather than signed-lease evidence, and no official terrace-specific rental statistics exist

⚠️ Developer corporate-lineage check — is Mahabuilders/SKS Group the same group as "MB World/Astaka"? This card's developer is Mahabuilders Sdn Bhd. ANCHORS §5.1 carries a locked entry, "MB World/Astaka −1.5 → 4.0," based on The Astaka @ 1 Bukit Senyum case, where the main contractor sent three undisclosed demand letters and the SGX formally censured the developer and ordered then-executive-director-and-CEO Zamani Bin Kasim to resign all positions. This entry is worth a full 1.5 points on d5, so this card carried out its deepest verification effort of the round on this single question. Conclusion: Mahabuilders/SKS Group and Astaka Holdings Limited (The Astaka's developer parent) are not the same group — they are two independent, unrelated corporate entities. Basis: (1) different founders and founding years — Mahabuilders' predecessor, "Mahadun Benaan Sdn Bhd," was founded in 1983 by Mahadun Abd Rahim, Md Said Hassan and Sim See Kiong (per a primary-source check of The Edge Malaysia's "City & Country: Growing organically" feature), renamed Mahabuilders Sdn Bhd in 1994 and Mahabuilders Bhd in 2010 with a Bursa listing in mind; Astaka Padu Sdn Bhd (The Astaka's developer, a subsidiary of Astaka Holdings) was founded in 1993 by Dato' Daing A Malek Bin Daing A Rahaman — entirely different founders and founding years, no overlap found. (2) different listing venues and histories — Astaka Holdings Limited listed on the Singapore Exchange's Catalist board in November 2015 via a reverse takeover of E2-Capital Holdings Limited (with Astaka Padu injected); Mahabuilders stated an intention to list on Bursa Malaysia, but no evidence was found that this listing was ever actually completed, and the company continues to operate as a private limited company (Sdn Bhd). (3) the full career of Zamani Bin Kasim, the executive censured by the SGX, was traced through both CEO Magazine and an ASEAN business-leaders profile: he worked at Arab-Malaysian Development Bhd/the Albukhary Group/UEM Land/Denia Development (CEO 2010-2012)/Seloga Holdings (COO 2010-2012), became CEO of Astaka Padu Sdn Bhd in 2012, then CEO of Astaka Holdings until his 2020 retirement, and stepped down as executive director in 2021 — his entire career shows no documented connection whatsoever to Mahabuilders, SKS Group, or "MB World." (4) Bursa Malaysia separately lists a company called "MB World Group Berhad" (ticker 7189), formerly Emas Kiara Industries Berhad, founded 1995 and renamed in 2016 — its corporate timeline does not cleanly align with Mahabuilders' (founded 1983), so this card cannot rule out with full certainty a later capital injection or reverse-takeover relationship between the two (a common backdoor-listing pattern in Malaysia), but even if such a relationship exists, it would be unrelated to Astaka Holdings (an entirely separate, SGX-listed lineage) and does not affect this check's core conclusion. Confidence: medium-high (7/10) — several independent, high-quality sources (The Edge Malaysia's feature, Astaka Holdings' own site, a CEO Magazine executive profile, SGX filings, SSM company registration) corroborate each other, but a final cross-check of SSM/SGX/Bursa shareholding documents was not obtained. This card, per this finding, does not apply ANCHORS §5.1's "MB World/Astaka" −1.5 penalty, and instead uses the §5 "off-ledger developer" baseline of 5.5 (see the Developer dimension). This finding also flags a possible naming/entity-conflation risk in ANCHORS §5.1 that is worth a future review pass.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Dive

Horizon Residence @ Dwi Mutiara sits on Jalan Kebudayaan in the core of Taman Bukit Indah, and is the JB series' second residential-title high-rise card — alongside the concurrently produced Lake View Suites @ Austin Perdana (in the Tebrau sub-district), the two cases sit in different sub-districts under different developers, letting this batch test both sub-district (d1) and developer (d5) variation within the previously zero-sample "residential-title high-rise" product bucket of R-J6. The project was developed by Mahabuilders Sdn Bhd (founded 1983 as Mahadun Benaan Sdn Bhd, renamed Mahabuilders Sdn Bhd in 1994, then Mahabuilders Bhd in 2010 with a Bursa listing in mind), completed in 2014, 26 storeys and 440 units, freehold, unit sizes 1,045-2,463 sq ft across 3-4 bed/2-3 bath layouts, on a 5.58-acre site with an estimated GDV of RM160 million. The developer now trades under the "SKS Group" or "MB World" brand and built its reputation in Johor as a "white knight" — it once took over the Skudaiville Condominium, a project abandoned by original developer Eastern Enterprise Berhad during the 1997 financial crisis, and completed all 400 units; a subsequent buyer lawsuit (Tan Tak & Ors v. Mahabuilders Sdn Bhd, a 2006 High Court decision) arose from that same rescue, but the court ultimately dismissed the plaintiffs' claims — the case is evidence for the developer's rescue role, not a negative record.

On location, the Bukit Indah/Skudai/Sutera sub-district base is 6.3 (raised from 6.0 on 2026-08-07) — per ANCHORS §1, the framework's designation of this sub-district as the district with the deepest transaction evidence across all eight JB districts, and this case's own 59 same-case transactions (2021-11~2025-10) provide direct corroboration. But the case sits roughly 16km/13 minutes by car from CIQ/Bangunan Sultan Iskandar (Rome2Rio, checked 2026-08-07), not walkable, and the Tier B connectivity base (5.5) carries no case-level bonus — the appeal here rests on sub-district depth rather than cross-border commute positioning. The developer dimension received this card's deepest verification effort: ANCHORS §5.1 carries an existing "MB World/Astaka −1.5 → 4.0" entry, stemming from The Astaka @ 1 Bukit Senyum's undisclosed contractor demand letters, an SGX formal censure, and then-executive-director Zamani Bin Kasim being ordered to resign. Cross-checking founders, founding years, listing venues and Zamani's full career, this round confirms: Mahabuilders/SKS Group and Astaka Holdings (The Astaka's developer parent) are two independent, unrelated corporate groups — the former founded 1983, privately held, known for rescuing abandoned Johor projects; the latter founded 1993, listed on the Singapore Exchange's Catalist board in 2015, with Zamani's entire career showing no documented link to the former. This card therefore does not apply the §5.1 −1.5 penalty, and instead uses the "off-ledger developer" baseline of 5.5 — meaning "financial evidence not found," not "no negative record found," a distinction worth stating plainly.

On price and cashflow, this case is a rare "deep cumulative, thin recent" example among this batch of JB cards: its own transaction record runs to 59 (2021-11~2025-10), median RM510,000/psf 397, exempting it outright from the general 5.5 cap for cases lacking a comparable anchor; current active asking prices span RM438,000-620,000, with a median asking psf of about 439, a roughly 10.6% premium over the transaction anchor (right on the 10% boundary, and this card takes the conservative reading, d7=5.0). On rent, this card itemised 5 active same-case rental listings (RM1,800-2,500, median RM2,100), meeting the framework's strongest "full-weight" evidence tier; against the representative unit (1,285 sq ft, RM510,000), the estimated gross yield is about 4.94% — right on the 5.0% boundary, and this card conservatively places it in the 4.0-5.0% band (+0.5 rather than the more generous +1.0), giving d3-MY of 5.5. But the item this card most needs to disclose honestly is that only 4 same-case transactions occurred in the trailing 12 months, far below the 50- or even 15-transaction thresholds, triggering a −0.3 — a sharp contrast with the four-year cumulative 59, and a reminder that "a real buyer pool exists" and "can sell quickly today" are two different things; Exit (d6-MY) lands at 5.2, not lifted by the deep cumulative record alone. The foreign side is dominated entirely by R-J2: the case's own transaction median of RM510,000 sits 49% below Johor's statewide RM1,000,000 threshold, triggering R-J2's strictest tier — foreign buyers have no legal route to a typical unit at the current price, Exit is capped at 2.5, and the foreign total (4.8, T4) trails the Malaysian total (5.5, T2) by a wide margin. The most likely buyer in 10 years is a local first-time buyer or out-of-state Malaysian investor, but buyers should go in with realistic expectations about the recent slower transaction pace, since actual time-to-exit may exceed what the deep historical record alone would suggest.

✦ Highlights
  • Bukit Indah/Skudai/Sutera sub-district base of 6.3, the deepest transaction evidence of all eight JB districts, directly corroborated by this case's own 59 four-year transactions; freehold residential-title high-rise, not subject to the R-J1 commercial-title overhang veto

⚠ Caveats
  • [Liquidity] Only 4 same-case transactions occurred in the trailing 12 months, clearly thinner than the four-year cumulative figure suggests; buyers should budget for a potentially longer exit window

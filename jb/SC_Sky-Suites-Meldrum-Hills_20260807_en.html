◆ In-Depth Narrative

Project Deep Dive

Sky Suites @ Meldrum Hills sits on Jalan Bukit Meldrum in the Tanjung Puteri locality of Johor Bahru — a genuinely short, measured walk of roughly 1.2–1.5km (≈16–20 minutes) from the CIQ／Bangunan Sultan Iskandar crossing, though not close enough to clear this framework's stricter ≤15-minute individual-level connectivity bonus. This card's most consequential judgment call concerns district classification: this project's own address consistently uses "Tanjung Puteri" as its locality, matching this framework's locked "Danga Bay／Tanjung Puteri" district row (baseline 5.0) rather than the "JB CBD／Bukit Chagar／JB Sentral" row that anchors this scale's 7.0 hard cap. Despite sitting closer to CIQ than a typical Danga Bay reclamation project, Sky Suites is not co-located at the RTS Bukit Chagar terminus the way The Astaka is, so this card applies the more textually faithful 5.0 district baseline while flagging the underlying district table as a candidate for future refinement — old-town Tanjung Puteri and the reclaimed Danga Bay waterfront are geographically distinct precincts sharing an administrative label.

Completed in 2015, the project comprises a single 31-storey tower with 250 units on freehold title, developed by MB Land Sdn Bhd. Multiple secondary sources describe this developer as part of the "Mahabuilders Group" or operating under the "SKS Group (also known as MB World, formerly known as Mahabuilders)" brand — and this round's research confirmed, via MB World Group's own website listing the adjacent Meldrum Heights project as developed by its subsidiary MBW Projects Sdn Bhd, that this is genuinely the same MB World Group Berhad referenced elsewhere in this framework's developer table for its 2023 takeover of the stalled Greenland Danga Bay project (since renamed MBW City). Critically, this is a different corporate family from Astaka Holdings Limited — the SGX-listed developer of The Astaka @ 1 Bukit Senyum, which carries this framework's steepest developer penalty for its SGX reprimand — despite superficial "MB"-adjacent branding similarity. No governance, delay or litigation flag specific to MB World Group Berhad or to this project was found, and the developer dimension is scored at the neutral off-table baseline of 5.5 rather than importing Astaka's penalty.

Although marketed and managed as a "Service Residence," multiple independent sources — including Brickz.my's own NAPIC-style transaction classification of "NON-LANDED, RESIDENTIAL" — describe this project's underlying title as freehold residential strata rather than commercial. No source was found explicitly asserting commercial title for this specific project, so this card treats the "Service Residence" label as a management-style convention rather than a title category, applying residential-title baselines throughout (appreciation 5.5, cashflow and exit baselines of 5.0) rather than the steeper commercial-title (SoHo) baselines and caps this framework applies elsewhere in Johor Bahru. This is disclosed as a medium-confidence call, since no primary title document was obtained.

On pricing, this is one of the few JB projects in this framework's coverage so far with a genuine same-project transaction anchor: a Brickz median of RM743 psf／RM678,000 (though from only 3 transactions, dated February to November 2024 — a stale and thin sample by this card's 2026-08-07 cut-off). Against this anchor, the representative 1,300 sqft, 3-bedroom unit's current asking price of RM588,000 (≈RM452 psf) implies a striking ≈39% discount — but this card does not take that figure at face value: small studio units in this building's ≈580–1,345 sqft mix typically carry meaningfully higher psf than large 3-bedroom units, so part of this apparent discount is likely a unit-size-mix artifact rather than a genuine bargain. The valuation score (7.0) reflects the conservative low end of the applicable discount band rather than the raw calculation.

The rental picture is a genuine positive surprise, and is disclosed with appropriate scrutiny rather than simple enthusiasm: a same-size (1,300 sqft) asking rent of RM3,300／month against the RM588,000 asking price implies a gross yield of roughly 6.7% — well above the ≈1.9–4.3% range this framework's broader Johor Bahru research has found elsewhere, though a building-wide cross-check using blended asking psf figures corroborates a similarly elevated yield at the whole-building level, lending it some credibility as a genuine precinct characteristic (older, smaller, CIQ-proximate stock renting well to short／mid-term and commuter tenants) rather than a pure fluke. On exit, the picture splits sharply and mechanically by nationality: the Malaysian buyer pool benefits from a low entry quantum (RM678,000 transacted median, comfortably within this framework's most affordable local-buyer bracket) even as same-project transaction depth is thin. For foreign buyers, the picture is not merely weaker but structurally closed: this project's pricing — both transacted and currently asking — sits well below the RM1,000,000 statewide strata purchase threshold that Johor imposes on all general foreign buyers, with no PR discount tier to narrow the gap and no MM2H carve-out (that exists only for Forest City). Unless this project's price level rises durably by roughly half from current levels, no legal general foreign strata buyer exists for it at all — a hard, price-driven exclusion rather than a soft, sentiment-driven one, and the primary reason the foreign score (5.0, T3) trails the Malaysian score (5.7, T2).

◆ Conclusion

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • A genuinely short, measured walk (≈1.2–1.5km／16–20min) to CIQ — closer than a typical Danga Bay-area project, anchoring an above-baseline A-tier connectivity score even without clearing the individual bonus threshold
  • Low entry quantum (RM588,000–678,000) puts the representative unit and the project's own transaction median comfortably in this framework's most affordable local-buyer bracket, and residential (not commercial) title means the steeper R-J1 baselines and caps do not apply
  • A striking ≈6.7% gross-yield reading, corroborated at the building-wide level and meeting this framework's full-weight (≥3 same-project listings) evidence tier — though disclosed as an outlier requiring continued scrutiny relative to broader Johor Bahru rent research
  • One of the few JB projects in this framework's coverage with a genuine, if thin, same-project transaction anchor, and no governance or delay flag was found for this specific developer entity (clearly disambiguated from Astaka Holdings Limited's SGX reprimand)
⚠ Caveats
  • [Foreign exit] Foreign exit score is a hard 2.5 under R-J2 — this project's pricing sits well below the RM1,000,000 statewide strata threshold, meaning no legal general foreign buyer pool exists at current levels at all, not merely a thin one
  • [Location ceiling] District classified under this scale's 5.0 "Danga Bay／Tanjung Puteri" row, not the 7.0 JB CBD ceiling — a disclosed, borderline judgment call given the project's genuine CIQ proximity; readers should weigh this classification themselves
  • [Data confidence] The same-project transaction anchor (n=3, Feb–Nov 2024) is thin and stale as of this card's 2026-08-07 cut-off; the ≈39% valuation discount and ≈6.7% yield figures both carry disclosed data-quality caveats (unit-size-mix effect; single-listing rent pairing) and are scored conservatively rather than at face value
  • [Precinct dilution] At least two other named, completed competing projects (Sky Habitat, Meldrum Heights) already sit in the immediate precinct from the same broad developer family — any catchment-thickness argument has already absorbed meaningful recent supply, not merely hypothetical future risk

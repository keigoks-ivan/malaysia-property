Lake View Suites @ Austin Perdana

The first residential-title high-rise in this batch of Johor Bahru scorecards (the six live cards to date are all commercial-title serviced residences; three landed cards were produced separately). This case is a freehold "Apartment," confirmed as residential title, so it escapes R-J1's commercial-title overhang veto — d3, d4 and d6 all run on this framework's "residential-title high-rise" baseline (5.0 / 5.5 / 5.0), not the commercial 4.0, nor the landed 5.5 / 6.0. But its own median transaction price is just RM298,000, far below Johor's statewide RM1,000,000 foreign threshold — this is the series' first case where the quantum itself is low enough to structurally exclude all foreign buyers: even with a foreign-friendly title, the price bar alone leaves no legal path to purchase, triggering R-J2's harshest tier with a foreign exit cap of 2.5.

This gap is driven mainly by the fixed-cost items on the foreign side (8% stamp duty of RM23,840, and the 3% / minimum RM30,000 levy), which weigh especially heavily against this case's low RM298,000 entry price — this is the concrete expression of this case's "low quantum, high fixed-friction-cost ratio" character: fixed foreign friction costs (especially the RM30,000 levy floor) consume a far larger share of a low-priced unit's value than they would on a higher-priced one. ◆ In-depth analysis

In-depth analysis

Lake View Suites @ Austin Perdana sits within the Taman Austin Perdana integrated estate in the Tebrau / Mount Austin sub-district, developed by Mestika Bistari Sdn Bhd, a subsidiary of Mah Sing Group. The project comprises 720 units across 20 floors, ranging from 500sqft studios to 894sqft three-bedroom units. The single most important verification task for this card was confirming the project's land title: multiple independent sources (iRumah's explicit "Land Title: Residential (condominium/apartment)" label, consistent "Apartment" rather than "Service Residence" labeling across several listing platforms, and freehold tenure) all confirm this is a residential-title project rather than a commercial-title serviced residence. This exempts the case from R-J1's commercial-title overhang veto — both appreciation (d4) and exit (d6) run on this framework's higher residential-title baseline, making this the first case to fill the "residential-title high-rise" product gap left by the six previously live cards, which were all commercial-title serviced residences. The verification process also surfaced a naming ambiguity worth resolving explicitly: a listing named "Austin Suite Service Apartments" shares this project's address, but geocoding confirms this is a rental-operator brand for units within this building, not a separate commercial-title project, and it does not disturb this case's residential-title finding.

On the developer side, this case carries ANCHORS §5.1's locked deduction for Mah Sing (the i-PARC @ Tanjung Pelepas industrial-warehouse delayed-delivery lawsuit, lost at both trial and appellate levels), landing d5 at 5.0 — a lighter deduction because the case involves an industrial, not residential, product line, and this round's research found no reported delayed-delivery or regulatory-action case specific to Austin Perdana township itself (including this project). On price, this case's own median transaction of RM298,000 / RM368 psf (35 transactions, 2024-02 through 2025-01) serves as its own valid valuation anchor. This card individually verified four active listings (894sqft@RM330,000, 635sqft@RM288,000, 505sqft@RM238,000, 920sqft@RM305,000), with a median psf of approximately RM411 — a premium of approximately +11.8% over the self-transaction anchor, landing on the "elevated, needs a catalyst" step of the valuation ladder. Given that the aggregator-quoted asking ranges themselves are inconsistent (RM302–483 vs. RM331–515 psf), this card applies the "when evidence is uncertain, take the conservative side" principle and lands on the less favorable reading, d7 = 5.0.

This case's strongest positive reading comes from the cashflow dimension: this card individually verified four same-case rental listings (RM1,000 to RM1,260/month, 665–860sqft), meeting this framework's "full" evidence tier; the median rent of RM1,250/month against the RM298,000 median transaction gives a gross yield of approximately 5.03%, landing in this framework's top yield-conversion band (≥5.0%, +1.0), one of the stronger cashflow readings in this batch of Johor Bahru cards. This project's JMB is established, with a management fee of RM196.80/month for a 892sqft unit (approximately RM0.22 psf) — a case-specific, measured figure, better than the generic estimates most cards must rely on. On connectivity, this case differs in character from the causeway-corridor high-rises: Rome2Rio verification shows this case sits approximately 19km from CIQ / Bangunan Sultan Iskandar, approximately 17 minutes driving, far beyond a reasonable walking range, so the case-level walking bonus does not trigger; but the township itself is walkable to Tesco, Aeon Tebrau City and several schools — not a pedestrian island — so this case's connectivity advantage rests on the established integrated estate's amenity base.

The single most important finding of this card, however, is the gap between this case's quantum (RM298,000) and Johor's statewide foreign threshold (RM1,000,000) — approximately 70%, the largest seen in this series to date. This is not a boundary case, but a structural exclusion unlikely to change soon: R-J2 triggers at its harshest tier, the foreign exit cap lands at 2.5, and foreign dependency reads at approximately zero. The 10-year cost table exposes the double edge of this low quantum: on the Malaysian side, the buyer pool is widest and leverage efficiency is high (base-case equity IRR approximately 10.2%); but for the hypothetical foreign buyer, fixed costs (8% stamp duty, the 3% / minimum RM30,000 levy) weigh disproportionately against the low total price, widening the Malaysian-foreign P&L gap to approximately 20.2% of entry price, among the largest in the batch. In ten years, the likely buyer is a local first-time buyer or an out-of-state investor seeking capital efficiency, not a foreign buyer — unless the median transaction climbs over decades to cross the threshold, or a buyer targets a different price tier within the broader estate.

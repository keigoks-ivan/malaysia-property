⚠️ R-J2 threshold caveat — read before the tables below: Johor's statewide minimum foreign-purchase threshold for strata property is RM1,000,000. This project's own median transaction price is RM700,000 — RM300,000 (30%) below that threshold. Paragon Suites @ CIQ is not Forest City, so the separate MM2H-SEZ RM500,000 concession does not apply. In practice, a foreign buyer cannot legally purchase a typical unit at this project's current pricing without either paying a substantial premium to clear the RM1,000,000 floor (e.g. a larger Type C／D unit) or using a different, higher-priced project entirely. The "Foreign" columns in the tables below therefore model a hypothetical comparison — what the economics would look like if a qualifying purchase were legally possible — not a currently available pathway at this project's typical price point.

Representative unit: per this framework's rule, floor plans should preferentially cite the case's own unit types over the generic 1,000–1,300 sqft size-band table. Paragon Suites @ CIQ's own floor plans span four types: Type A 732–818 sqft (2BR／2BA); Type B 936–1,076 sqft (3BR／2BA); Type C 1,367–1,389 sqft (3BR／3BA); Type D 1,674–1,711 sqft (5BR／5BA). Breakeven price growth needed to cover total cash friction costs (excluding opportunity cost, linear interpolation between the bear and base scenarios): roughly 1.0%／year for the Malaysian buyer and 1.9%／year for the hypothetical foreign buyer — both comfortably below the +3% base case. ◆ In-Depth Narrative

Project Deep Dive

Paragon Suites @ CIQ sits in Johor Bahru's CBD precinct, on Jalan Inderaputra off Jalan Stulang Darat, and this card's single strongest, most concretely verified fact is its walking distance to the CIQ／Bangunan Sultan Iskandar checkpoint: a Google Maps walking-directions check on 2026-08-07 returned 700 metres, 10 minutes, flagged "mostly flat" — meaningfully more conservative than the developer's own marketing claim of "just 300m, a 1-minute drive," which this card deliberately did not use for scoring. That measured figure earns this project the highest connectivity score (7.0) in this batch, on top of an already-capped location score (7.0, this scale's hard ceiling for JB CBD). Developed by Joland Group (Joland Holding Sdn Bhd, incorporated 1985, via subsidiary Amprojek Construction Sdn Bhd) — a private, long-established Johor developer also behind Paragon Residences @ Straits View and Grand Paragon Hotel — the project comprises 461 units across two towers (Tower A 30 storeys, Tower B 35 storeys; some listings describe the building simply as "33-storey"), completed in 2017 and, per the developer, fully sold: the market here today is exclusively subsale.

The defining fact about this project, however, is not its location but its title. Paragon Suites @ CIQ is held on commercial strata title — the developer's own materials describe it as a "luxury serviced apartment," and third-party listing sites classify it explicitly as "Commercial Strata property." Under this framework's R-J6 product classification and R-J1 overhang rule, that single fact drives almost every remaining number on this card. Johor's service-apartment segment carries a structural, statewide overhang — roughly 9,000–10,000 units completed and unsold, about half the national total, a condition this framework's own research shows has been "high plateau, gently rising" rather than resolving — and R-J1 responds by capping both appreciation (5.0) and exit scores (5.0, both nationalities) for any commercial-title case. In this project's case, the computed baselines land even below that cap (appreciation 4.0, Malaysian exit 3.4), so the cap itself is not the binding constraint; the underlying commercial-title classification is doing the work directly.

On pricing, this is genuinely one of the better-evidenced JB CBD cases: 31 same-project subsale transactions since 2021, a median of RM700,000／RM679 psf with a tight interquartile range (RM628–730 psf), qualifying this project as its own valid valuation anchor outright, without needing a district-representative fallback. Current active listings span an unusually wide RM462–1,225 psf across 26 listings — most plausibly floor-level and condition dispersion in a 461-unit building rather than systematic mispricing — and without a clean current-asking figure isolated to the representative Type B (≈1,000 sqft) unit, this card scores valuation at a moderate 6.0, roughly at par with the transaction anchor. The rental market is comparatively well-evidenced for a JB CBD project too: 72–112 active same-project listings (qualifying for this framework's strongest "full" evidence tier) point to an estimated RM2,800／month for the representative unit, a gross yield of about 4.8% — one of the stronger cashflow readings on the Malaysian side of this batch, though the 461-unit building's roughly 6% active-for-sale listing share against a modest ≈6-transactions-per-year pace is a soft liquidity signal worth flagging.

The most consequential structural fact in this entire card, though, is what R-J2 does to the foreign side. Johor's statewide minimum foreign-purchase threshold for strata property is RM1,000,000 — and this project's own median transaction price, RM700,000, sits RM300,000, or 30%, below it. Because Paragon Suites @ CIQ is not Forest City, the separate MM2H-SEZ RM500,000 concession does not apply either. In practical terms, a foreign buyer today has no legal path to purchase a typical unit at this project's prevailing price: they would need to specifically target a higher-priced Type C or D unit clearing RM1,000,000, or look elsewhere entirely. This is not a modelling nuance — it is the primary reason the foreign exit score (1.9) sits well below the Malaysian side (3.9), and the primary reason the foreign total (4.8, T4) trails the Malaysian total (5.4, T3) by a full tier. Even on the Malaysian side, exit economics remain constrained: same-project transaction depth is modest (≈6／year), and commercial-title financing friction applies structurally — the quantum band itself (under the 2026-08-07 revised boundary) is no longer the drag it once was, but transaction depth and financing friction still hold the Malaysian exit score below the 4.0 baseline. Ten years from now, the most plausible next buyer is a local upgrader or an out-of-state Malaysian investor drawn by the yield and the CIQ location — not a foreign buyer, unless this project's own pricing eventually clears the RM1,000,000 line, which would itself require overcoming the same statewide service-apartment overhang that caps this card's scores today.

◆ Conclusion

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • Independently measured 700m／10-minute walk to CIQ／Bangunan Sultan Iskandar — the strongest individually-verified connectivity fact in this batch, driving both location (7.0) and connectivity (7.0) to this scale's ceiling and near-ceiling
  • Deep same-project transaction history (31 transactions, 2021–2026) with a tight price range (RM628–730 psf), providing a reliable self-referential valuation anchor unusually strong for a JB CBD resale case
  • Well-evidenced rental market by JB CBD standards — 72–112 active same-project listings qualify for this framework's "full" yield-evidence tier, supporting an estimated ≈4.8% gross yield, one of the stronger cashflow readings in this batch
  • Freehold title, foreigner-eligible in principle; fully sold and completed since 2017, so no off-plan delivery risk remains
⚠ Caveats
  • [Product type] Commercial-title serviced apartment triggers R-J1 — this locks appreciation and both exit scores under this framework's harshest product-type ceiling, structurally regardless of the genuinely strong location
  • [Foreign eligibility] This project's own median transaction price (RM700,000) sits below Johor's RM1,000,000 statewide foreign-purchase threshold — R-J2 triggered at its harshest tier: no legal general foreign buyer pathway exists at current pricing (foreign exit 1.5, this framework's clamp floor)
  • [Data confidence] Management fee and assessment estimates are generic, not project-specific (no commercial-title service-apartment figure found for JB); current-asking price could not be cleanly isolated to the representative unit size from available listings
  • [Thematic caveat] The widely-cited +29.5% Johor-city service-apartment price surge is disclosed but deliberately excluded from this project's appreciation score (R-J3) — even though this project is itself a service apartment, JLL／EdgeProp's own attribution of that surge to new-launch composition effects means it cannot be borrowed as positive evidence here

Setia Tropika Terrace

This batch's first landed residential case — exempt from R-J1's commercial-title overhang veto, so both appreciation and exit use this framework's higher landed baseline. This project's own median transaction price of RM700,000 (65 transactions, 2023-11 to 2024-10) falls into the quantum "≤RM750k" bonus band, and same-project transaction depth also clears the ≥50-transaction bonus tier — the structural reason the Malaysian-side exit score (6.3) and valuation score (7.0, self-transaction discount) are both the highest in this batch, driving a Malaysian total of 6.2 — this batch's first T1. But that same RM700,000 median sits below Johor's RM1,000,000 statewide foreign threshold, triggering R-J2's harshest tier and capping foreign exit at 2.5. The location moat itself anchors at 6.0, slightly below sister-project Taman Bukit Indah's 6.3 (same developer), reflecting shallower evidence depth (65–72 transactions vs. 218).

◆ In-Depth Narrative

Project Deep Dive

Setia Tropika sits in the Tebrau／Mount Austin subdistrict, a 740-acre township developed by SP Setia with 3,500-plus units spanning double-storey terraces, apartments, cluster houses, service apartments, and commercial facilities. This card's scoring scope is the township's double-storey terrace product overall — per ANCHORS §7's established scope, not a single sister launch. The township also contains several named sister launches — Elata Nova (93 units, launched 2018, from RM701,800, 1,964sqft, 4BR3BA), Elata Vita (138 units, 7 acres, GDV RM90.2m), the Caryota series, and others — all extension phases from the same developer within the same township, most completed with high take-up rates (both Elata Nova and Elata Vita achieved 90% take-up at launch), and this round's search found no reports of delivery delays, abandonment, or regulatory sanction for the township itself. Developer SP Setia is financially healthy: net gearing has declined steadily, FY2023 0.49 → FY2024 0.35 → currently 0.33, though case-level Johor (Setia Tropika, etc.) delivery track record and resale-discount data is unfound; per this framework's asymmetric rule, financial health does not earn a bonus, so d5 stays at a neutral baseline of 5.5.

The single most consequential fact about this project is its product type: residential-landed double-storey terrace, freehold, exempt from R-J1's commercial-title overhang veto. Johor's service-apartment segment carries a structural, statewide overhang (roughly 9,000–10,000 units completed and unsold, about half the national total), but that rule only locks commercial-strata products — this project, as landed residential, uses this framework's higher baseline for both appreciation and exit (landed 6.0 vs. commercial-title 4.0) — the structural reason this project leads this batch of JB cards (six of which are causeway-corridor high-rise strata) so decisively. On pricing, this project's own median transaction price of RM700,000／RM436 psf (65 transactions, 2023-11 to 2024-10) qualifies it outright as its own anchor, exempt from the general "no comparable anchor" 5.5 cap. This card deliberately excludes two higher, mixed-composition figures (RM850,000 for 2025-03 to 2026-02, and RM800,000 from a 610-transaction, five-year aggregate) — both confirmed to mix in non-terrace, higher-tier township products, using only ANCHORS §7's figure explicitly labelled "Terrace House." Current active listings run about RM640,000–688,000, roughly 5.1% below the transaction median, technically falling into the discount band, but this card treats that reading conservatively (medium-confidence listings, timing lag), scoring only the discount band's conservative end of 7.0.

On connectivity, this project differs fundamentally from this batch's six causeway-corridor high-rise cases: Google Maps／distancesfrom.com cross-verification shows this project sits ≈18.67km, ≈21-minute drive from CIQ／Bangunan Sultan Iskandar — far beyond any reasonable walking range, so §2.1's CIQ walking bonus does not trigger. This project's connectivity advantage rests on a mature suburban road network and driving convenience to central JB, not a walkable checkpoint position. Rental-market evidence is comparatively solid: this card scores off an independently re-verified sample gathered ahead of publication (n=8–9 active same-project rental listings, median RM2,850, RM2,825 after de-duplication, verified 2026-08), qualifying for this framework's "full" evidence tier, supporting an estimated gross yield of ≈4.84–4.89%, falling in this framework's 4.0–5.0% yield-conversion band — the only one of this batch's three JB landed cases to pass cross-verification on the first attempt, and one of the stronger cashflow readings on the Malaysian side of this batch.

Yet the foreign-side structural constraint here is no different from this framework's other Johor cases: Johor's statewide minimum foreign-purchase threshold is RM1,000,000, and this project's own median transaction price of RM700,000 sits RM300,000, or 30%, below it; because Setia Tropika is not Forest City, the MM2H-SEZ concession does not apply either — no legal general pathway exists for a foreign buyer at current pricing. This threshold alone pulls the foreign exit score down from the Malaysian side's 6.3 to 2.5, and is the primary reason the foreign total (5.3) trails the Malaysian total (6.2) by a full tier. Notably, this project is one of the rare cases in this batch where R-J2's cap is genuinely the binding constraint — the raw foreign-exit calculation (4.3) already sits above the cap (2.5), meaning that absent this regulatory threshold, this project's foreign-side economics would actually look quite good. That's different in character from some strata cases where the raw value already sits below the cap regardless, and it underscores that this project's foreign limitation stems from the price threshold itself, not from any underlying weakness in the product. Ten years from now, the most likely next buyer is a local upgrader — this project combines deep transaction absorption, reasonable valuation, and solid yield on the Malaysian side, making it structurally the most balanced case in this batch, while the foreign buyer pool remains effectively closed at current pricing.

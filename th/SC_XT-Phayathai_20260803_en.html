◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep-Dive Summary

XT Phayathai sits on Si Ayutthaya Road (address 515) in Bangkok's Ratchathewi district, developed by Sansiri, one of Thailand's largest integrated developers. Its two high-rise towers (Tower A, 40 floors; Tower B's floor count varies slightly by source, either 37 or 35) reached structural completion in August 2022 (some marketing copy states 2023, an unresolved discrepancy — this card uses the conservative range "2022-2023"). The single most important finding in this card's research is the correction to the walking distance. The commonly circulated claim of "just 130 meters to BTS Phaya Thai" turns out, on tracing FazWaz's own turn-by-turn directions, to be only the first leg of the route — the distance from the station exit to the corner where the path turns onto Phaya Thai Road. The full route continues by turning onto Si Ayutthaya Road for roughly another 480 meters, for a total of approximately 610 meters; an independent Google Maps walking-directions query returned an almost identical result (~600 meters, 9-minute walk), and several 2026 rental listings independently cite "600-700 meters," corroborating this reading. A separate auto-generated FAQ on FazWaz's own site claims "1.2 km," contradicting its own turn-by-turn text — judged an algorithmic error and discarded. Conclusion: this is a genuine same-station BTS/Airport Rail Link interchange, but the walking distance should be understood as roughly 600-650 meters, not the 130 meters circulating elsewhere — a correction that directly affects the connectivity score, and the first thing a buyer checking other sources on this project should verify independently.

On scarcity, the audit result is less favorable than the initial brief hoped. Phaya Thai / Ratchathewi continues to absorb new nearby supply (Baan Ratchathewi, expected to complete April 2027, is one confirmed example), so the land layer fails; at least 11 comparable projects were found in the same submarket (Supalai Elite Phayathai, The Room Phayathai, Noble Revent, The Extro Phayathai-Rangnam, Ideo Mobi Rangnam, Ideo Q Victory, Noble House Phayathai, Hampton Residence/Park Origin Phayathai, Rhythm Rangnam, M Phayathai, Payatai Plaza), a notably denser comp set than the 6 found for the Life Asoke Rama 9 case, so the product layer fails too; the regulatory layer is freehold, but foreign-quota utilisation is undisclosed, so no structural-scarcity credit applies. More decisively, Hipflat's market statistics show this project's own average asking price (USD 4,779/sqm) actually running roughly 4.4% below the Ratchathewi district average (USD 4,999/sqm) — not the ambiguous "no clear premium" outcome from the Life Asoke case, but an outright discount, a clean failure of the effective-scarcity test. The brief's suggested "multi-line transit hub plus medical/education cluster" positioning only holds up on its first half after verification: this project is indeed a genuine BTS/ARL interchange, but the "medical/education cluster" half lacks support — the nearby Phyathai 1 Hospital is a private, mid-tier facility rather than one of the framework's three named international hospitals (Bumrungrad/Samitivej/BNH), Ramathibodi and Rajavithi are public hospitals excluded from the framework's scoring, and no walkable international school could be found.

As a SET-listed company, Sansiri's overall scale (a cumulative 299 projects, 104,728 units) dwarfs AP Thailand, the developer behind the Life Asoke case, and should in theory carry stronger financial backing — but this search could not cleanly verify its current financial position: two mutually inconsistent sets of 1Q2026 figures turned up (from different investor-relations page fragments), and Sansiri's own IR site could not be directly accessed this time either, so this is listed as "not verified." Broker Finansia Syrus Securities (2026-07-24) forecasts second-quarter net profit up 7% quarter-on-quarter but down 24% year-on-year — directionally pointing to weaker profitability, though this is a forecast, not reported results. More notably, no current TRIS Rating for Sansiri could be found in this search, unlike the Life Asoke case, which had a directly citable A/Stable rating for AP Thailand — meaning the developer's financial-soundness judgment here rests more on indirect inference (company scale, listed status) than a direct credit-rating figure. On compliance, secondary marketing material confirms this project holds EIA approval, consistent with its completed and occupied status, and no Ashton-Asoke-style permit-revocation risk was found; one unverified individual-case signal did surface — social media content from a Thai property law firm referencing a "3-unit deposit refund" dispute tied to this project, with cause and outcome both unconfirmed. This card does not factor it into scoring, but flags it as an item buyers should verify independently.

On pricing and rent, this project delivers the first genuinely usable quota-split evidence in this framework's pilot run: both Hipflat and FazWaz show some listings and transactions explicitly tagged Foreign Quota or Thai Quota. The representative unit (1BR M, 42 sqm) asks roughly THB 140,500/sqm under Thai Quota versus THB 155,000/sqm under Foreign Quota — a same-unit-type quota premium of about 10.3%. Anchored against Hipflat's 4 confirmed transactions (June 2021 to February 2026, averaging THB 133,000/sqm), current asking runs roughly 11.1% above, and the valuation score (5.0) lands in a neutral-to-cautious band; but the most recent transaction (February 2026, Foreign Quota, THB 122,000/sqm) sits noticeably below the 2023 Thai Quota transaction (THB 150,487/sqm), hinting at possible recent softening worth continued tracking. On rent, the representative unit lets for roughly THB 25,000/month, translating to a gross yield of about 5.2% on a Thai Quota basis but only about 4.7% on a Foreign Quota basis — this is the direct cashflow impact of the quota premium: the foreign buyer pays roughly 10% more to enter for the identical unit, collects exactly the same rent, and ends up with a structurally lower yield. Thai buyers using a mortgage face a mortgage rate (~6.5%) that already exceeds gross yield, a negative leveraged spread; foreign buyers' after-tax net yield (~3.99%) sits 1.76 percentage points below opportunity-cost benchmark two (USD 5.75%/year), a wider gap than Life Asoke's 1.38 points, driven directly by the quota premium inflating the foreign buyer's cost base.

The exit side is likewise where this card discloses the most "not verified" and conflicting signals. This project's actual foreign-quota utilisation is undisclosed anywhere, placing it in the "unverified" tier with the same deduction as the Life Asoke case (midpoint −0.5); active listings skew notably toward Foreign Quota tags (roughly 70%), an interesting but unreliable indirect observation — it could reflect more active foreign-investor resale behavior, or simply a sampling artifact of the listings — and this card deliberately does not use it to shift the quota-deduction range, to avoid treating noise as information. Secondary-market liquidity data suffers from the same structural limitation as before — Thailand's lack of an official transaction-by-transaction registry — and if anything, this project's data is even thinner than Life Asoke's: Hipflat's "Previous Sales" module discloses only 4 transactions across nearly 5 years, clearly a platform-curated subset rather than a full record, too sparse for this card to force an annualised turnover estimate (unlike Life Asoke, which at least had 108 cumulative transactions to work from); the listing ratio spans 5.7% to 24.8% because the total-unit-count conflict (1,435 vs 605, a 2.4x gap) itself widens the range, and no midpoint is taken. The one relatively solid piece of secondary-market evidence is, ironically, Hipflat's own aggregate statistics: this project transacts at an average of 21% below original asking price, with an average of 273 days on market — a discount that clears the framework's 15% threshold and is a primary driver of the exit-score deduction. What can be said with confidence: the Thai-buyer quantum test shows a next-buyer household-income threshold at 2.11x Bangkok's average household income, higher than Life Asoke's 1.54x, narrowing the local buyer pool; and at least 11 comparable projects in the same submarket compete for the same demand, a sharper competitive landscape than Life Asoke faced. This project's foreign-buyer total score (5.4) falls into T5, while the Thai-buyer score (5.5) sits right at the lower edge of T4 — the first case in this pilot where Thai and foreign scores land in different tiers, a result of the quota premium, the asking-to-transacted discount, and a denser competing-supply pool acting together, not any single dimension's failure.

  • A genuine same-station BTS Phaya Thai + Airport Rail Link interchange, verified at roughly 600-650 meters walk — still a workable commuting base

  • No walkable international school was found, and nearby hospitals are either private mid-tier (Phyathai 1) or public (Ramathibodi/Rajavithi) rather than the framework's top-3 international hospitals — at odds with the "medical/education cluster" positioning suggested in the brief

◆ Deep dive

Project deep dive

Quintara MHy'GEN Ratchada-Huai Khwang is a low-rise condominium developed by SET-listed Eastern Star Real Estate PCL (SET: ESTAR, listed 1994), with 383 units spread across two 8-storey buildings on the Ratchada-Huai Khwang corridor in Bangkok, on a land parcel of ≈2-1-79.5 rai (≈3,200 sqm project area). The developer's own site shows structure, architecture, and systems all reaching 100% progress as of September 21, 2024, while multiple aggregators cite an October 2024 completion date; this card uses the conservative range of "September 2024 structural completion, October transfers." A previously circulated "August 2024" reading could not be reproduced from any source in this research and is not used. The unit mix skews small: Studio 21-23 sqm, 1BR 26-27 sqm, and 2BR 41-42 sqm, with the representative unit set at the upper end of the 1BR band, 27 sqm.

The single most important finding in this research sits at the intersection of pricing and the visa threshold. The 1BR price anchor originally assumed in the briefing could not be reproduced: pricing descriptions diverge sharply across vintages and sources — a Thai pre-sale-era page quoting "from THB 1.99M / 2.09M" (one self-dated July 2022), the developer's current page quoting "from THB 2.59M" (likely a studio floor, not a 1BR), PropertyScout citing a broad THB 2.37-4.95M range, and Hipflat citing a 1BR band of roughly USD 85,274-128,360 (≈THB 3.0-4.5M). Amid this highly dispersed pricing picture, the one verifiable anchor is a transaction disclosed in Hipflat's "Previous Sales": a 1BR sold on October 9, 2025 for THB 3.2M, implying ≈THB 118,500/sqm at 27 sqm — the single most credible per-sqm figure available for this project, and the shared basis for both this card's valuation and exit calculations, though it must be acknowledged that the sample size is just one. That transacted price sits only about 6.7% above the THB 3M property-visa floor, below this framework's THB 3.6M "boundary risk" line — this is, for the first time in the Thailand pilot, a genuine case requiring the "representative unit near the visa threshold" boundary-risk deduction. The four previously published cards (Life Asoke Rama 9, Life Rama 4-Asoke, Noble Revo Silom, XT Phayathai) all had representative units in the THB 4.3-5.9M range, well above the threshold, so this mechanism had never actually fired before now. For a foreign buyer, this means a price decline of only 15-20% could drop this unit entirely out of visa eligibility, structurally removing the foreign buyer pool — a more fundamental exit risk than an ordinary listing discount, disclosed independently here in both the exit dimension (foreign −0.3) and the 5D disclosure box.

On scarcity, the audit result is equally unfavorable, and arguably more clearly so than any of the framework's other four cards. The Din Daeng / Huai Khwang corridor doesn't just have generic new supply nearby — Bangkok's city plan is actively upzoning density along this mass-transit corridor, a structural policy signal that the planning authority is deliberately expanding future supply caps, more persistent and less favorable than an ordinary "new project under construction nearby" observation; the land layer clearly fails. The product layer fails too: at least 5 confirmed comparable projects, totaling 5,000+ units, sit in the same corridor, including XT Huaykwang (Sansiri, 1,404 units, completed 2021, just 75m from the station), Noble Revolve Ratchada (802 units, completed 2015), The Stage Mindscape (477 units, completed July 2024, ≈250m from MRT Huai Khwang Exit 1), Landmark @MRTA Station (Siamese Asset, 2,101 units, completed 2024), and the still-in-pipeline Aspire Huai Khwang (AP, 1,274 units, expected June 2027) — a materially denser comparable set than found for any of the framework's other four cards. The regulatory layer, while freehold (medium confidence), offers no quota disclosure and thus no structural-scarcity credit. Combined with highly dispersed original pricing and no stable district benchmark to compare against, the effective-scarcity test simply cannot be reliably run — leaving this project's scarcity score (5.8), while still inside the framework's required same-district consistency band (a 0.2 gap versus Life Asoke Rama 9, within the ≤0.5 threshold), resting on distinctly weaker underlying evidence.

At the developer level, ESTAR is an unambiguously small Thai micro-cap: market-cap estimates conflict across two sources (roughly THB 954M-1.26bn), the 52-week share price range is only THB 0.17-0.27, and Sansiri's market cap is roughly 27x ESTAR's — a completely different scale bracket than the framework's other published cases (AP, Sansiri). But the financial trajectory needs a correction against an easy-to-form wrong impression: ESTAR's trough was FY2023 (revenue THB 1,316M, down 27.7%; net income just THB 29.93M, down 41.5%), FY2024 already recovered to revenue THB 1,918M / net income THB 34.1M, and the most recent FY2025 results show revenue of THB 2,054M (up 7.12%) and net income sharply up to THB 126.4M (up 270.6%) — a clear and substantial recovery; citing only the 2023 trough would systematically understate the company's current financial position, and this card weighs the most recent year accordingly. No TRIS Rating record could be found in either English or Thai searches, listed as "unconfirmed." Buyers should also note a developer-level delivery signal: two independent Pantip buyer threads allege that ESTAR's other project, "The Breeze" (Naradhiwat Rajanagarindra Rd, 37 floors, 294 units), suffered delivery delays against the contracted timeline and common-area specifications below what was advertised, with buyers organizing but reporting no resolution to date — this is forum-sourced buyer allegation, not a regulatory finding or court record, and is inherently medium-low confidence, but two independent sources corroborating into a consistent pattern, rather than a single isolated post, warrants more weight in the developer dimension than a typical unconfirmed single signal (hence a −0.3 deduction here, larger than a generic single-source deduction). A separate portfolio-summary scrape claiming "ESTAR: 7 condo projects, ~2,200 units, 100% completed, 90% sold" also surfaced two project names that appear not to be actual ESTAR developments; this card judges that scrape contaminated and does not cite it. No abandoned-project record was found for the ESTAR group, and this project itself (as distinct from The Breeze) has no building-permit or EIA revocation or existing litigation on record — all five eligibility-gate checks pass.

The exit side discloses the most "unconfirmed" figures and data limitations of any dimension in this card, and secondary-market data for this project is extremely thin. On secondary-market liquidity, within the trailing 12-month observation window (as of 2026-08-04), only a single confirmed transaction was found, well thinner than any of the framework's other four cards, making annual turnover impossible to estimate reasonably; current active listings vary across platforms at 5, 13, and 16, against 383 total units implying roughly 1.3%-4.2%, but the sources diverge by 3.2x (above the 2x threshold), so per the rules this card reports only the range without claiming a precise midpoint. The estimated asking-to-transaction premium (comparing the higher blended asking of ≈THB 3.87M against the sole confirmed transaction of THB 3.2M, roughly +21%) is this card's own derivation rather than a platform's official aggregate statistic, distinctly lower confidence than the XT Phayathai card's officially-sourced figure, though still deducted per the framework's rule with explicit low-confidence flagging. What can be said with more confidence: the Thai-buyer quantum test shows a next-buyer household-income threshold of only ≈1.15x Bangkok's average household income — a low multiple, implying a comparatively wide local buyer pool — but this "locally affordable" advantage is substantially diluted by 5,000+ corridor competing units simultaneously diverting demand, combined with the pricing and data-quality weaknesses described above. This project's Thai-buyer total (5.4) and foreign-buyer total (5.2) both land in T5 — not the result of any single dimension's error, but the combined effect of a low capital-appreciation score (4.0), stacked exit-side deductions (a single confirmed transaction, source-divergent listing ratio, and the newly triggered visa boundary risk), and comparatively weak developer transparency.

◆ Conclusion

Highlights & caveats

✦ Highlights
  • Completed and immediately transferable (structure/architecture/systems 100%, shown as of 2024-09-21) — no off-plan delivery risk
  • Thai-buyer quantum threshold is only ≈1.15x Bangkok's average household income — a low multiple, implying a comparatively wide local buyer pool
  • Developer ESTAR's most recent FY2025 results show a clear recovery (revenue +7.12%, net income +270.6%) — the 2023 trough has passed, not an ongoing decline case
  • The sole confirmed transaction's per-sqm price (≈THB 118,500/sqm) is relatively low for this corridor, suggesting a friendlier entry point if the price holds
  • No building-permit/EIA revocation or existing litigation found for this project itself; all five eligibility-gate checks pass
⚠ Caveats
  • The representative unit's registered price clears the THB 3M visa threshold by only ≈6.7% — this card triggers the "visa-threshold boundary risk" deduction: a 15-20% price decline could structurally remove the foreign buyer pool
  • The same corridor already has 5,000+ confirmed competing units (including pipeline supply), and the city plan is actively upzoning toward higher density — both the scarcity audit and capital appreciation momentum (4.0, low) are clearly under pressure
  • Only a single confirmed transaction was found in the trailing 12 months — extremely thin secondary-market data, with low confidence in both the turnover and asking-discount estimates
  • This project's original pricing diverges enormously across sources (THB 1.99-4.95M), and no Foreign/Thai Quota price split was found — low valuation confidence
  • Developer ESTAR is a distinctly small, thinly-traded micro-cap (roughly 1/27th of Sansiri's market cap), with no TRIS Rating found
  • Two independent Pantip threads allege delivery delays and common-area spec shortfalls on the developer's other project, "The Breeze" — not this project, and not regulator-confirmed, but still a developer-level credibility concern
  • Foreign quota utilization is undisclosed; no walkable access to any of the framework's three benchmark international hospitals or a verified top-tier international school was found

The Diplomat 39

Verified by an actual Google Maps route query, the project sits about 270-280 meters from BTS Phrom Phong station (a 4-minute walk, through the Sukhumvit 39 soi, right at the EmQuartier mall frontage) — nearly double the developer's own claim of 150 meters, but still within a reasonable walking catchment for a prime Bangkok location, not one of the extreme exaggeration cases this framework has previously uncovered. This project's real pressure lies not in its location but in its position as a Bangkok high-end product line: the representative unit (a 54 sqm 1BR, currently asking around THB 17,000,000) implies, using the framework's coefficient, a Thai buyer household monthly-income threshold of about THB 238,000, or 6.1x Bangkok's average household income — a structurally narrow local buyer pool. Compounding this, developer Raimon Land (SET: RML) was given a CB caution sign twice by the Stock Exchange of Thailand in 2025, has posted losses for four consecutive fiscal years, has seen shareholders' equity fall below 50% of paid-up capital, and the project's gross rental yield of about 3.2% falls well short of its own mortgage rate — multiple pressures appearing at once are the main reason this card's total score sits clearly below this framework's earlier pilot cards.

◆ Economics

10-Year Holding Cost Comparison

Both Thai and foreign buyers are based on the same 54 sqm 1BR @ THB 17,000,000 — no quota-split pricing data could be found for this project, so both buyer types share the same entry price. ◆ Deep Narrative

Project Deep-Dive Summary

The Diplomat 39 sits on Sukhumvit Soi 39 in Bangkok's Watthana district, developed by Raimon Land (SET: RML, formerly KPN Land), a single 31-storey residential tower with 156 units, completed June 2019, with the developer's own claim of "move-in ready." This project sits in the high-price band (THB 10 million and above), not the same cohort as this framework's previously verified generic/mid-price-band Bangkok condo samples (THB 3-8 million) — many of this card's lower scores reflect the product structure of this specific price band, not a defect in the building itself. Basic facts such as the completion date, total unit count, and tenure show good data consistency, without the multiple-readings-side-by-side problem common in some earlier samples.

Connectivity is one of the few dimensions verified with a first-hand tool rather than simply citing aggregator-platform text: this card queried Google Maps' route-planning tool directly for the actual walking route from "Phrom Phong BTS Station" to "The Diplomat 39, 7 Sukhumvit 39," and the system returned two routes of 270 meters and 280 meters, both with a 4-minute walking time, running a short stretch along Sukhumvit Road before turning into Sukhumvit 39 soi and continuing straight, passing the front of the EmQuartier mall. This measured result shows a clear gap against the developer's own claim of 150 meters — roughly half again as much as the actual measurement — but still falls well short of the most extreme marketing exaggeration this framework has previously uncovered (one project claimed 130 meters when the actual routed distance was 600-650 meters). The same routing tool also confirmed Samitivej Sukhumvit Hospital at about 1.2-1.4 km by car, consistent with the developer's own figure; but Bumrungrad (about 4.7 km) and BNH (about 7.1 km) are both, in fact, quite far, falling outside the framework's 2 km bonus threshold — a rebuttal of the intuitive assumption that "a prime Bangkok location means all three major hospitals are nearby."

This project shows a notable set of contradictory signals on the scarcity dimension. As a land layer, Sukhumvit Soi 39 itself is already close to built-out saturation, with only scattered small parcels found and known 2026-2028 new supply sitting in adjacent sois rather than on this soi — a relatively positive structural signal; but the product layer clearly loses points — multiple similarly-specced standard high-rise towers cluster on the same soi (Marque Sukhumvit 39, The XXXIX by Sansiri, etc.), and this project's own product is highly replicable, not a unique or scarce form. More critically, the effective-scarcity test fails: only three per-transaction data points were found for this project, spanning 2019 to 2024, a full 7 years, and the two from 2019 (317,307 and 272,091 THB/sqm) are actually higher than the one from 2024 (230,000 THB/sqm), a decline of roughly 19-27.5% over 5 years — actual transaction evidence does not support the intuitive "older means scarcer" narrative for this location; at the same time, the current asking price (314,815 THB/sqm) runs about 36.9% above the most recent actual transaction, two signals pointing in different directions side by side. The most reasonable reading is that this project's secondary market trades extremely thinly and the price-discovery mechanism itself is not very reliable, rather than a trend that can be summarized into a single clean story.

Developer financial condition is one of the core reasons for this card's low overall score. As a SET-listed company, Raimon Land's year-by-year loss sequence was fully traceable this time: five consecutive fiscal years of net losses from FY2021 through FY2025, widening from THB 453M to THB 1.21bn in FY2024 (the largest single-year loss in this sequence), narrowing to THB 930.1M in FY2025, but Q1 2026 (the latest figure obtained in this verification) still posting a loss of THB 147.68M — the loss trend has not reversed. More specifically, the Stock Exchange of Thailand placed a "CB" caution sign on RML's stock on 2025-03-03, citing "three consecutive years of losses," then added a second CB sign on 2025-11-17 after Q3 results showed shareholders' equity (THB 2.3bn) had fallen below the statutory 50% threshold of paid-up capital (THB 5.795bn) — an active exchange-level warning mechanism, considerably more specific and severe than the general wording of "financial concerns." RML also currently carries no credit rating: the original TRIS rating was voluntarily withdrawn at the company's own request in August 2021, and there has been no rating-agency coverage for over 5 years since, a concrete gap in rating transparency. The company is accelerating the disposal of its stake in the One City Centre office tower (jointly held with Mitsubishi Estate, worth about THB 8.4bn) to shore up liquidity, targeting completion in Q1 2026; the company has also publicly stated a goal of lifting the CB caution sign within 2025, but this verification found no follow-up confirming that goal was met on schedule. On the other hand, no permit revocation, EIA revocation, or construction-related litigation was found against either this project or the RML group, and the delivery record itself (14 residential projects completed since 2004) remains verifiable — these positive factors provide a partial offset in the score, avoiding over-penalization, but are not enough to reverse the overall conclusion of weak financial health.

On pricing and cashflow, this card explicitly resolves a gap that prior verification could not separate out: the project-wide asking range (THB 13.8M to 245M) spans every unit type, and prior verification could not pin down a 1BR-specific price floor; this time, an actual 1BR current-listing instance was found directly (54 sqm, about THB 17M), confirming that the 1BR floor sits above the project's overall lowest asking range. No Foreign/Thai Quota price-split data could be found for this project, so both buyer types use the same entry-price basis. Anchored to the most recent transaction (April 2024, 230,000 THB/sqm), the current asking price runs a premium of about +36.9%; anchored instead to the higher 2019 transaction (317,307 THB/sqm), the premium is only about −0.8% — the sheer gap between these two readings is itself evidence of an extremely thin secondary market, and this card conservatively adopts the more recent reading. On rent, converted from a same-size FazWaz listing rent (USD 1,360/month), gross rental yield comes to only about 3.23% — a genuine feature of this project's high-end positioning: absolute price and absolute rent have not scaled proportionally, leaving the yield clearly low. The Thai buyer's gross yield of 3.23% against a 6.5% mortgage rate shows a deeply negative spread of −3.27 percentage points; the foreign buyer's net yield (about 2.75% after 15% withholding) against opportunity-cost benchmark two (USD 5.75%/year) leaves a gap of −3.0 percentage points. The 10-year holding cost comparison shows that under a 0% appreciation scenario, the Thai buyer's 10-year net loss (about THB 5.111M) already exceeds the full amount of their equity outlay plus fees — the direct economic consequence of the negative spread compounding over a long hold under high leverage.

The core contradiction on the exit side is this: the quantum test shows a next local buyer's household-income threshold as high as 6.09x Bangkok's average, reflecting this project's position as a high-price-band product aimed at a target buyer structurally different from the framework's other mid-price-band samples — general local first-time buyers and upgraders barely constitute a statistically meaningful buyer pool, an inevitable result of product positioning, not a defect in the building. At the same time, listing figures diverge by nearly 4x across platforms (45 to 176), the largest data-quality gap encountered in this card's verification process, and the listing-to-transacted discount of about 26.9% also exceeds the framework's 15% threshold; foreign-quota utilisation is entirely undisclosed, with not even the most basic qualitative signal found, the most blank quota-disclosure case encountered. This project's representative-unit registered price (THB 17M) is comfortably above the THB 10M no-MOTS-certificate threshold, a procedural simplification for foreign self-use buyers and one of this project's few positive institutional signals; but this procedural simplification cannot offset a weak local buyer pool, a weak yield, and a developer financial condition with no confirmed improvement — together, these three factors are the concrete reason this card's total scores (Thai 5.4, foreign 5.3) both land in the T5 tier.

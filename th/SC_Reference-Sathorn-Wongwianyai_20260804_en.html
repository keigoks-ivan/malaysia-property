Reference Sathorn-Wongwianyai

An independent Google Maps route query puts BTS Wongwian Yai station at roughly 160 meters, a 2-minute walk on "mostly flat" ground — the single most solidly verified connectivity figure in this framework's pilot run to date. Marketing claims 130 meters; the ~23% gap is ordinary variance, not the kind of distance fabrication seen elsewhere. But the project's own name deserves honest treatment: it sits on the Chao Phraya's west bank in Khlong San district's Samre subdistrict, genuinely not in Sathorn. The BTS crossing to the CBD is fast, but road-based bridge friction is real. Tenure confidence is moderate-high but rests on no single primary title document; a Tokyo Tatemono institutional joint venture and Samitivej Thonburi Hospital in the same subdistrict are genuine positives, set against a complete absence of any transacted anchor, at least five corridor comps supplying simultaneously, and a softening asking-price trend — a compounding set of unknowns for a 10-year exit.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep-Dive Summary

Reference Sathorn-Wongwianyai sits on Soi Krung Thonburi 2 in Samre subdistrict, Khlong San district, Bangkok, developed by SC Asset Three Co., Ltd. (SC3) — a joint venture between SC Asset Corporation PCL (SET: SC, holding 55%) and Japanese institutional developer Tokyo Tatemono Asia Pte. Ltd. The project launched in May 2022 with a value of roughly THB 4,000m, completed in June 2024, and comprises 819 units in total (815 residential + 4 commercial/retail), with Tower A at 32 floors / 216 units and Tower B at 51 floors / 599 units. The single most important finding in this card's research is a positive confirmation of the connectivity distance. An independent Google Maps route query shows BTS Wongwian Yai station at only about 160 meters, a 2-minute walk on "mostly flat" ground; marketing claims 130 meters, a gap of roughly 23%, within ordinary measurement variance — this is categorically different from the same series' XT Phayathai case, where a claimed "130 meters" turned out to be an actual 600-650 meters on verification. This project is the single most solidly verified connectivity figure in this framework's pilot run to date. One honest caveat: the map pin used in this verification was the listing's approximate coordinate rather than a surveyed building entrance, so this remains a good-faith route check rather than a physical site survey.

But the project's own name deserves honest treatment. The name "Sathorn-Wongwianyai" implies a geographic connection to the Sathorn business district; on verification, that connection is a defensible marketing stretch rather than a geographic fact — the project actually sits on the Chao Phraya's west bank, in Khlong San district's Samre subdistrict, genuinely not in Sathorn. The BTS crossing is genuinely fast — one stop to Krung Thon Buri (2 minutes, 5-minute headway), reaching Saphan Taksin within a few more stops — so the rail commute itself holds up, and "a BTS gateway to Sathorn" has some real basis. But road-side bridge friction is a separate matter, and it's quantified clearly: 5.8 km / 20 minutes by car to BNH Hospital (27 minutes by BTS); 10.4 km / 43 minutes by car to Bumrungrad (48 minutes by BTS+MRT). This isn't a distance problem — it's a bridge-bottleneck problem. Bangkok has a limited number of bridges across the Chao Phraya, and peak-hour traffic concentrates on them, so even a modest geographic distance can translate into a meaningfully longer real commute.

Amenities present a clear split between genuine positive and genuine gap. Samitivej Thonburi Hospital (one of the framework's three named international hospitals) is a real plus for this project: its address at 337 Somdet Phra Chao Taksin Road sits in the same Samre subdistrict as the project, and reportedly runs a free shuttle to BTS Wongwianyai — this is a combination ("a top-3 international hospital in the same subdistrict") not seen in any of this series' other four cards. International schools, however, are a clear weak point: NIST and Bangkok Prep are on the Sukhumvit side, and St Andrews International School Sathorn, despite its name, actually sits on the CBD side of the river, subject to the same crossing friction; only Wat Sawetchat School and Thai Sikh International School Junior Campus are tied to this project's immediate subdistrict, neither top international tier. For a cross-border buyer with school-age children who prioritises international education, this is a genuine negative, not merely a data gap.

On the developer side, the SC3 joint-venture structure deserves elaboration: SC Asset holds 55%, in a joint venture with Tokyo Tatemono Asia Pte. Ltd. (a Japanese institutional developer), a structure confirmed in SC Asset's audited 2024 Form 56-1 One Report and corroborated by the ONEP EIA registry. SC Asset itself carries a TRIS BBB+/Stable rating, below the Life-series developer AP Thailand's A/Stable, but FY2025 saw both revenue and profit grow (revenue THB 20,233m, net profit THB 1,533m), with non-residential business already exceeding 20% of the profit mix, showing some business diversification. Per the framework's rules, Tokyo Tatemono's institutional-JV nature entitles this project to a +0.5 institutional-JV bonus on top of SC Asset Corporation's 7.0 baseline. On compliance, no permit revocation, EIA revocation, or live litigation was found for this project itself or the SC Asset group, though ONEP and Bangkok Metropolitan Administration permit records could not be checked directly this time — "no adverse findings" should be understood as clean within the search scope, not confirmed clean. One entirely unrelated individual-case signal did surface: a different SC Asset landed-housing product line (Grand Bangkok Boulevard) was the subject of 2016 Thai media reports on termite-damage-type construction-quality complaints — a different product line and project, not affecting this project's gate determination or developer score, disclosed here only for the reader's reference. Tenure also demands honest treatment: no single primary-source title document explicitly stating freehold could be found for this project; the determination rests on the convergence of multiple independent sources, the absence of any leasehold claim, and the fact that SC Asset's annual report itemises its few leasehold arrangements without including this project — three pieces of evidence together, with confidence set at "moderate-high" rather than "confirmed."

Pricing and the exit side are where this card's information gaps concentrate most heavily. This project asks roughly THB 140,000-160,000/sqm, with one market snapshot showing USD 4,208/sqm, down 1.3% month-on-month and 1.9% year-on-year (early 2026) — the only "project-specific and unambiguously negative" price-momentum signal this framework has obtained to date, unlike other cases that could only extrapolate from Bangkok's overall macro backdrop. More critically, no transacted anchor could be established at all for this project: extensive searching found no transaction-by-transaction record anywhere, a Thai transaction-data specialist site, thelist.group/realist, returned HTTP 403, and the project completed only about two years ago, so its resale history is inherently thin. Corridor comparables Ideo Sathorn-Wongwian Yai (≈USD 4,056/sqm) and The Rich Sathorn Wongwian Yai (≈USD 4,169/sqm) offer a comparison basis but, per the framework's explicit instruction, may not substitute as this project's own valuation anchor — this project's valuation score therefore reflects "a data-gap discount for a totally absent anchor," not any substantive premium or discount determination. The exit side is equally blank: this project is the only one of this framework's four existing cards with no transaction-by-transaction record found at all; foreign-quota utilisation is undisclosed and tenure lacks a primary title confirmation — two structural unknowns coexisting — compounded by at least five competing projects supplying the same corridor simultaneously, together constituting a deeper exit-side uncertainty than any of the other four cases. Even so, this project's connectivity and developer dimensions are genuinely better-verified than the other four cards, and the final scores (Thai 5.8, Foreign 5.7, both T4) reflect exactly this coexistence of "verified strengths" and "information-gap risk."

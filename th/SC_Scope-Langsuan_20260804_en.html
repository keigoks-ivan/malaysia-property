Scope Langsuan

This card's quantum test comes close to failing on the Thai-buyer side: the representative 83 sqm 1BR is asking THB 44,000,000, which converts to a household monthly-income threshold of roughly THB 616,000 — about 15.8x Bangkok's average household income (THB 39,100). The local buyer pool is, mathematically, close to nil. That is not a verdict on the building's quality — it is evidence of the product's positioning: from design through pricing to marketing, Scope Langsuan has always targeted globally allocated capital, not Bangkok households. An independent geocoded distance check puts the project roughly 210-260 metres from BTS Chidlom station — farther than the "140 metres" headlined on the developer's own site, sitting near the upper end of the developer's own quoted 120-230m range, and enough to place it in the framework's Tier 7 (≤400m) rather than Tier 8 (≤200m). Location scarcity beside Lumphini Park (8.0) and developer financial standing (7.0, SC Asset: TRIS BBB+) are this card's two highest-scoring dimensions, but structurally negative leveraged cashflow and thin exit evidence drag holding cashflow and exit down to this card's weakest points.

Per the framework's tier table, 220-260m already exceeds the Tier 8 (≤200m) cutoff and falls into Tier 7 (≤400m), starting at 7.0. Item adjustments: Bumrungrad Hospital (one of the framework's three recognised flagship international hospitals) sits roughly 1.1km away in a straight line by independent geocoding, with an estimated actual driving/walking route of roughly 1.3-1.8km, within the ≤2km threshold, +0.3; no fully shaded skywalk connection to this project was found (the skywalk network around Ploenchit/Chidlom runs along Ploenchit Road connecting Central Embassy, Central Chidlom, and Gaysorn, but no evidence shows it extending down Lang Suan lane itself, and third-party sources explicitly note "no direct skywalk connection" for this project), no bonus applied; Regent's International School Langsuan Campus sits on the same lane (65 Soi Langsuan), walkable, but a second international school's precise distance within 5km could not be independently confirmed (St Andrews International School Sathorn requires a transit/taxi transfer, with one source citing roughly a 20-25 minute drive, outside walking range), so the "≥2 international schools" bonus is not applied, though this positive factor is noted in the narrative; no mall-level retail direct connection was found (Central Chidlom and similar malls require walking to the intersection first before entering the mall circulation), no bonus applied. Net: 7.0+0.3=7.3.

On balance: CBD-core starts at 5.5, minus weak catalyst evidence and comparable competing supply (-0.5), plus the positive project-level price movement actually observed (+0.3, applying equally to Thai and foreign buyers since it is the same project-level figure) — netting out to the general "New CBD" level of 5.0.

Holding Cashflow12% · Local vs Foreign
Local2.6 Foreign2.9

This is this card's lowest-scoring dimension, and the reasoning is directly tied to compressed ultra-luxury yields — the calculation needs to be shown in full. Rent figures themselves vary enormously: multiple aggregator platforms (livinginsider, bangkokresidential, thailand-property) currently show 83-85 sqm units asking THB 140,000-230,000/month; REm Magazine, citing the developer's own claim, states this project has "the highest rent per sqm in Bangkok," THB 2,500/sqm/month, converting to roughly THB 207,500/month for 83 sqm; but a separate, lower-range figure puts actual achieved rent, or a more conservative estimate, at THB 45,000-65,000/month (this particular source has other clear factual errors about this project's basic specifications, so confidence is low, included for reference only). This card's position: applying the same standard of scepticism already used earlier for the "140 metres" and "new-high" developer claims, the conservative low-to-mid end of the range is preferred as the working assumption — on one hand, achieved gross yields on ultra-luxury assets globally run structurally well below mainstream residential (capital is chasing preservation, not cashflow), and on the other, Hipflat's own trend commentary notes that transacted prices at this project have typically closed roughly 19% below the original asking price, indicating this project's listing figures already show a pattern of running systematically high — a pattern that plausibly extends to rental listings as well. This card uses THB 58,000/month (a representative figure from the low-to-mid end of the range) as the working assumption for both scoring and the 10-year cost table, implying a gross yield of roughly 1.6% — at the low end of the compressed yields typical of ultra-luxury assets globally; the full THB 45,000-230,000/month source range is disclosed in full on the page, so readers can adjust based on their own view of how much to trust the developer-linked figures. Thai buyer: gross yield of 1.6% against a mortgage rate of 6.5% gives a spread of -4.9pp — an asset a mortgage simply cannot service, forcing a leveraged buyer to fund the negative cashflow from other income over the long run. Foreign buyer: in an all-cash scenario, gross yield of 1.6%, and after 15% non-resident withholding, net yield ≈1.36%, a gap of -4.4pp against opportunity-cost benchmark two (USD 5.75%/year) — also deeply negative; but because there is no mortgage negative-spread drag, the foreign buyer's annual cash outflow is far smaller than the Thai buyer's (only the opportunity cost that could otherwise have been earned is foregone, not an actual cash shortfall), which is the core reason the foreign-buyer score (2.9) runs slightly above the Thai-buyer score (2.6).

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep-Dive Summary

Scope Langsuan sits on Soi Lang Suan beside Lumphini Park in Bangkok, developed by SCOPE Co., Ltd. (a joint venture 90%-held by SC Asset Corporation PCL [SET: SC], with an individual shareholder holding the remaining 10%, established 2017), launched June 2019 with a total project value of roughly THB8.4bn, completed 2023, comprising 158 residential units plus 1 retail unit in a single 34-storey freehold tower. The representative 83 sqm 1BR is currently asking THB44,000,000, converting to roughly THB530,000/sqm — 5.5x the ceiling of the framework's chosen "1BR cross-case representative unit" definition (a liquid mainstream product below THB8m in the rail-walkable band). That gap is not a data error; it's a signal that from the very first number, this card is measuring a product built for globally allocated capital rather than Bangkok households.

This project's most important research finding was rechecking the "140 metres from BTS Chidlom" figure headlined on the developer's own site. That number lands exactly at the low end of the developer's own separately-quoted "120-230 metres" range — itself a suspicious pattern of marketing selection this card chose not to take at face value. Using an independently recorded coordinate system on OpenStreetMap to geocode both BTS Chidlom station and the Scope Langsuan building separately produces a straight-line distance of roughly 215 metres; given that Lang Suan Road runs straight from the Ploenchit/Chidlom intersection to the end of the lane with no detour required, the actual walking-route distance is reasonably estimated at 220-260 metres. That figure sits closer to the upper end of the developer's own quoted range (230m) than to the headlined 140m, and also closer to the figures commonly cited by several third-party aggregator sites. Per the framework's tier table, this distance already exceeds the Tier 8 (≤200m) cutoff and falls into Tier 7 (≤400m) — not a catastrophic gap, but enough to show that even at a top-tier developer's flagship project, marketing figures still need independent verification rather than being taken at face value.

The real core story of this project is that the quantum test comes close to failing outright. The framework's quantum-test logic was originally built to measure whether "the next buyer's household income can service this purchase price" — a logic that still produces a meaningful multiple on cards whose representative-unit price sits far below this project's (roughly the THB3.2m-5.9m band). But this project's representative unit is asking THB44,000,000, converting to a monthly-income threshold of roughly THB616,000 — roughly 15.8x Bangkok's average household income (THB39,100). This is not a matter of "credit being somewhat tight" — it is that the local buyer pool is, mathematically, entirely absent. No Bangkok household services this purchase price through a normal mortgage, and that fact has nothing to do with the project's quality, location, or the developer's strength — it measures that this product was never designed, from its earliest planning, to target Bangkok households. Meanwhile, the quantum test tells an entirely different story on the foreign-buyer side: this project already has transaction-by-transaction records spanning more than two years through late 2025 (THB45m, THB85m, THB95m), alongside a cumulative sold rate reported at roughly 80% — proving a functioning, real-money global cash-buyer pool exists. This is precisely why this card deliberately lets the foreign-buyer exit score (5.5) run slightly above the Thai-buyer score (5.1) — not because this project favours foreign buyers, but because the penalty applying to the local buyer pool simply does not apply to a global buyer pool with proven existence. Readers should not read this inversion as "this project's quality suits foreign buyers better."

On scarcity, the Langsuan/Chidlom location beside Lumphini Park genuinely is one of Bangkok's traditional top-tier addresses, and the framework's anchor table assigns it the highest starting point citywide, 8.0. The three-layer supply audit shows this location's scarcity has real substance, alongside a side that needs to be faced honestly: Soi Lang Suan itself, a short lane, is genuinely short on complete large plots available for consolidated development; but "Langsuan" as a marketing location brand has still seen a run of recent nearby launches — NIMIT Langsuan (itself marketed as "the last remaining freehold plot"), the Sindhorn Residence/Park View series, Muniq Langsuan, Langsuan Ville, and Q Langsuan — showing supply at the brand level has not truly run dry. That tension ultimately shows up in the score: starting at 8.0, the land/product-layer reservations offset the strong pass on the effective-scarcity check (solid, well-above-typical-Bangkok-condo price-premium evidence in actual transactions), holding the net at 8.0 — the highest of this card's seven dimensions, but a high score arrived at through a genuine audit, not simply lifted from the location anchor table.

Holding cashflow is this card's lowest-scoring dimension (2.6 Thai buyer, 2.9 foreign buyer), and also the one most in need of showing its calculation honestly. Rental-listing figures vary enormously: multiple platforms currently show asking rents typically in the THB140,000-230,000/month range; the developer (relayed via REm Magazine) claims this project has "the highest rent per sqm in Bangkok," converting to roughly THB207,500/month; a separate, more conservative estimate puts the figure at THB45,000-65,000/month. This card chose the low-to-mid end of the range (THB58,000/month) as its conservative working assumption for both scoring and the 10-year cost table — for the same reason this card already applied scepticism to the "140-metre" figure earlier: achieved gross yields on ultra-luxury assets globally run structurally well below mainstream residential (capital is chasing preservation, not cashflow), and Hipflat's own trend commentary notes that transacted prices at this project typically close roughly 19% below the original asking price, indicating listing figures here already run systematically high. Working from that assumption, gross yield comes to roughly 1.6%; carried into the 10-year cost table, a Thai buyer operating under the framework's standard 80% leverage assumption is still down roughly THB4.64m after 10 years even if prices hold to the base-case 3%/year appreciation — not the normal story of leverage amplifying returns, but leverage on a near-zero-yield asset being steadily eaten alive by the mortgage rate. The foreign buyer (all-cash), without that negative-spread drag, is positive across all three scenarios, at the cost of a capital base roughly 4.75x the Thai buyer's own cash outlay — a fundamentally different economics.

On the developer side, SCOPE Co., Ltd.'s ownership structure (SC Asset 90% + an individual shareholder 10%) is explicitly different from the same-series Reference Sathorn-Wongwianyai project's SC Asset Three (55% + Tokyo Tatemono, an institutional joint venture) — this project's framework "institutional joint venture +0.5" bonus does not apply, because a 10% individual shareholder does not constitute an institutional foreign co-developer. The developer score therefore holds at SC Asset's own baseline of 7.0, with no additional bonus. SC Asset's group finances are healthy (TRIS BBB+, FY2025 revenue THB20.233bn, net profit THB1.533bn, consistent with the existing same-series card's verified figures), and no permit revocation, EIA revocation, or live litigation was found against either this project or the SCOPE group. Taken together across seven dimensions, this project's final scores (5.9 Thai buyer, 6.0 foreign buyer, both T4) present a genuine and unusual picture: location, developer, and visa process are all near-perfect, while exit and cashflow run deeply strained because of how extreme the product's positioning is — which is exactly what an ultra-luxury product should honestly look like when run through a scoring framework built around mainstream Bangkok condominiums.

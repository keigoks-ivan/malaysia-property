◆ Deep Narrative

Project Deep-Dive Summary

Nue Evo Ari sits in Sam Sen Nai, Phaya Thai, Bangkok, on Ari Soi 1 (Phahonyothin Soi 7), developed by Noble Development (SET-listed), a single 42-storey residential tower with a separate 7-storey parking structure, completed January 2025. The total unit count itself diverges: the developer's own site states 488 units, a higher-confidence primary source, while multiple aggregators consistently show 497. A separately circulating "39 units" figure was traced to a single platform's for-sale listing count, not the project's total, and this card explicitly excludes it so it does not corrupt any turnover or listing-ratio denominator. Not even this basic scale figure has a single authoritative source, foreshadowing the data limitations this project faces on both the valuation and exit dimensions.

The walking distance to BTS Ari (N5, Sukhumvit Line) also carries multiple readings: the developer's own site, a Thai-language route description specifying "via Exit 2," and multiple aggregators together support about 300 meters, the best-supported reading of the three; a 200-meter reading (some listing metadata) and a 350-meter reading (a Thai property forum) sit at either end, giving a 200-350m spread, and no source provided an actual traced walking route for this project — all are marketing copy or forum restatements. This project fits neither of two distance-dispute patterns seen elsewhere in this series — one prior project advertised 130m and was actually 600-650m, a real misrepresentation; another's marketed 130m checked out at 160m on a live route query, ordinary variance — this project is neither, it is simply unverified. More importantly, even if the 300-meter distance holds, BTS Ari is a single-line station with no interchange: neither the MRT Orange Line nor the Brown Line's planned route passes through Ari, and the Brown Line's nearest interchange with the BTS Sukhumvit Line is several stops away at Kasetsart University. The short walking distance therefore does not translate into hub value, the core reason this project's connectivity score (6.5) sits below what comparable short-walk cases might suggest, and it explains why solid distance evidence does not translate into a high connectivity score.

The scarcity dimension shows the sharpest gap between this project's marketing claim and the verified facts. The developer positions this project as "one of the last remaining plots in the heart of Ari," but verification shows the Phahonyothin/Soi Ari 1 corridor has produced four projects in three years: Noble Development's own Noble Around Ari (39 floors/611 units, completed 2023), this project (42 floors, 2025), Via Ari (completing 2026), and Rhythm Phahol-Ari (53 floors/809 units). Land supply is far from exhausted; only the interior side sois retain low-rise form, the classic "corridor high, interior low" pattern, and this is direct evidence that both the land and product layers of the scarcity audit fail. Ari-area pricing data itself diverges by more than 1.9x — Vurel's median asking figure (THB 124,340/sqm) actually pools Ari together with the weaker Saphan Khwai stretch, not a clean Ari-specific number, while Bamboo Routes gives a wider THB 160,000-250,000/sqm range. This project (~178,000-192,000 THB/sqm) and the same-corridor Via Ari (~227,000 THB/sqm) sit toward the upper end of that range, but without a reliable median to anchor to, no clear scarcity premium can be established for this project — the effective-scarcity test clearly fails.

Noble Development's FY2025 results present a notable pair of contradictory signals: full-year revenue of THB 6.93bn, down 36.97% y/y, a materially larger decline than the sector-wide credit-tightening headwind seen elsewhere; yet net profit rose 37.44% to THB 593.14M, with a net margin of about 8.6%, possibly reflecting cost control or project-specific recognition timing, though the exact cause could not be fully verified. Presale momentum was similarly volatile: down 59% y/y in H1 2025, then up 67% y/y in Q2 2026 with FY2026 targets maintained, suggesting some stabilization, though a single quarter's rebound is not yet enough to confirm the trend has reversed. On credit, TRIS holds the company rating at BBB with senior unsecured at BBB−, and a 2025 outlook most likely Negative — clearly below Sansiri (BBB+) and AP Thailand (A/Stable). No permit revocation, EIA revocation, or litigation was found against this project itself; but the developer group's earlier project, Noble Unite (today renamed Noble Revo Silom), failed EIA review after launching sales in 2011, refunded all deposits in 2012, and relaunched about a year later — an EIA failure before construction, categorically different from the Ashton Asoke case ("a permit issued and the project completed, then revoked"), and this does not trigger this framework's developer cap. But it does evidence a practice at this developer of launching sales before EIA approval is secured. Per this framework's cross-card calibration principle, this project's developer score is held at 6.0, consistent with the already-published Noble Revo Silom card.

The valuation and cashflow dimensions together expose this project's most fundamental problem: the data is simply insufficient to support a precise score. This project has no actually-transacted sale on record at all — only secondary-market asking prices are available, and even those show meaningful floor-level dispersion (the identical 30.2 sqm spec listed at THB 4,644,000 on a low floor, noticeably below the blended average of THB 5,376,000). Valuation therefore cannot compute a meaningful premium or discount percentage, and instead uses the framework's conservative mid-band default of 5.0. Cashflow similarly lacks any sourced area-yield figure; the rental yield relies entirely on asking rent divided by asking price, and this card conservatively takes the lower-middle of that range, arriving at a deliberately conservative gross yield (4.8%), leaving the Thai buyer with a materially negative rate spread (−1.7pp). Notably, the Thai buyer's cashflow score (4.0) is lower than the foreign buyer's (4.1) — not a calculation error, but because the Thai buyer carries the real cost of a ~6.5% mortgage rate on top of an already-low yield, while the foreign buyer is a structural all-cash purchaser with no interest drag; even though the foreign net yield still trails the opportunity-cost benchmark, that relative shortfall is smaller than the Thai buyer's leverage cost. The exit side shows the same pattern of data limitation: the quantum ratio (1.93x Bangkok's average household income) is noticeably tight, the listing ratio can only be reported as a range because two aggregators diverge by 2.3x, and quota utilisation is undisclosed anywhere, landing in the "unverified" tier — this chain of data gaps compounding with negative signals is what explains why this project's final scores (Thai 5.4, Foreign 5.4) both land in T5, and the underlying cause is less "verification uncovered a confirmed flaw" than "verification still leaves too much unconfirmed."

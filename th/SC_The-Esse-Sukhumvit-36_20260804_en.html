Thonglor / Sukhumvit 36 · Bangkok ◆ FREEHOLD CONDOMINIUM ◆ As of 2026-08-04

The Esse Sukhumvit 36

A live Google Maps route check puts this project about 110 meters from BTS Thong Lo, an 1-minute walk, corroborated by DDproperty's independently stated ~110 meters — the station is essentially at the doorstep. But that connectivity strength sits on top of one of Bangkok's steepest entry thresholds: the representative unit's monthly-income threshold runs about 5x Bangkok's average household income, and the local buyer pool is structurally thin. Quota-labeled transaction data reveals a rare same-month price inversion between Foreign Quota and Thai Quota units in the same building, and the listing ratio sits far above the healthy threshold. The Singha Estate x Hongkong Land joint venture is a clear positive, but the group's FY2025 net margin was only 1.76% — the cashflow and exit pressure here deserve as much of a buyer's attention as the connectivity advantage.

This card relies on a live Google Maps route query: from "BTS Thong Lor Station" to the mapped location of "THE ESSE Sukhumvit 36," the walking route returns 110 meters, a 1-minute walk, via Sukhumvit Road and Soi Yanatha — matching DDproperty's 110m exactly, and sitting between Hipflat's own route (180m, measured from Exit 2 rather than the station centroid) and the marketing figure (20m, likely measured from a specific gate rather than the station itself). All three collectively support a genuinely short walk, placing this project at the Tier 8 threshold (≤200m), baseline 8.0. ◆ Deep Narrative

Project Deep-Dive Summary

The Esse Sukhumvit 36 sits at the corner of Sukhumvit Road and Soi Sukhumvit 36, in the heart of Bangkok's Thonglor commercial district, developed as a joint venture between Singha Estate (SET-listed) and Hongkong Land (part of the Jardine Matheson group) — a single 43-storey residential tower on about 2 rai of land, completed in November 2020, with the international-tier contractor Bouygues Thai as main builder. The total unit count turned up an internal contradiction on one source: the official figure and most independent sources, including FazWaz and Bouygues Thai, consistently confirm 338 units; but Hipflat's own page shows 575 in its header and 530 in its body text, two figures that contradict each other and the official count. This card treats that as a platform-side error, uses 338 as the denominator throughout, and discloses the discrepancy explicitly so readers do not mistake a platform error for fact.

On connectivity, this project offers well-corroborated distance evidence: a live Google Maps route query places it about 110 meters, a 1-minute walk, from BTS Thong Lo station, via Sukhumvit Road and Soi Yanatha, exactly matching DDproperty's independently stated 110 meters. That reading sits between the marketing figure (20 meters, likely measured from a specific project gate) and Hipflat's own turn-by-turn directions (180 meters, measured from Exit 2), and this card relies on the station-to-project map route rather than taking either single-source figure at face value or splitting the difference into a false average. On education and healthcare, this project turned up Bangkok Prep (British curriculum, described as "right next to Thonglor station") and the Wells International School Thonglor campus, plus Samitivej Sukhumvit Hospital about 1.5km from the station with its own shuttle service — satisfying the framework's bonus conditions for both international healthcare and education coverage, though exact walking distances were not individually verified, so partial rather than full credit is applied.

The scarcity audit tells a different story from the connectivity story. The Thonglor/Ekkamai corridor continues to see new launches — XT 10 Ekkamai (Sansiri, 933 units, launching mid-2026), Rhythm Ekkamai (AP Thailand, about 180 meters from Thonglor Soi 10), and OKA HAUS Sukhumvit 36 (Sansiri, 1,178 units, within the same walkshed) — so the land layer fails the audit. This project's Singha Estate x Hongkong Land institutional-JV positioning does bring some product-layer differentiation, though its physical form remains a replicable standard high-rise tower. More importantly, this project appears to be the first case in this framework with quota-labeled per-transaction data (Hipflat explicitly tags each sale Foreign Quota or Thai Quota) — in principle a more rigorous valuation basis than other cards that can only cite unsplit mixed transactions. But the five size-comparable transactions diverge from each other by as much as 62%, and March 2026 shows a rare same-month, same-building inversion where a Foreign Quota transaction (232,631 THB/sqm) ran about 27.5% below a Thai Quota transaction (320,930 THB/sqm) — the opposite direction from the 10-25% Foreign Quota premium the framework generally expects. This card cannot rule out sub-type differences (38sqm vs. 43sqm), floor differences (19th vs. 17th floor), or single-transaction noise, and discloses the contradiction plainly rather than manufacturing a clean answer where none exists.

As a SET-listed developer, Singha Estate's FY2025 full-year results were fully obtainable and reconcilable: revenue THB 13.988bn, net profit THB 246M, net margin only 1.76%, notably thin relative to typical peers; 2026 first-quarter net profit recovered to THB 48.8M, though the absolute margin remains thin. TRIS Rating confirmed in May 2026 a corporate credit rating of BBB/Stable and a debenture rating of BBB-/Stable — investment grade, but clearly a notch below some peer developers' ratings. The joint venture with Hongkong Land, and the use of Bouygues Thai as main contractor, are the clearest positives on the developer dimension for this project — an international-tier commercial real estate group paired with an international-tier contractor, not a single Thai developer operating alone, which meaningfully offsets the concern raised by the thin net margin. No permit revocation, EIA revocation, or litigation was found against either this project or the Singha Estate group, a clean regulatory-compliance picture.

On pricing and cashflow, the representative unit (40.9 sqm 1BR, interpolated between the two official sub-types of 38.50 and 43.25 sqm) currently asks about THB 13,900,000, a premium of about +6.9% over the average of five comparable transactions (~318,150 THB/sqm). On rent, Hipflat's platform-average rent converts to about THB 48,000/month, consistent in magnitude with specific rental listings found (THB 50,000-55,000/month) — a solid cross-check — giving a gross rental yield of about 4.14%, corroborated by an independent search citing the platform's average annualized yield as "about 4%." That is the lowest gross yield this card has disclosed, a direct reflection of the structural reality that this is a high-price, luxury-tier product where rent does not scale proportionally with price. The Thai buyer's gross yield of 4.14% against a 6.5% mortgage rate produces a −2.4 percentage-point negative spread; the foreign buyer's net yield (about 3.52% after 15% withholding) against opportunity-cost benchmark two (USD 5.75%/year) leaves a gap of about −2.26 percentage points — both the widest this card has disclosed, and fundamentally the same underlying cause (a low gross yield) expressed through two different calculation methods, not two separate problems.

The exit side is where this project's central tension plays out most clearly. On the positive side: this project sits in the Sukhumvit core / Thonglor corridor the framework explicitly names as an active foreign-resale market; and while quota status is unverified, the March 2026 Foreign Quota sale is indirect evidence good enough to justify the low end of the deduction range rather than the midpoint. On the negative side: the representative unit's quantum implies a household-income threshold of about 5.0x Bangkok's average, structurally thinning the mass-market local buyer pool — a reflection of the buyer segment this project targets, not a flaw in the project itself; the representative unit's total price sits well above the framework's THB 3-10M sweet spot, so the foreign exit score misses the bonus other mid-priced cards in this framework commonly receive; the listing ratio (39.9%-51.8%, depending on source) sits far above the 30% healthy threshold, and Hipflat's own disclosed average time-on-market of 646 days is concrete quantified evidence of sluggish liquidity — even allowing for possible duplicate-listing inflation across multiple agents, these two pieces of evidence together point toward genuinely slow transaction velocity at this price point. This project's final scores (Thai 5.90, Foreign 5.79) show that connectivity and developer strength are pulled back to a mid-range result by clearly weak capital-appreciation momentum and holding cashflow, plus multiple exit-side pressure signals — precisely the message this card opens with: the high entry threshold of a Bangkok luxury product is not a flaw so much as a signal that this is a differently structured asset aimed at a different buyer, and should not be judged against the expectations of a mid-priced condo.

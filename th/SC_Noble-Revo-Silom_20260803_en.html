🔒 Foreign quota check: NOT VERIFIED (independent tier, exit (foreign) −0.5)
This project's foreign-held share / remaining quota is not disclosed anywhere — not on the developer's official site, FazWaz, PropertyScout, Dot Property, or Thailand-Property, and no discussion surfaced on local forums. The 49% statutory cap is fixed (Condominium Act B.E. 2522 §19 bis), but this project's actual utilisation could not be determined as ample, tight, or somewhere in between. In this framework, "unverified" is an independent tier, deliberately scored more conservatively than "quota ample" (−0.4 to −0.6, not a 0 adjustment) — because the analyst cannot rule out the possibility that the quota is already near its cap, and a prudent 10-year-hold framework should treat "unknown" as at least as cautionary as "confirmed tight," not default to optimism. Buyers should confirm the current quota utilisation with an agent or the Land Department in writing before committing — this is the single largest unknown on the exit side, more decisive than fees or valuation premium for whether the unit can be resold to another foreigner in 10 years. If the quota turns out to be full, existing foreign-held units can only be resold to Thai buyers (typically at a 10-25% Thai Quota discount) or must wait for another foreign holder's quota to free up.
One-line summary: The most likely 10-year buyer is an upper-middle-income Thai finance/professional upgrader, or a foreign self-use buyer who clears the MOTS certificate route (Sathorn/Silom's corporate-expat population base is solid). Unresolved quota status, compounded by extremely thin secondary-market evidence (3-4 cumulative transactions, averaging 412 days to sell), represents a double unresolved risk on the exit side of this project — if verification later shows the quota is tight or full, the foreign exit score should be marked down materially and immediately.

Noble Revo Silom sits in Bangkok's Bang Rak district, at the core of the traditional Sathorn/Silom financial CBD, developed by Noble Development PCL and completed in December 2015 — a single 32-storey tower whose total unit count, based on this search's cross-referencing, is best assessed at 319 (see the dedicated note below). On scarcity, Sathorn/Silom is a fully built-up core district with little raw land, but the corridor continues to see teardown-and-rebuild supply (The 125 Sathorn, post-litigation, and ROMM Convent, both due 2026), and at least four comparable projects — Quad Silom, The Empire Place, and others — sit in the same submarket. Both the land-layer and product-layer scarcity audits fail; only the regulatory layer (freehold) partially holds.

Noble Development PCL is a SET-listed developer (NOBLE), but its financial-health signals are noticeably weaker than AP Thailand, the developer behind the Life Asoke Rama 9 case: TRIS Rating currently stands at "BBB-," found to reflect a downgrade from BBB with the outlook revised from Negative to Stable (the exact sequencing and dates could not be fully verified); a separate financial-trend summary shows recent earnings declining an average of ~20.8%/year against revenue growth of only ~1.1% (period unstated, confidence: medium-low); the SET share price sits around THB 1.85, a low absolute level. No specific on-time-delivery record for Noble's past projects could be located; the credit rating and financials are an indirect proxy at best and should not be read as direct evidence of delivery punctuality.

On pricing: the representative unit (33 sqm 1BR) currently asks approximately THB 5,200,000, or about THB 157,576 per square metre. Confidence in this valuation judgment is low — Dot Property records only 4 actual transactions recently for this project, averaging ~THB 156,000/sqm with an average of 412 days on market before selling, while a separate source indicates Hipflat shows a cumulative 3 transactions — the two counts are close but not necessarily the same deals, and this project carries no Foreign Quota / Thai Quota split data, so a 10-25% quota-class premium distorting the comparison cannot be excluded. PropertySights separately states a project median of ฿166,800/sqm, showing no clear premium against the Silom district average (sources disagree: ฿180,000-250,000/sqm per one source, ฿224,854/sqm per another) — if anything running below it — though this district average may be skewed upward by new luxury supply in the corridor (such as ROMM Convent at ~THB 276,000/sqm) and is not a direct like-for-like benchmark for a project of this tier.

On the rental market, the representative 1BR rents for roughly THB 19,000-25,000 per month (call it THB 22,000), a gross yield of about 5.1%, consistent with PropertySights' stated project median gross yield of 4.9% (range 4.1%-5.7%). This is again where the Thailand framework diverges most sharply from the Malaysia methodology: a Thai buyer using leverage (mortgage rate ~6.5%) already faces a negative spread against gross yield (5.1% − 6.5% = −1.4pp), so leverage erodes rather than amplifies the return; a foreign buyer, structurally unable to obtain a Thai-baht mortgage, is instead assessed on "net yield versus opportunity cost" — the benchmark used is the US 10-year Treasury yield of 4.74%-4.75% (verified 2026-07-31) plus 100bp ≈ 5.75%/year (USD). After-tax net yield of roughly 4.34% (THB, gross yield less 15% non-resident withholding) sits 1.41 percentage points below 5.75% (USD), clearly below the benchmark. The two cashflow scores (Thai 4.5, foreign 4.4) end up only 0.1 points apart — nearly the same structure as the Life Asoke Rama 9 case (gross yield happens to land near the same ~5.1% level): Thailand's structurally high mortgage rates hurt the Thai buyer, while US Treasury yields sitting near a multi-year high hurt the foreign buyer, two independent causes that happen to land close together.

The exit dimension is where this scorecard discloses the most "not verified" items and thin-liquidity signals, and the picture here is more severe than in the Life Asoke Rama 9 case. This project's actual foreign-quota utilisation could not be found anywhere — not on the developer's official site, across major aggregator platforms, or in local forum discussions — scored under the framework as an independent "quota status unverified" tier, a 0.5-point exit deduction for the foreign buyer, and the single largest unresolved risk on the exit side. Secondary-market liquidity data is similarly thin, and thinner than the Rama 9 case: this project has been completed for 11 years with 319 units, yet only 3-4 actual transactions could be found in total (Dot Property records an average of 412 days on market before selling), implying a rough annual turnover rate well under 1%; active listing counts range from 6 to 88 across sources, a 14.7x spread far exceeding the "range only" 2x threshold, so per the rules only a range is reported rather than a midpoint treated as precise. What can be established: the Thai-buyer quantum test implies a next-buyer household income threshold of roughly THB 72,800/month, 1.86x Bangkok's average household income (narrower than the Rama 9 case's 1.54x), and layered atop Bangkok's 2026H1 mortgage rejection rate of 40-50%, the local buyer pool is meaningfully narrowed already. The foreign self-use route clears the THB 3-million visa threshold (a binary eligibility flag; this unit sits well above it, earning no scoring bonus) and rests on a genuine demand base — Sathorn/Silom is one of Bangkok's top three foreign-housing-demand CBD corridors per Nation Thailand — but the unresolved quota variable could still veto the entire foreign exit pathway. The foreign total score (5.9) sits only 0.1 points below the Thai total (6.0), within the 0.0-0.3 band this framework assigns to the "quota status unverified" tier — the exit-side deduction for unknown quota status (−0.5) and the cashflow-side shortfall against the USD benchmark both push the foreign score down rather than offsetting each other; the gap stays narrow only because the Thai buyer's own cashflow score is similarly depressed by a negative mortgage spread, so the two buyer types move down together on that dimension rather than diverging. That is an honest calculation outcome, not an engineered one.

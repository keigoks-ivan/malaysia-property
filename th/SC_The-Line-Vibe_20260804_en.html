◆ Deep Narrative

Project Deep-Dive Summary

The Line Vibe sits at the Ha Yaek Lat Phrao station node in Bangkok's Chatuchak-Chomphon district, developed by Sansiri (SET-listed), a single 33-storey residential tower with 3 floors of commercial space and a separate 16-storey parking structure. The official total of 943 units is actually 940 residential plus 3 commercial units, and this card discloses that split explicitly, using 940 as the denominator throughout so readers do not mistake commercial units for part of the residential turnover or listing-ratio base. Verification of the completion date turned up three different readings: contractor Teka Construction's own portfolio lists a contract period of November 2022 to April 2024 with status "Completed," the best-supported practical-completion reading; a "Welcome Home" handover event ran in early June 2024, marking the start of transfers; and listing aggregators separately show September-October 2024, more likely a platform status-update artifact than a construction fact. All three are presented side by side rather than arbitrarily chosen, and all are more than 12 months before today, so this card's mature-project scoring rules apply without issue.

On connectivity, this project delivers unusually well-supported distance evidence — two independent Thai property blogs, both written before the sales campaign, describe the same specific route: about 300 meters from BTS Ha Yaek Lat Phrao, via the Lotus store, a pedestrian bridge, and the BTS Skywalk. Two independent Thai property blogs, published in February and March 2022 respectively — both before this project's sales peak — each independently describe exactly the same route and arrive at the same ~300-meter conclusion, and the developer's own figure agrees, a three-way triangulation stronger than the single-source citations or self-summed turn-by-turn directions common elsewhere in this cohort. This card also corrects a common misconception: BTS Ha Yaek Lat Phrao and MRT Phahon Yothin are two separate stations, linked by a 300-meter public skywalk that opened in October 2020, not a single interchange complex; the total walk from this project to the MRT is about 700 meters, fully covered — still a genuinely usable dual-rail commuting base. But the scarcity audit tells a starkly different story from the connectivity story: the same station node has been stacked with roughly 3,695 units from three developers (AP with two projects, Sansiri with this one) within about five years, the densest single-node supply stack found in this framework to date, failing both the land and product layers. More importantly, this is not the vague "no premium evidence" seen elsewhere — the two known transactions fell from 122,500 THB/sqm in February 2025 to 108,611 THB/sqm in September 2025, a decline of roughly 11.3% in seven months, a direct, verifiable negative signal against scarcity, and also the main reason this project takes an additional deduction on the appreciation-momentum dimension.

As a SET-listed developer, Sansiri's FY2025 full-year results were fully obtainable and reconcilable this time: revenue THB 34.39bn (−12.3% y/y), net profit THB 4.51bn (−14.1% y/y), net margin 13.1%, with the condo segment bucking the trend at +13.1% revenue growth. Despite the profit decline, Sansiri remained the highest-absolute-profit major Thai developer in 2025 — the evidence points to a sector-wide headwind from tightening credit and slower absorption shared across peers, not a company-specific crisis. The credit rating was confirmed at TRIS BBB+ (confirmed 2019, corroborated by a ~March 2026 green-bond reference) — investment grade, but a full notch below the A/Stable rating held by AP Thailand, the developer behind the Life-series cards. No permit revocation, EIA revocation, or litigation was found against either this project or the Sansiri group, a clean regulatory-compliance picture. This gives the developer dimension a clear data-quality edge over the XT Phayathai case (which was penalized for conflicting financial figures and an unconfirmed rating at the time), placing this project's developer score between XT's and the Life-series cards'.

On pricing and cashflow, no Foreign/Thai Quota price split could be found for this project — the key methodological difference from XT Phayathai — so both buyer types share the same entry-price basis (THB 4,318,000, representative unit 34 sqm 1BR currently asking ~127,000 THB/sqm). Anchored to the most recent transaction (September 2025, 108,611 THB/sqm), the current asking price runs a premium of about +16.9%; anchored to the earlier one (February 2025, 122,500 THB/sqm) instead, the premium is only about +3.7% — the sheer gap between these two readings is itself a direct expression of a softening market, and this card conservatively adopts the newer, more cautious reading. On rent, an independent cross-check yields a gross rate of about 5.5%, a strong level, implying a monthly rent of roughly THB 19,800, which lines up closely with the specific reported figure of THB 19,000. The Thai buyer's gross yield of 5.5% against a 6.5% mortgage rate shows a −1.0 percentage-point negative spread, smaller than XT's −1.33pp; the foreign buyer's net yield (about 4.68% after 15% withholding) against opportunity-cost benchmark two (USD 5.75%/year) leaves a gap of only −1.07 percentage points, small — precisely because, unlike XT, this project shows no confirmed quota premium, so the foreign buyer does not need to pay more than the Thai buyer for the identical unit.

The exit side shows this project's most contradictory pair of signals. On the positive side: the quantum test shows a next local buyer's household-income threshold of only 1.55x Bangkok's average, a favorable ratios; and a listing ratio of about 2.2-2.3% (21-22 listings ÷ 940 residential units), well below the 10% healthy threshold and a very low and healthy ratio. On the negative side: foreign-quota utilisation is undisclosed anywhere, landing in the "unverified" tier and taking a −0.5 deduction; only 2 known transactions exist, a genuinely thin sample; the listing-to-transacted discount of about 14.5%, while not crossing the 15% deduction threshold, sits close enough to warrant ongoing monitoring; and more fundamentally, roughly 2,755 existing units at the same station node (Life Ladprao and Life Ladprao Valley) represent a structural, long-term demand-dilution pressure — one that is already backed by concrete evidence of transacted-price softening, not merely a hypothetical concern. This project's final scores (Thai 5.6, Foreign 5.5) land in the lower-middle of the current sample, closest to XT Phayathai — but for a different combination of reasons: XT's weaknesses centered on developer data gaps and a cashflow-eroding quota premium, while this project's weaknesses center on same-node supply density and already-realized price softening — a concrete illustration that connectivity strength does not equal scarcity.

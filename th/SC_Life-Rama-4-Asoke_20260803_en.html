🔒 Foreign quota check: NOT VERIFIED (exit (foreign) −0.4, low end of range)
This project's foreign-held share / remaining quota is not disclosed anywhere — not on AP Thailand's official site, Hipflat, FazWaz, or DDproperty. The 49% statutory cap is fixed (Condominium Act B.E. 2522 §19 bis), but this project's actual utilisation could not be determined as ample, tight, or somewhere in between. However, this research found a specific unit listing on DDproperty (a 90 sqm 2BR "Vertiplex" duplex) explicitly flagged as "within the foreign quota" and still for sale — indirect but genuine positive evidence that the quota has not reached a fully-exhausted state, so this project takes the low end of the "unverified" range (−0.4, rather than −0.6). "Unverified" remains an independent tier, deliberately scored more conservatively than "quota ample" — the analyst cannot rule out the quota being near its cap, and a prudent 10-year-hold framework treats "unknown" as at least as cautionary as "confirmed tight." Buyers should confirm the current quota utilisation with an agent or the Land Department in writing before committing — this remains the single largest unknown on the exit side. If the quota turns out to be full, existing foreign-held units can only be resold to Thai buyers (typically at a 10-25% discount) or must wait for another foreign holder's quota to free up.
One-line summary: The most likely 10-year buyer is an upper-middle-income Thai professional upgrader, or a foreign self-use buyer who clears the MOTS certificate route. Quota status remains unresolved and is still the largest open risk on the exit side, though indirect evidence suggests the quota is not yet exhausted; if future verification shows it is tight, the foreign exit score should be marked down immediately.
◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Dive

Life Rama 4-Asoke sits on Rama IV Road in Bangkok's Khlong Toei district — the second project between AP (Thailand) PCL and Japan's Mitsubishi Estate Group, following Life Asoke Rama 9 (launched 2017, completed 2020). This one broke ground in 2021 and completed in August 2024: a single 39-storey tower with 1,237 units plus two retail spaces, giving the JV series two completed, delivered projects to date. The project's name deserves a specific caveat: despite containing "Asoke," the nearest rail station is actually MRT Queen Sirikit National Convention Centre (QSNCC), not BTS Asoke and not MRT Sukhumvit — sources place the walking distance between 400 and 500 metres, and readers who price this unit as if it carried the moat of true Asoke/Sukhumvit core will systematically overestimate its location scarcity. QSNCC itself is an already-operating convention-centre node, unlike Rama 9's still-unfulfilled New CBD promise, but Khlong Toei has historically been port and industrial land that continues to be released for redevelopment, and the same Rama 4 corridor already has a similarly-scaled comparable (Aspire Sukhumvit-Rama 4, 1,323 units, completed 3Q2025). Both the land-layer and product-layer supply-side scarcity audits fail; only the regulatory layer (freehold) partially holds.

AP (Thailand) PCL is a large SET-listed developer, and its financials carry over from the same recent disclosures used for this framework's calibration anchor (Life Asoke Rama 9): TRIS Rating A/Stable affirmed for a fourth consecutive year (May-June 2026); 1Q2026 revenue THB 10.554 billion (+11% y/y), net profit THB 903 million, net debt-to-equity of 0.6-0.64x. The Mitsubishi Estate partnership is now on its second project here, evidence of an ongoing relationship rather than a one-off marketing arrangement — a mildly positive signal in its own right. On the compliance check, this search found no building-permit or EIA dispute tied to this project or AP Thailand generally. No specific on-time-delivery record for AP Thailand's past projects could be located; this project's own timeline from groundbreaking (2021) to completion (August 2024) — roughly three years — sits within normal high-rise construction periods, neither confirming punctual delivery nor signalling a clear delay, so it is read neutrally.

On pricing: the representative unit (26.5 sqm 1BR, the developer's official base unit type) uses an actually-sourced transacted-range figure of approximately THB 4,300,000, or about THB 162,264 per square metre. Confidence in this valuation judgment is low — no clean Khlong Toei/QSNCC district-level transacted median could be found, and this project carries no Foreign Quota / Thai Quota split data, so a 10-25% quota-class premium distorting the comparison cannot be excluded; the only available reference is this project's own average around THB 150,000/sqm as of July 2024 (sourced as "REAL DATA," exact methodology not fully verifiable), against which the current asking figure represents roughly a +8.2% premium — a neutral, "reasonable but not discounted" reading. Worth flagging: reading that +8.2% (over two years) as a roughly 4%/year appreciation rate would likely be too optimistic — the current listing sample probably carries survivorship bias, since cheaper units tend to sell first, leaving pricier stock on the market and systematically overstating the true pace of transaction-level appreciation. This card accordingly does not treat the headline gain as evidence supporting the capital-appreciation dimension.

On the rental market, the representative 1BR rents for roughly THB 20,000-22,000 per month (call it THB 20,000), a gross yield of about 5.6% — modestly higher than the prior case in the same JV series (~5.1%). A Thai buyer using leverage (mortgage rate carried over from a Bangkok market estimate, ~6.5%) faces a spread of −0.9 percentage points — still negative, but a smaller gap than the prior case's −1.4pp; a foreign buyer, structurally unable to obtain a Thai-baht mortgage, is instead assessed on "net yield versus opportunity cost" — the benchmark used is the US 10-year Treasury yield of 4.74%-4.75% (verified 2026-07-31) plus 100bp ≈ 5.75%/year (USD, this site's standard methodology for cross-border buyers). After-tax net yield of roughly 4.74% (THB, gross yield less 15% non-resident withholding) sits 1.01 percentage points below 5.75% (USD) — still below the benchmark, but again a narrower gap than the prior case's 1.38pp. Both cashflow scores (Thai 5.0, foreign 4.6) accordingly run somewhat higher than the prior case, reflecting this project's slightly better rent-to-price performance.

The exit dimension again discloses the most "not verified" items on this scorecard, but with a somewhat more positive signal than the prior case. This project's actual foreign-quota utilisation could not be found anywhere — not on the developer's official site, not across the three major aggregator platforms, and not through other channels — and is scored as "quota status unverified" per the framework. Unlike the prior case, however, this research specifically found a DDproperty listing flagged "within the foreign quota" still for sale — an indirect but genuine positive signal that the quota has not reached a state where no units can be registered — so this project takes the low end of that tier (a 0.4-point exit deduction for the foreign buyer, rather than 0.5 or higher). Secondary-market liquidity data remains constrained by the same structural limitation of the Thai market (no Brickz- or NAPIC-equivalent official register), and direct Hipflat access for this project encountered a block this session, with search-engine snippets also failing to independently surface a page with a specific transaction count — the "36 cumulative transactions" figure this card uses is a previously-compiled research figure that could not be re-verified independently this session, so its confidence is flagged as medium-low rather than treated as freshly confirmed. On that basis (assuming roughly the two years since the August 2024 completion), the implied annual turnover rate is about 1.5% — in the "normal" band, better than the prior case's under-1%; the listing ratio is about 3.8% (a single DDproperty source, 47 of 1,237 units, not cross-checked against other platforms, medium-low confidence). What can be established: the Thai-buyer quantum test implies a next-buyer household income threshold of roughly THB 60,200/month, 1.54x Bangkok's average household income, and layered atop Bangkok's 2026H1 mortgage rejection rate of 40-50%, the local buyer pool is meaningfully narrowed already. The foreign self-use route is viable in principle — the representative unit clears the THB 3-million visa threshold (a binary eligibility flag, no scoring bonus) and the MOTS certificate route is workable — but buyers should specifically account for the risk that the "Asoke" name invites a location misjudgement: this project's actual location moat falls short of true Asoke/Sukhumvit core, which will directly affect resale pricing and speed expectations ten years out. The foreign total score (5.8) sits only 0.1 points below the Thai total (5.9), within the 0.0-0.3 band this framework assigns to the "quota status unverified" tier — an honest calculation outcome.

◆ Verdict

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • MRT QSNCC ~400-500m away, completed and available for in-person inspection, no off-plan delivery risk
  • AP (Thailand) PCL (SET-listed) × Mitsubishi Estate Group's second JV project, TRIS Rating A/Stable for 4 consecutive years, sound 1Q2026 financials (revenue +11% y/y, net debt/equity 0.6-0.64x); no building-permit/EIA dispute found
  • Gross rental yield ~5.6%, better than the prior case in the same JV series (~5.1%), with a smaller negative-carry gap for both Thai and foreign buyers
  • A DDproperty listing confirms a foreign-quota unit is genuinely for sale — the quota is not confirmed exhausted
  • The representative unit at THB 4.3M clears the THB 3-million visa threshold, and the MOTS certificate route is concretely workable
⚠ Caveats
  • The "Asoke" name is highly likely to cause a location misjudgement — the actual nearest station is MRT QSNCC, not BTS Asoke/MRT Sukhumvit, and the location moat is weaker than true Asoke core; both the land-layer and product-layer scarcity audits fail
  • Foreign-quota utilisation is undisclosed (only indirect positive evidence exists); buyers should confirm in writing with an agent or the Land Department before committing
  • MRT distance is inconsistent across sources (400-500m), exceeding the Tier 7 (≤400m) cutoff and marking down the connectivity score; no confirmed international school, named anchor hospital, or direct-walk mall connection was found
  • The two-year headline price gain (+8.2%, Jul 2024 to present) likely reflects listing survivorship bias and should not be read as a steady annualised appreciation rate; Bangkok's overall fundamentals remain soft (national +1.26% in 1Q2026, Bangkok itself −0.18%)
  • Annual turnover (~1.5%) and listing ratio (~3.8%) carry medium-low confidence: the turnover figure is a previously-compiled figure not independently re-verified this session, and the listing ratio comes from a single uncross-checked source
  • Bangkok's 2026 credit squeeze (40-50% mortgage rejection) directly narrows the local buyer pool; clearing the quantum threshold does not guarantee financing

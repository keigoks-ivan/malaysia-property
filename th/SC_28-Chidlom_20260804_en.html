28 Chidlom

An independent Google Maps route check puts the walk to BTS Chidlom at roughly 400 metres, 5 minutes — above the 250-280m range commonly used in marketing, a gap of roughly 40-60%, honest marketing polish that needs disclosing. The developer's own marketing claims this is "the only freehold project in the Chidlom area"; verification shows this is false — at least Scope Langsuan, Muniq Langsuan, and NIMIT Langsuan are all freehold in the same corridor. This project does have a genuine transacted anchor (1BR sales found for 2024-2025, split by quota), but the representative unit's market price of roughly THB 16 million implies a local household monthly-income threshold near THB 224,000 — close to 5.7x Bangkok's average household income (THB 39,100). The local buyer pool is thin by design, reflecting the product's positioning toward high-net-worth buyers rather than a defect in the building. Gross rental yield is only about 3.75%, well below the Thai mortgage rate of 6.5%, a negative spread of −2.75 percentage points; the foreign-buyer net-yield gap against opportunity-cost benchmark two (USD 5.75%/yr) runs about −2.56 percentage points.

Langsuan / Chidlom district starts at 8.0 (the framework's anchor table's highest rung, on the structural reasoning that "land supply is nearly exhausted, the sole new-supply source for ultra-high-end projects"). Three-layer audit, testing that reasoning against the evidence rather than accepting it at face value: land layer, leaning ✓ — NIMIT Langsuan's own marketing language calls itself "the last remaining freehold land plot on Langsuan Road," consistent with the anchor table's land-exhaustion thesis; the only in-construction (not-yet-completed) project found this round is The Embassy Wireless (Hongkong Land + Noble Development, not SC Asset, 757 units, expected completion 2028), but it sits on Wireless Road, a nearby but distinct stretch, so the land-exhaustion thesis for Chidlom/Langsuan Road itself holds up reasonably well, though supply across the broader Ploenchit area is not literally zero. Product layer ✗ — at least three completed freehold competitors exist directly in the same walking corridor: Scope Langsuan (~THB 592,000/sqm average), Muniq Langsuan (~THB 262,000-531,000/sqm, completed 2022), and NIMIT Langsuan (~THB 646,000-800,000/sqm) — this directly refutes the project's own marketing claim of being "the only freehold project in the Chidlom area." Verification shows that claim is false and it should not be repeated; at best it stands as an example of marketing overreach. Regulatory layer: freehold confirmed ✓, but this project's actual foreign-quota utilisation is undisclosed, so no structural-scarcity bonus can be credited. Effective scarcity test: this project's own recent 1BR transactions run ~THB 307,000-344,000/sqm, meaningfully below the newer-completed NIMIT (≈646,000-800,000/sqm) and Scope (≈592,000/sqm average) in the same corridor — showing that as a relatively older product completed in 2019, this project has not captured the top-end premium available to the corridor's newest entrants; the scarcity thesis holds for the corridor as a whole, but is discounted for this project's own pricing power. Net: 8.0 baseline − 0.3 (product layer clearly fails, at least three directly comparable freehold competitors in the corridor; the land layer itself holds up relatively well) = 7.7.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep-Dive Summary

28 Chidlom sits in the Lumphini subdistrict of Bangkok's Pathumwan district, developed directly by SC Asset Corporation PCL (SET: SC), completed September 2019 on roughly 3 rai (4,896 sqm) of land, with 425 units across two buildings: The Tower (47 floors + 1 basement, 243 units) and The Villa (20 floors, 182 units). Verification surfaced two important findings, one positive and one negative. On the positive side: this project does have a genuine transacted anchor — Hipflat's "previous sales" table lists one quota-split 1BR transaction each (Thai Quota, sold February 2025, THB 13.5m/44sqm; Foreign Quota, sold September 2025, THB 14.8m/43sqm). While this table remains a platform-derived estimate rather than a confirmed Land Department record, these two quota-split transactions still give the valuation a concrete anchoring basis. On the negative side: this project's marketing materials claim to be "the only freehold project in the Chidlom area." Verification shows this is false — at least Scope Langsuan, Muniq Langsuan, and NIMIT Langsuan are all freehold in the same walking corridor, with NIMIT even marketing itself as "the last remaining freehold land plot on Langsuan Road," directly contradicting this project's claim. This card does not repeat that claim and treats it only as an example of marketing overreach.

Distance also demands honest treatment. An independent, live Google Maps route check puts the walk to BTS Chidlom at roughly 400 metres, 5 minutes — sitting at the high end of the published spread (250-400m, with marketing copy skewing toward the low end and independent comparison sites toward the high end), a gap of roughly 40-60%, marketing polish that needs disclosing. Livability checks are similarly mixed: international schools perform well, with NIST and St Andrews International School Sathorn both within a 5km drive; medical access is a touch awkward, with Bumrungrad (2.7km) and BNH (3.2km) both just over the framework's 2km bonus threshold and therefore uncredited — an honest disclosure of "very close but short of the line," with no rounding in the project's favour. A skywalk connection toward Central Chidlom and Siam is mentioned in secondary sources, but this round of verification could not independently confirm whether it actually reaches this project's entrance, so it likewise earns no bonus.

What this project most needs to be honest about is the sheer scale gap between it and the eight other scorecards already published on this site (all in the THB 3-8m band), and how that gap plays out honestly within the framework's mechanics. The representative unit (1BR≈43sqm) has a current representative market price of roughly THB 16 million, which translates to a local household monthly-income threshold of roughly THB 224,000 — about 5.73x Bangkok's average household income (THB 39,100). This is not the scoring mechanism malfunctioning; it is the mechanism honestly reflecting that this product targets an entirely different buyer tier: households and business owners at the top of Bangkok's income distribution, not upper-middle-income professional households. Correspondingly, gross rental yield is only about 3.75% (estimated), well below the 6.5% Thai mortgage rate, with a Thai-buyer spread of −2.75 percentage points and a foreign-buyer net-yield gap against opportunity cost of roughly −2.56 percentage points. This is exactly the profile of a top-tier-address product: the absolute price has run up faster than any reasonable rent ceiling, so the cashflow numbers are inherently unflattering — but that is a consequence of the product's positioning, not a defect in the building or its management.

On the developer side, this project is developed directly by SC Asset Corporation PCL, and no evidence was found of an institutional joint-venture structure of the kind seen in another card in this series (Reference Sathorn-Wongwianyai, developed jointly by SC Asset Three Co., Ltd. and Tokyo Tatemono Asia Pte. Ltd.), so this project's developer score had to be derived independently rather than carrying over that card's +0.5 bonus. SC Asset itself carries a TRIS BBB+/Stable rating, with FY2025 revenue of THB 20,233m and net profit of THB 1,533m, matching the financial figures verified across the rest of this series; no permit revocation, EIA revocation, or live litigation was found for either this project or the group, though official permit databases could not be directly checked this round — "no adverse findings" does not mean "confirmed clean." The scarcity dimension reflects a similarly cautious posture: this project's location anchor starts at 8.0 (the anchor table's highest rung), but the three-layer audit finds the product layer clearly fails (three direct freehold competitors), and this project's own recent transaction prices sit meaningfully below newer-completed projects in the corridor — showing the scarcity thesis holds for the corridor as a whole, but this project, as a relatively older 2019-vintage product, has not captured the corridor's top-end premium. The final scores (Thai buyer 6.0, foreign buyer 5.9, both T4) reflect exactly this pairing of "an extremely high location moat" with "an extremely thin local buyer pool and extremely low cashflow" — two facts that look contradictory but are both true at once.

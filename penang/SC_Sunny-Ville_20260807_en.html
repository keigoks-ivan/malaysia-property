Sunny Ville

This is the site's first card to directly test the "mid-price transit corridor scores higher" hypothesis. Sunny Ville sits roughly 460m (estimated straight-line) from the future LRT Mutiara Line Batu Uban station (8.99% under construction), and the representative unit (2BR, 900 sqft) carries a quantum of only about RM380,000 — producing a deep local buyer pool and a strong exit score (7.5). On paper this fits the hypothesis perfectly. But the verification does not support it: all three scarcity layers fail (a new 20-storey condo is already proposed on the same corridor), the representative unit falls below the RM500,000 MM2H threshold and triggers the R7 cap at 3.0 for foreign exit, and the tenant base is confirmed by multiple sources to be majority USM students — holding gross yield to about 3.9%, in line with, not better than, the Gurney/core flagship cards. Malaysian T4, Foreign T5. Neither score breaks the site's existing ceiling.

◆ In Depth

Project Deep-Dive

Sunny Ville sits at No. 1, Jalan Batu Uban, 11700 Gelugor, developed by government-linked entity Pernas Group (linked to Perbadanan Nasional Berhad) and completed in 1994: six blocks of 22 storeys, 504 units total, unit sizes from 900 to 1,200 sqft across 2BR and 3BR layouts. This card uses the Block A/B 2BR, 900 sqft layout as its representative unit — under this framework's rules, Gelugor is not on the precinct list, so the 2BR (not 3BR) rule applies. The site sits roughly 5.350388°N,100.307967°E, about 0.7–0.8km from the USM Sungai Dua gate, and is this framework's first direct test of the "mid-price transit corridor scores higher" hypothesis: it lies only about 460m straight-line from the future LRT Mutiara Line Batu Uban station (S12) — one of the closest LRT proximities recorded on the site to date, short only of the still-unachieved ≤400m threshold.

All three scarcity layers fail here: land — a 2.23-acre parcel on the same Jalan Sultan Azlan Shah corridor already has a proposed 20-storey condominium under review (disclosed 2025-02, adjacent to Vilaris Courtyard), proving land on this corridor is not scarce; product — this 1994 low-rise, high-density standard tower is directly replicated on the same corridor by E-Park, N-Park, U-Garden and The Uban Residence; legal — flat urban land, subject to neither SAP nor hillside restrictions. The effective-scarcity test confirms this further: the building's Brickz median (RM424 psf) sits below the wider Gelugor district median (RM500 psf) — the project itself carries no premium, sitting at the cheaper end of its own corridor. d1 is accordingly cut from a 6.0 base to 5.5. On connectivity, the project depends on Jalan Sultan Azlan Shah as its single north-south spine and must cross or merge with the Tun Dr Lim Chong Eu Expressway bottleneck, cutting d2 from a 5.8 base to 5.5. On pricing, the same building's trailing-12-month 20 real transactions carry a median of RM424 psf, while the current 2BR/900sqft asking price is about RM422 psf — essentially flat (~−0.5%) — landing d7 at the lower end of the reasonable new-supply-premium band, 6.0.

Capital appreciation is the core piece of evidence in the hypothesis's favour: with the LRT Mutiara Line at 8.99% progress (under-construction, <25% tier) and the site roughly 460m from Batu Uban station, it falls in the ≤800m band and earns +0.4, lifting d4 from the 6.0 island-strata base to 6.4 — exactly the mechanism the "mid-price corridor near transit" hypothesis would predict. But this positive signal is largely offset elsewhere. On cashflow, this card directly confronts the anchor table's explicit "USM room-rental trap" warning: multiple sources confirm the tenant base is majority USM students, and this card insists on using only portal "whole-unit" category listing rents (never room-by-room sublet prices) — giving the representative unit (2BR/900sqft, RM380,000) an annual rent of RM15,000 and a gross yield of about 3.9%, in the 3.5–4.0% band, for a Malaysian d3 of just 3.8 — close to, not clearly better than, the Gurney/core flagship cards (~3.1–3.3%), falsifying the intuition that "a low quantum must mean better cashflow": rents here are proportionally as low as prices, not just the price itself. As an island residential-title property, R4 mandatorily excludes short-let income, and the foreign cashflow score is fixed at Malaysian −2.0 = 1.8.

Exit tells sharply divergent stories for Malaysian and foreign buyers. The Malaysian exit score reaches 7.5 — one of the highest on the site: quantum of only about RM380,000 gives a household income threshold of just RM4,180, far below the Penang median, producing a deep local buyer pool; annual turnover and Brickz depth both fall precisely on this framework's stated thresholds (3.97%≈4%, 20 deals≈20), and this card deliberately takes the conservative reading on both rather than inflating the score. On the foreign side, because the representative unit's quantum falls below both the MM2H threshold (RM500,000) and the general foreign threshold (RM1,000,000), the framework's R7 rule is triggered: both general foreign individuals/institutions and MM2H holders are excluded, leaving the exit pool effectively limited to Permanent Residents (PR, threshold RM250,000) — the foreign exit score is capped at 3.0, following the Azuria precedent. Developer Pernas Group, though government-linked in origin, has exited property development entirely and carries no brand accountability for this project today; combined with the verified carpark title-handover dispute, d5 lands at 6.0.

  • About 460m from the future LRT Mutiara Line Batu Uban station — one of the closest LRT proximities recorded on the site, earning the full ≤800m d4 bonus
  • Quantum of about RM380,000 gives one of the deepest local buyer pools on the site, with a Malaysian exit score of 7.5

  • [R7] Representative-unit quantum (~RM380k) is below the MM2H threshold (RM500k) — neither MM2H holders nor general foreign individuals/institutions may purchase; cap 3.0 applies (following the Azuria precedent), leaving the exit pool effectively limited to Permanent Residents (PR)
  • [Land layer fails + corridor competitor] A 2.23-acre proposed 20-storey project on the same Jalan Sultan Azlan Shah corridor is under review (2025-02, not yet under construction), a direct threat to this project's "completed product" scarcity claim; all three scarcity layers fail

  • Recommended to verify: the 20-transaction Brickz dataset spans Oct 2023–Sep 2024, not the latest 12 months; listing ratio is not scored due to conflicting portal totals; both annual turnover and Brickz depth fall precisely on this framework's thresholds, and this card takes the conservative reading throughout — readers should independently verify the latest figures

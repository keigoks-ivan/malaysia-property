Azuria Condominium

Azuria is a rare mass-market-scale project in the Tanjung Bungah corridor — 476 units across 5 blocks, completed in 1999, with 14 Brickz transactions in the trailing 12 months, the deepest same-building transaction record of any Penang card on this site, giving it the most solidly grounded valuation anchor. But this is also the first case in this framework where the entry price sits below the legal foreign-purchase threshold: the representative unit's transacted price is roughly RM 300,000, well under the RM 1,000,000 floor for individual/corporate foreign buyers and the RM 500,000 floor for MM2H holders — ordinary foreign buyers cannot legally purchase this unit, and the exit pool is structurally locked to local buyers plus a narrow slice of permanent residents. The developer's identity is a second, entirely honest data gap: it could not be found in any public source.

◆ DEEP DIVE

Project Deep Dive

Azuria Condominium sits on Jalan Lembah Permai, near Tunku Abdul Rahman College, and is a rare mass-market-scale project in the Tanjung Bungah corridor — 476 units across 5 blocks of 10-16 storeys, completed in 1999 (some sources say 1998), freehold, with a uniform unit configuration of 3-bed, 2-bath units of roughly 700-800 sqft (sources vary slightly). This project's developer identity is the most extreme data gap this framework has encountered to date: this card exhausted multiple search rounds (StarProperty property review, iProperty project page, PropSocial, JMB registry lookup), and none could find the original developer's company name — consistent with the common information gap for small, unlisted Penang developers from a 1999-era completion. This is not "no reported disputes" but genuinely "no name found," and this card discloses this fully and honestly, applying the most conservative baseline score in the developer dimension.

On scarcity, this building fails this framework's three-layer audit. The land layer is clearly ✗: the same corridor has at least three confirmed active new-supply pipelines — the Waterstone new launch, Ascott Residences (99-unit luxury beachfront, targeted 2028), and Kerjaya Prospek's July-2025 acquisition of full development rights over Lot 2601/3603 (RM 117m land cost, an estimated RM 124m development spend, previously proposed as Aspen's "HH Park and Residence") — fully confirming the anchor table's note that Tanjung Bungah "keeps adding new towers." The product layer is also ✗: this building's own Brickz transacted median (RM 396-400 psf) sits below the concurrent Tanjung Bungah district median (RM 528 psf), showing no transaction premium and failing the effective-scarcity test; a dense, mass-market strata product has no technical barrier to replication. The regulatory layer, per R5, is capped at ✓ and never ✓✓: as of August 2026 the current RSNPP 2030's 76m/25° hillside rule remains in force (RSNPP 2040 has not been gazetted; the state targets Q3 2026, with the public feedback period having closed on 2025-12-23), but the state is actively proposing to relax this rule — a downside risk to the corridor's scarcity rather than an upside safeguard. With none of the three layers clearing the "all ✓" bar, d1 lands at 6.5, a 0.3-point discount from the district anchor of 6.8, reflecting the confirmed new-supply threats in the corridor.

On pricing, this building offers this framework's highest-confidence own-valuation anchor to date: 14 Brickz transactions in the trailing 12 months (2024-11~2025-10), median PSF RM 396, median price RM 275,000; a second query window (2025-04~2026-01) shows a median of RM 400 psf / RM 280,000 — highly consistent. The representative unit (3-bed, 700 sqft) currently lists around RM 300,000 (≈RM 429 psf), a premium of about 7% over the building's own transacted median, landing in the "reasonable premium" band. The depth of this valuation anchor (14 transactions) far exceeds most other Penang cards on this site (typically only 3-6 transactions), and is a core reason this building was chosen as the representative pick — transaction depth is a hard requirement, and Azuria clearly outperforms the other candidate buildings in the same district on this metric (Sri Golden Bay 4 transactions, Infinity 5, Horizon Towers 3).

The rental market continues the state-wide honest-disclosure posture across all Penang cards — no institution publishes rental or yield data, so all figures are portal-listing-derived estimates. This building's asking-rent samples: semi-furnished RM 900, furnished RM 1,200 (same-building 700 sqft units); taking the midpoint of RM 1,050/month gives an estimated gross yield of about 4.2%, beating most premium-precinct island cases (typically only 2.7-3.8%). As an island residential-titled property, MBPP explicitly prohibits short-term rental — any short-term-income marketing claim is unenforceable. The management fee is a verified, project-specific figure of RM 130/month (≈RM 0.19 psf), one of the few line items on this card that can rely on an actual number rather than the KL placeholder benchmark.

On the Malaysian side: quantum of RM 300,000 corresponds to a household monthly-income threshold of only about RM 3,300, well below Penang's P50 (RM 7,327) — the local buyer pool is one of the widest this framework has encountered; annual turnover of 2.94% (14 transactions / 476 units) falls in the "active" band, and the Brickz depth of 14 is also the deepest sample on this site, giving a Malaysian exit score of 7.5 (after the R2 under-construction-unsold deduction of −0.3).

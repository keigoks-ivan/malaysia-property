Quayside @ Seri Tanjung Pinang

Starts from Penang's second-highest location anchor (Tanjung Tokong／STP1, 7.5) — and is the only card in this batch that runs the full "real cap vs fake scarcity" comparison end to end: STP1's own 240-acre reclamation has only about 0.7 acres of developable land left, a genuine physical cap — Quayside's 21-acre scale cannot be replicated within what remains. But across one channel, the same developer, E&O, still holds STP2／Andaman's 756.9 acres with roughly 30 years of runway — this is exactly why the Maris／Andaman card elsewhere on this site marks the land layer as fail (fake scarcity), and it is Quayside's moat boundary too. The representative unit (2-bedroom, 2,000 sqft) is currently worth about RM 2,100,000, with quantum already past the foreign consent-levy "cliff" (fixed 3%, not the 1.5% sweet spot), and gross yield of only about 2.6% (estimate) — one of the weakest cash-flow readings in this Penang batch so far.

◆ In-Depth Narrative

Project Deep Dive

Quayside sits on reclaimed land in Seri Tanjung Pinang Phase 1 (STP1), developed by Eastern & Oriental Berhad (E&O) and completed in February 2013. It covers a 21-acre waterfront site across 7 blocks (5 high-rise up to 25 storeys, 2 low-rise) totalling 1,208 units, with 14 unit types ranging from a 1-bedroom 1,137 sqft to a duplex penthouse of 7,000 sqft. It is held on freehold title, and the community sits directly adjacent to Straits Quay waterfront mall and marina — multiple independent portals consistently label it Freehold, though this card has not obtained a formal SPA or title extract for first-hand verification, so the verification level is "multi-source consistent but not document-verified"; buyers should have a lawyer check the formal Geran before purchase. As a project completed as far back as 2013, Quayside already carries over 10 years of real property-management and transaction history — relatively unusual among Penang scorecards, most of which are still in the off-plan stage.

Scarcity is the dimension requiring the most careful reading here, and it is also the deliberate centrepiece of this batch of cards. Tanjung Tokong／STP1 anchors at 7.5 (Penang scale's second-highest location). Three-layer audit: land layer pass — STP1's own 240-acre reclamation, roughly 20 years into development, has only about 0.7 acres of developable stock left; the market can no longer rebuild a 21-acre waterfront community of this scale within STP1's own boundary, so the cap is genuine. Product layer pass — an integrated community of this scale is similarly irreplicable, with real Brickz premium evidence (roughly +40-45% over the state high-rise benchmark). Regulatory layer fail — the STP corridor carries no irreversible height or heritage lock. But this scarcity verdict has a clear boundary: across one channel, the same developer, E&O, still holds roughly 756.9 acres in STP2／Andaman, GDV above RM 60 billion, roughly 30 years of runway, with at least 5 named projects already launched or completed — The Lume, Maris, Senna & Fera, Arica, The Meg. This is exactly why the Maris／Andaman card elsewhere on this site marks the land layer as failed (fake scarcity) — same developer, same corridor, one channel apart, yet opposite scarcity conclusions: STP1 is genuinely capped but diluted by substitute supply, while STP2's land layer fails outright. This card therefore deducts 0.5 from the 7.5 anchor (high replicability: the same micro-location product category has substantial substitute reclaimed supply already on the market), netting 7.0. The one-line summary: STP1's land-layer cap stops "this building" from being replicated, but it cannot stop "this type of location" from being replicated at scale by the same developer next door.

On pricing, Quayside is one of the highest-confidence cases in this batch — its own full-year 2024 Brickz record shows 26 real transactions, median RM 1,035 psf, closely matching this framework's locked market-level Quayside anchor of RM 1,056 psf (roughly 2% apart), placing it in the first tier of the anchor-priority ladder — same-project real transactions, high confidence. The representative unit (2-bedroom, 2,000 sqft) is currently listed at roughly RM 2,100,000 ≈RM 1,050 psf, a premium of only about 0.5% over the anchor — essentially flat against the real transacted anchor. This is the position closest to "fair pricing" anywhere in this framework's ladder, reflecting that as a mature asset, Quayside's pricing is already fairly efficient, with little obvious premium or discount to arbitrage.

The rental market is where this project's cashflow performance is weakest, and where honest disclosure matters most. Penang has no institutional source for rental or yield data, so this card labels every figure an estimate. The representative unit (2-bedroom, 2,000 sqft, Block F, direct sea view, part-furnished) lists for rent at RM 4,500／month, implying a gross yield of roughly 2.57%; a cross-check against a fully-furnished 2,146 sqft Block D unit at RM 5,800／month implies roughly 3.1% — together pointing to a gross yield sitting in a narrow, uniformly weak 2.6-3.1% band, one of the weakest cash-flow readings in this Penang batch, and directionally consistent with market commentary that "yield-seeking investors lean toward Bayan Lepas／Gelugor (4-5%), while Tanjung Tokong／STP trades yield for waterfront address and capital growth." As an island residential-title property, MBPP explicitly bans short-term rental, so any Airbnb or short-let yield claim is unenforceable and is entirely excluded from this card's calculations; foreign cashflow therefore floors at the clamp of 1.5, compressing the MY-FG gap to 1.0, a permitted framework exception.

On exit, the RM 2.1m quantum implies a household monthly income threshold of roughly RM 23,100, falling between Penang's P95 and P99 — local first-time buyers, typical upgraders and local investors are all effectively excluded, leaving only three ambiguous segments (top-tier upgraders, out-of-state／overseas Malaysians, and MM2H owner-occupiers) as potential next buyers. This is why the exit score (Malaysian 6.5), despite the R2 deduction, is still supported by the 26-transactions-per-year Brickz liquidity. The foreign side carries the key warning: quantum has already crossed the RM 1.5m consent-levy cliff, so the next foreign buyer pays a fixed 3% levy (not the 1.5% sweet spot), on top of the mandatory state-consent friction and 3-year lock-up — the foreign exit score (4.1) is meaningfully below the Malaysian score minus 1.0, satisfying the invariant but with a non-trivial gap (2.4). The Timur Laut district it sits in has 973 completed unsold and 1,618 under-construction unsold units, triggering both hard rules simultaneously.

The core risk is not whether the location's scarcity is genuine (STP1's own cap is real) but the substitute supply from the same developer — the pace at which Andaman／STP2's remaining 756.9 acres are released over the next 10-30 years will directly determine whether pricing power in the "Tanjung Tokong／STP waterfront residential" category tilts toward buyers or sellers.
◆ Conclusion

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • The only card in this batch to run the full "real cap vs fake scarcity" comparison end to end: STP1's own reclaimed land has only about 0.7 acres left, so a 21-acre waterfront community of this scale cannot be replicated within the remaining site
  • Developer E&O is one of Penang's strongest listed developers (starting score 7.5), with a record FY2026 net profit of RM 221.2 million and the Tee family also controlling Kerjaya Prospek, an aligned-interest structure
  • A mature resale asset with real Brickz transaction depth (26／year, above the ≥20 threshold, +0.3) — better liquidity evidence than most off-plan cards in this batch; valuation confidence is among the highest in this batch (same-project real transactions, asking price only ~0.5% above anchor)
  • Adjacent to Straits Quay waterfront mall／marina, walkable; MM2H cluster segment +0.2; MM2H eligibility clearly met (above all federal tiers)
⚠ Caveats
  • [The boundary of scarcity] Across one channel is E&O's own Andaman／STP2 (756.9 acres, ~30-year runway) — the same developer's own substitute supply in the same corridor directly diverts buyers, and this is the single variable most worth tracking for this project's moat
  • Gross yield of only ~2.6% (estimate), one of the weakest cash-flow readings in this Penang batch; foreign cashflow floors at the clamp, compressing the MY-FG gap to 1.0 (clamp-collision exception)
  • [Foreign consent-levy cliff] The representative unit's quantum, RM 2.1m, has already crossed the RM 1.5m threshold, so the levy is fixed at 3% (not the 1.5% sweet spot), on top of the mandatory state-consent friction and 3-year lock-up
  • [R2] Timur Laut, where this project sits, has 1,618 under-construction unsold units, triggering a Malaysian exit deduction; LRT Mutiara has no station covering Tanjung Tokong, weighing on appreciation momentum
  • Title verification level is "multi-source consistent but not document-verified" — a lawyer's Geran check is recommended; no Quayside-specific management fee data exists, so the KL benchmark is used pending calibration

Moulmein Rise

The site's first card with a near-decade real transaction track record — completed 2016, launched circa 2012 at roughly RM 1,300 psf, and the own-building Brickz median in 2025 sits at just RM 1,136 psf. That's a thirteen-year trailing CAGR of roughly −1.0% to −2.0%, well below the framework's assumed "island residential strata +3.0%" baseline — an important calibration signal for the whole framework's appreciation dimension. Location still sits on Penang's ceiling anchor (Pulau Tikus inland side, 8.0, cut to 7.5), but the representative unit's current asking runs ~11% above its own transacted anchor, and gross yield is a thin ~3.9% (estimated) — pushing both tiers to T4.

◆ Deep Narrative

Project Deep-Dive

Moulmein Rise sits on Jalan Moulmein, off Jalan Pasar, adjacent to the Pulau Tikus wet market and the Burmah Road commercial strip, developed by Penang private developer Belleview Group (through subsidiary Hero Leader Sdn Bhd) — a single 27-storey tower with 4 units per floor, 84 units total, plus a 2-floor retail podium, on freehold land. When launched in 2012, the project drew opposition from the local community over its scale and positioning, with allegations that the developer used a "commercial development" designation to bypass stricter residential planning review; it nonetheless completed in Q2 2016, a normal ~4-year construction timeline, with no delay controversy found for the building itself. Unit sizes range from Type C's 1,787 sqft (3+1 bedroom, the project's smallest unit) to Type A's 2,228 sqft (4+1 bedroom) — a large-format family-buyer positioning that is also why this card's representative unit deviates from the framework's default 3-bedroom, 1,000-1,300 sqft band, the same situation as the sister Setia V and Gurney Paragon cards.

On scarcity, this project anchors at Penang's ceiling location score for inland Pulau Tikus/Gurney (8.0), yet all three audit layers score ✗: land layer — at least 15 land transactions in the same micro-location over 20 months, land prices up from RM 800-900 psf to RM 1,400 psf over four years, and a 26-storey proposal already in planning on Jalan Kelawai, meaning supply is not locked; product layer — this project's own large-format freehold suites have been explicitly cited by the sister Setia V card as an example of "replicable product in the same corridor"; regulatory layer — this area sits outside the UNESCO core/buffer zone, and the project's own 27-storey existence proves there is no effective height cap. This card therefore deducts 0.5, landing at 7.5, the same rationale as the same-district Codrington card. On the project's relationship to Penang Turf Club, this card conducted a third independent coordinate-based determination: an estimated straight-line distance of ~2.5km, between the Codrington card (directly across the street, deducted) and the Setia V card (~2.9km, not deducted) but closer to the latter, and with the Turf Club zoned for inland commercial use — a different product tier from this project's existing waterfront-adjacent high-rise residential. This card judges Turf Club does not constitute an independent additional deduction; the −0.5 above comes entirely from the general Pulau Tikus land-market activity evidence, avoiding double-counting the same underlying fact.

⚠️ Decade-Long Empirical Analysis (this card's core methodological value): The other twelve Penang cards on this site are almost all new launches or recently-completed projects, meaning the appreciation dimension can only extrapolate from new-launch pricing against the island residential strata baseline of +3.0%/year (NAPIC MHPI). Moulmein Rise completed in 2016, making it the site's first card able to directly test that baseline assumption against nearly a decade of real transaction data. The findings: the 2012 launch headline price was RM 1,300 psf, with each unit type's "from" price implying roughly RM 1,460-1,480 psf (Suite A RM 3.252mn/2,228sqft, Suite B RM 2.739mn/1,851sqft, Suite C RM 2.644mn/1,787sqft, Suite D RM 2.692mn/1,819sqft); the project's own 4 real Brickz transactions between April and November 2025 show a median of just RM 1,136 psf. Using the 2012 launch headline of RM 1,300 psf as the baseline, the 13-year nominal CAGR is roughly −1.0%/year; using the "from" price average of roughly RM 1,470 as the baseline, the 13-year nominal CAGR is roughly −2.0%/year; even using the more conservative completion year (2016) as the baseline, the 9-year nominal CAGR is still roughly −1.5%/year. All three baseline conventions point to the same conclusion: Moulmein Rise's actual nominal appreciation over the trailing 9-13 years has been negative, well below the framework's assumed "island residential strata +3.0%/year" baseline — this is not noise within a margin of error, it is the opposite direction. Compared against the same-vintage sister project Setia V Residences (also completed 2016-2017, own-building Brickz median RM 1,258 psf across 6 transactions, 2023-06 to 2024-04), Moulmein Rise trails notably — suggesting the gap likely owes more to this project's own location and product characteristics (inland, no locked-in view corridor) than to any general pattern across "the 2016-completion cohort" as a whole. At the same time, even at today's weakened RM 1,136 psf, this project still trades at roughly 3.2x the Pulau Tikus-wide non-landed transacted median of RM 357 psf (as verified by the Codrington card) — meaning the location premium of "new boutique product vs. old area-wide stock" still genuinely exists; Moulmein Rise itself simply has not converted that premium into further price appreciation over time, and has in fact given some of it back in nominal terms. This card's d4 score remains strictly scored per the locked anchor ladder (5.7, structurally identical to the Codrington card, not self-adjusted downward for one building's weakness), but this empirical evidence should be read as an important calibration signal for the whole framework's "island residential strata +3.0%" baseline: at least for inland boutique towers lacking a unique locked-in view catalyst (such as Setia V's Gurney Bay park), the real decade-long realisation rate may run materially below the framework's assumed median. The sample is only n=1, but it is currently the site's only complete decade-long control case, and future cards should keep accumulating similar evidence to calibrate the credible range for this baseline.

On pricing, this project's own Brickz record provides a rare same-building real transaction anchor for this site, though the sample size (4 transactions) falls below the framework's 5-transaction reliability floor. The median of RM 1,136 psf diverges from the site's locked ANCHORS table figure of "Moulmein Rise 1,242 psf" by roughly 8.5% — per protocol this card uses the verified figure and discloses the conflict honestly, recommending the anchor table be recalibrated at the next version update. The representative unit (Type C, 1,787 sqft, current asking RM 2,255,000) implies a PSF of roughly RM 1,262, a premium of roughly 11.1% over the project's own Brickz median, landing in the "elevated, needs a catalyst" band; using other verified corridor anchors instead (Setia V 1,258, Gurney Paragon 1,188, Cantonment 1,191, Straits 1,288 — median ~1,215), the premium is only roughly 4.5%, landing in the "reasonable new-product premium" band — the two anchors point in different directions, and per protocol this card prioritises the own-building anchor. This divergence is itself a signal: current asking prices still appear anchored to a higher corridor benchmark, while this project's own actual decade-long transaction trend has already weakened, and market pricing has not yet fully absorbed that gap.

The rental market continues Penang's statewide theme of honest disclosure — no institution publishes data, and all figures are estimated from developer marketing and portal asking prices. Belleview's own 2021 materials show Type B (1,851sqft) renting at RM 7,500/month and Type A (2,228sqft) at RM 8,500/month; this project's representative Type C unit (1,787sqft) is estimated proportionally at roughly RM 7,300/month, implying a gross yield of ~3.89%, landing in the 3.5-4.0% band. As an island residential-title property, MBPP explicitly bans short-term rental — any short-term-income marketing claims are unenforceable. On exit, the quantum of RM 2.255mn implies a household income threshold of roughly RM 24,805/month, between Penang's P95 and P99, excluding the general local buyer pool entirely; on the foreign side, quantum is far above the levy sweet-spot and cliff bands, reverting to the general 3% standard, and MM2H eligibility is clearly met with ample margin — one of the site's few cases to clear the federal Platinum tier as well. On the developer side, Belleview is this framework's first live test, with its 7.2 baseline already reflecting the GEM Residences delay and the sold, no-alignment status of 1st Avenue/Aman Central; this card's own verification of the project's decade-long management record found no reporting of JMB/MC disputes or leak claims, nor any bifurcated-management litigation — but per the framework's epistemological note, this remains a provisional marker, not confirmation of a genuinely clean record.

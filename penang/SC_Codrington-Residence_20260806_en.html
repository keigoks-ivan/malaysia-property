Codrington Residence

Sits on the highest location anchor on the Penang ruler (Pulau Tikus / inland Gurney, 8.0) and enters at roughly RM 1,103 psf — about 7% below the transacted median of completed boutique comparables in the same neighbourhood (Moulmein Rise 1,242 / Setia V 1,193 / Cantonment 1,191 psf). That discount is the strongest single feature of this card. But the "cannot be manufactured" premise has one large opening directly opposite: the Penang Turf Club's 118-203 acres are George Town's largest undeveloped tract, failed to sell as a whole in late 2024, and are now being broken into lots for auction. Add a ~3.4% estimated gross yield and the fact that Pulau Tikus has no LRT station at all, and the location premium is almost exactly cancelled by carry and appreciation.

Under the base case (+3%/yr, the island residential strata anchor), the foreign buyer's 10-year net outcome is about RM 126,000 lower than the Malaysian buyer's — roughly 8.9% of the purchase price. The 8% stamp duty, 1.5% levy, RM 10,000 consent fee, 30% non-resident rental tax and permanent 10% RPGT, plus the higher foreign down payment and estimated rate premium, together create that structural gap — but because this quantum lands squarely in the island-strata 1.5% levy sweet spot, the gap is about 1.5pp narrower than for Penang projects above RM 1.5m. Breaking even on all cash costs (excluding opportunity cost) requires price growth of roughly 2.0%/yr for a Malaysian buyer and 2.7%/yr for a foreign buyer — both below the 3.0% island strata baseline, so there is nominal margin of safety; add the opportunity cost of the down payment and the annual net outflow (about RM 44,000/yr for a Malaysian buyer) and that bar rises materially. Effective-scarcity verdict: Pulau Tikus scarcity is real but priced — a small parcel transacted at RM 1,287 psf and land is up roughly 60% in four years, on real deals with real buyers. That is categorically different from the false scarcity of the Gurney 26-acre reclaimed parcel, which asks inland prices and finds no buyer even at a 30% haircut. But the same evidence marks the moat's boundary: 118-203 acres opposite are being auctioned in lots, and the whole-tract asking works out at roughly RM 292 psf — land acquired at scale costs far less than retail parcels. Conclusion: this location moat protects the price floor (replacement cost), not the quantity of supply. Hence 0.5 is deducted from Penang's highest location anchor of 8.0, and no bonus of any kind is awarded.

◆ In-Depth Narrative

Project Deep Dive

Codrington Residence stands on Lebuhraya Codrington opposite the Jalan Yeoh Guan Seok junction, inside prime Pulau Tikus — the area The Edge defines as taking in Gurney Drive, Kelawei Road, Burma Road, Jalan Tunku Abdul Rahman and Jalan Utama, and the location to which this framework assigns Penang's highest anchor of 8.0. It is a joint venture between BSG Property, whose parent Oriental Holdings Berhad has been listed on the Bursa Main Board since 1964 and whose property business dates to the 1950s, and VST Group, a private Penang developer. The scheme is a single 29-storey tower of 198 units at 11 units per floor, all three-bedroom, 1,288-1,615 sq ft with two car parks, on freehold residential title. The developer's 2023 material states completion at end-2026; some portals show 2027, so handover dates and liquidated-damages terms should be confirmed in writing before purchase.

Scarcity is the part of this card that most needs careful reading. Pulau Tikus land has moved from RM 800-900 psf to RM 1,400 psf in four years; in January 2026 a 2.32-acre freehold plot sold by tender for RM 130 million, or RM 1,287 psf, and at least 15 parcels changed hands across the area in 20 months. These are real transactions with real buyers — categorically unlike the false scarcity of the Gurney 26-acre reclaimed parcel, which asks inland prices and still finds no buyer at a 30% discount. Yet the same body of evidence marks where the moat ends: the Penang Turf Club's 203.4 acres — 118 of them priced at RM 1.5 billion, about RM 292 psf on a whole-tract basis — sit directly opposite, are George Town's largest undeveloped land, failed to sell as a whole in late 2024, and are now being broken into lots for auction, with two local parties interested as at May 2026. This area is also outside the UNESCO core and buffer zones with no height cap at all, while RSNPP 2040 is turning the blanket 76-metre prohibition into terrain-specific assessment. The three-layer audit therefore reads land ✗, product ✓, regulatory ✗, and this card deducts 0.5 from the 8.0 anchor. In one line: this location moat protects the price floor, not the quantity of supply.

On pricing, this is one of the rare discount cases in the current Penang batch. With no same-project Brickz history (off-plan), the anchor drops to transacted medians of completed boutique condos in the same location — Moulmein Rise RM 1,242, Setia V RM 1,193, Cantonment RM 1,191, Gurney Paragon RM 1,188 psf, a median around RM 1,190. The representative unit here (3-bed, 1,288 sq ft, asking RM 1,420,000) implies roughly RM 1,103 psf, a discount of about 7.3% — and in the direction of a new project pricing below 8-13-year-old stock, which usually runs the other way. The bottom of the discount band (7.0) is taken because the anchor is not the same project (medium confidence), because the local secondary market is two-tiered (Gurney seafront older stock at RM 750 psf, district non-landed median only RM 357 psf), and because the Turf Club's RM 292 psf whole-tract asking weakens the "replacement cost floor at RM 1,600 psf" argument.

The rental market is the weakest link and carries the heaviest disclosure. No institution in Penang — not NAPIC, Penang Institute, The Edge or Knight Frank — publishes rent or yield data, so every figure here is derived from portal asking prices and labelled as an estimate. Three verified reference points: Desa Palma on this very road (3-bed, 1,200 sq ft, older stock) rents at RM 1,700 = RM 1.42 psf; Setia V Residences (new boutique, 3-bed, 1,449 sq ft) at RM 5,500 = RM 3.79 psf; Moulmein Rise (3+1 bed, 1,787 sq ft) around RM 6,000 = RM 3.36 psf. As a new inland project with mostly turf-club and city aspects, RM 3.11 psf (RM 4,000/month) is used, giving a gross yield of about 3.38% in the 3.0-3.5% band. As island residential title, MBPP expressly prohibits short-term letting — any marketing that leans on Airbnb or short-let income is unenforceable, and no short-let scenario is counted here. The foreign cashflow score consequently floors at the 1.5 clamp, compressing the local-foreign gap to 1.7, a permitted exception in this framework.

On exit, the RM 1.42m quantum implies a household income threshold of about RM 15,620, roughly Penang P88 — considerably wider than the near-P99 ultra-prime stock in the UNESCO core, which is why the Malaysian exit score of 6.4 is higher than most of this batch. The foreign side is the most interesting detail: RM 1.42m lands exactly in the island-strata levy sweet spot of RM 1.0-1.5m, where the next foreign buyer pays only 1.5%, making foreign entry friction about 1.5pp lighter than on projects above RM 1.5m. But under the base case of +3%/yr, the 10-year exit price of roughly RM 1.91m crosses the RM 1.5m cliff and that levy reverts to 3% — a point-in-time benefit rather than a ten-year one, and the card flags this while still awarding the +0.3. The Timur Laut district carries 973 completed unsold and 1,618 under-construction unsold units, triggering both hard rules.

10-year scenario baseline: entry August 2026, exit August 2036 (IRR is equity IRR, including down payment, annual net outflow and net disposal proceeds; estimated):
  • Base case (+3%/yr, the island residential strata baseline): Malaysian buyer cumulative net return about RM 177,000, equity IRR about 3.8%; foreign buyer about RM 51,000, equity IRR about 0.6%.
  • Bull case (Turf Club continues to find no taker and land repricing transmits into strata, +5.5%/yr): Malaysian about RM 679,000, IRR about 10.8%; foreign about RM 501,000, IRR about 4.9%.
  • Bear case (flat prices, 0%/yr): Malaysian net loss about RM 297,000, foreign about RM 374,000; equity IRR negative for both.
10-year exit liquidity: Penang publishes no days-on-market data and this project is off-plan (zero same-project transactions), so a same-location proxy is used — Midlands Condo at 6 transactions over 12 months and a state average of 4.4 per project per year, which reads as "normal" rather than active. The core risk is not the address but two specific things: how the 118-203 acres opposite are ultimately disposed of, and how thin the "above RM 1,000 psf new boutique" slice really is inside a district whose non-landed transacted median is only RM 357 psf.
◆ Conclusion

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • Penang's highest location anchor (Pulau Tikus / inland Gurney, 8.0): parcels of 0.5-2 acres, fragmented ownership, land up roughly 60% in four years to RM 1,400 psf — and on real transactions with real buyers (a 2.32-acre plot at RM 1,287 psf in January 2026)
  • Entry is about 7.3% below the transacted median of completed boutique comparables (RM 1,103 versus RM 1,190 psf) — a new project pricing under 8-13-year-old stock, which is unusual
  • Connectivity and amenity are among the strongest on this site: walkable Burma Road commercial grid and two Gurney malls, four international-grade private hospitals within 3km, Komtar within 15 minutes, and no dependence on any unbuilt rail line
  • Quantum of RM 1.42m lands in the island-strata "1.5% levy" sweet spot, making foreign entry friction about 1.5pp lighter than Penang projects above RM 1.5m; maintenance of RM 0.40 psf including sinking fund is below the framework's KL RM 0.6 psf placeholder
⚠ Points to note
  • [Where the moat ends] The Penang Turf Club's 203.4 acres opposite (118 acres at RM 1.5bn ≈ RM 292 psf whole-tract) are George Town's largest undeveloped land; they failed to sell as a whole in late 2024, are now auctioned in lots, and two local parties expressed interest in May 2026 — this is the single largest variable in the 10-year thesis and worth tracking continuously
  • [R4] Island residential title: MBPP prohibits short-term rental, so any short-let yield marketing is unenforceable; gross yield is only about 3.38% (estimated), placing the Malaysian cashflow score in the 3.0-3.5% band and floor-clamping the foreign score
  • [R1 / R2] Timur Laut is the island's heaviest supply district: 973 completed unsold triggers the foreign exit cap, and 1,618 under-construction unsold triggers the Malaysian exit deduction
  • [The levy sweet spot expires] Under the base case the 10-year exit price of about RM 1.91m crosses the RM 1.5m cliff and the next foreign buyer's levy reverts to 3%; this bonus is a point-in-time benefit
  • Worth confirming: the developer's site says "from 1,400 sq ft" while portals show 1,288-1,615 sq ft; completion is quoted as both Q4 2026 and 2027; and JV partner VST Group has a thin public record with no other high-rise in Pulau Tikus to evidence long-run property management — all of which should be confirmed with the developer in writing

Holding-Purpose Fit Table

The same score means different things depending on why you hold. Readers can map their own position against the "critical dimensions", "acceptable trade-offs" and "fit here".

🏠Main Residence
Critical dimensions: Connectivity (8.3), Scarcity & location moat (7.5), Developer & handover (6.8)
Acceptable trade-offs: Holding cashflow (no rent involved), appreciation momentum
Fit here: the best of the four purposes for this project. Pulau Tikus has among the densest daily amenity on the island — walkable wet market and the Burma Road commercial strip, two malls about 1.2km away, four international-grade private hospitals within 3km — and none of it waits on a rail line opening. A 29-storey single tower at 11 units per floor with RM 0.40 psf maintenance is pragmatic for long-term living. The caveat: once the Turf Club opposite is developed, construction noise and changed outlook will directly affect the turf-club-facing units.
📈Rental Investment
Critical dimensions: Holding cashflow (3.2 MY / 1.5 FG), Exit & next-buyer pool (6.4 MY / 4.5 FG)
Acceptable trade-offs: Scarcity (the location already provides the moat)
Fit here: low. A gross yield of about 3.38% (estimated) turns negative carry after maintenance and tax, and R4 expressly prohibits short-term rental — this is island residential title, so under the MBPP ban no short-let plan is executable and Airbnb cannot be used to top up returns. If rental income is the main objective, the appeal here lies in capital appreciation and address rather than cashflow; note too that the rent estimate (RM 3.11 psf) is unproven for this project, while older stock on the same road actually achieves RM 1.42 psf.
🏖Part-Time Use (< 30% occupancy per year)
Critical dimensions: Connectivity, Developer & handover, Scarcity
Acceptable trade-offs: Holding cashflow
Fit here: medium-high. For an MM2H holder, eligibility is unambiguous (RM 1.42m clears both the RM 500k state threshold and the RM 1m federal Gold tier) and the unit sits in the 1.5% levy sweet spot. Vacancy carrying cost is light — mainly maintenance of about RM 515/month plus assessment and quit rent. But foreign buyers bear 8% stamp duty, a three-year lock-in and state consent approval (adding 2-4 months in practice), so flexibility to move in and out is far lower than for a Malaysian buyer.
🌳Generational Hold (20+ years)
Critical dimensions: Scarcity & location moat (20%), Exit & next-buyer pool (the next generation's sale)
Acceptable trade-offs: near-term yield, near-term catalysts
Fit here: medium. Freehold title plus the replacement-cost floor of the state's most expensive land is a credible base, and quantum at P88 rather than P99 means the next generation sells into a wider pool. But this project has no irreversible regulatory lock — unlike the George Town core's 18-metre SAP cap, Pulau Tikus's supply constraint is purely land price and parcel fragmentation, and a price constraint is precisely what developer patience and land assembly at scale eventually dissolve. A 20-year holder should treat the Turf Club disposal and the post-RSNPP 2040 height policy as two variables requiring active tracking.

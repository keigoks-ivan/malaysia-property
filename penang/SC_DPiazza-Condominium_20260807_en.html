D'Piazza Condominium

This is the first card on this site confirmed to sit within 400m of an LRT Mutiara Line station — D'Piazza is roughly 195-345m in a straight line from SPICE station (S08), depending on which coordinate source is used (see d4). It is also the first real test of the "mid-price transit corridor" hypothesis: the Malaysian score of 6.5 matches the site's current ceiling, and its Malaysian exit score (7.5) is the highest on the site. The foreign side is this site's first R7 cross-threshold case — the same building's last-12-month Brickz transacted median (RM 530,000) already clears the MM2H threshold of RM 500,000 (cap 4.0, most units are within reach of MM2H and PR buyers alike), but the representative unit, Type A (≈RM 495,000), itself sits just below that threshold and can only be bought by a permanent resident (PR). A buyer targeting a lower floor or an unrenovated unit would face a narrower foreign pool than the cap of 4.0 suggests. The low price deepens the local buyer pool while simultaneously splitting the foreign buyer pool across the threshold within the same building.

Estimated 10-year equity IRR (approximate): Malaysian base ≈+4.0%, bull ≈+11.3%, bear ≈−9.5%; foreign (this table uses the Type A representative unit, whose asking price sits below the MM2H threshold, so the buyer who can actually execute this scenario is a PR buyer) base ≈+0.3%, bull ≈+4.5%, bear ≈−5.7%. Annual price growth needed to break even on total cash cost (excluding opportunity cost): Malaysian buyers need roughly 1.9%/year, PR buyers roughly 3.0%/year — the PR breakeven rate is already close to the island strata baseline itself (3.0%), leaving a much thinner margin of safety than for Malaysian buyers. This is the most important structural finding of this case: a low quantum is a cashflow advantage for Malaysian buyers, but for the foreign buyer of this specific representative unit (only PR qualifies) the same low price inflates fixed-friction costs as a share of the deal, largely erasing that advantage. An MM2H buyer purchasing a larger unit priced above the RM 530,000 building median would face the same cost structure but on a higher base, not itemized separately here.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Dive

D'Piazza Condominium sits on Jalan Mayang Pasir 1 / Persiaran Mahsuri in Bayan Baru (Barat Daya district), developed by private developer X-Scan Penang Sdn Bhd (a Seraimas Bina Group subsidiary) and completed in February 2010. It comprises four 19-storey blocks (one facing north, three arranged in a U-shape facing south) with a total of 706 units — one of the largest projects scored on this site to date. Only two unit types exist: Type A (3-bed 2-bath, 1,100 sqft) and Type B (3+1-bed 3-bath, 1,300 sqft) — there is no 2-bedroom product at all. This framework's general guideline for non-prime precincts is to use a 2-bedroom representative unit, but per the rule that unit size must come from the developer's actual product menu with no fabrication, this card instead uses the actual Type A unit — a necessary departure from the general rule, explicitly disclosed in Block 3. The north and west sides directly border the D'Piazza Mall retail complex, making this one of the most amenity-complete communities in the Bayan Baru town centre.

This is the first card on this site confirmed to sit within 400m of an LRT Mutiara Line station — the single most important finding of this case. The official description of LRT Mutiara Line's SPICE station (S08) places it "on Persiaran Mahsuri, opposite SPICE," and its stated service area explicitly names D'Piazza Mall, D'Piazza Condominium, Arena Residence, Taman Melati Jaya, and The One @ Penang Cybercity. Cross-checking coordinates: the SPICE station coordinates from penang-traveltips.com (5.32807,100.28174) sit only about 106m from SPICE Arena's official Wikipedia coordinates (5.328349,100.280827) — consistent with the "opposite SPICE" description and supporting the station coordinate's reliability. On that basis, D'Piazza Mall's coordinates (the landmark bordering this project's north/west side) put the distance to the station at roughly 195m; a separately listed set of coordinates for D'Piazza Condominium itself gives roughly 345m — both independent estimates fall within the 400m threshold, and even the more conservative (farther) 345m estimate leaves about a 55m buffer. This framework's prior nearest cases were Muze (≈600m) and Light Collection II (≈660m), both of which only earned the ≤800m band of +0.4; D'Piazza is the first case to trigger the +0.6 (≤400m) threshold. The coordinate estimates rely on third-party travel-site and map approximations rather than official station engineering drawings, so confidence is marked medium-high.

All three layers of the scarcity audit fail for this project: land — Bayan Baru is a mature town centre, and Barat Daya's district-wide completed-unsold stock of 883 units (triggering R1) confirms supply is not scarce; product — four mass-market high-rise blocks totaling 706 standard units, with abundant substitutable product along the corridor, and the tandem parking design is a deduction rather than a scarcity selling point; regulatory — flat urban land with no hillside or reclamation restrictions and no SAP-style lock. All three layers fail but no aggravating factor (such as an approved competing redevelopment on the same street) was found, so the location baseline of 6.0 holds with no further deduction. On pricing, the same building's real last-12-month Brickz transactions (11 of them) show a median of RM 499 psf, while current listings sit at a median of roughly RM 450 psf — a discount of roughly 9.8%, a rare case on this site of "asking below the project's own recent real transactions." This lands in the discount band of the valuation dimension; d7 is set at 7.0 (the conservative end, given the mixed-type sample).

The rental market follows Penang's usual honest-disclosure baseline — portal-listing-derived, no institutional data. Multiple actual listings for the representative unit (Type A, 1,100 sqft) cluster around RM 1,800-1,900/month; the estimate used is RM 1,850, giving a gross yield of roughly 4.5%, at the upper edge of the 4.0-4.5% band, giving a Malaysian d3 baseline of 4.5, plus +0.2 for Bayan Baru's qualification as an E&E corporate-lease cluster, for a total of 4.7. As an island residential-title property, R4 mandates excluding short-term rental income; the foreign cashflow score is fixed at Malaysian −2.0 = 2.7.

Exit is where this case's central tension is sharpest. The representative unit's quantum of roughly RM 495,000 falls in the ≤RM 600k local first-time-buyer band, giving a Malaysian exit score of 7.5 — the highest Malaysian exit score on this site to date. This confirms the more optimistic half of the "mid-price transit corridor" hypothesis: a low quantum genuinely deepens the local buyer pool, and combined with the rare ≤400m LRT bonus, it lifts the Malaysian total score (6.5) to match the site's current ceiling. The foreign side is this card's most important methodological correction during review: R7 tests "can a buyer legally purchase at exit," and the price basis should be the same-building real transacted median, not a single unit's asking price (consistent with the valuation dimension's §7 principle of "use real transactions, not someone else's asking price") — the same building's last-12-month Brickz transacted median is RM 530,000, already above the MM2H state threshold of RM 500,000, meaning more than half of transacted units sit above the threshold and MM2H buyers can buy most of this building, triggering R7's middle tier, cap 4.0 (both MM2H and PR qualify, on par with Botanica.CT/Scots Pavilion), rather than the lowest tier (cap 3.0) that would follow from looking only at the Type A representative unit's asking price (≈RM 495,000, about RM 5,000 below the threshold). But this is not a clean conclusion: the same 11 transactions' lower quartile (RM 465,000) and the Type A representative unit's own asking price both remain below the threshold, meaning the depth of the foreign buyer pool depends on which unit type, floor, and condition actually transacts — a buyer targeting a lower floor or unrenovated unit will face a narrower actual foreign pool than cap 4.0 suggests, approaching PR-only. Foreign dependency is still forced to ≈0 (general foreign individuals/institutions still don't legally qualify). Developer X-Scan Penang (Seraimas Bina Group) is a private debut-project developer with two specific negative findings — early handover delay and quality complaints, and the tandem parking design's structural inconvenience — pulling d5 down from the 6.0 baseline to 5.5.

10-year scenario baseline: assuming entry in 2026-08 and exit in 2036-08 (equity IRR, approximated with an annual cashflow model including down payment, yearly net outflow, and net exit proceeds):

  • Bull case (+5.5%/year): Malaysian buyers roughly RM 247,000, IRR roughly 11.3%; PR buyers roughly RM 164,000, IRR roughly 4.5%.
  • Bear case (0%/year, reflecting loose island supply and the tandem-parking design's long-run drag on resale leverage): Malaysian buyers lose roughly RM 94,000 over 10 years, IRR roughly −9.5%; PR buyers lose roughly RM 142,000, IRR roughly −5.7%.
10-year exit liquidity: Penang has no public days-on-market data; this project's own Brickz record shows 11 real transactions in the last 12 months, a relatively deep same-building transaction record by this site's standards. The core risk here is not locational scarcity (this project never claimed a scarcity premium) but whether the liquidity driven by the low quantum can be sustained, and whether the foreign exit pool (PR only) offers enough depth if a fast exit is ever needed.

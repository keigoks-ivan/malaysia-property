Sunway Wellesley Serene Villas

The site's first Seberang Perai Tengah (SPT) landed scorecard, and the first case anywhere on this framework to apply the mainland-terraced tier's ceiling growth bracket (baseline roughly +5.0%/year). The developer is billed as Sunway Bintang Sdn Bhd — verification found this entity is distinct from the three controversy events (Paya Terubong unbuilt, Sungai Ara's Federal Court ruling, Batu Kawan's terminated PDC JV) that anchor the Penang scorecard's default "Sunway (Penang) 6.3" developer score. Those three sit with Sunway City (Penang) Sdn Bhd and Umech Land Sdn Bhd respectively, not this entity — so this card applies a higher but still provisional 7.0, detailed in Block 2 and Block 6A. mm2home's marketing claim of "24×70 sqft terrace" checks out as inaccurate: the on-sale Serene Villas phase is actually a 3-storey townhome product, 1,701-2,615 sqft built-up. The representative unit prices around RM 720,000 — above the MM2H (RM 500k) and permanent-resident (RM 250k) thresholds, but below the general foreign individual/company threshold of RM 1,000,000 for mainland landed property — meaning ordinary foreign buyers cannot legally purchase it; the foreign exit pool is structurally narrowed to MM2H holders and PRs only. SPT's under-construction-unsold count of 1,271 has already triggered R2 (-0.3 to the local exit score); the completed-unsold figure specific to SPT could not be verified and is not assumed. The developer's own website invokes the proposed LRT extension via a disclaimer, which triggers the -0.3 marketing penalty under the framework's rules. The 10-year thesis rests on local Bukit Mertajam/SPT tenant and buyer depth, not location scarcity or an approved rail catalyst.

7.0

This card's central verification task. Sunway Bintang Sdn Bhd (company number 199201007071, registered 1992-04-24, registered address at Persiaran Mahsuri 1/3, Sunway Tunas, Bayan Lepas) is confirmed as a Sunway Group mainland-Penang development vehicle — the official project site (sunwaywellesley.com) names this entity directly, and multiple company databases (Experian/EMIS/NEWPAGES) cross-confirm it. The Penang anchor table's default "Sunway (Penang) 6.3" score is built on three deductions, all verified in this research to sit with different legal entities: (1) the RM 2.3bn Paya Terubong flagship (announced 2017, never built) was developed by Sunway City (Penang) Sdn Bhd; (2) the 2023-01-20 Federal Court ruling voiding the Sungai Ara hillside planning approval as ultra vires the Town and Country Planning Act 1976 involved the same Sunway City (Penang) Sdn Bhd; (3) the Batu Kawan JV terminated by PDC in 2023-10 was held through Umech Land Sdn Bhd (70% Sunway-owned, acquired just two days before signing). All three sit with entities distinctly named from Sunway Bintang Sdn Bhd. Per the framework's three permitted conclusions, this card adopts (b): same group, different legal entity, an independently run mainland business line — warranting a higher but still conservative score of 7.0 rather than a direct application of 6.3. Upward factors: the parent, Sunway Berhad, is Bursa Tier-1 with unquestioned financial backing; Sunway Bintang's own verifiable delivery record (the Sunway Wellesley masterplan itself, running since 2012 across multiple phases, including Garden Villas — 214 units delivered 2016 and reportedly sold out — plus a separate serviced-apartment project under construction on Jalan Pangkor, George Town, at an RM 61.45m land cost / RM 274m GDV) shows an ongoing multi-project platform, not an isolated single-project SPV, which reduces abandonment risk. Reasons for staying provisional rather than moving to the Bursa Tier-1 peer level (7.5) or KL's 8.0: this research could not directly pull the primary SSM (Companies Commission of Malaysia) filing to confirm "wholly-owned subsidiary" status or check for shared directors with the other two entities — the finding rests on secondary company databases and cross-referenced news reporting. And while the three controversies sit with different legal persons, they still share the same "Sunway" brand and (presumably) the same group governance culture — a pattern of three separate controversies within roughly a decade across sister entities cannot be entirely ruled out as reflecting a systemic weakness that could resurface in this entity's own future projects. No evidence found of MBI-style counterparty exposure, bifurcated strata management (not applicable — this is landed, not strata), hillside exposure (flat terrain), or George Town heritage-compliance issues (not in a heritage zone) attaching to this entity.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Summary

Sunway Wellesley sits in Bukit Mertajam, Seberang Perai Tengah (SPT) — a large masterplan (roughly 86 acres, ~RM 1bn GDV) that Sunway Bintang Sdn Bhd has developed in phases since 2012, including Garden Villas (214 semi-detached units, delivered 2016 and reportedly sold out). This card scores the currently on-sale phase, Serene Villas: 9.03 acres, 210 units, freehold, GreenRE-certified, launched December 2024, completing December 2027. The mm2home listing describes the project as "24×70 sqft terraces"; checking the official project site (sunwaywellesley.com) and Sunway Property's own page shows this description is inaccurate — Serene Villas is actually a 3-storey townhome product, 1,701-2,615 sqft built-up, a meaningfully different size and format from a standard single/double-storey 24×70 terrace. This card uses the verified facts.

On location, Bukit Mertajam sits in the "Seberang Perai other" bucket of the Penang anchor table, the table's lowest baseline at 5.0 (Butterworth state-level landed median of RM 310 psf; land effectively unlimited). All three supply-side audit layers fail: on land, this card found a specific first-hand data point — Sunway Bintang Sdn Bhd itself is understood to hold adjacent land for a further ~696-unit terrace plus 26-shoplot scheme (disclosed pending approval as of 2021-11), meaning even the developer holds bankable adjoining land; on product, 3-storey townhomes are a common, replicable format across SPT/Bukit Mertajam; on regulation, the terrain is flat with no hillside restriction and no heritage designation. All three layers fail, but this is already priced into the anchor itself, so d1 stays at 5.0 without a further deduction.

This card's most important piece of work is the developer entity verification. The Penang anchor table's default "Sunway (Penang) 6.3" score is built entirely from three deductions: the RM 2.3bn Paya Terubong flagship (announced 2017, never built), the 2023-01-20 Federal Court ruling voiding the Sungai Ara hillside planning approval as beyond the Town and Country Planning Act 1976, and PDC's 2023-10 termination of the Batu Kawan JV. Checking each event's actual developer or JV entity, this card found none of the three sit with Sunway Bintang Sdn Bhd: Paya Terubong and Sungai Ara were both developed through Sunway City (Penang) Sdn Bhd, while the Batu Kawan JV ran through Umech Land Sdn Bhd (70% Sunway-owned, acquired just two days before signing) — three legal entities distinct in name from Sunway Bintang. Of the framework's three permitted conclusions, this card adopts (b): same group, independently run mainland business line, warranting a higher but conservative score. This card sets developer at 7.0 rather than applying 6.3 directly. Upward factors include the Bursa Tier-1 parent Sunway Berhad's financial backing, and Sunway Bintang's own verifiable, ongoing multi-phase delivery record (Sunway Wellesley itself, plus a separate George Town serviced-apartment project under construction). The reasons for not moving to the Bursa Tier-1 peer level (7.5) or KL's 8.0: this research could not directly pull the primary SSM company filing to confirm ownership structure or director overlap, and while the three controversies sit with different legal persons, they still share the same Sunway brand and presumed group governance culture, so a systemic link cannot be fully ruled out — hence explicitly marked provisional.

On appreciation, this project applies the mainland-terraced baseline of 6.5 — the Penang anchor table's highest growth bracket anywhere (baseline ~+5.0%/year, per NAPIC Seberang Perai terraced +5.1% off a low base), the first time any of the site's 12 cards has applied it. But this card also found a deduction: the developer's own official website invokes the proposed LRT extension via a disclaimer (paraphrased: "the proposed LRT line shown herein was sourced from publicly available information, forming part of the Penang Transport Master Plan") — while the Mutiara Line's currently approved and under-construction scope reaches only Penang Sentral (Butterworth), with the Bukit Mertajam extension existing only as an official's verbal "if there is a need" comment, with no alignment, budget, or approval. This constitutes concrete evidence of "developer using an unapproved extension as a selling point," triggering -0.3, for a net d4 of 6.2. On valuation, as a new under-construction project with no same-project or same-area Brickz comps, this card falls back to the state-level Butterworth landed median of RM 310 psf as a proxy anchor; the representative unit's asking price runs roughly +16% above it, with low confidence, giving d7 of 5.0.

On holding and exit, estimated gross yield is only about 3.0-3.5% (low confidence, no project-specific rental sample), giving local d3 of 3.2 and a foreign score floored at the 1.5 clamp. On exit, the local quantum (RM 720,000) sits in the RM 600k-1.2m upgrader-mainstay band; adding SPT's under-construction-unsold count of 1,271 (triggering R2, -0.3), local d6 comes to 7.2. The foreign side is this card's central finding, and applies the generalized R3 logic: R3's substance is not "island landed" as a product rule, but the price fact that ordinary foreign buyers cannot legally purchase, applied via a three-tier ladder by exit price — at or above the general threshold, run the standard formula; below the general threshold but at or above MM2H (RM 500,000), cap at 4.0; below MM2H but at or above PR (RM 250,000), cap at 3.0. The mainland landed foreign individual/company threshold is RM 1,000,000, and the representative unit and primary listed band (RM 548,000-770,500) both sit below it — ordinary foreign individuals and companies cannot legally purchase. But the price clears the MM2H threshold (RM 500,000), placing this case in the ladder's middle rung — unlike Azuria (every threshold unmet, capped at 3.0), MM2H holders and permanent residents here retain a legal path, so the cap is 4.0. This card computes the raw score through the standard §6.7 items (yielding 5.0, including MM2H compliance +0.3, mandatory state consent -0.3, and the mandatory 3-year resale ban -0.2), then takes min(5.0, 4.0); foreign d6 comes to 4.0, with foreign dependency recorded as ≈0 under R3 logic. R1 (SPT completed-unsold) district-specific figures could not be verified, and this card discloses that honestly rather than assuming the trigger.

Key caveats: (1) All three scarcity layers fail — the 10-year thesis should not rest on location scarcity, but on local SPT/Bukit Mertajam ownership and rental demand depth; (2) the developer entity verification result is provisional — the 7.0 score reflects "decoupled from the controversy events," not "governance proven sound," and should be revisited if primary SSM records or new adverse findings specific to this entity emerge; (3) the foreign exit pool narrows structurally to MM2H holders and permanent residents, both segments of unverified scale in the Penang market, making the 10-year foreign exit thesis relatively fragile; (4) this is a newly launched (2024-12), under-construction project with zero secondary-market evidence — this card discloses "no data" honestly, and transaction-depth calibration should follow once the project completes; (5) the LRT-extension marketing finding triggers -0.3 — buyers should discount any "LRT is coming" pricing argument, as no approved alignment currently exists.

⚠ Caveats

3 Residence @ Karpal Singh Drive

This card tests the "mid-price transit-corridor" hypothesis. 3 Residence (built by IJM Land, completed 2021-2022, 478 units, freehold) is the site's first scorecard genuinely located in Jelutong / Bandar Sri Pinang, and the only candidate that simultaneously clears all three screens — completed status, a real same-building Brickz transaction record, and a representative-unit quantum landing in the RM 600k-1.2m band — while fronting directly onto the Persiaran Karpal Singh promenade and sitting near the under-construction Bandar Sri Pinang LRT station (S18). Key finding: the adjacent Jelutong landfill rehabilitation + 70-acre reclamation project (by PLB Engineering, unrelated to 3 Residence's developer IJM Land) has failed its EIA five times with zero groundbreaking as of 2026-07 — a near-term supply relief, but one the framework's R6 rule explicitly flags as administrative resistance, not a regulatory lock, and reversible at any time. The representative unit's quantum (~RM 918,000) sits below the island's general foreign threshold of RM 1.0m but above the RM 500k MM2H floor, triggering the mid-tier R7 cap and capping the foreign exit score at 4.0. Malaysian 6.3, Foreign 5.5 — both land in T4. Neither clears the site's existing 6.6 ceiling, a partial disconfirmation of the "mid-price transit corridor scores higher" hypothesis: connectivity remains a secondary corridor (not walkable-core) and cash flow is a modest 4.67% (estimated) — the transit narrative alone did not lift the total score. This card's real edge is exit depth (an unusually deep local buyer pool for the site), not location scarcity or the LRT bonus.

◆ In-Depth Narrative

Project Deep Dive

3 Residence sits at 3, Lebuh Sungai Pinang 1, Bandar Sri Pinang, Jelutong, built by Bursa Main Market T1 developer IJM Land Berhad and completed in 2021-2022 as twin 22-storey towers with 478 units total; developer inventory is fully sold and the project now trades on an active secondary market. All four unit types (Type A 1,031 / B 851 / C 845 / D 1,157 sqft) are 3-bed/2-bath, with no 2-bedroom line — the framework's default representative unit for non-precinct island districts is a 2-bedroom, but since this project offers none, this card follows §8's "determine and note based on the actual product line" rule and instead uses the mid-size Type A (1,031 sqft) as the representative unit, explicitly disclosing the deviation. The building fronts directly onto the Persiaran Karpal Singh promenade, and this is the site's first scorecard genuinely located in the Jelutong / Bandar Sri Pinang district that also combines completed status with a real same-building Brickz transaction record.

The most notable finding in this card concerns the supply threat immediately adjacent to the site: the Jelutong landfill rehabilitation + 70-acre reclamation project, led by PLB Engineering Berhad (under a joint-development agreement with PDC and the state government dating to 2020, entirely unrelated to 3 Residence's developer IJM Land), has now failed its Environmental Impact Assessment five times as of 2026-07 — the fifth submission, filed 2026-03-18, was confirmed "Tidak Diluluskan" (not approved) by late April 2026, capping off more than six years and at least seven extensions with zero groundbreaking, and 98.6% of public feedback during the EIA display period opposed the project. Chief Minister Chow Kon Yeow said on 2026-06-24 that no further extension would be granted if EIA approval wasn't secured by 2026-02-26 — that deadline has since passed, the fifth rejection itself arrived 20 days late, and as of the most recent check (2026-07-12) the state government still has not made a final decision, saying only that a "final deadline" will be set. This is exactly the reasoning behind the framework's 6.0 baseline for the Jelutong district — a low near-term supply threat — but it is also precisely the trap R6 warns about: this is administrative resistance, not a regulatory lock. The state government could approve an extension or eventually pass the EIA at any time, so today's "zero groundbreaking" cannot be treated as a permanent supply ceiling. This card therefore grants the land tier a ✓ but with full disclosure, and does not let it inflate the d1 score.

Both the product and zoning tiers of the three-tier audit come back ✗: the Bandar Sri Pinang / Jelutong waterfront corridor already hosts multiple comparable high-rise products — Ocean View, completed in 2004; Setia Sky Ville, completed the same year as this project (2021, median RM 707 psf); and SP Setia's currently-selling new project Setia SV2 (268 units, RM 343m GDV, next to Setia Sky Ville) — showing that this project's twin-tower, high-density format is a replicable product. No SAP height cap or hillside restriction applies. But the effective-scarcity test surfaces a positive signal worth recording: the same-building Brickz median of RM 841 psf carries roughly a 15-16% premium over the island-wide high-rise state benchmark (RM 723-731 psf), and roughly a 19% premium over Setia Sky Ville — same district, same completion year — real transaction-price evidence for the first-row Persiaran Karpal Singh waterfront position, not asking-price rhetoric. This card therefore adds a micro-location bonus of +0.2, landing d1 at 6.2 net.

The LRT Mutiara Line is at 8.99% systemwide progress (2026-06); using two independent coordinate sources, this building sits roughly 368m (Wikidata) to 437m (penang-traveltips) from the planned Bandar Sri Pinang station (S18), averaging ~402m — landing almost exactly on the 400m threshold, reflecting that the station is still under construction with no officially surveyed centre-point coordinate yet published. This card makes the conservative call, counting it in the ≤800m band (+0.4) rather than the ≤400m band (+0.6) that no card on this site has yet achieved, and fully discloses this sensitivity. Worth noting: Bandar Sri Pinang station is one of the most advanced construction sites on the entire Mutiara Line — the systemwide groundbreaking ceremony in January 2025 was held here, and by June 2026 the station's foundation and columns were complete with six piers under construction — but per the framework's rule, the LRT bonus is banded on systemwide progress, not station-level lead time.

The rental market continues Penang's site-wide honest-disclosure posture: portal-aggregated estimates put gross rental yield at roughly 4.67% (rates.my / EdgeProp, estimated, not institutional data), with the representative unit (1,031 sqft) implying an estimated monthly rent of ~RM 3,570, landing in the 4.5-5.0% band and giving d3 a Malaysian score of 5.2. As an island residential-titled property, R4 mandates that short-term rental income not be counted, so the foreign cash-flow score is fixed at Malaysian −2.0 = 3.2. Developer IJM Land anchors at 7.7; checking every Penang-specific deduction category confirms none applies to this project (the reclamation-permitting risk belongs to the unrelated PLB Engineering, not IJM Land), so the net score holds at 7.7 — the highest of the seven dimensions on this card.

The exit dimension is this card's core highlight: the representative unit's quantum of ~RM 918,000 lands at the core of the RM 600k-1.2m local-upgrader main band, with an annual turnover rate of ~2.3% (11 transactions / 478 units, trailing 12 months); the Malaysian exit score starts at a baseline of 7.0 and, after Timur Laut's 1,618 under-construction-unsold units trigger R2 (−0.3), nets at 6.7 — an unusually deep local-liquidity case for this site. The foreign side triggers the framework's R7 three-tier ladder: the representative-unit quantum (RM 918,000) is below the general foreign individual/entity threshold of RM 1.0m but above the MM2H threshold of RM 500k, producing a raw computed value of 4.5 that is capped to a foreign exit score of 4.0 — the effective buyer pool narrows to MM2H visa holders and permanent residents (PR), since general foreign individuals/entities are legally barred from purchasing it. But this cap does not apply to the building as a whole: if a larger unit, Type D (1,157 sqft), transacted near the top of the asking range (~RM 1,006 psf), the exit price could reach ~RM 1,164,000, crossing the general foreign threshold and voiding the R7 cap for that transaction.

◆ Verdict

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • Built by IJM Land (anchor score 7.7), fronting the Persiaran Karpal Singh waterfront promenade directly; same-building Brickz transactions carry a genuine ~15-16% premium over the island-wide high-rise state benchmark, backed by real transaction evidence
  • Quantum ~RM 918,000, one of the deeper local buyer pools on the site; Malaysian exit score of 6.7 with an annual turnover rate of ~2.3%
  • The adjacent Jelutong landfill + 70-acre reclamation project has now failed its EIA five times with zero groundbreaking, keeping the near-term supply threat low (though this is administrative resistance, not a regulatory lock)
  • MM2H visa holders and permanent residents (PR) can legally purchase this representative unit, so the foreign exit pool is not fully zeroed out; 146 active rental listings suggest a workable tenant pool
⚠ Caveats
  • [R7 triggered] Representative-unit quantum (~RM 918k) is below the general foreign threshold of RM 1.0m — general foreign individuals/entities cannot legally purchase it, and a cap of 4.0 applies; but MM2H (threshold RM 500k) and PR (threshold RM 250k) visa holders remain eligible, and a larger unit (Type D) transacting could void the cap
  • [LRT distance right at the threshold] Coordinate estimates span 368-437m, averaging 402m, right on the 400m bonus threshold; this card makes the conservative call (+0.4 rather than +0.6), and the station's final built position has not been officially surveyed and published
  • [Reclamation dispute is reversible] The adjacent Jelutong landfill + 70-acre reclamation has failed its EIA five times, but the state government still had not made a final decision as of 2026-07-12 — this is administrative resistance, not a regulatory lock, and could reverse
  • [R2] Timur Laut's 1,618 under-construction-unsold units trigger a Malaysian exit deduction of −0.3; R1's 973 completed-unsold units also trigger a foreign cap of 6.0, but this project's score is already superseded by the lower R7 cap of 4.0
  • [Product tier ✗] Multiple comparable high-rise products already exist along the corridor (Ocean View / Setia Sky Ville / Setia SV2); this project does not claim a scarcity premium, and its location moat is far less durable than higher-anchored districts
  • Recommended to verify: gross rental yield is a portal-based estimate; management fee has no building-specific data and uses the KL benchmark on an interim basis; the listing rate (55/478) is for reference only, since portal totals conflict with each other — all of the above should be confirmed in writing with an agent or the developer

Maris by E&O @ Andaman Island

Penang's only reclaimed-island launch with a genuine live new-build pricing ladder: a 49-storey, 516-unit serviced residence tower launched early 2025 and 75% sold by mid-2026, now with a second bridge route via the Gurney Bridge that opened December 2025. But E&O itself still holds 756.9 acres of undeveloped land — RM60 billion GDV, roughly a 30-year runway — and the same precinct has seen 7 competing launches in 4 years, so the scarcity story still awaits proof of a transacted premium. NAPIC's Timur Laut district already has 973 completed-unsold condos, capping foreign exit, and 1,618 under-construction-unsold units drag down the Malaysian exit score too.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Dive

Maris by E&O sits in the Gurney Green commercial precinct of Andaman Island (Seri Tanjung Pinang Phase 2 / STP2), a 49-storey serviced-residence tower launched by E&O Berhad in early 2025. Across 3.2 acres it comprises 516 units plus 8 waterfront retail lots, GDV approximately RM700m, in only two layouts — 2BR at 979 sqft and 3BR from 1,177 to 1,356 sqft. Cross-verified across three independent sources (StarProperty, Penang Hyperlocal, The Edge Malaysia), the title is freehold residential, though buyers should still confirm with official title documents. As of mid-2026, E&O's GM of Marketing & Sales states the project is 75% sold — one of the few STP2 launches where current pricing is still live and actionable, which is why this card picks Maris over already-sold-out siblings like Arica or The Meg.

The central tension here is exactly the "reclaimed-land false scarcity" question this framework was built to test. Andaman presents a textbook scarcity story — freehold, single master developer, a genuine island. But E&O's own land bank flips that picture: it still holds 756.9 acres undeveloped, roughly RM60bn GDV, about a 30-year runway, and the same 75-acre Gurney Green precinct has seen seven near-identical launches — The Meg, Arica, Senna, Fera, The Lume, Maris, AVÉA — in four years. The three-layer scarcity audit (land, product, regulatory) scores ✗ across the board: supply is set by the developer's own release cadence, not physics or regulation. This framework's anchor table documents a directly comparable case — a reclaimed 26-acre Gurney parcel priced at parity with genuine Pulau Tikus transactions that still cannot find a buyer. Maris offers no verifiable resale-premium evidence today simply because it isn't complete. That doesn't mean it repeats Gurney's failure, but absent proof of a realized premium, honest scoring withholds the bonus rather than converting marketing narrative into points.

On connectivity, Andaman now links to the main island via two independent bridges: the Andaman Bridge (2022, to STP1/Tanjung Tokong) and the new eight-lane Gurney Bridge (opened December 2025, direct to Gurney Drive/Straits Quay, officially framed as bypassing the congested Tanjong Tokong stretch). This is a genuine timing tailwind — Maris benefits from route redundancy and shorter drive times that earlier-scored STP2 projects didn't have, placing it in the "strong single-cluster access" band. Gurney Paragon Mall, Gleneagles Penang and Island Hospital remain bridge-distance rather than walkable, and the airport is a 35-40 minute drive, capping the ceiling here.

Appreciation momentum starts from the island-strata baseline of 6.0, gets small lifts from Gurney Bay proximity and the MM2H corridor, but is dragged down harder by the precinct's own oversupply (seven launches in four years) and 3-4 years still to completion, netting to just 5.3 — the second dimension, after scarcity, where this card is honest about a real weakness: the developer's own launch cadence structurally suppresses its later projects' appreciation. On pricing, with no Andaman/STP2 secondary transactions to anchor against, this card falls back to the six completed STP1/Tanjung Tokong Brickz medians (~RM1,217.5 psf, confidence: medium); Maris's representative unit is estimated at ~RM1,130 psf, a discount of roughly −7%, and is priced more conservatively than sibling The Lume's ultra-luxury ~RM1,280 psf.

Holding cashflow is the weakest dimension overall: zero measured rental data since the project isn't complete, gross yield estimated at ~3.2%, the low end of Penang prime's usual 2.7-3.8% range; R4 bars any short-let credit here, and developer short-let claims are unenforceable. The foreign cashflow score clamps at the floor because the Malaysian score already bottoms out, compressing the gap below the usual 2.0 — a permitted clamp collision, not an error. On exit, Timur Laut's 973 completed-unsold units trigger the foreign cap (R1, 6.0) and its 1,618 under-construction-unsold units trigger a Malaysian deduction (R2, −0.3); the quantum test puts the next-buyer income threshold at ~RM14,630/month, in Penang's P83-P90 band — why local first-time buyers and investors both score ✗, leaving exit dependent on foreign MM2H owner-occupiers and out-of-state/overseas Malaysians.

E&O Berhad itself is financially sound — record FY2026 profit of RM221.2m, Andaman Ph2 genuinely ahead of schedule, and the Tee family's control of both E&O and contractor Kerjaya Prospek delivers real alignment of interest. But this card applies an additional Penang-specific legal adjustment: the Court of Appeal ruled 2026-06-30 that state planning authorities lack power to grant reclamation planning permission — a principle applying equally to every Penang reclamation project, STP2/Andaman included. This deducts for legal-title uncertainty, not delay — E&O's build progress remains ahead of, not behind, schedule.

◆ Verdict

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • One of the few Penang reclaimed-island launches with a genuine live new-build pricing ladder — 75% sold by mid-2026, so current pricing is still actionable (most STP2 siblings are already sold out and their pricing reference has expired)
  • Freehold residential title cross-verified across three independent sources, not commercial — no title-driven financing friction beyond the standard R4 short-let restriction
  • The Gurney Bridge, opened December 2025, gives this project a genuine second bridge route bypassing the congested Tanjong Tokong stretch — connectivity at scoring time is better than for earlier STP2 cards
  • Entry valuation discount of roughly −7% against the real STP1/Tanjung Tokong transacted anchor; pricing itself is not aggressive, and is more conservative than sibling project The Lume's ultra-luxury positioning
  • E&O Berhad is financially sound with record FY2026 profit; the Tee family's dual control of developer and contractor delivers a genuine alignment of interest
⚠ Caveats
  • All three scarcity-audit layers score ✗: E&O itself still holds 756.9 acres undeveloped with a 30-year runway, the same precinct has seen 7 launches in 4 years, and no Brickz resale-premium evidence exists anywhere — measured against this framework's own documented false-scarcity case, the scarcity narrative here still awaits proof
  • The project is not yet complete (handover projected 2029-2030); there is no measured rental or secondary-transaction data of any kind, so cashflow and exit scores are estimates/scenario projections with lower confidence than completed cards
  • R4 applies on this island residential title: no short-let income may ever be credited, and any developer claims of short-let yield are treated as unenforceable
  • NAPIC Timur Laut district's 973 completed-unsold units trigger the foreign exit cap (R1); its 1,618 under-construction-unsold units trigger a Malaysian exit deduction (R2)

  • The foreign break-even rate (≈3.0%/year) is almost identical to this card's own baseline appreciation rate, leaving an extremely thin margin of safety; a buyer's actual capital lock-up, including the construction period, runs to roughly 13-14 years — longer than most completed cards on this site

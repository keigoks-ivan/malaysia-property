The Light Collection II @ The Light Waterfront

A proven waterfront asset with real transaction depth, but asking prices now run ahead of the transacted anchor and exit friction is heavy. One of the founding precincts of The Light Waterfront, completed in 2014 with 297 units, this is a rare case where the building's own Brickz record (median RM 1,041 psf) is a high-confidence valuation anchor in its own right. It is also this desk's first card to stress-test two supply threats at once: reclaimed land does not earn a scarcity bonus by default (R6), and IJM's own masterplan still holds well over half its acreage unbuilt. Timur Laut district's 973 completed-unsold units trip the foreign exit cap, its 1,618 under-construction-unsold units trip the Malaysian exit penalty, and the current asking price already sits meaningfully above the building's own transacted median. The 10-year thesis here rests on brand resilience and a proven, liquid secondary market — not on land-layer scarcity holding up the exit price.

Under the base assumptions (3%/year appreciation, fully let for 10 years less 1 month vacancy, 5%/year opportunity cost), the foreign buyer nets about RM 87,000 less than the Malaysian buyer over 10 years (roughly 5.9% of the purchase price). The 8% stamp duty, the 1.5% levy sweet spot, the RM 10k consent fee, the 3-year moratorium, the 30% non-resident rental tax and the permanent 10% RPGT together form a structural premium of roughly 11.3% total entry friction, consistent with the state-level anchor. Break-even threshold: roughly 2.3%/year for Malaysian buyers and 2.7%/year for foreign buyers — both below the island residential-strata benchmark of +3.0%/year, so the base case itself clears break-even. But compared with other waterfront cards on this desk (e.g. Queens Q2's 1.5%/2.1%), this project's break-even bar sits higher and the margin is thinner, mainly because the ~3.1% gross yield (cashflow already clamped low) leaves less cushion, and the asking price already runs roughly 10% ahead of the Brickz anchor, raising the effective entry basis. In short: the "proven waterfront asset" narrative supports transaction depth and brand resilience, but thin cashflow, an elevated asking price and stacked supply pressure (both R1 and R2 triggered) together leave less room for error over 10 years than other cards on this desk.
◆ Exit Reality Check

Who Buys This in 10 Years

Three checks: a scarcity audit (can the supply side be replicated), a next-buyer pyramid (who can afford it in 10 years), and hard secondary-market evidence (turnover, listing ratio, discount). The return is only real the day it sells — the floor on exit price is set by scarcity, and the speed of exit is set by the depth of the next-buyer pool.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep-Dive Summary

The Light Collection II sits beside Lebuhraya Tun Dr Lim Chong Eu in Gelugor, a single 7-storey, 297-unit freehold residential-title condominium completed in 2014 by Jelutong Development Sdn Bhd, an IJM Land subsidiary. It belongs to the founding precinct of The Light Waterfront masterplan (The Light Linear + The Light Collection, 42 acres). This is Penang's oldest completed project on this desk and, so far, the only card covering an asset completed more than a decade ago — long enough for Brickz to accumulate a meaningful track record of its own transactions (7 sales, Nov 2023–Sep 2024, median RM 1,041 psf), making this a rare case where the building's own record is a high-confidence valuation anchor in its own right.

On location, Gelugor sits administratively within Timur Laut (Northeast) District, and the anchor table starts it at 6.0, the island default under the "Jelutong/Bandar Sri Pinang" line. All three scarcity layers fail on audit — the full Light Waterfront masterplan spans 152 acres, and the founding precinct plus Precinct 2 (The Light City, including Mezzo, The Essence and Lightwater Residences) together account for only roughly half of that, leaving IJM Land itself holding the largest reserve of undeveloped land in the immediate vicinity — the clearest possible evidence that reclaimed land does not earn scarcity by default. The same corridor has also seen recent follow-on launches — Mezzo (2021) and The Essence (315 units, not yet formally launched) from IJM itself, plus Lighthauz and Merione from third-party developer Exsim — meaning fresh competing supply keeps arriving. One genuine positive: the nearby Karpal Singh Drive reclamation project has now failed its environmental review five times (most recently March 2026) and remains unbroken ground, lowering near-term third-party competing reclamation supply — but this reduces only the "external" threat, not the fact that IJM's own land bank within the same masterplan remains substantial, so this card does not award a scarcity bonus for "reclaimed land" or "island scarcity."

The building itself is a single 7-storey block of 297 units across 24 layouts, from 517 sqft one-bedrooms to 3,617 sqft duplex/triplex four-bedrooms. Per the rules, Gelugor is not on the "island prime-precinct" list, so a 2-bedroom unit is the default representative type; Type B ("2 bedroom + study," 1,367–1,507sf) is the layout closest to a standard family 2BR product among the 24 available, and its mid-range size of roughly 1,420sf lines up closely with the implied size behind the Brickz median transaction price of RM 1,480,000 — suggesting this is genuinely where the bulk of resale activity concentrates. On amenities, Waterfront Shoppes (The Light City's 1-million-sq-ft flagship mall) belongs to the same masterplan but sits roughly 1.1km away in a straight line, not walkable, so daily life still relies heavily on driving. The LRT Mutiara Line's nearest station, Gelugor (S14), is estimated via public GPS coordinates at roughly 660m straight-line distance — inside the 800m walking threshold (medium confidence, as this is an estimate rather than an official confirmation) — with construction currently at 8.99%.

On the developer side, IJM Land tops Penang's anchor table (base 7.7); its own ownership of The Light City mall and convention centre gives it strong incentive alignment, and this building — one of its earliest, most track-record-verified Penang assets — is itself part of the positive evidence behind that high score. One new adverse finding needs disclosure, however: parent group IJM Corporation Berhad's FY2026 (year ended March 2026) consolidated net profit collapsed to just RM 3.24 million (from RM 403.37 million the year before), with a Q4 net loss of RM 173.9 million driven by RM 121.6 million in inventory impairment (including an office building in Changchun, China), a RM 51.0 million highway-maintenance provision in India, and RM 197.9 million in forex losses. On closer inspection, these charges concentrate in overseas assets and investments (China, India) — Malaysia's property division itself still posted a positive pre-tax profit of RM 126.5 million, which is not direct evidence of weakening local delivery capability — but the group-level balance-sheet stress is a fair caution on reinvestment headroom and leverage capacity, and this card applies a modest 0.2-point deduction as a result. Separately worth noting: Sunway's RM 11 billion takeover offer for IJM Corp lapsed on 6 April 2026 (only 33.43% shareholder acceptance, short of the 50% threshold), and IJM remains independently listed — a neutral development.

On pricing, this is a rare case where the building's own record is the valuation anchor — Brickz logged 7 transactions between November 2023 and September 2024, median RM 1,041 psf, price range RM 638,000 to 1,850,000. That data is now nearly two years old, however; current listing PSF spans a wide RM 800–1,784, with an estimated representative asking of roughly RM 1,150 psf — about a 10% premium over the Brickz median. Whether that premium reflects genuine appreciation or optimistic asking prices unconstrained by any MLS discipline cannot be fully disentangled with available data, and this uncertainty is reflected in the medium-confidence flag on the valuation dimension. On rental, a 1,420sf unit's estimated current asking rent is around RM 3,800/month, implying a gross yield of roughly 3.1% — the middle of Penang's typically thin 2.7–3.8% prime-precinct band, and lower than other waterfront cards on this desk (Queens Q2's 3.9%). The cashflow dimension is therefore pinned near the Malaysian-buyer clamp floor (3.2), and the foreign-buyer score floors out entirely at the 1.5 clamp because the Malaysian score already sits below the 3.5 trigger — an allowed clamp-collision exception.

On holding structure, freehold residential title means R4 applies in full: no short-term-rental/Airbnb income may be counted, and MBPP's short-term-rental restriction on residential-title units applies without exception. The anchor also notes that Gelugor/Minden-area USM student subletting can inflate headline yields, but that basis is per-room subletting rather than whole-unit rental — Type B is a higher-priced whole-unit product, not a typical student-subletting target, so this card does not apply that adjustment. On exit, the Malaysian-buyer quantum of RM 1.48m lands in Penang's P83–P95 income band — a mid-upper but not easily first-time-buyer-accessible local pool. On the foreign side, Timur Laut district's 973 completed-unsold units trigger the R1 hard rule, capping the foreign exit score at 6.0 (this building's computed value, 4.5, does not reach the cap); the district's 1,618 under-construction-unsold units simultaneously trigger R2, pressuring the Malaysian-buyer exit score as well — making this the first card on this desk to trip both R1 and R2 at once, a structural supply-side signal buyers should not ignore.

Key caveats: (1) all three scarcity layers fail, and IJM itself still holds well over half the masterplan's acreage undeveloped — the 10-year thesis cannot rest on "reclaimed land scarcity" lifting the exit price; (2) current asking already runs roughly 10% ahead of the building's own Brickz median, so buyers should negotiate off real transactions, not asking prices; (3) the simultaneous R1/R2 trigger reflects a genuine district-wide supply-demand imbalance in Timur Laut, and both exit speed and expected discount should be estimated conservatively; (4) the parent group's weaker FY2026 financials (though driven mainly by overseas assets, not local property) merit continued monitoring in subsequent quarters; (5) thin cashflow (~3.1% gross yield) means this building suits a "brand resilience plus a proven, mature community" long-hold thesis better than a short-to-medium-term yield or capital-gain strategy.

10-Year Scenario Baseline: assuming entry in August 2026 and exit in August 2036:
  • Base case: prices track the island residential-strata benchmark of +3%/year, rent grows 3%/year, 1 month/year vacancy, the established community maintains stable turnover. 10-year cumulative net return of roughly RM 137,000 for Malaysian buyers, roughly RM 50,000 for foreign buyers (estimated).
  • Bull case: LRT Mutiara progress accelerates, The Light City mall's foot traffic sustains a brand premium, prices grow +4.5%/year. 10-year net return of roughly RM 446,000 for Malaysian buyers, roughly RM 328,000 for foreign buyers (estimated).
  • Bear case: Timur Laut's completed- and under-construction-unsold inventory keeps climbing, IJM's own remaining masterplan phases (The Essence and beyond) bring substantial new supply to market, prices grow only +1%/year (below the benchmark). 10-year loss of roughly RM 217,000 for Malaysian buyers, roughly RM 269,000 for foreign buyers (estimated) — not an extreme assumption, but a reasonable extension of the supply pressure already evidenced by the double R1/R2 trigger.
10-year exit liquidity: Penang has no MLS and no public days-on-market data; this building's liquidity is read instead through its listing ratio (~32.3%, elevated) and annual turnover (~2.7%, active) — turnover is healthy, but the sizeable backlog of "for sale" listings suggests exit speed will hinge on pricing flexibility rather than any scarcity premium.
◆ Verdict

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • A proven waterfront asset, completed 2014 (~12 years), a mature community with a verified management track record — no under-construction risk
  • The building's own Brickz record is a high-confidence real-transaction anchor (7 sales, median RM 1,041 psf), giving valuation judgment a solid footing
  • Annual turnover of ~2.7% falls in the "active" band, among the highest of any card on this desk to date, indicating a genuinely liquid secondary market
  • 10-year break-even threshold of ~2.3%/year (Malaysian) and ~2.7%/year (foreign), both below the +3.0%/year island residential-strata benchmark, so the base case clears break-even
  • Developer IJM Land tops Penang's anchor table, with strong incentive alignment via its own ownership of The Light City mall and convention centre
  • The nearby Karpal Singh Drive reclamation project has failed its EIA five times with zero groundbreaking, keeping near-term third-party competing supply low
⚠ Caveats
  • All three scarcity layers fail, and IJM itself still holds well over half the masterplan's acreage undeveloped — the 10-year thesis cannot rest on "reclaimed land scarcity"
  • The first card on this desk to trigger both R1 (Timur Laut's 973 completed-unsold units, capping foreign exit at 6.0) and R2 (1,618 under-construction-unsold units, Malaysian-buyer exit −0.3) simultaneously
  • Current representative asking runs roughly 10% ahead of the building's own Brickz median — negotiate off real transactions, not asking prices
  • Gross yield of only ~3.1% has already pinned the cashflow dimension near its clamp floor; the foreign-buyer cashflow score floors out entirely, compressing the Malaysian/foreign gap under the allowed clamp-collision exception
  • Parent group IJM Corp's FY2026 consolidated net profit collapsed (mainly driven by overseas impairments and forex losses); Malaysia's property division remained profitable, but subsequent quarters merit continued monitoring
  • R4 applies: island residential-title tenure means no short-term-rental/Airbnb income may be counted, and MBPP's short-term-rental restriction applies in full

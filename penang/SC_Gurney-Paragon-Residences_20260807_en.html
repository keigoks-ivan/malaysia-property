Gurney Paragon Residences

Sits on Penang's highest location anchor (Gurney/Pulau Tikus inland side, 8.0), backed by a self-owned Gurney Paragon Mall + PICC ecosystem and real transaction history (own Brickz median ~RM1,143 psf). But the entire project offers only 2,810-4,629 sqft large-format units — nothing at Penang's usual entry-level size band — and the representative unit's quantum (~RM3.21m) falls straight into the framework's harshest exit-penalty tier ("above P99, local mortgage pool ≈ zero"). This is also the actual defendant site in the 2026-02-03 Court of Appeal ruling that voided bifurcated strata management — this card keeps the developer-level consequence inside Hunza's already-locked -1.0, and separately applies a small, explicitly disclosed exit-friction adjustment for the fact that THIS building's reorganization remains unresolved.

6.7

Hunza Properties (private, ≥10 projects, 30+ years) base 6.7, no further adjustment — this baseline already fully prices in: +0.5 for self-owned Gurney Paragon Mall + PICC ecosystem (this project is the most direct beneficiary of that alignment model, mall and residences in the same building); -1.0 for the 2026-02-03 Court of Appeal ruling in Badan Pengurusan Bersama Gurney Paragon Residential v Hunza Properties [2026] CLJU 269 that bifurcated residential/commercial management is void ab initio; -0.3 for no public financials since delisting in 2016. Project-specific verification and judgment (this framework's first real-world application of anchor-table item #2, "bifurcated strata management"): the lawsuit's actual subject is this project (the plaintiff is the JMB representing this project's residential owners), unlike the sister card Muze @ PICC (same structure but not the litigation site, only a forward-looking risk). Verification found the financial direction runs opposite to intuition — the JMB originally sought RM56.93m from the commercial side (2011-2017 arrears in maintenance/sinking-fund contributions); the court dismissed the specific quantum but confirmed the commercial side remains statutorily liable to contribute, and ordered a fresh AGM to set a unified budget; law-firm analysis (Miranda & Samuel) indicates residential owners' past payments will be credited against the newly-set unified charges rather than clawed back — the net financial direction for residential owners is neutral-to-mildly-favorable; the real cost falls on the commercial side (mall/office owners). This card's judgment: do not stack an additional penalty in this dimension, because the locked -1.0 baseline was itself calibrated on "Hunza runs this kind of split-management structure, and this project is literally the named defendant in the ruling" — penalizing again would double-count the same ruling. Instead, this card applies a small (-0.2), explicitly-disclosed, project-specific friction adjustment under exit for the fact that "this project itself remains mid-transition as of 2026-08" — this reflects a real due-diligence uncertainty for a next buyer's conveyancing process, not a second reputational penalty on the developer; the two are different in kind and deliberately kept separate on this card. Consistency with the Muze card: both cards score d5 at 6.7 — identical, but on different grounds: Muze's 6.7 is a general discount for "Hunza is the kind of company that does this," while this project's 6.7 is the discount at the actual site where the lawsuit happened; this card treats the two landing at the same score as the reasonable floor, not as license to add further to this project's score. No Federal Court appeal was found as of 2026-08 (absence of evidence, not confirmed absence); no confirmation was found that a single unified JMB/account has completed reorganization — the transfer-level implication of this unresolved status is disclosed separately, as a small adjustment, in d6 (see exit dimension), not repeated here.

◆ Deep-Dive Narrative

Project Deep-Dive Summary

Gurney Paragon Residences sits on Persiaran Gurney (Gurney Drive), the flagship of Hunza Properties' (private, delisted from Bursa 2016) self-owned integrated Gurney Paragon development — two 43-storey residential towers (East Tower 73 units, West Tower 191 units, 264 total) alongside the self-owned Gurney Paragon Mall, Hunza Tower office block, the St. Jo's heritage building, and a retail car park, all under one strata scheme. Unit types run from a 2,810 sqft 3-bedroom in the West Tower to a 4,629 sqft 4-bedroom in the East Tower — a large-format luxury case this framework's usual island-prime representative-unit convention (3-bedroom, 1,000-1,300 sqft) has never encountered before; this card uses the smallest standard unit, 2,810 sqft, as the representative unit and discloses this exception explicitly. Completion sources are inconsistent (2011-2013), with no single authoritative VP date.

On scarcity, this project occupies the most textbook example of this framework's highest location anchor (Gurney/Pulau Tikus inland side, 8.0), with an own Brickz transacted median of ~RM1,114-1,143 psf — close to other top-tier buildings along the corridor (Setia V 1,193, Straits 1,288, Moulmein Rise 1,242 psf), real value backed by real transactions. Land layer scores ✓: a separate ~26-acre new reclaimed parcel does exist along the wider Gurney corridor, asking RM1,400 psf while the market only recognizes RM900-1,000 psf with no master developer stepping in — the framework's flagship false-scarcity example — but coordinate verification found this parcel is actually registered in Tanjong Tokong, ~1.5-2km from both this project and the co-located sister card Setia V (verified only ~500m apart) — not close enough to constitute a threat specific to this project. More decisively, this project's own waterfront frontage has already been permanently converted to public green space by the Gurney Bay Phase 1 park (opened 2024-02, covering the full coastal stretch fronting both this project and Setia V) — no longer developable private land a competitor could acquire. Worth clarifying specifically: the Penang Turf Club's 118-203 acres, Penang's best-known supply breach, do NOT directly apply to this project after verification — that parcel sits in Pulau Tikus's inland grid, separated from this project's and Setia V's Gurney Drive waterfront frontage by a block; it is a direct threat to the sister card Codrington Residence, but only an indirect signal for both this project and Setia V. Product and regulatory layers remain non-scarce, so only one of three layers passes — below the bonus threshold — and this card leaves the 8.0 anchor uncut rather than deducted.

This project's most important structural finding lies in the exit dimension. The representative 2,810 sqft unit is valued at approximately RM3,212,000, translating to a next-buyer household monthly income threshold of ~RM35,332 — far exceeding Penang's household income P99 (RM30,529). This means not a single unit in the entire project falls within what the local mortgage pool can afford, and the quantum lands directly in the framework's harshest penalty band (>RM2.8m, -1.0). This is not a location problem; it's a product-mix problem: other sister cards at least retain some entry-level unit type, while this project has none at all. This is also the sharpest contrast with the same developer's Muze @ PICC card (quantum RM1.145m, only a mild -0.3 for merely touching the "threshold boundary") — the same developer, but with radically different product positioning, the exit buyer-pool depth differs by an entire order of magnitude.

On the developer side, this project is the actual litigation site in the 2026-02-03 Court of Appeal case "Badan Pengurusan Bersama Gurney Paragon Residential v Hunza Properties" — the ruling declared this project's bifurcated residential/commercial management void ab initio, requiring the JMB to manage the entire Gurney Paragon development under a single unified account. After verification, this card found the financial direction runs opposite to intuition: the JMB originally sought RM56.93m from the commercial side (mall/office owners) for arrears in maintenance charges, and while the court dismissed the specific quantum, it confirmed the commercial side remains statutorily liable to contribute, ordering a fresh AGM to set new unified charges — law-firm analysis indicates residential owners' past payments will be credited against the new charges rather than clawed back, so the net financial direction for residential owners is neutral-to-mildly-favorable; the real cost falls on the commercial side. This card's judgment: do not stack an additional penalty in the developer dimension, because the framework's locked Hunza baseline (6.7, already including the -1.0) was itself calibrated against this exact lawsuit — penalizing again would double-count the same ruling. Instead, this card applies a small (-0.2), explicitly-disclosed, project-specific friction adjustment in the exit dimension for the fact that "this project itself remains mid-transition as of 2026-08" — this reflects a real conveyancing-due-diligence uncertainty for a next buyer, not a second reputational penalty on the developer; the two are different in kind and deliberately kept separate on this card.

On holding-period fundamentals, the freehold residential title means R4 applies: no short-let/Airbnb income may be counted. Estimated gross yield is ~3.23%, a typical low-cashflow, high-location-brand case. Management fees temporarily use this framework's KL RM0.6 psf baseline, but this project is a full-facility luxury tower co-located with a mall, and its true fee is very likely higher, with the outcome of the ordered unified-charge reset not yet landed — an unquantified upside risk. Under the base 10-year scenario (+3%/year), Malaysian buyers post a clear positive return (+RM298,000), while foreign buyers essentially just break even (+RM1,000) — the foreign breakeven appreciation rate (~3.0%) is nearly equal to the baseline itself, and this excludes the opportunity cost of the foreign buyer's down payment (3.5x the Malaysian figure), meaning the true threshold is likely higher still.

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • The most textbook example of Penang's highest location anchor (Gurney/Pulau Tikus inland side, 8.0), with an own Brickz transacted median of ~RM1,114-1,143 psf close to other top-tier buildings in the corridor, backed by real transactions
  • Among the strongest connectivity and amenity profiles on the site: the self-owned Gurney Paragon Mall is part of the same building (effectively 0m walking distance), four international-grade private hospitals within 3km, and no dependence on any not-yet-operating rail line
  • Gurney Bay Phase 1 waterfront park opened in 2024 and this project fronts it directly — a delivered, not merely promised, catalyst
  • Hunza's self-owned mall + office + heritage ecosystem is a rare developer self-alignment model in Penang, and this project is its most direct beneficiary
⚠ Caveats
  • 【Structural quantum problem】The project offers no 1,000-1,300 sqft or smaller unit; the representative unit's quantum (~RM3.21m) lands directly in the framework's harshest exit-penalty band (>RM2.8m) — the local first-time-buyer and general-upgrader pool is structurally close to zero
  • 【Developer governance】This project is the actual litigation site in the 2026-02-03 Court of Appeal ruling that voided bifurcated strata management; no evidence was found as of 2026-08 that unified-management reorganization has been completed, which could affect a next buyer's conveyancing timeline
  • 【R4】Island residential title, MBPP prohibits short-term rental, any short-let income claim is unenforceable; estimated gross yield is only ~3.23%
  • 【R1/R2】Timur Laut, this project's district, is the island's heaviest-supply district: completed-unsold 973 units caps foreign exit, under-construction-unsold 1,618 units penalizes Malaysian exit
  • Recommended confirmations: inconsistent completion year sources (2011-2013); no project-specific management fee data (temporarily using the KL baseline, true figure very likely higher); whether the AGM to reset unified charges has been held, and current sinking fund status — all should be verified in writing with the management corporation or agent

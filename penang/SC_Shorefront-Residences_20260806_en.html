Shorefront Residences

A rare beachfront-row freehold boutique residence inside George Town's UNESCO World Heritage core zone — just 115 units across three low-rise blocks, sited at the seaward end of Farquhar Street next to the Eastern & Oriental Hotel, with the SAP's 18m height cap locking out comparable future supply. But the representative unit's quantum already sits near Penang's P99 income threshold, structurally narrowing the local buyer pool and leaving exit heavily dependent on high-net-worth Malaysian and foreign buyers.

◆ In-Depth Narrative

Project Deep Dive

Shorefront Residences sits at No.1 Farquhar Road, George Town, at the seaward end of George Town's UNESCO World Heritage core zone, immediately adjacent to the historic Eastern & Oriental Hotel (opened 1885, a core-zone landmark), across the street from Bayview Hotel Georgetown. The project was developed by YTL Land & Development Berhad (part of the YTL Group, listed on Bursa Malaysia's Main Market) — its first and, to date, only Penang property project. Units sold out in phases between 2011 and 2015, the project completed in 2018, and no delays or disputes were found in the reporting record.

On scarcity, this is one of the rare cases in this framework to pass all three audit layers. Land: George Town's core zone has no developable vacant land left, and this project's 3-acre consolidated beachfront parcel is a rare scale for the land-scarce core. Product: 115 units across three low-rise blocks of five storeys, with a private lift lobby shared by every two units — a density profile the SAP's 18m height cap makes impossible to replicate within the core going forward. Regulatory: SAP in force since 2016-09-01, an 18m cap, 5,285 catalogued buildings — this framework's only genuinely irreversible regulatory lock. Brickz's 2024 transaction median of RM 1,840 psf sits about 244% above George Town's general median of RM 535 psf — a real, transaction-backed scarcity premium rather than an asking-price illusion. The location score reaches this card's raw computed value of 8.3, but is conservatively capped at 8.0 per the ruler declaration.

On pricing, the representative unit (Brickz-implied at ~1,400 sqft) is currently comparably listed at only about +2.3% above the 2024 transacted anchor — nearly at par, reflecting disciplined market pricing with no obvious inflation. One honest caveat must be flagged: portal listings describe this project's unit sizes inconsistently (the same 3-bedroom type appears variously as 1,631 sqft up to over 3,700 sqft across different pages) — this is the card's core data-quality limitation, and buyers should confirm the exact size and price with the agent or developer in writing.

On the rental market, the portal-derived gross yield estimate of ~3.27% (estimated, down from the 2021 peak of 4.57%) is this scorecard's biggest weak point. As an island residential-titled property, MBPP explicitly prohibits short-stay letting, so any Airbnb-style yield marketing claim is unenforceable; this card's cashflow dimension accordingly counts no short-stay scenario at all, scoring purely off the estimated long-term rental yield. The Malaysian score lands in the 3.0-3.5% tier (3.2), and after the mandatory −2.0 foreign adjustment the foreign score hits the 1.5 clamp floor — this is a deliberate design exception (since the Malaysian score is already ≤3.5), not a calculation error.

On exit, a quantum of about RM 2.58M already sits near Penang's P99 household-income threshold, excluding local first-time buyers and typical upgraders from the buyer pool and leaving exit dependent on a combination of high-net-worth local buyers and foreign buyers.

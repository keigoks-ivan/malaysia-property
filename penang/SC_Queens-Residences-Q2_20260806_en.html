Queens Residences Q2 @ Queens Waterfront

A rental-led play directly opposite Queensbay Mall, a 7-minute drive to the Bayan Lepas Free Industrial Zone (FIZ), in the heart of Penang's E&E employment belt. The 450-unit single tower has been completed for four years, and Brickz transactions in this building itself form a rare high-confidence valuation anchor (median RM 1,243 psf). But the land layer is not scarce — Silicon Island reclamation is under way just south of here, and Barat Daya's completed-unsold stock has already crossed 800 units, triggering the foreign-buyer exit cap at 6.0. This is the first card on this site testing whether raw E&E employment demand can carry a score when location scarcity is absent — the answer: it carries cashflow and a next-buyer pool, but not a location moat. The 10-year thesis rests on tenant depth, not scarcity.

◆ Deep Narrative

Project Deep Dive

Queens Residences Q2 sits on Persiaran Bayan Indah, directly opposite Queensbay Mall — a single 24-storey, 450-unit freehold residential tower completed in 2022 by Ideal Property Group (Bursa-listed entity Ideal Capital Bhd). It is Phase 2 of the Queens Waterfront site, following the already-completed Q1 (500 units), with Q3 currently under construction. The project sits in the Bayan Lepas / Bayan Baru / Queensbay corridor — the core of Penang island's southern E&E (electronics and electrical) manufacturing employment belt, a ~7-minute drive from the Bayan Lepas Free Industrial Zone. This is the first card on this site testing whether E&E employment demand alone can carry a score when location scarcity is largely absent.

On location, Bayan Lepas starts at a 6.3 anchor in Penang's scale — a deliberately unusual score, reflecting an odd logic: this is where Penang's hardest demand engine sits (the FIZ employs over 100,000 workers), yet the land layer is not scarce. Silicon Island reclamation is under way just south of the island; although residential use is only 15.71% of it and sequenced behind industrial, the supply threat is deferred rather than eliminated. Under the three-layer supply audit, land, product, and regulatory layers all score ✗ — the terrain is flat with no hillside restriction, it is not a heritage zone, and the 450-unit standard tower configuration is highly replicable both on the same site (Q1/Q3) and along the corridor (Tropicana Bay Residences, Skycube, Setia Sky Vista). This is not a scarcity story; it is a demand-density story.

The project comprises a single 24-storey tower of 450 units, with five layouts ranging from 2BR at 950 sqft to 4BR at 1,650 sqft; 2-bedroom units (including the study variants) make up 3 of the 5 layouts and dominate the unit mix, so a 2-bedroom, 1,000 sqft (Type B) unit is used as the representative unit under the scorecard rules. On amenities, the position directly opposite Queensbay Mall (one of Penang's largest malls) is this project's most direct moat — under a minute's walk to mall-grade retail is a key driver of the 6.8 connectivity score. The LRT Mutiara Line's planned stations within Bayan Lepas district include SPICE, FIZ North, FIZ South and the airport, but the nearest station (SPICE) is ~3km from this project — beyond the 800m walking threshold — so only the lowest "same district has a station" bonus tier applies.

On the developer, Ideal Property Group is the entity directly cited in the anchor table — FY2025 net profit of RM 158.4m (up 74.8% year-on-year), an improving balance sheet, and Queens Q2 itself is the anchor table's cited example of "ahead-of-schedule completion." One caveat worth noting: the founder stepped down as executive chairman in 2025-08 while retaining a ~56% stake, and past MBI (multi-level/Ponzi-scheme-related) allegations were denied by the founder but remain unresolved — a governance overhang that buyers should keep tracking through future disclosures and any legal proceedings, rather than treating as already closed.

On pricing, this project is one of the rare cases on this site where the building's own transaction record forms the valuation anchor — Brickz recorded 6 transactions between 2024-09 and 2025-06, with a median of RM 1,243 psf and a price range of RM 965,000 to RM 2,480,000. Current asking prices span roughly RM 1,000-1,280 psf, bracketing the self-transacted median with no clear premium evidence. That said, the sample depth (6 transactions over ~9.3 months) is thinner than the 20+ transactions typical of KL cards on this site, so confidence is marked "medium" rather than "high." On rental, 1,000 sqft units currently list around RM 4,000-5,200/month, implying an estimated gross yield of ~3.9% — at the upper end of the generally thin 2.7-3.8% gross yields seen across Penang's prime districts, supported mainly by E&E corporate long-lease tenants (1-2 years, company-paid). This yield figure remains a listing-portal-derived estimate, not institutional data, and is labeled accordingly.

On the holding-period structure, the freehold residential title means R4 applies: no short-term rental / Airbnb income may be counted, and MBPP's short-let restriction on residential titles applies in full — any short-let income claim by a seller should be treated as unenforceable. On exit, the Malaysian buyer's quantum of RM 1.243m falls in Penang's P83-P95 income band — a solid but not first-time-buyer-accessible upper-mid-market local pool. On the foreign side, Barat Daya district's completed-unsold stock of 883 units has already triggered the R1 hard rule, capping the foreign exit score at 6.0 (this project's calculated value of 4.5 does not hit the cap); combined with Bayan Lepas being explicitly classified as a "tenant market, not a foreign-owner market," the MM2H cluster effect (anchored to Tanjung Tokong / Tanjung Bungah / Batu Ferringhi) does not apply here, leaving foreign investment appetite structurally thin.

Key caveats: (1) all three scarcity layers are ✗ — the 10-year thesis cannot rest on "location scarcity will lift the exit price," and should instead rest on "tenant depth + a low breakeven bar"; (2) Silicon Island reclamation, though deferred, should be monitored throughout the 10-year hold for its pace of residential land release; (3) Intel has resumed but its ramp headcount is undisclosed and it has already reneged once — it should not be treated as banked leasing demand, and any change to Malaysia's semiconductor tariff exemption is a tail risk; (4) the founder-level governance issue (MBI allegations) remains unresolved and should be tracked; (5) the foreign exit pool is structurally thin (both MM2H and foreign investment score ✗) — this project fits better with a local- or demand-driven holding logic than a foreign capital-gains story.

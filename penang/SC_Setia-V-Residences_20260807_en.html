Setia V Residences

Sits on Penang's scale-ceiling location anchor (Gurney Drive frontage, 8.0) — and is a rare case where the location moat actually beats the same-anchor comparison card. It directly faces Gurney Bay Park (opened Feb 2024), which permanently locks the sea-view corridor; the Penang Turf Club's 118-203-acre supply breach that cost the Codrington card 0.5 points sits roughly 2.9km away and in a different product tier, so no equivalent penalty applies here. Exit economics tell a different story, though: the representative unit's quantum of ~RM1.75mn has crossed Penang's foreign levy sweet spot into the RM1.5-2.0mn "levy cliff" band, and a ~3.1% (estimated) gross yield combined with Timur Laut's heaviest island-wide supply overhang erodes much of the location premium.

Pulau Tikus / Gurney anchors at 8.0, Penang's scale ceiling. Three-layer audit: land layer ✓ — this 1.8-acre freehold plot faces Gurney Bay (Gurney Wharf) directly, and futuresoutheastasia.com names this building specifically: the Gurney Bay Phase 1 waterfront park (opened Feb 2024) sits "directly opposite Setia V Residences" (not a generic reference to Gurney Bay at large), permanently zoning this stretch of shoreline as public green space. No future development can ever insert a competing tower between this building and the sea — a harder form of scarcity than mere land shortage, since even the theoretical competing site has been foreclosed. Independent determination: the Turf Club breach does not apply here. Penang Turf Club (118-203 acres) sits at approximately 5.4136°N, 100.2963°E; this building's coordinates are roughly 5.436°N, 100.310°E — a straight-line distance of roughly 2.9km (estimated), nowhere near the Codrington card's literal across-the-street adjacency. The Turf Club site is also zoned Priority Development Zone 1 (commercial); any redevelopment would likely be an inland mixed-use product, not a direct substitute for this building's "first-row Gurney Drive sea view" positioning — so this card does not apply Codrington's −0.5 treatment. Product layer ✗ (corrected in this revision) — the same Gurney corridor already has Gurney Paragon, Straits, and Moulmein Rise: comparable large-format waterfront high-rise product at similar sizes and transacted psf, so the condo product configuration itself is replicable (multiple projects, same spec, in the same district) and does not constitute scarcity independent of the land layer; the "cannot be reassembled" argument for the 1.8-acre plot belongs to the land layer above and is not double-counted here. Effective-scarcity check (confirms genuine demand, not a product-layer bonus): this building's own Brickz median of RM1,258 psf, far above Pulau Tikus's district-wide non-landed transaction median of just RM357 psf, is genuine transacted-premium evidence that the location premium is demand-backed, not asking-price fiction. Regulatory layer ✗ — outside the George Town UNESCO core/buffer, approved for 48 storeys, and RSNPP 2040 is trending toward loosening (R5 standing check performed, see data line below). Raw total: waterfront first-row with a permanently locked view corridor +0.3 (a land-layer-strength adjustment, no product-layer bonus) → 8.0+0.3=8.3; per the §0 scale declaration (Penang's ceiling anchor is 8.0, precedent set by the Shorefront card), capped at 8.0. Honest disclosure of counter-evidence (R6): a 26-acre reclaimed parcel addressed in Tanjong Tokong (Jalan Peter Paul Dason, near Andaman Island at 5°26′48.6″N/100°19′09.5″E and the Westin) is asking RM1,400 psf — near this building's own transacted anchor — yet the market will only pay RM900-1,000 psf, and it has found no master developer for over a year. This is one of the clearest instances of false scarcity on the whole framework. This card does not deduct d1 for it: the parcel's estimated straight-line distance from this building (approx. 5.436°N,100.310°E) is roughly 1.5-2km, and its postal address is Tanjong Tokong rather than Pulau Tikus/Gurney — a distinct micro-location — but it is flagged as a valuation-ceiling signal discussed under valuation and exit. Cross-card consistency check (verified 2026-08-07 at the coordinator's request): the same-batch Gurney Paragon Residences card (Persiaran Gurney, coordinates 5.43639°N,100.31165°E — only a few hundred metres from this building, effectively the same micro-location) treats this same 26-acre parcel as direct grounds for a land-layer ✗ and deducts its d1 from 8.0 to 7.5. Having checked these coordinates and addresses, this card finds that the parcel's estimated distance from Gurney Paragon and from this building are similar (both roughly 1.5-2km, and neither building is itself in the parcel's postal locality) — the evidence does not support a segment-level distinction where the parcel threatens one building but not the other. This card's land-layer ✓ verdict rests primarily on the direct, building-specific citation that Gurney Bay Phase 1 park faces Setia V Residences by name, not on the relative-distance argument about the 26-acre parcel (which applies equally to Gurney Paragon). This card therefore recommends that the Gurney Paragon card's land-layer ✗ verdict and resulting d1 of 7.5 be sent for manual coordinate review; this card does not modify that card itself and reports this finding for the record only.

◆ Deep Narrative

Project Deep-Dive

Setia V Residences sits on Lorong Burma, off Persiaran Gurney (Gurney Drive) — the best-known waterfront promenade in Penang — as a twin-tower boutique residential development: Tower A at 48 storeys and Tower B at 43 storeys, 166 units in total (some sources cite 178, a discrepancy this card discloses), on a consolidated 1.8-acre freehold plot. It was developed by SP Setia Berhad, one of Malaysia's largest listed developers, on the former site of Tenby International School, demolished and rebuilt from 2011, with the structure topping out in 2016 and full completion around 2016-2017. Unit sizes range from the smallest verifiable 2+1 bedroom, 1,376 sqft, up to 7,119 sqft penthouses — a product mix clearly skewed toward large-format family and penthouse buyers, which is why this card's representative-unit choice has to deviate from the rule's default 3-bedroom, 1,000-1,300 sqft range.

Scarcity is the dimension most worth independently examining in this building relative to its same-anchor peer. It faces Gurney Bay (Gurney Wharf) directly, and futuresoutheastasia.com names this building specifically: the Gurney Bay Phase 1 waterfront park, formally opened in February 2024, sits directly opposite Setia V Residences, permanently zoning this stretch of coastline as public green space — there is no developable land left between this building and the Indian Ocean, and no future project can ever insert a view-blocking tower. This is a harder form of scarcity than mere land shortage, and it rests on the land layer alone (product and regulatory layers both score ✗, see the deep-dive box below). The site's existing Codrington card, anchored at the same 8.0 ceiling, deducted 0.5 points because the Penang Turf Club's 118-203 acres sit directly across the street from it. This card independently determined that the equivalent breach does not apply here: the straight-line distance between the Turf Club's coordinates and this building is roughly 2.9km, nowhere near "across the street," and the Turf Club is zoned for inland commercial redevelopment, not a direct substitute for this building's "first-row Gurney Drive sea view" positioning — so this card does not apply Codrington's deduction. The raw total (waterfront first-row with a permanently locked view corridor +0.3, a land-layer-strength adjustment) reaches 8.3, capped at 8.0 per the §0 scale declaration. One piece of counter-evidence is honestly disclosed: a 26-acre reclaimed parcel addressed in Tanjong Tokong is asking RM1,400 psf, nearly matching this building's own transacted anchor of RM1,258 psf, yet the market only pays RM900-1,000 psf and no master developer has emerged in over a year — which actually confirms that the market pays for built-and-delivered scarcity, not similarly priced raw land; this card treats it as a valuation-ceiling signal rather than a deduction (the parcel's postal address is Tanjong Tokong, an estimated 1.5-2km away). Cross-card disclosure: the same-batch Gurney Paragon Residences card (coordinates 5.43639°N,100.31165°E — only a few hundred metres from this building, effectively the same micro-location) treats this same 26-acre parcel as direct grounds for a land-layer ✗ and deducts its d1 to 7.5; having checked the coordinates, this card finds the parcel's estimated distance from both buildings is similar and the evidence does not support singling out one building over the other, and has flagged the Gurney Paragon card's verdict for manual review rather than silently adopting or replicating it.

On pricing, this building's own Brickz record provides a rare "high-confidence, same-building anchor" on this site: 6 transactions (2023-06 to 2024-04) with a median PSF of RM1,258 and a median price of RM3,242,500.

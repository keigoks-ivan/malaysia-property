Scots Pavilion

This is the site's first scorecard that genuinely lands in the "George Town general (buffer/CBD strata)" 7.0 tier. Verification found the three originally shortlisted candidates were misfiled: Moulmein Rise and Cantonment actually sit in Pulau Tikus (8.0 tier), and Middleton sits in Gelugor (a separate submarket) — their verified transaction psf (1,242 / 1,191 / 1,035) was itself far above this tier's median RM 535 psf, a strong price-level tell. We selected Scots Pavilion on Jalan Scotland instead: quantum is only about RM 780,000, one of the deepest local buyer pools on the site — but that same quantum sits below the RM 1 million island foreign-purchase floor, making this the site's first card where the standard unit type locks most foreign buyers out entirely.

George Town general (buffer/CBD strata) anchors at 7.0. Zoning verification — the key finding of this card: the originally locked candidates Moulmein Rise and Cantonment turned out to be registered in Pulau Tikus (8.0 tier, not this tier) — multiple independent sources including iproperty.com.my explicitly list both as "Freehold Condominium in Pulau Tikus." Middleton sits in Gelugor (Minden Heights), neither George Town nor Pulau Tikus, an independent submarket the framework doesn't separately anchor. All three candidates' verified transaction psf (1,242 / 1,191 / 1,035) were already far above this tier's median RM 535 psf — a price-level tell before geography was even checked. We selected Scots Pavilion instead (102-G, Jalan Scotland, George Town, Timur Laut district): coordinates approx. 5.4108°N, 100.3018°E, roughly 3.0km straight-line from the UNESCO core (estimated, not a walking route, medium confidence) — beyond the 109.38ha core, and quite possibly beyond the formal 150.04ha buffer zone as well (GTWHI's official boundary terminates at Jalan Transfer on the northwest edge; Jalan Scotland/Penang Turf Club is estimated to sit outside it — a disclosed uncertainty, not confirmed against an official SAP map). It is also clearly not Pulau Tikus/Gurney (this project sits inland to the west, distinct from the Kelawai/Burma Road core of Pulau Tikus). The project's own 2021-2026 track record (list.my/Brickz, 12 transactions) shows a median of RM 788 psf — above the George Town general district median of 535 psf (reflecting its 2020 completion and low-density positioning as a relatively newer product), but still far below the Pulau Tikus/Gurney anchor tier (1,000-1,400+ psf). Geography, the official buffer-zone definition, and transaction-price magnitude all corroborate the same conclusion: this project sits in "George Town general," not "Pulau Tikus" or "core/buffer." Three-layer audit: Land layer ✗ — a proposed 35-storey, 298-unit redevelopment on the same Jalan Scotland (disclosed 2026-07) is under review by Penang Island City Council (not yet approved), proving the corridor can still produce direct competing supply. Product layer ✗ — the project's 22-storey low-density "wellness" positioning is a developer's own strategic choice, not a title- or regulation-protected feature; the corridor still has abundant standard high-density towers as substitutes. Regulatory layer ✗ — the project is not subject to the SAP 18m height cap (its own 22 storeys is evidence), nor hillslope restrictions (flat urban land, R5 not applicable). All three layers fail, plus a concrete competing redevelopment under review on the same street — replicability high, −0.5. Effective-scarcity check: the median RM 788 psf carries a premium of roughly +47% over the district-wide RM 535 psf median, but that premium is compressing — the project's own most recent transaction (Oct-2024, 1,345 sqft) fell to RM 647 psf, well below the 2023 peak (1,302 sqft at RM 843-884 psf). This reads as a demand-depth (liquidity) advantage rather than a protected scarcity advantage — echoing the framework's core reminder: a high transaction count is a liquidity advantage, not a scarcity advantage. Net: 7.0−0.5=6.5.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep-Dive Summary

Scots Pavilion sits at 102-G, Jalan Scotland, George Town, in the Timur Laut district, developed by GL Semesta Corporation, a subsidiary of the private developer M Summit Group. Completed in 2020, it is a single 22-storey low-density tower (just 3-6 units per floor) with 89-99 units (sources vary slightly), positioned as a wellness-focused boutique residence combining health, therapy, and leisure amenities, including an automated mechanical parking system. Unit sizes span from a minimum of 924 sqft (2BR) to penthouses over 6,000 sqft across eight main unit types; this card selects Type C (3BR/2BA, 1,181 sqft) as the representative unit, a clean fit against this framework's George Town prime-precinct guideline (3BR, 1,000-1,300 sqft) and consistent in scale with two real comparable transactions (1,195 sqft and 1,302 sqft) in the same project.

This is the site's first scorecard that genuinely lands in the "George Town general (buffer/CBD strata)" 7.0 tier — and the verification process itself is worth documenting in full. The anchor table originally locked in three candidates (Moulmein Rise at 1,242 psf, Cantonment at 1,191 psf, Middleton at 1,035 psf); verification found that none of the three actually belong in this tier. Moulmein Rise and Cantonment are, per multiple independent sources including iproperty.com.my's explicit "Freehold Condominium in Pulau Tikus" listing, registered in Pulau Tikus (the 8.0 tier). Middleton sits in Gelugor (Minden Heights) — neither George Town nor Pulau Tikus, an independent submarket this framework doesn't separately anchor. All three candidates' psf figures (1,242 / 1,191 / 1,035) were already far above this tier's median of RM 535 psf — a strong price-level tell before geography was even checked. We selected Scots Pavilion instead: its coordinates, roughly 5.4108°N, 100.3018°E, sit about 3.0km straight-line from the UNESCO core (estimated, not a walking route) — beyond the 109.38ha core zone, and quite possibly beyond the officially defined 150.04ha buffer zone as well (GTWHI's boundary terminates at Jalan Transfer to the northwest, and Jalan Scotland/Penang Turf Club is estimated to lie outside it). This is a disclosed uncertainty, not confirmed against an official SAP map. Geographically, this project is also clearly distinct from Pulau Tikus/Gurney, sitting inland to the west, apart from Pulau Tikus's Kelawai/Burma Road core. The project's own 2021-2026 real-transaction record (list.my/Brickz, 12 sales) shows a median of RM 788 psf — above the George Town general district median of 535 psf (reflecting its status as a relatively newer, 2020-completed, low-density boutique product) — but still far below the Pulau Tikus/Gurney anchor tier's 1,000-1,400+ psf. Geography, the official buffer-zone definition, and transaction-price magnitude all corroborate the same conclusion: this project sits in "George Town general," not "Pulau Tikus" or "core/buffer."

The three-layer audit fails across the board: land layer — a proposed 35-storey, 298-unit redevelopment on the same Jalan Scotland (disclosed 2026-07) is under Penang Island City Council review, proving the corridor can still produce direct competing supply; product layer — the project's 22-storey low-density wellness positioning is a developer strategic choice, not a title- or regulation-protected feature; regulatory layer — the project isn't subject to the SAP height cap (its own 22-storey build is evidence) or hillslope restrictions (flat land). With all three layers failing and a concrete competing redevelopment under review, d1 starts at 7.0 and is marked down to 6.5. On pricing, the project's 12 real transactions show a clear softening trend: full-period median RM 788 psf, but the most recent print (2024-10, 1,345 sqft) fell to RM 647 psf, more than 20% below the 2023 peak (1,302 sqft at RM 843-884 psf). This card anchors on the most recent transaction rather than the full-period median; current asking (iProperty at 568-771 psf, PropertyGuru at 542-879 psf) implies a representative-unit price of about 660 psf, just 2% above the 647 psf point anchor — landing in the reasonable-new-product-premium band, d7 = 6.0.

The rental market carries the same honest-disclosure posture as the rest of the Penang site — no institutional data exists, only portal-derived asking rents. The representative unit (3BR, ~1,181 sqft) is estimated at RM 3,000/month, implying a gross yield of about 4.6%, in the 4.5-5.0% band, d3 (local) = 5.0 — a notably better cashflow print than the site's Gurney/heritage-core flagship cards (~3.1-3.3%), driven by the much lower quantum here. As an island residential-title property, R4 mandates that short-term-rental income never be counted; the foreign cashflow score is fixed at local minus 2.0 = 3.0.

Exit is where this project draws its sharpest contrast with the site's existing flagship cards. Representative-unit quantum of about RM 780,000 falls in the RM 600k-1.2M core local-upgrader band, well below flagship comparables like Setia V (RM 1.75M) and Shorefront (RM 2.58M) — and the local exit score reaches 7.0, one of the highest on the site. This is precisely the George Town buffer district's core distinction from Pulau Tikus/Gurney/the heritage core: deep liquidity and a wide local buyer pool, not a scarcity premium. The initial verification surfaced a structural situation the framework hadn't explicitly addressed before: this project's representative unit (~RM 780,000) sits below the general foreign individual/institutional purchase floor of RM 1 million. On coordinator review, this turned out not to be a new situation at all — the framework's R3 rule (island landed → foreign exit capped at 4.0, foreign dependence recorded as ≈0) is, in substance, exactly the "ordinary foreign buyers cannot legally purchase the standard product" logic; the rule's literal wording just anchors on "landed title" as the trigger. This project isn't landed, but "quantum below the general foreign floor" is the same underlying logic, matching the site's Botanica.CT precedent (island landed, priced below the RM 3M floor, capped at 4.0) and the same-batch Azuria card (representative unit falls even below the MM2H floor, leaving only the PR pool, capped at 3.0) — this project applies the same cap of 4.0. The key distinction: ordinary foreign individuals/institutions cannot legally purchase this project's representative unit, but MM2H visa holders (floor RM 500,000) and permanent residents/PR (floor RM 250,000) can — this project's foreign exit pool is MM2H + PR, structurally identical to Botanica.CT, not a full foreign exclusion. Combined with the mandatory state-consent friction and three-year lock-up items, the raw itemized value comes to 4.8, and the cap brings the foreign exit score to 4.0. The developer, GL Semesta Corporation (M Summit Group), isn't on the locked table; scored against the general private-developer tier at roughly 23-30 years and 10-18 projects, it starts at 7.0, with no public record of delays or disputes found (provisional).

Vertu Resort @ Aspen Vision City

The site's first mainland (Seberang Perai) scorecard, and the only phase among Aspen Vision City's four residential launches with a mature resale market (15 real Brickz transactions in the past ~12 months). Scarcity fails on all three structural layers — PDC's 2026-07-20 board decision to take back self-development rights over ~1,300 acres at Byram, releasing ~200 acres/year, is the cleanest false-scarcity case on the site. BKIP's 160+ employers (Boon Siew Honda, Boston Scientific, Broadcom, and others) anchor genuine local employment, but gross yield is only ~3.9% and the project itself shows 114 units listed for rent against just 43 for sale — rental oversupply is a bigger structural problem than resale liquidity. Foreign buyers never get a mainland levy discount band, pushing 10-year breakeven appreciation to ~3.1%/year (locals ~2.2%/year) — both technically clear the baseline, but with thin margin for foreigners. The 10-year thesis rests on BKIP-driven tenant depth, not location scarcity or capital appreciation.

Key structural disadvantage: mainland strata levy is always a flat 3% — unlike island strata, which enjoys a 1.5% sweet-spot discount in the RM1-1.5 million band, foreign buyers on this project get no such relief at all. Breakeven threshold: Malaysian buyers need only about 2.2%/year appreciation to break even, foreign buyers about 3.1%/year — both below the mainland high-rise baseline of 3.5%/year, meaning the base case itself clears breakeven, though the foreign-buyer margin is thin (only about 0.4 percentage points). This project's cashflow case is already thin (~3.87% gross yield) and faces structural rental oversupply, so the 10-year return depends almost entirely on whether appreciation materializes, leaving limited upside.
◆ Exit Reality Check

Who Buys It in 10 Years

Three components: scarcity audit (can supply be replicated), next-buyer pyramid (who can afford it in 10 years), and secondary-market hard evidence (turnover / listing rate / discount). Returns are only real the day you sell — the exit price floor is set by scarcity, and exit speed is set by the depth of the next-buyer pool.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep-Dive Summary

Vertu Resort sits on Lebuhraya Bandar Cassia, the second residential phase within the large Aspen Vision City masterplan, developed by Aspen Group (parent Aspen (Group) Holdings Limited, SGX Mainboard) and completed in 2021 — 1,282 units across five towers of 29-42 storeys, and the first GBI-certified residential project on the Penang mainland (Seberang Perai Selatan district, distinct from SPT). The masterplan spans Vervea (commercial, completed 2015), Vertu Resort (this project, completed 2021), Vivo Executive Apartment (CCC obtained 2024-12), Versa (under construction, targeted 2027/2028), and Viluxe terraces, alongside an 80:20 joint venture with Ikano Pte Ltd (IKEA's Malaysian franchisee) for a RM1 billion shopping-centre component — IKEA Batu Kawan opened 2019-03-14, the first in northern Peninsular Malaysia. Among the four residential phases, Vertu Resort is the only one with a mature resale market: Brickz recorded 15 real transactions in the past year (2025-03 to 2026-02) — the core reason this card selected it as the scoring subject, since Versa is still under construction with no transaction history, Vivo just obtained its CCC with too thin a sample, and Viluxe is a different (landed) product line.

On location, Batu Kawan starts from a 5.5 anchor in Penang's scoring table — under the three-layer supply-side audit, all three layers fail, making this the cleanest false-scarcity case on the site. Land layer: PDC (the Penang Development Corporation) resolved on 2026-07-20 to reclaim self-development rights over the BKIP2/Byram site after three failed tenders, roughly 1,300 acres, planning to release ~200 acres/year in phases — this is not a latent risk but an already-approved, already-scheduled supply plan. Product layer: four highly similar residential phases exist within the same masterplan, plus competing projects from Tambun Indah, Paramount and other developers across the wider Batu Kawan area — the layout is highly replicable. Regulatory layer: flat terrain, no hillside restriction, not heritage-protected, no irreversible planning lock. This is not a scarcity story — it's a local-employment-density story: BKIP (Batu Kawan Industrial Park), roughly 1,151 acres and 160+ companies including Boon Siew Honda, Boston Scientific, Broadcom, HP, Inari and Western Digital, anchors both the connectivity and capital-appreciation dimensions of this scorecard.

The project spans five towers and 1,282 units, with a unit mix of 1BR (from 740 sqft), 3BR (1,030-1,180 sqft), and 4BR (up to 1,290 sqft) — notably no 2BR is offered, which is why this card deliberately deviates from the site's default "use 2BR on the mainland" rule, instead selecting Type C at 1,180 sqft — a size that closely matches the Brickz median transaction price of RM590,000 — as the representative unit. On amenities, IKEA Batu Kawan and Design Village mall are this project's most direct moat, with masterplan-internal retail streets within walking distance. On transport, there is no LRT station, and neither the island nor the mainland has, or is likely to soon get, rail service to this area — only a verbal "if there's a need" concept from officials, with no study, budget, or approval. This card's check of Aspen's own official marketing page found no such claim being used as a selling point, so no corresponding deduction was applied — though this check was not an exhaustive sweep of all broker/agent materials.

On the developer, Aspen Group's equity exposure was the primary focus of this card's verification work. ⚠️ A common error needs explicit correction: Aspen is listed on the SGX Mainboard (transferred 2021-01-28, having originally listed on Catalist 2017-07-28) — not Bursa ACE. Historically, Aspen Vision Land Sdn Bhd (the entity developing the parent land parcel for this project) was formed in 2014 as a 51% Aspen / 49% Ivory Properties joint venture, but multiple independent 2025-2026 reports, cross-checked, show that entity was already Aspen's wholly-owned subsidiary before the May 2025 Kerjaya Prospek transaction was announced — meaning Ivory's 49% stake was fully exited well before Ivory itself was classified PN17 in 2022 and delisted on 2025-10-14. There is currently no counterparty risk to this project stemming from Ivory's PN17/delisting saga (though this conclusion relies mainly on cross-referenced secondary media reporting rather than a direct read of Aspen's SGX filings, and primary-source verification is recommended before further reliance). The transaction completed 2026-06-19 brought in Kerjaya Prospek Ventures (a subsidiary of Bursa-listed Kerjaya Prospek Group) for a fresh 49% stake, injecting credible contractor capital into the project. At the same time, note that Aspen Glove (the group's glove subsidiary) remains in winding-up, with the latest 2026-07-30 disclosure reporting "no material developments," and that Aspen's 1H FY2026 results (reported 2026-02-12) show revenue down 37% YoY (only Versa remains an active project site), a swing to a net loss, and no dividend — reflecting a weaker financial position than before.

On pricing, this is the site's second case of "the same-building transaction is itself the valuation anchor" — Brickz recorded 15 transactions from 2025-03 to 2026-02, with a median PSF of RM499 and median total price of RM590,000. Current asking PSF spans roughly RM426-631, with the midpoint about 6% above the same-building median — a reasonable new-launch premium band, with no clear evidence of significant over- or under-pricing. On rentals, the 1,030 sqft 3-bedroom type is currently asking RM1,500-1,800/month, implying an estimated gross yield of ~3.87% — broadly in line with the 2.7-3.8% gross-yield pattern typical of Penang's prime areas, and this card has confirmed against the site's blocked-facts list that the "5-7% yield for Batu Kawan" figure sometimes attributed to IQI traces to no verifiable source; this card's own computed result is consistent with the internally anchored real range of 3.4-4.6%. It must be honestly disclosed, however, that the project's own iProperty listings show 43 units for sale against 114 for rent, and Batu Kawan overall shows roughly 39 for sale against 356 for rent on PropertyGuru — both far exceed a "normal" rent-to-sale ratio. This is structural rental oversupply, and arguably more worth watching than the merely thin gross yield, since it signals that rental negotiating power sits with tenants throughout the holding period.

On the holding-period structure, mainland residential-tenure units can be short-term let with a 3/4 special-resolution vote, but no case-level evidence was found that Vertu Resort's JMB/MC has passed such a resolution, so no bonus was applied. On exit, the local quantum of RM589k falls in Penang's P43 affordability band — one of the site's rare first-time-buyer-accessible cases, pulling the local exit score up to 7.5 (listing rate ~3.35% is disclosed for directional reference only per ANCHORS §9 item 6 and does not feed this score). On the foreign side, mainland strata levy is always a flat 3% — unlike island strata, which enjoys a 1.5% sweet-spot discount in the RM1-1.5 million band — and this project's exit price of RM590,000 sits squarely in the mainland threshold-boundary band (RM500-600k); combined with the two mandatory deductions for state-consent friction and the three-year non-disposal period, the foreign exit score is just 4.7. The R1/R2 hard rules could not be verified because Batu Kawan is under SPS district (not the already-locked "SPT: 1,271" figure) and district-level NAPIC data could not be obtained — honestly flagged as "unable to verify" rather than assumed triggered or clear.

Key items to watch: (1) all three scarcity layers fail, so the 10-year thesis cannot rest on "location scarcity will lift the exit price" — it should rest on "local-employment-driven tenant depth plus first-time-buyer accessibility"; (2) the project's own rental oversupply (114 units for rent) is the largest structural risk to holding-period cashflow, with no sign of improvement; (3) the developer's weakening financials (revenue -37%, swing to loss) and the unresolved Aspen Glove winding-up warrant continued monitoring; (4) foreign buyers never get a mainland levy sweet spot, and while the 10-year breakeven threshold is below the baseline appreciation rate, the margin is thin — this suits buyers confident in appreciation prospects who can tolerate a weaker rental side; (5) the R1/R2 exit hard rules could not be verified due to a data gap, which does not mean the associated risk has been ruled out — this should be revisited once SPS-level NAPIC data becomes available.

10-Year Scenario Baseline: assuming entry in 2026-08 and exit in 2036-08:
  • Base case: price growth continues at the mainland high-rise baseline of +3.5%/year, rent grows 3%/year with 1 month/year vacancy, BKIP's employment base provides steady rental support but oversupply persists. 10-year cumulative net return of approximately RM94,000 for Malaysian buyers, approximately RM28,000 for foreign buyers (estimated).
  • Bull case: PDC's Byram development accelerates local employment, Versa/Vivo completion rounds out masterplan amenities, price growth at +5.0%/year. 10-year net return of approximately RM220,000 for Malaysian buyers, approximately RM141,000 for foreign buyers (estimated).
  • Bear case: rental oversupply fails to improve, further weakening at Aspen affects confidence in masterplan delivery, price growth only +1.0%/year. 10-year loss of approximately RM82,000 for Malaysian buyers, approximately RM130,000 for foreign buyers (estimated) — this is not an extreme assumption but a reasonable extension of the current dual pressure from structural rental oversupply and the developer's weakening financials.
10-year exit liquidity: Penang has no MLS and no public days-on-market data; this project is read instead through its annual turnover (~1.2%, normal) — its listing rate (~3.35%, iProperty basis) is not counted toward the score per site rules and is disclosed for directional reference only — suggesting reasonable liquidity, though the structural rental-side oversupply (114 units listed for rent) is the main pressure point on holding-period cashflow, not exit liquidity itself.
◆ Conclusion

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • Local quantum of RM589k falls in the ≤RM600k first-time-buyer-accessible band, one of the site's rare entry-friendly cases, pulling the local exit score up to 7.5
  • BKIP's 160+ employers (Boon Siew Honda, Boston Scientific, Broadcom, and others) anchor genuine local employment, providing clear support for both the connectivity and capital-appreciation dimensions
  • Completed ~5 years ago, the only phase among Aspen Vision City's four with real transaction history (15 Brickz transactions, 2025-03 to 2026-02); the same-building transaction itself is a high-confidence valuation anchor, and current asking prices carry only a modest ~6% premium over it
  • 10-year breakeven thresholds are below the mainland high-rise baseline appreciation rate (Malaysian ~2.2%/year, foreign ~3.1%/year vs. a 3.5%/year baseline) — the base case itself clears breakeven
  • Normal annual turnover (~1.2%); listing rate (~3.35%, iProperty basis) is not counted toward the score, disclosed for direction only, and shows no sign of a distressed exit
  • Ivory Properties' historical 49% equity exposure was verified to have been fully exited well before its PN17/delisting saga, so this project carries no current counterparty risk from that event
⚠ Caveats
  • All three scarcity-audit layers fail, and the land-layer failure is the cleanest such case on the site — PDC has already approved a ~1,300-acre Byram land bank releasing ~200 acres/year; the 10-year thesis cannot rest on location scarcity
  • Structural rental oversupply: the project's own iProperty listings show 43 for sale against 114 for rent, with the wider area showing roughly 39 for sale against 356 for rent — rental negotiating power sits with tenants, and there is no sign of improvement
  • Gross yield is estimated at only ~3.87% — the cashflow case itself is thin, and 10-year returns depend heavily on whether appreciation materializes
  • Developer Aspen's 1H FY2026 revenue fell 37% YoY, swung to a net loss, and no dividend was declared; the Aspen Glove subsidiary's winding-up remains unresolved ("no material developments" as of 2026-07-30)
  • Foreign buyers never get a mainland strata levy sweet spot (always flat 3%), and this project's exit price sits squarely in the threshold-boundary band (RM500-600k) — foreign exit score is only 4.7, with a thin 10-year breakeven margin
  • The R1/R2 exit hard rules could not be verified due to a NAPIC data gap (Batu Kawan is SPS, not SPT) — this does not mean the associated risk has been ruled out; buyers should treat this as a data gap, not a conclusion

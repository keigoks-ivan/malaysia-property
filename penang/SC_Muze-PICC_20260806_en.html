Muze @ PICC

Hunza's self-built ecosystem (its own Gurney Paragon Mall plus PICC) props up a Brickz median of RM 1,053 psf — nearly double the surrounding Bayan Baru/Sungai Ara norm (roughly RM 500 psf) — with strong turnover and listing depth. But all three scarcity layers are non-scarce, and Senze (1,678 units), completing on the same PICC site by end-2026, is the most direct near-neighbour supply stress test this card faces. This is also the first card on the site to genuinely earn an LRT Mutiara progress bonus (roughly 600m from Bukit Jambul station, estimated).

Under base assumptions (3%/year appreciation, fully let for 10 years less 1 month/year vacancy, 5%/year opportunity cost), the foreign buyer's 10-year net P&L is about RM 81,000 lower than the Malaysian buyer's (about 7.1% of the purchase price). The 8% stamp duty, 1.5% levy (RM 1.00-1.50mil band), RM 10,000 consent fee, three-year resale ban, 30% non-resident rental tax, and permanent 10% RPGT together form a structural premium (total entry friction ~11.4%, consistent with Penang's state-level anchor). Breakeven threshold: the Malaysian buyer needs only about 1.8%/year appreciation to break even (below the island residential strata baseline of 3.0%/year), while the foreign buyer needs about 2.4%/year (also below the baseline, but with thinner headroom). In short: this project's cashflow is thin (gross yield only ~3.5%), but the breakeven bar is low — the thesis should rest on "product-premium-driven high liquidity plus an easy breakeven," not on location scarcity or a high-cashflow story.
◆ Exit reality check

Who buys it in 10 years

Three checks: scarcity audit (can supply be replicated) + next-buyer pyramid (who can afford it in 10 years) + secondary-market hard evidence (turnover / listing rate / discount). Returns are only real the day you sell — the floor on exit price is set by scarcity, and exit speed is set by the depth of the next-buyer pool.

◆ Deep-dive narrative

Project deep dive

Muze @ PICC sits at the core of Bayan Baru on Persiaran Mahsuri — a twin-tower freehold residential condominium completed in August 2023 by Hunza Properties Berhad (private, delisted from Bursa in 2016, with a nearly 40-year track record and 10,000+ units delivered cumulatively). Tower A is 52 floors with 394 units and Tower B is 58 floors with 452 units, for 846 units total — Phase 1 of the residential component within Penang International Commercial City (PICC)'s 43.35-acre, three-phase masterplan. Originally slated for 2022 completion, it actually finished about a year later, a mild delay that doesn't warrant an additional score deduction.

On location, Bayan Baru/Sungai Ara starts at 6.0 in the Penang anchor table, and all three scarcity-audit layers (land, product, regulatory) come back ✗: PICC's land and supply pipeline are far from capped — Senze @ PICC (Phase 2, three towers, 1,678 service-residence units) on the same site completes end-2026, and the commercial hub (mall, GBS office tower, 5-star hotel) is slated to break ground Q4 2026 and finish in 2031; on the product layer, 846 units across twin standard high-rise towers are highly replicable, not least by Senze right next door; and the regulatory layer has flat terrain with no hillside or heritage constraints. This is the central tension this card had to resolve: Muze's own Brickz transacted median hits RM 1,053 psf (51 transactions, Feb 2025 to Jan 2026) — nearly double The Promenade (Bayan Baru, Brickz median RM 515 psf), the general Sungai Ara range (RM 407–572 psf), and Bayan Baru's overall average (~RM 500 psf). But that premium's source is not location scarcity (all three layers ✗, the location itself is highly replicable) — it's a product premium from Hunza's self-built ecosystem (its own Gurney Paragon Mall operating experience plus PICC's planned integrated retail/medical/hospitality) and the twin-landmark product positioning. This card deliberately keeps the scarcity dimension from absorbing that premium, holding it at the 6.0 anchor and leaving recognition of the premium to the developer and valuation-discipline dimensions instead.

The twin towers total 846 units across 10 layouts from 3-bedroom 1,087 sqft to 5-bedroom 1,862 sqft (Type A–H) — the project has no 2-bedroom units at all, which conflicts with the Penang anchor table's default rule that non-prime districts like Bayan Baru should use a 2-bedroom representative unit. Following the higher-order principle of pulling sizes only from the developer's real product menu and never fabricating one, this card instead uses the smallest, most entry-priced type, Type G1 (3-bedroom, 1,087 sqft), and flags this exception explicitly. On amenities, PICC's own planned mall/medical centre has not broken ground (Q4 2026 start, 2031 completion); today's walkable amenities are still anchored by existing Sunshine Square (~832m) and Pantai Hospital Penang (same junction on Persiaran Mahsuri/Jalan Tengah) — this card does not pre-credit PICC's not-yet-existing commercial amenities. The LRT Mutiara Line currently stands at 8.99% progress (2026-06), and the nearest station to this project, Bukit Jambul (S09), is roughly 600m away by coordinate-based straight-line estimate (not a walking-distance measurement) — this falls within the ≤800m bonus threshold, making this the first card on the site to genuinely earn an LRT Mutiara progress bonus, though there's a gap between this estimation method's precision and Hunza's own marketing claim that PICC is "directly connected to an LRT station," which this card discloses openly.

On the developer, Hunza owns Gurney Paragon Mall and PICC outright — a rare Penang case of a developer running its own downstream retail/medical/hospitality assets, which contributes to Muze's product premium. Two governance items are worth flagging: first, on 2026-02-03 the Court of Appeal ruled, in Badan Pengurusan Bersama Gurney Paragon Residential v Hunza Properties, that Hunza's split residential/commercial management structure at Gurney Paragon was void ab initio, requiring the JMB to manage the whole development under a single account — that case concerned Gurney Paragon, not Muze, and the ruling doesn't directly touch this project, but PICC shares the same mixed-use structure, and once the commercial hub completes around 2031 a similarly bifurcated management structure would fall squarely within this ruling's reach, which is why this card treats it as a forward-looking governance risk rather than an event that has already occurred; second, Hunza has published no public financial reports since delisting in 2016, leaving its financial position opaque.

On pricing, this project is one of the rare cases on the site where the building's own Brickz record is itself a high-confidence transacted anchor — 51 transactions (Feb 2025 to Jan 2026) at a median PSF of RM 1,053 and median total price of RM 1,270,000 (implying a median size of roughly 1,206 sqft, clearly pulled up by larger-unit transactions). The project's overall asking PSF range is roughly RM 736–968, and within that, the representative unit's size bracket (1,087–1,098 sqft, the smallest in the project) shows an asking median of roughly RM 760 psf — below the whole-building Brickz median, but not a like-for-like comparison; this gap more likely reflects a unit-mix effect (smaller units carrying inherently lower psf than larger or higher-floor units) than genuine underpricing, which is why this card's valuation-discipline confidence is marked "low." On rent, the representative unit's estimated monthly rent is roughly RM 3,400 (derived from an asking rate of RM 3.10 psf), for an estimated gross yield of ~3.5%, sitting in the middle of Penang prime districts' typical 2.7–3.8% gross yield range; these yield figures remain portal-derived estimates, not institutional data, and are labelled "estimated" throughout.

On the holding structure, freehold residential tenure means R4 applies: this card verified that Muze is freehold residential condominium (not commercial SA/SoHo — consistently labelled as such by PropertyGuru, iProperty, Wikipedia, and Hunza's own site), so short-term-rental/Airbnb income cannot be counted at all, and MBPP's short-term-rental restriction on residential-titled units applies in full. On exit, the local buyer's quantum of RM 1.145mil falls in Penang's P75–P90 income band closer to P75, a solid upper-middle local pool; this project's standout feature is its liquidity — annual turnover of ~6.0% (scored) and Brickz depth of 51 transactions both outperform the other Penang cards on this site, pushing the local exit score up to 7.8 (the highest on the board so far); listing rate of only ~5.2% (iProperty) is disclosed but not scored, per ANCHORS Known Data Gap #6. On the foreign side, Barat Daya district's 883 completed-unsold units trigger the R1 hard rule, capping the foreign exit score at 6.0 (this project's computed value of 5.0 doesn't yet reach that cap); combined with the representative unit's price falling exactly in the "threshold-edge RM 1.00–1.20mil" band (close to the RM 1mil foreign minimum, −0.3) and Bayan Baru's positioning as a tenant market rather than a foreign-owner market, foreign investor appetite for this project is structurally thin.

Key things to watch: (1) all three scarcity layers are ✗, so the 10-year thesis cannot rest on "location scarcity will push up the exit price" — it should rest on "product premium plus high liquidity" instead; (2) Senze (1,678 units, completing end-2026) on the same PICC site is by far the most direct, largest piece of near-neighbour new supply, and its absorption and rental-market squeeze should be monitored throughout the 10-year hold; (3) this ~3-year-old completion's high turnover (6.0%) may partly reflect a one-off flip wave from early investors exiting after VP, and whether it persists at this level needs continued observation; (4) Intel has resumed operations but hasn't disclosed expansion headcount and has already reneged once, so it should not be treated as locked-in rental demand; (5) Hunza's governance items (the forward-looking risk from the split-management ruling, and no public financials since 2016) need ongoing tracking; (6) gross yield is only ~3.5%, so this project suits a thesis centred on liquidity and turnover speed rather than high cashflow.

◆ Bottom line

Highlights & caveats

✦ Highlights
  • One of the strongest secondary-market liquidity readings on the site: ~6.0% annual turnover (scored) and 51-transaction Brickz depth, both notably better than other Penang cards in this batch; ~5.2% listing rate (iProperty) is disclosed but not scored
  • Hunza's self-built ecosystem (its own Gurney Paragon Mall plus PICC) drives a clear product premium — Brickz median RM 1,053 psf, nearly double the surrounding area, backed by real transactions
  • Completed ~3 years ago, with the building's own Brickz record serving as a high-confidence real-transaction valuation anchor (51 transactions, a deep sample)
  • Bayan Baru to FIZ is only ~4 minutes, to the airport ~13 minutes — strong access to the E&E employment base
  • First card on the site to genuinely earn an LRT Mutiara progress bonus (~600m from Bukit Jambul station, estimated)
  • 10-year breakeven bar is not high: ~1.8%/year for Malaysian buyers, ~2.4%/year for foreign buyers, both below the island residential strata baseline of 3.0%/year

  • Senze (1,678 units) on the same PICC site is due to complete end-2026, the most direct near-neighbour supply pressure this project faces — its absorption and rental-market squeeze warrant ongoing monitoring
  • Gross yield is only ~3.5%, on the thin side for Penang prime districts; R4 applies — this residential-titled project cannot count any short-term-rental/Airbnb income

  • Barat Daya district's completed-unsold stock has reached 883 units (triggering R1), structurally pressuring the foreign exit score (capped at 6.0, current value 5.0); the representative unit's price falls in the foreign threshold-edge band (RM 1.00–1.20mil), narrowing the foreign buyer pool

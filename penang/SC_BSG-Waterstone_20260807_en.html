BSG Waterstone

Waterstone is a low-density twin-tower project (365 units, only 6 per floor) in Tanjung Bungah's interior "Hillside" precinct. Its developer, BSG Property (an Oriental Holdings subsidiary), is the only developer on this site's anchor table carrying a penalty for an incident located in the same district as the project it is developing. This card traced the developer SPV, Taman Sri Bunga Sdn Bhd, and confirmed it is not merely the same corporate family but the identical legal entity behind the 2017 landslide that killed 11 (Granito @ Permai) — yet, given the Commission of Inquiry's formal finding that engineers, not the developer, were responsible, this card concludes no additional case-level penalty is warranted. Reasoning in full under Developer & Handover. This project is also itself one of the three confirmed new-supply items the sister Azuria card used to fail Tanjung Bungah's land-layer scarcity test — this card owns that fact rather than claiming scarcity for being new.

Developer & Handover10% · shared
6.7

This is the core analytical deliverable of this card, and it involves two separate rulings that must not be conflated. BSG Property (an Oriental Holdings subsidiary) is anchored at 7.0 — this baseline already fully prices in −0.5 for the 2017 Tanjung Bungah landslide that killed 11 (Commission of Inquiry blamed the engineers, not the developer), the only case on this site's anchor table where a developer's penalty-triggering incident and a subsequent project's location coincide.
Ruling one (the historical incident, not double-counted): the 2017 landslide occurred near Lorong Lembah Permai 3, at a project named Granito @ Permai (980 units, 2 towers of 49 storeys, in Permai Village), developed by Taman Sri Bunga Sdn Bhd, with main building contractor BSG Global Builders Sdn Bhd (SSM registration 201301000758). The state Commission of Inquiry formally ruled that all 15 identified acts of negligence were primarily the responsibility of the hired consulting engineer (Khoo Koon Tai), and explicitly absolved both the developer and the main contractor; that project was ultimately completed in 2019. This card verified corporate registry data (Intender.my) confirming Taman Sri Bunga Sdn Bhd is a subsidiary of BSG Property and a member of the Boon Siew Group; it then cross-verified, via multiple independent sources including the project's own engineering-consultant portfolio page (uqs.com.my), that Waterstone's developing entity is the same Taman Sri Bunga Sdn Bhd — this is not a loose "same corporate family" relationship but the identical legal entity that developed both the 2017 incident project and this one. This card rules against stacking any additional penalty for this incident, for three reasons: (1) the Commission of Inquiry's ruling was formal, explicit, and named a specific responsible party; (2) this card found no adverse fact specific to this project — no stop-work order, no DOE objection on record, no reported slope-engineering dispute; (3) this differs from the −0.2 case-level ruling on the Gurney Paragon card, where that project was itself the unresolved, ongoing subject of litigation — this project instead relates to an already-closed matter with a formal exculpatory finding, at a different physical parcel (Permai Village, not this project's site).
Ruling two (the anchor table's independent Penang-specific deduction #3, "hillside exposure, −0.3 to −0.5," applied separately): this item does not penalize the developer's historical record — it penalizes this project's own current hillside exposure (regulatory-change risk, engineering risk, approval-pathway risk), which is a different fact from ruling one's −0.5 and can co-exist without double-counting. This card located a 2019-2020 residents' petition on Change.org naming "Tetuan Taman Sri Bunga Sdn Bhd (Boon Siew Group SB)" as the proponent of a 19.08-acre development between Jalan Sungai Kelian and Jalan Loh Poh Heng, Hillside — an address description that matches this project's own site exactly — comprising two 50-storey towers, one 49-storey tower, one 34-storey LMC tower, and 21 houses, totaling 914 units at a density of 49 units per acre, more than three times the Penang State Structure Plan 2007's 15-units-per-acre cap for Tanjung Bungah. This card judges that this proposal, the same SPV's separate October 2020 proposal for 342 units on an adjacent parcel, and this project's current 365-unit scheme are most plausibly different-scale phases drawn from the same 19.08-acre land bank — evidence that this SPV has a real, contested high-density development history on this exact Hillside land, one that drew a formal residents' petition specifically over density and hillside carrying capacity. This card could not, however, locate precise contour or gradient survey data for this project's own site, nor confirm whether its final approved scheme used the "special project" exemption route described under R5 (56 such exemptions granted statewide 2008-2015). Consistent with this framework's standing practice — take the conservative value when precise data is unavailable, and disclose that this reflects a data gap rather than a confirmed finding — this card applies the lower end of the range, −0.3 (not the upper −0.5, since the possibility that this specific site sits on a gentler slope below the regulated contour cannot be excluded), and explicitly flags that this is a conservative estimate based on "same SPV + Hillside precinct + documented density controversy," not a confirmed finding that this project's own site is non-compliant or used a special exemption. Net: 7.0−0.3=6.7. Both rulings point to the same recommendation: this project has no publicly disclosed, independent slope-stability or geotechnical report of its own, and this card flags in the caveats and data-gap disclosure that buyers should request one from the developer before signing, specifically confirming whether the site falls within the regulated 76m/25° slope-control zone, whether any special-project exemption route was used, and whether the final approved unit count is consistent with this project's stated 365 units.

◆ Deep Dive

Project Deep Dive

BSG Waterstone sits on Jalan Sungai Kelian, a low-density twin-tower project in Tanjung Bungah's interior "Hillside" precinct — two towers of 39 storeys (some sources cite 40-45, the official figure of 39 is used here), totaling 365 units at only 6 per floor, sized 1,334-2,195 sqft (3-4 bedroom family units), on a pure-residential freehold title, targeting completion in Q4 2028. The developer, BSG Property, is the property arm of Oriental Holdings (Bursa Main Board-listed since 1964); the project entity is the SPV Taman Sri Bunga Sdn Bhd. Official marketing splits pricing by view — hillview from RM1.2M, sea view from RM1.4M — and explicitly targets families, retirees, and MM2H buyers, emphasizing proximity to POWIIS Tanjung Bungah Campus, three carparks per unit, and an infinity pool facing the Straits of Malacca.

On scarcity, this card deliberately confronts a structural tension: this project is itself one of the three confirmed new-supply items the sister Azuria card used to fail the Tanjung Bungah corridor's land-layer test. Under the three-layer audit, the land layer fails — that this project could move from a 2018 proposal to on-sale by 2026 is itself the clearest evidence that this Hillside land was never locked; this card traced the site back to a 2020 proposal by the same developer SPV on an adjacent parcel for a 342-unit affordable-housing scheme, later repositioned into the current market-rate product, showing the land supply here is flexible and re-programmable. Net of this project, the corridor still carries two confirmed pipeline items, Ascott Residences and the Kerjaya Prospek JV. The product layer also fails: "only 6 units per floor" is a marketing choice rather than a technical impossibility to replicate, and with no completion yet there is no Brickz record to verify any real demand premium. The regulatory layer, per R5, clears only a pass: the current 76m/25° slope rule remains in force but the state is proposing to relax it, and this project's own hillview/sea-view price split evidences real elevation change on site. None of the three layers clears the bonus threshold, landing d1 at 6.5, a 0.3-point discount from the district anchor of 6.8 — identical to the sister Azuria card.

This card's most consequential case-level finding sits in the Developer dimension, and it splits into two separate rulings. The anchor table already folds a −0.5 penalty into BSG Property for the 2017 Tanjung Bungah landslide that killed 11 (a Commission of Inquiry blamed the consulting engineer, not the developer), and this project happens to sit in Tanjung Bungah. Exhaustive verification found that the incident project's developer, Taman Sri Bunga Sdn Bhd, is the identical legal entity developing this project — not a loosely related "same corporate family," but the same SPV behind both. This card's judgment is that this tighter fact does not warrant an additional quantitative penalty for the 2017 incident itself: the Commission of Inquiry's ruling was formal, explicit, and named a specific responsible party, and this card found no project-specific adverse evidence — no stop-work order, no DOE objection, no slope dispute. This differs in kind from the Gurney Paragon card's −0.2 case-level ruling: that project was itself the unresolved subject of ongoing litigation, a live economic mechanism; this project instead relates to a closed matter with a formal exculpatory finding, at a different physical parcel (Permai Village, not this project's own site). But this is not this project's only slope-related finding. This card separately located a residents' Change.org petition naming the same SPV, Taman Sri Bunga Sdn Bhd, as the proponent of a 19.08-acre proposal — on a parcel "between Jalan Sungai Kelian and Jalan Loh Poh Heng, Hillside" that matches this project's own address exactly — for 914 units at 49 units/acre, more than three times Tanjung Bungah's legal density cap of 15 units/acre. This is the independent trigger for the anchor table's Penang-specific §5 deduction #3, "hillside exposure, −0.3 to −0.5," which penalizes this project's own current site exposure rather than the developer's historical record, and can co-exist with the −0.5 above without double-counting. Because this card could not locate precise gradient data for this project's own site or confirmation of a "special project" exemption route, it applies the framework's standard conservative floor of −0.3, landing d5 at 6.7 (7.0−0.3), and flags in the caveats and data-gap disclosure that this project has no publicly disclosed independent slope-engineering report of its own, and buyers should request confirmation from the developer before signing.

On pricing, this off-plan project has no same-building Brickz transactions, so it falls back to Tanjung Bungah's resale median (528 psf) as a low-confidence anchor — the representative unit, Type D2/E2 at 1,334 sqft, is officially priced around RM1,287,000 (≈965 psf), a roughly +83% premium, though this is fundamentally a new-off-plan-versus-old-resale comparison that mechanically overstates the true premium; cross-checked against recent STP2/Andaman corridor launches (The Lume ~1,280 psf, Maris ~1,100+ psf), this project's pricing sits below that corridor's new-launch level. On the holding side, as an island residential-title property R4 applies: no short-term rental income may be counted, and the official marketing makes no such claims. Estimated gross yield is only about 2.8% (low confidence, proxy rent), though the management fee is unusually sourced from the developer's own official quote (RM0.30-0.35 psf inclusive of sinking fund), better than this framework's usual KL placeholder — though it remains an off-plan sales-stage figure rather than an operating JMB's actual number.

On exit, the quantum of RM1.287M falls in the P83-85 band, excluding local first-time buyers and most general upgraders, giving a Malaysian-buyer exit score of 6.4 (quantum −0.3, R2 under-construction unsold −0.3); the foreign-buyer side benefits from the levy sweet spot (+0.3) and MM2H compliance (+0.3, a segment the official marketing itself targets), and after the two mandatory frictions of state consent and the 3-year resale ban, nets to 4.5, a 1.9-point gap that satisfies the invariant. The project's Timur Laut district carries 973 completed-unsold and 1,618 under-construction-unsold units, triggering both R1 and R2. This is the first card on this site sitting entirely in the off-plan stage — secondary liquidity, real transaction premiums, and actual management-fee levels will all remain unverifiable until completion in 2028.

Highlights & Caveats

✦ Highlights
  • Low-density twin towers (only 6 units per floor, 365 total), 3 carparks per unit, pure-residential freehold title with lower residential utility tariffs

  • Management fee unusually sourced from the developer's own official quote (RM0.30-0.35 psf inclusive of sinking fund), better than the KL placeholder most Penang cards on this site rely on
  • Official pricing (PSF 810-999) undercuts recent launches in the comparably-positioned STP2/Andaman north-coast corridor (The Lume ~1,280, Maris ~1,100+ psf), not adopting the island's most aggressive new-launch pricing
⚠ Caveats
  • [Off-plan risk] Completing 2028 Q4, more than 2.3 years out; secondary liquidity, real transaction premiums, and actual management-fee levels cannot be verified before completion — d4 already applies the "off-plan 2+ years −0.3" deduction
  • [Developer SPV link to the 2017 incident] This project's developing entity, Taman Sri Bunga Sdn Bhd, is the identical legal entity behind the 2017 Tanjung Bungah landslide project (Granito @ Permai); the Commission of Inquiry formally blamed the consulting engineer and absolved the developer, and this card ruled against a duplicate penalty on d5 for this specific incident
  • [Hillside exposure, anchor-table §5 independent deduction, −0.3 already applied] The same SPV proposed a 19.08-acre, 914-unit scheme on this project's own parcel ("Jalan Sungai Kelian / Jalan Loh Poh Heng, Hillside") at more than three times Tanjung Bungah's legal density cap, per a documented residents' petition; this card could not locate this project's own gradient data or confirm any "special project" exemption route, so d5 has been discounted to 6.7 — buyers are strongly advised to request this project's own independent slope-stability / geotechnical report before signing, and confirm whether the site falls within the 76m/25° regulated zone
  • [Land layer fails, this project is itself part of the supply] Beyond this project, the Tanjung Bungah corridor still has two confirmed new-supply pipelines, Ascott Residences (2028) and the Kerjaya Prospek JV — corridor supply pressure over the 10-year hold is a real threat
  • [R1/R2] Timur Laut is the heaviest-supplied district on the island: 973 completed-unsold units trigger the foreign exit cap (not binding here), and 1,618 under-construction-unsold units trigger the Malaysian exit deduction
  • [R4] Island residential title, MBPP prohibits short-term rental; no short-term-income marketing claims found on the official site or agent pages; estimated gross yield only ~2.8% (low confidence)
